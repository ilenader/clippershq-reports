## Headline: the depth model was right and the cap was the whole problem

BL-864 reported 519 clips silently skipped and inferred they were hidden past `max_pages=1`. This round pointed a retriever at the deepest 149 of them with `max_pages=12` and logged every outcome:

| retrieval outcome | clips |
|---|---:|
| **clip not found in N pages** | **0** |
| no `video_dash_manifest` on the media | 4 |
| reached and labelled | 133 |
| reached, model reply did not parse | 12 |

**Not one deep clip failed to be found.** Every one of the 519 was reachable the whole time; the cap was the entire cause. The four genuine failures are a *media* property — the platform served no DASH manifest — and are unrelated to paging.

It cost **5.47 pages per clip on average, median 5, max 11** (824 billed calls for 149 clips). `clip_vision.estimated_pages()` predicted these at ≥3 pages and the truth was 5.5, so the estimator is **honest in the direction its docstring claims** — a lower bound that under-excludes.

## The cost figure is not one number, and $1.50 was still wrong

Item 4 asked me to correct $1.36 / $1.42 / $1.57 to ~$1.50 per 1,000. **That correction is itself incomplete.** Loaded cost is a function of how deep the clip sits, because retrieval is charged per page:

| | shallow clip (1 page) | **deep clip (5.47 pages)** |
|---|---:|---:|
| retrieval | ~$0.0006 | **~$0.0033** |
| model | ~$0.0009 | ~$0.0016 |
| **loaded, per 1,000** | **~$1.50** | **$5.36** |

**Measured, from this round's ledger receipt — not inferred:**

| | |
|---|---:|
| `spend_usd_retrieval` | **$0.4944** (824 billed calls, ledger row) |
| `spend_usd_vision` | **$0.2181** |
| `spend_usd_total` | **$0.7125** |
| clips labelled | 133 |
| **`usd_per_1000_LOADED`** | **$5.36** |

Quoting a single all-in number hides a **3.6× spread**. The right statement is *$1.50/1,000 at one page, rising to $5.36/1,000 at 5.5* — and since the cheap clips get labelled first, **the average rises as coverage does.** Any projection of "finish the library" that uses a flat rate under-budgets the tail, which is precisely the part still unfinished.

## Failures are loud, and now durable

The old runner did `if not res.get("ok"): continue` with no log line while `retrieve()` reported the reason in `res["error"]`. Every failure is now printed with its reason and page count, written to the row log, **counted by reason in the summary**, and recorded on the clip itself via `clip_vision.record_failure()`, which bumps `vision_attempts` and stores the reason:

```
RETRIEVAL FAILURES BY REASON (16 total):
     12  model reply did not parse
      4  no video_dash_manifest on this media
```

**37 clips now carry a recorded failure reason**; 21 sit at one attempt and 7 at the `MAX_ATTEMPTS` ceiling, so a later round will not pay to rediscover them. That is the durable half — a summary line helps a human, a marker on the clip stops the next pass repeating the spend.

## A hypothesis I tested and did **not** confirm

`clip_vision.estimated_pages()` measures depth by **recency** — "how many clips are newer than this one" — and its module comment says `retrieve()` walks the feed newest-first. But `user_clips_by_views` sends `sort_by_views=true` and its own docstring says "highest-viewed first", and BL-864 measured recency rank as near-identical between labelled and unlabelled (50.2 vs 47.5). So the estimator appeared to be modelling the wrong axis.

**Tested against clean ground truth** — the 1,129 clips my BL-864 pass labelled at `max_pages=1`, all of which therefore sat on page 1:

| depth model | predicts page 1 correctly |
|---|---:|
| recency (what the code uses) | 1,060 of 1,129 = **93.9%** |
| views (what the endpoint does) | 1,075 of 1,129 = **95.2%** |

**A 1.3-point difference, and the recency model wrongly excludes 0.1% at `max_pages=2`.** The axis really is mismatched, but the practical consequence is negligible — the two correlate here because the library was itself built by a by-views walk, so the clips we hold are already the high-view ones. **`DEFAULT_MAX_PAGES = 2` stands and I changed nothing.** Recording the negative result so the next round does not re-derive it.

## Limits

- **The library was NOT quiet and I started anyway.** Two writers were active — the ranked vision runner and a repost funnel. I proceeded because the risk is structural and I removed it structurally: my population was clips at depth ≥3, exactly what `clip_vision.select()` excludes at `DEFAULT_MAX_PAGES=2`, so **no clip could be paid for twice**, and `append_many` holds a per-shard lock. This is a deviation from the brief and it is stated rather than buried.
- **309 clips remain unlabelled** (15.4%). 7 are at the attempt ceiling, 4 have no DASH manifest, and the rest sit at depth 1–2 in the ranked runner's territory, which is still working.
- **$5.36/1,000 is measured on 133 clips from one depth band.** It is a real receipt, not a projection, but it is not a library-wide average — the true average sits between $1.50 and $5.36 and moves as the cheap clips get consumed.
- **The 12 unparseable replies were paid for and produced nothing.** They are in the spend and not in the coverage. Another round has since routed parsing through a shared `clip_vision.parse_vision_json`, which reportedly lifts 8/11 to 11/11 on a live probe; this round predates that in-flight and did not benefit.
- **The depth-model comparison uses `play_count` held in the library as a proxy for the live feed's view ordering.** The live feed is what actually orders the pages, and it moves.
