**This is the part that saves you a decision.** The **19.0%** is *your* want rate among
discovered pages — the fraction of what the funnel finds that you actually want. It is a fact
about the pages out there and about the seeds that find them. **No answer to either question can

So neither answer moves the arithmetic behind the cost target:

| | |
|---|---|
| $3.00 per 1,000 needs | a **40.8%** want rate |
| you are at | **19.0%** |
| 81% of every dollar | lands on pages you reject |
| **effect of Q1 or Q2 on any of the above** | **none** |

What the answers *do* move is the judge's **own** pass rate — 21.0% today on the 100, down to
20.0% (Q1 *yes*) or 19.0% (Q2 *no*). Both answers make the judge **stricter**. If the judge is
ever used to gate spend, a stricter judge rejects some pages you want (`footyeight`,
`killerxftbl07`), so on the 623 evidence a *no* would cost supply without buying accuracy.

**Both of these are accuracy questions, not cost questions.** They are worth answering to stop
the judge being wrong on pages you have already marked. They are not a route to $3.00.

## 5. The sheet, and how it was verified

`output/bl1289_questions/index.html` — **a subdirectory, never `output/` itself**, because a
top-level `output/*.csv` is a send surface the send guard scans, and deliverables written there
have turned two guards red.

Per page it shows: the grid at full width (click to open the original), the exact lines the
judge read, the judge's verdict and its **written reason verbatim**, which question the page
turns on and *why*, a clickable profile link, and whether the judge currently agrees with you.

| check | result |
|---|---|
| cards | **19** — 3 under Q1, 16 under Q2 |
| grid images resolved on disk | **19 of 19**, none zero-byte |
| profile links well-formed | **19 of 19** (Instagram or TikTok, matching the page's platform) |
| external requests | **0** |
| written into a subdirectory, not `output/` | yes |

**The marking was tested, not assumed.** The page's real script was run under node v24 against a
DOM and `localStorage` stub: click Q1, click Q2, mark two pages, download, mark one more.
Counter went `0/0 → 1/1 → 2/2 → 4/4 → 4/0` (after download) `→ 5/1`. A reload from a seeded
store restored every button and reported `saved=4 waiting=2` correctly.

**And that test found a bug, which is the reason for doing it.** The first build compared
timestamps with `Date.now()`, so a mark made in the **same millisecond** as a download counted as
already-downloaded and the bar read **"0 waiting"** while an answer existed only in the browser.
That is the false-green shape that cost 50 marks once, rebuilt in a new place. Replaced with a
monotonic sequence number, which cannot tie. If `localStorage` itself ever throws, the counter
switches to **"SAVE FAILED - download now"** and the bar turns red — it never fails quietly.

**Browser check: blocked, not skipped.** Chrome refused `file://` and then showed an error page
for `http://127.0.0.1:8791` while `curl` returned 200 for the same URLs — an extension
site-permission limit. I stopped rather than dig, and verified structurally and functionally
instead. **The sheet has not been rendered in a browser by me**; if anything looks wrong when you
open it, that is why.
