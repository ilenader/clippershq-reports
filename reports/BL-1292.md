## 3b. The defect you intuited — and you were right

You said you did not believe the 97. Here is exactly why you were right.

**The marks file does not store what you think it stores.** For each page it stores:

* the handle,
* **the judge's own verdict at the time** (the v4 judge), and
* **your GOOD or BAD button press on that verdict.**

**It does not store a reason. There is no reason field at all — not on one of the 100 rows.**

So "the right answer" for a page is not something you wrote. It is **v4's answer, wherever you
pressed GOOD** (97 pages), and **the opposite of v4's answer, wherever you pressed BAD**
(3 pages: `acog.hitbox`, `maestremoros`, `thedunkcentralhq`).

That makes the score a piece of arithmetic rather than a measurement:

> **score = 97 − (pages where the new judge disagrees with v4 and you had pressed GOOD)
> + (pages where it disagrees and you had pressed BAD)**
>
> **v4: 97 − 0 + 0 = 97.  v8: 97 − 9 + 1 = 89.** The identity reproduces both exactly (BL-1290).

**A judge that copied v4 word for word would score 97 without looking at a single picture.**
The only way to beat 97 is to flip exactly those three pages. **97 was not the judge being
good. It was the judge being unchanged.**

This does not mean v8 is right and v4 wrong. **It means the file cannot tell you.** A silent
button press confirming a verdict is not the same as a written reason, and only the second one
can settle a disagreement.

## 3c. What a real measurement needs

Four things, none of them exotic:

1. **A reason on every mark.** *Why* is this page wrong — dialogue, still pictures, not a repost
   page, wrong language? Without it, a disagreement cannot be attributed to a rule.
2. **Marks made without seeing the judge's answer first.** If the verdict is on screen when you
   press the button, the button measures agreement with the screen.
3. **Pages the rules were not written on.** A rule tuned on the same 100 pages it is scored on
   is memorised, not measured. (`docs/THRESHOLDS.md`: of 25 tuned numbers, **21 have no
   held-out number at all**, and all 4 that do were **refuted** by it.)
4. **The brief must not name the pages it will be scored on.** Ten of the fourteen briefs on
   disk name pages from your marks **with your verdict and your reasoning written out** — a
   judge reading one of those is being told the answer. This is live: see §4 item 2.

**768 is the honest number**, and it already assumes the machine judges 24 hours a day and that
discovery can feed it 33,333 pages daily — which has never been demonstrated. The bottleneck is
judging, and no filter helps: the recency rate and the email rate are properties of the
population, not of speed.

### $3.00 per 1,000 Instagram pages — **not reachable on this architecture**

At 1.923 purchases per walked page, one walked page costs **$0.0012253**. For 1,000 wanted
pages to cost $3.00 you would have to walk only 2,448 of them, which means a want rate of
**40.8%**.

**You are at 19.0%.** You would have to more than double the quality of what discovery brings
in, and **no filter can do that** — a filter can only remove pages, and every filter tested
removes wanted ones too.

**$3.00 is reachable, but only by changing the order of operations** (§4 item 1), which lands
at **$0.78** — clearing the target by 3.8×. Not by tuning anything.

### 1,000 pages per million tokens — **below the physical floor**

Asking a model to look at a picture and answer costs about **1,232 tokens** no matter what.
That alone caps you at **811.7 pages per million** — even with an infinitely large batch and
zero overhead. **Your target is 23% above the floor of the thing itself.**

And 811.7 is the impossible case. **Today, at 8 pages per agent, it is 117 pages per million.**
Reaching even 300 would need batches of ~32, four times beyond anything whose accuracy has been
measured.
