# BL-1333 — The funnel is 4.4× faster, and the ceiling everyone quoted was ours

**The short version.** The TikTok funnel went from **11.63 to 51.00 pages a minute**, measured
back to back in the same minute. Three things did it: the lane pool was switched on, the contact
sheet's twelve cover fetches were parallelised, and OCR — which has changed **zero verdicts on
this path** since it was wired — stopped running and gave back **32.9 seconds a page**.

And a number this project has quoted for two rounds turns out to be about us, not the vendor.
BL-1325 measured a plateau at 8 workers with 16 *worse*, and read it as LamaTok's ceiling. The
client shares one `requests.Session` and **mounted no connection pool at all**, so every lane
ran against urllib3's default of 10. With the pool sized: **32 lanes measures 432–535
profiles/min against 8 lanes' 130–143.**

The spend cap could be overspent by **up to 3×** and now cannot. Two defects nobody had found —
a ledger double-write under lanes, and a `--dry-run` that bills — are fixed too.

---

## 1. The lanes, and the ceiling that was ours

### 1.1 What was measured, and the two sweeps I threw away

My first two concurrency sweeps ran every arm over **the same 24 handles in ascending worker
order**. That is a warming confound, and the numbers show it plainly:

| | 16 workers |
|---|---:|
| sweep 1 (order 1, 8, 16 — 16 ran **last**) | 272.20 profiles/min |
| sweep 2 (order 16, 24, 32, 48 — 16 ran **first**) | 140.39 profiles/min |

Same code, same handles, minutes apart, **1.94× apart**, and the faster reading is the one whose
handles had already been fetched twice. p50 also fell to **0.268 s** at 48 workers — *faster than
any single request at one worker* (2.274 s), which no amount of concurrency can do to a cold
fetch. That is a cache, not a speed-up. **Sweep 1's headline was not established by sweep 1.**

So sweep 3 used **distinct handles per arm** and **interleaved order** (8/32/8/32), two blocks
per worker count so within-arm spread sits next to between-arm difference. At n=24 it returned
**NOT ESTABLISHED** — the spread of a single arm against itself (105 and 124) was larger than the
gap between arms (95). Sweep 4 repeated it at **n=48**:

| block | workers | profiles/min | p50 | retries |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 8 | 142.65 | 2.323 s | 0 |
| B | 32 | **432.14** | 0.381 s | 0 |
| C | 8 | 130.12 | 2.488 s | 2 |
| D | 32 | **535.30** | 0.265 s | 0 |

**Within-arm spread: 8 lanes 12.5, 32 lanes 103.2. The worst 32-lane reading beats the best
8-lane reading by 289.49** — larger than either arm's own variability. **ESTABLISHED.** p50 falls
rather than rises, zero retries at every level, and LamaTok publishes no rate limit.

> **The uncomfortable corollary: BL-1325's plateau was measured at n=24 per arm, and at that n
> this round could not distinguish 8 from 32.** The old figure was not wrong through
> carelessness; it was under-powered, and it hardened into a shipped constant.

### 1.2 What shipped

`api_client.POOL_SIZE = 64`, mounted on the client's Session. **Nothing in this repo mounted an
`HTTPAdapter` anywhere before today.**

`tiktok_finder.DEFAULT_LANES = 16`, up from 1. Not 32, and the reason matters: a shipped default
should not sit at the edge of what was tested, and the way 8 became wrong was by being chosen
from an under-powered measurement. 16 is double the old default, half the best measured point,
and inside the region every reading agrees is healthy. **The ceiling has still not been found**;
`scratch/bl1333_lanes4.py` finds it for about $0.12.

BL-1327 built the pool and left it at 1, arguing that concurrency on a *billed* path must be
opt-in. That argument is answered rather than waved away: what made it true was a cap that did
not hold, and §4 fixes the cap in the same round.

### 1.3 The grid-mixing bug: already fixed, and I did not need to fix it

The prompt warned that rows built their grid from `result["sheets"][-1]`, which under N lanes
attaches **another page's pictures**. Checked: that expression appears **zero times** in the
shipped module. BL-1327 fixed it when it built the pool — the sheet is held in a local `sh` — and
`tests/test_bl1327_lane_pool.py:183` already stamps the handle into each sheet path and asserts
all 12 rows match at 8 lanes. Nothing to do. **The parallel cover fetches are a fresh instance of
the same family**, and §2 pins that one.

## 2. The cover fetches, parallel and still in order

Twelve plain-HTTP CDN fetches per page, serial, measured by BL-1327 as the new bottleneck at 29.1
pages/min once the vendor stage was 8× faster. Now parallel, `COVER_WORKERS = 12` — one worker
per tile, because a page cannot use more.

**The risk is not failure, it is silent scrambling.** A contact sheet with the tiles in the wrong
order looks exactly like a contact sheet. `ThreadPoolExecutor.map` yields in **submission** order,
and one task owns one video's whole three-url fallback chain, so a slow first cover cannot
overtake a fast second one. Appending from futures as they *complete* would have been the natural
way to write it and would have been wrong forever.

`tests/test_bl1333_speed.py` forces exactly that: video 0 is made the slowest and video 11 the
fastest, and the sheet must still read 0…11. It also proves the fetches genuinely overlap (a
test that only checked order would pass on a serial implementation), that a missing cover is a
counted refusal that shifts nothing, and that `workers=1` gives a byte-identical answer.

`fetch_bytes` now goes through a **pooled Session** too — it was calling module-level
`requests.get`, a fresh connection per cover, at 12 covers × 16 lanes against a pool of 10.

**Measured effect:** at 8 lanes the stage table shows 141 cover fetches costing 38.7 s of HTTP
compressed into **11.9 s** of sheet time — a **3.25× compression inside the page** — and 0.794 s
per page for the whole sheet.

## 3. OCR: 32.9 seconds a page, for nothing

`on_screen` has exactly **one** consumer in this module — the `not has_text and talking` gate in
`judge_author` — and `talking` is computed from `speech_fracs`, which `discover()` has never
passed and which has **no call site anywhere in the repo**. So the rule cannot fire and OCR
changes **zero** TikTok verdicts, while `config.json` ships `read_on_screen: true`.

It is now skipped, and **as a condition rather than a deletion**: `ocr_can_change_a_verdict()`
reads the same input the rule reads, so the day somebody plumbs `speech_fracs` through, OCR
switches itself back on. Turning the config key off would have fixed one run; this fixes the
path. Every skip is counted in `result["ocr_skipped_inert"]` — a stage that stops running must
say so, or the next round reads `ocr_pages: 0` as "these pages had no text".

**Saving: 32.9 s/page = 9.14 hours per 1,000 pages, 0 verdicts changed.** In this round's own
runs, 22 pages skipped it in one arm alone.

> ⚠️ **The premise that OCR is inert "on every path" is false, and the asymmetry matters.**
> On **Instagram** it is load-bearing: `meme_finder.run_funnel` wires `attach_top_text` and
> `attach_speech` unconditionally, and two rules read the result — the **promo** gate
> (`meme_finder.py:1374`) and the **foreign-language** gate (`:1395`), both of which only fire
> when `source == "top_text"`. Instagram has no `read_on_screen` flag to turn off, and it should
> not get one. **TikTok is inert precisely because `speech_fracs` was never plumbed the way
> Instagram plumbs `speech_frac`.**

## 4. The cap could be overspent by 3×, and now cannot

`api_client.py:306` says it at the counter itself: *"ACTUAL HTTP requests made (incl. retries) —
the true billed count. Callers meter THIS, not one-per-logical-call."* Both funnels did exactly
what that forbids.

| | |
|---|---|
| the meter | `reserve(1)` per **logical** call |
| the retry budget | `stop_after_attempt(3)` — three billed attempts per logical call |
| **worst case** | **3× the cap.** $1.00 → $2.9988 |

Not hypothetical. BL-1327 saw 271 attempts for 256 logical calls on a quiet minute; **BL-1326
measured 188 logical against 272 billed — a 45% under-report — during an ordinary 502 window.**

**Fixed** by reconciling `used["calls"]` up to the client's real `http_requests` *before* the
ceiling test, never decreasing it (a reservation whose request has not issued yet legitimately
trails, and refunding it would be the opposite bug). The same reconcile runs inside the ledger
flush, which is the last chance to charge the final call's retries.

**Instagram's exposure is narrower, and saying otherwise would be its own error.** I wrote a
comment claiming a flat 3× there and it was wrong: HikerAPI bills from the **receipt**, and
`ig_client._request` calls `_unbilled()` on a 5xx and on a network error — **a 502 storm does not
inflate the Instagram bill.** What does: a receipt-billed 429, and any endpoint whose receipt
reports `reqs > 1` while `reserve(1)` assumed one. Smaller than TikTok's clean 3×, still a cap
that did not hold. Corrected in the source.

### 4.1 A second defect, found by the same investigation: the ledger double-writes

`_flush_spend` computed its delta **unlocked** and its own comment claimed it was "safe to enter
twice". It is not. `record_aux_spend` is **append-only and not idempotent** — no run id, no
dedupe. Two lanes crossing the checkpoint together both read the same delta, both append a row,
and both advance the mark: **the ledger is billed 2× delta for delta calls.** That is the
double-count BL-923/BL-839 fixed on the main-run path, reopened by the lane pool and dormant only
because lanes shipped at 1. It now takes its own lock — separate from the spend lock, because the
write is I/O and holding the spend lock across it would block every lane.

### 4.2 The proof

**No existing test asserted the cap holds against actual HTTP attempts.**
`test_bl1327_lane_pool.TheCapStillBinds` re-implements `reserve` inside the test and never
touches a client; `test_cap_reserve.py` does not list either finder. Every existing cap test
meters the very number that was wrong.

`tests/test_bl1333_cap.py` drives the shipped closure against a client that retries three times
per logical call: the ceiling holds to within the in-flight call's attempts, **and the same test
run against the old logical-only meter spends ≥ 3× the cap** — the counterfactual is asserted,
not described. Plus: a quiet run is unchanged, a landed request never refunds a reservation,
`cap=0` still means unlimited, and four source checks that fail if `run_funnel` stops
reconciling.

### 4.3 `--dry-run` is not free

It lives in `clippershq/paste_batch.py`, and it skips **only** `edit.py`, ~550 lines after the
paid call. BL-1328's own record reads `dry_run: true` with `billed_calls: 11, spent_usd: 0.0066`.

Everything a dry run exists to exercise — the 8-second floor, the burned-in-line OCR, the drop
placement — is computed from the media envelope and the CDN bytes it points at, so **there is no
useful dry run that does not buy it**. The fix is therefore not to make it free, which would make
it useless:

- **It says what it costs, before it spends it** — link count, how many are already cached, and
  the exact dollar figure, plus a pointer to `--estimate-only`, which is genuinely free.
- **The second dry run over the same links is free.** The envelope is cached for 45 minutes and
  read back on a repeat — the common case, since you dry-run, change a setting, dry-run again.
  Deliberately short-lived and never used for a render: the manifest's CDN urls carry an
  `x-expires`, and BL-1239 measured that every *stored* url had expired.

## 5. The free TikTok read: wired, and worth $0.00 in the funnel

BL-1331 probed it and left it in `scratch/` with no production caller. It is now
`tiktok_finder.free_stats()`, with a caller: it fills the follower count on rows that **never
bought a profile** — pages cut by the recency gate or a free rule, whose sheet row currently
shows a blank where the size of the account should be. Free, bounded, in the lane pool.

**And it replaces no paid call at all.** `profile_of` is bought **for the bio** — the bio is where
the email is — and `og:description` carries no bio and no link. The third count is **Likes**, not
video count, so it maps to `hearts` and *not* to `videos_total`; a test asserts that, because
putting a like total in the field the evidence floor reads would be a quiet disaster.
**Saving per 1,000 pages in the shipped funnel: $0.00.** Anyone quoting one is pricing a call this
cannot make.

Instagram is **0 of 60** — flat zero from the first request, the logged-out login wall, the same
one that took BL-1329's capture from 788 grids to nought. `free_stats` refuses a non-TikTok host
rather than spending seconds rediscovering that.

The parser bug BL-1331 caught is carried into the shipped code with its reason: **the separator
before the third label is a comma, not a period**, and a regex expecting a period filed every
live answer as "nothing came back". A benign zero from a regex is still a benign zero, so a shell
and a shape-change are now different return values.

## 6. The picture sorter: ordering on both, gate on neither

| | AUC |
|---|---:|

| TikTok, against the judge's verdicts | **0.7096** |

**At 0.7096 a gate is not a filter, it is a biased coin.** And even at 0.9695 the zero-loss
threshold killed 0 of 9 held-out wanted pages — a Wilson upper bound of **29.9%**. "Kills
nothing" is a claim neither sample can support.

Verified in the shipped objects rather than asserted: `judge_batches.build(allow_drop=False)`,
`siglip_probe.decide(allow_drop=False)`, and a below-threshold page returns **JUDGE** unless the

## 7. The honest rate, and what binds now

### 7.1 TikTok, measured end to end

| | pages/min | pages/hour |
|---|---:|---:|
| as shipped this morning (serial, OCR on) | **1.36** | 82 |
| serial, OCR skipped, covers parallel | **11.63** | 698 |
| **16 lanes — what ships now** | **51.00** | **3,060** |

**4.39× from lanes alone**, on top of the ~8.5× that skipping OCR buys. Vendor cost is unchanged:
**~1.5 billed requests per page walked**, $0.0006 each × 1.062.

### 7.2 Where the bottleneck sits now — the stage table

Measured at 8 lanes, 20 pages, 201 lane-seconds available:

| stage | seconds | s/call | share of lane time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **`videos_from_handle` (BILLED)** | **92.1** | **4.605** | **45.9%** |
| `profile_of` (BILLED) | 47.8 | 3.186 | 23.8% |
|  of which: cover HTTP (free) | 38.7 | 0.275 | 19.3% |
| `contact_sheet_for` (whole sheet) | 11.9 | 0.794 | 5.9% |

**The bottleneck is back at the vendor, but on a different call than before** — the search-by-
handle that buys the evidence, at 4.6 s, nearly half of all lane time. The contact sheet, which
BL-1327 left as the limit, is now 5.9%.

### 7.3 His 100 pages/minute: achievable, and here is what binds

**Yes, on TikTok.** 51/min at 16 lanes, with the vendor stage measuring 432–535 profiles/min at
32 — **the lane count is what binds, and it is a constant, not a wall.** 32 lanes should land
near 100/min end to end; that is an extrapolation from the measured stage share and is the one
number here I have not run.

**No, on Instagram, and not for a speed reason.** Its capture is a logged-out browser at 26.1
pages/min across 6 lanes, and BL-1329 measured a hard **session ceiling**: 1,200 pages captured,
then **0 of the next 524** — all login wall. Instagram binds on **access**, not throughput. More
lanes there buy nothing.

**What I did not do:** `videos_from_handle` is 45.9% of lane time and BL-1325 measured that
**114 of 249 authors were bought and then cut by a free rule** — 3.19 s/page of pure waste. The
obvious fix is to cut before buying. **It is not safe.** The recency gate needs the newest post,
discovery yields 1.15–1.37 videos per author, and a sample can only *under*-estimate how fresh a
page is — so gating on it would drop live pages. That is a pre-judge gate, and every one ever
tested here kills wanted pages: the shipped filter 17.4% [10.2, 28.0], a pre-sort 13 of 148 out
of sample, the dialogue veto 85 against 92.

## 9. What I would do next

**Find the real lane ceiling.** 32 measured 432–535 profiles/min and nothing throttled. The
experiment is written and costs $0.12.

**Re-time the whole thing at 32 lanes.** The 100 pages/min claim is an extrapolation from a stage
share; one run settles it.

**Fix `harvest_run.py:Budget.reserve`** — same logical-call metering, not fixed here.

**And chase $0.0066 that is not in the ledger.** BL-1328's dry run recorded `spent_usd: 0.0066`
in its own batch file, and I cannot find a matching row in `spend.json`. Either it ran with a
different `--spend` path or the row was lost. It is small, and it is the same direction of error
as the cap defect: real money the ledger does not show.

**Refused:** any pre-judge gate; dropping stale pages before buying their evidence; raising
`DEFAULT_LANES` to the edge of what was tested; and writing `config.json` with three rounds live.
