## The one number

**10 of 12 dialogue clips have a source speech-to-bed contrast under 6 dB — median 2.0 dB, and
2 are NEGATIVE.** The voice is not meaningfully above the music that was already in the clip.
**Our bed is not what buries the dialogue.**

---

## The operator's complaint, answered

> *"if there is a dialogue, it needs to be heard a lot — but in most cases it depends what song
> it is."*

He is right that it depends. The measurement says it depends on **the clip**, not on our song.
Policy places the bed **5–8 dB under the speech level** — a sound rule *if* the speech stands
above the clip's own music. Measured across the 12 dialogue renders:

| source speech-to-bed contrast | n=12 |
|---|---|
| min | **−4.18 dB** |
| p25 | −1.29 dB |
| **median** | **+2.02 dB** |
| p75 | +5.19 dB |
| max | +7.30 dB |

At a median contrast of 2 dB, placing our bed 5–8 dB under the voice lands it **at or above the
music already in the clip**. The dialogue was competing before we arrived. On the two negative
clips the source's music is *louder than its own dialogue*, and no bed placement fixes that.

This is not a new mechanism — it is MEMEBOT-020's finding, generalised. That round found *one*
clip whose speech sat 0.5 dB below its own music and treated it as an outlier. **It is the
median case.**

**Two corrections to the brief's premises, both load-bearing:**

1. **`duck.bed_plan` does not mix the bed "7–14 dB under speech".** The shipped constant is
   `BED_OFFSET_DIALOGUE_DB = (-8.0, -5.0)`. The wider range the brief remembers was replaced.

different mixes. What *is* true is that **4 classes collapse to 2 distinct mixes**, and the two

## 2. Speech-band versus bed energy — and why I report no masking rate

I built the obvious instrument: speech band 300–3400 Hz (the project's own
`DUCK_DEFAULTS["key_band"]`) over bed band 40–250 Hz, per 100 ms, on the output. **It does not
work, and that is what I am reporting instead of its output.**

```
my band-ratio "speech detector" vs the labelled class:  33% agreement on music-only clips
SBR median   dialogue-over-music -1.70 dB    music-only -1.58 dB    ranges fully overlap
```

A band ratio measures **spectral tilt, not speech** — music has plenty of energy between 300 and
3400 Hz. That instrument produced a *"64% of speech frames masked, 9 of 12 clips over 50%"*
figure that reads exactly like a finding. **It is discarded**, because the thing that produced it
cannot tell a voice from a bright guitar.

**The second instrument failed too, and that failure IS a finding.** Both inputs are on disk, so
the mix can be solved rather than inferred: `P_out ≈ P_src + g²·P_bed`, least squares over ~200
frames, recovering the applied gain. A negative control recovers a known gain to **0.000 dB**.
On real clips it returns ~zero bed every time, because:

> **THE RENDERER TIME-WARPS THE AUDIO.** `edit.py:_floor_trim_budget` walks trim *and speed*
> down to clear the 8-second floor. Measured: 19.41 s → 17.18 s, 9.27 s → 8.20 s,
> 15.00 s → 13.43 s — about **1.13× speed-up, and different per clip.**

So **no output-versus-source comparison is valid without first resampling by the per-clip
ratio.** Any future verification of this mix must measure that ratio first. I also found that
`start_sec`/`end_sec` on the record is the **song** window (always exactly 20.0 s), not the
source trim — a second way to align the wrong things.

**What survives both failures** is the source-only contrast in the table above: no alignment, no
separation, no bed required. That is what the answer rests on.

## 3. A rule per class, with numbers

The evidence says the class alone is **not enough** to decide the mix — which is exactly what
the operator meant by *"you need to understand it a bit better"*. Route on the clip's **own
measured contrast**:

| class | condition | rule | n |
|---|---|---|---:|
| `music-only` | — | **mute; bed solo −20..−18 dBFS.** Unchanged — no dialogue to lose, and muting is the only free way to remove the original song. | 18 |
| `silent` | — | **mute; bed solo.** Unchanged, **untested by use.** | 0 |
| `dialogue-*` | contrast **≥ 8 dB** | **keep; bed 5–8 dB under speech** (today's policy). The voice clears its own music, so a bed under the voice is under everything. | **0 of 12** |
| `dialogue-*` | contrast **2–8 dB** | **keep; bed 10–12 dB under speech.** At that headroom, −5 dB puts our bed level with the clip's own music and the voice fights two. | **8 of 12** |
| `dialogue-*` | contrast **< 2 dB** | **do not give this clip a dialogue treatment** — mute and treat as music-only, or drop it. No bed placement rescues a source whose music already beats its dialogue. | **4 of 12** |

Thresholds come from the measured distribution (p25 −1.29, median 2.02, p75 5.19), not taste.
**Note that today's policy applies to zero of the twelve measured clips.**

**The honest caveat: my contrast metric is the same band ratio that failed as a speech
detector.** On clips the label already calls dialogue it is a reasonable proxy for
voice-versus-music balance and the **ordering** is meaningful; the **absolute dB is not
calibrated** against a speech reference. That calibration is one measurement pass, not a
redesign — see below.

## What I did NOT do, and why

**I did not render 15 clips and judge them by listening.** I cannot listen. Rendering 15 would
have cost about $0.01 and produced audio I can evaluate no better than the 30 already measured —
and I have just shown both of my instruments for evaluating it are unreliable. Spending to
generate evidence I cannot read would be theatre. The 30 renders already on disk are a larger
sample and cost nothing.

**What would actually close item 3:** a human listens to 15 renders spread across the contrast
bands above and marks each dialogue **audible / marginal / buried**. That supplies the
calibration my contrast metric lacks and turns the thresholds from distribution-derived into
perception-derived. It is one sitting, and it is the only thing that can do it.

## 4. The measurement limit, honoured

The 40–250 Hz signed correlation returns ~0 on dialogue clips **because the bed is mixed far
under the speech** — a low r there is *not* a missing song. I used 40–250 Hz as my bed band, so
this applies to my own numbers, and **every table above reports dialogue and music-only
separately** for that reason. The two classes are never pooled anywhere in this report.

## 6. The undecodable path — the brief's figure is two different numbers

```
xHE-AAC profile present     18 of 30   (60%)
ffmpeg CANNOT decode         8 of 30   (27%)   -- all 8 are xHE-AAC
xHE-AAC that decodes FINE   10 of 30           -- the profile over-predicts by 1.8x
```

**"~35% carry an xHE-AAC profile ffmpeg cannot decode" conflates two questions.** 60% carry the
profile; 27% actually fail. **A guard refusing on the profile name would refuse 10 clips that
work.** Decodability has to be tested by decoding — one second of ffmpeg.

**No clip shipped silent.** Of the 8 undecodable sources, 2 route `keep` (dialogue-over-music —
the exact risky combination the brief names) and both shipped with audio. The two clips I first
recorded as having no output audio were **my own bug**: my summariser bailed when the *source*
would not decode and never measured the *output*. Verified with `volumedetect`: **−23.4 dB and
−18.8 dB mean.** Fixed — the clips whose source will not decode are now precisely the ones whose
output is always measured.
