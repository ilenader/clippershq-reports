## 1. THE CAUSE: an empty reply was recorded as a parse failure

`scratch/bl872_label.py:388` recorded `"unparseable response"` for **two different events**:

* a reply we received and could not read — our parser's fault, and we paid for it;
* a reply that **never arrived** — the vendor's fault, billed at $0.00.

`is_tooling_error` matches on `"unparse"`, so both counted toward the clip's retirement budget.

**MEASURED on 137 real calls** (`scratch/bl872_rows.jsonl`, the batch's own log):

```
parsed ok                          118
did not parse                       19
  ...EMPTY body AND $0.00 cost      14      <- 73.7% of the "parse failures"
  ...real body, real cost            5
finish_reason across all 137 calls: {None: 136, stop: 1}
```

So **14 vendor outages were charged to 14 clips' retirement budgets** — the precise thing the
`PARSER_GEN` block and `_TRANSPORT_MARKERS` exist to prevent, happening on three quarters of
the failures that looked like parse faults.

### The three-way split, across the whole library

`clip_vision` already records the split per clip. Reading it (`scratch/bl1003_evidence.py`,
2,661 clips, 503 carrying a vision failure):

| kind | clips | share |
|---|---|---|
| transport | 334 | **66.4%** |
| content | 104 | 20.7% |
| tooling | 65 | 12.9% |

**"Failing 2 calls in 3" is the transport share — 66.4%.** It is the vendor 5xx-ing
(`openrouter HTTP 5xx` 239, `Bad Gateway` 95), and those calls **cost nothing**: the driver's
`record_vision` starts `if not cost: return`, so a 502 writes no ledger row at all. The failure
rate costs **wall time, not money**.

### The parse rate, with its denominator

```
over ALL CALLS            118/137 = 86.1%    <- what the old accounting reported
over REPLIES THAT ARRIVED 118/123 = 95.9%    <- the parser's own rate
benchmarks                93% speech pool · 73% full library
```

The parser is **at or above its historical rate**. 86.1% was being compared against a 93%
benchmark computed over replies, and losing for a reason that had nothing to do with parsing.

**The MAX_TOKENS/censored-sample trap is ruled out with evidence, not assumed:**
`finish_reason` is `None` on 136 of 137 calls and `stop` on the last. **Zero** `length` or
`max_tokens`. The ceiling is not the cause.

## 2. THE FIX, at cause

`clip_vision.failure_reason_for_reply(raw, cost=, finish_reason=)` decides the wording **from
the evidence, in one place**, instead of asking every caller to remember a different string:

| evidence | reason | classified |
|---|---|---|
| empty body | `empty response from the model (cost $…)` | **transport** — never retires |
| `finish_reason` in (length, max_tokens) | `reply truncated at the token ceiling (…)` | tooling |
| anything else | `unparseable response` | tooling |

plus `"empty response"`, `"empty reply"`, `"no response body"` added to `_TRANSPORT_MARKERS`,
and `bl872_label.py` wired to call it.

## 3. Retirements re-checked against PARSER_GEN

`scratch/bl1003_retire.py`, every retirement re-decided by calling `should_skip`, not by
reasoning about it.

* **29 retired**: 24 content, 5 tooling, **0 transport** — the existing rule was holding.
* **1 tooling retirement re-opened** by the current decision (`gen=0 < PARSER_GEN=2`).
* The other **4 tooling retirements were already re-labelled successfully** — skipped as
  `already_labelled`, not as `vision_failed_2x`. All 5 correctly handled.
* **0 of the 349 clips with transport failures were ever retired.**

The mechanism works. What was broken was upstream of it: 14 transport failures wearing a
tooling reason, which would have retired those clips at the second occurrence.

## Honest limits

* **My own instrument was wrong first.** The failure histogram classified the *normalised*
  reason string, so `openrouter HTTP N` read as CONTENT — normalising digits to `N` destroys
  exactly the substring `is_transport_error` matches. 239 vendor 5xx were mislabelled in my own
  output while the split beside it, computed on the raw string, had them right. Fixed and noted
  in the script.
* **A second instrument raised rather than defaulted**, which is the contrast this round is
  about: `build_newer_index(rows)` got the dict's keys instead of its records and threw
  `AttributeError` loudly. Caught into a default it would have produced an empty index and a
  clean, plausible **"0 clips re-opened"** — BL-999's defect exactly.
* **No new calls were made**, so the fix is proven on replayed real calls rather than on fresh
  ones. That is stronger evidence, and it was also the only option with a labeller live.
* **`clip_pipeline.py` was not touched** — BL-899 holds it. The cap finding is reported and
  pinned, not fixed.
* **The 14 empty replies are not re-driven here.** Their clips carry an `unparseable` reason
  from before the fix and will be re-tried on their next selection; the fix prevents the
  *retirement*, it does not rewrite history. `PARSER_GEN` is the mechanism for that and it did
  not need bumping — these clips are at 1 attempt, not 2.
