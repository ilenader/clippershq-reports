## 3. The defect: the rendition picker failed toward the *expensive* option

```python
return min(pool, key=key) if prefer == "smallest" else max(pool, key=key)
```

Only the exact string `"smallest"` selected the small rendition. **Anything else — `worst`,
`low`, `min`, a typo, `None` — silently returned the HIGHEST-bandwidth stream.** I hit it in
the live run: `prefer="worst"` on a 115k/679k ladder returned the 679k rendition.

In a module whose stated purpose is stopping 1,000 clips becoming 8.5 GB on a 90%-full disk,
a silent fallthrough to the most expensive option is the wrong direction to be wrong in.
Inverted so that only the literal `"best"` selects the top:

```python
return max(pool, key=key) if str(prefer).lower() == "best" else min(pool, key=key)
```

Verified across the whole input space:

| `prefer` | picked |
|---|---|
| `'best'` | 1080×1920 @ 1,023,691 bps |
| `'smallest'` · `'worst'` · `'low'` · `'typo'` · `None` | 360×640 @ 115,424 bps |

`max_height=720` + `best` correctly returns 360×640, since 1280 and 1920 both exceed the cap.

**This is the only pipeline edit in the round** — two lines and a docstring.

## 4. The subtitle track: 12 of 72 clips, and it cannot replace OCR

### The coverage premise does not hold

The brief and BL-787 both state `video_subtitles_uri` is present on 12/12 clips. Measured
live across 4 accounts (48 clips) and against 6 saved payloads (72 clips):

| account | clips | manifest | **subtitles** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| clipcapture_tv | 12 | 12 | **12** |
| ashoraif | 12 | 12 | **0** |
| popcorncliip | 12 | 12 | **0** |
| shadowmovies1 | 12 | 12 | **0** |
| culturemeetsonline | 12 | 12 | **0** |
| decibeldrops | 12 | 12 | **0** |
| **total** | **72** | **72 (100%)** | **12 (16.7%)** |

**BL-787's "12/12" was one account, generalised to the endpoint.** The manifest genuinely is
100%. The subtitle track is **16.7%, and account-concentrated** — all-or-nothing per account,
which suggests it is a creator-side captioning setting rather than a platform feature.

### And where it exists, it answers a different question

Run on the 3 clipcapture_tv clips that have both, using saved payloads whose URLs were still
inside the ~105 h window — **$0.00, no paid call**:

| | subtitle | OCR |
|---|---|---|
| clip 1 | *"Khaleesi, please. You said you'd never leave…"* | **"The Lesson She Learned Made Her Unstoppable"** + `@GSMoviesedite` |
| clip 2 | *"Where's my sister? Stop him. Come on…"* | **"Khal Drogo Gave Viserys The Crown He Truly Deserved"** |
| clip 3 | *"Christian Cole, traitor… come forth to hear your fate"* | **"The Traitor Finally Faced The Price Of His Choices"** |

| measure | result |
|---|---|
| subtitle fetch | **0.18 s** per clip |
| OCR, 6 frames | **13.8 s** per clip (range 5.8–17.8) |
| speed ratio | **75×** |
| **OCR words also present in the subtitles** | **36% (mean)** |

**The subtitles are the spoken dialogue. The OCR is the burned-in overlay hook.** They are
different signals, and the overlay hook — the thing the repost detector and the caption
features actually consume — was never spoken, so it cannot be in a dialogue transcript. The
36% overlap is incidental words (`the`, `said`, `never`, `stop`).

> **Subtitles cannot replace or reduce the OCR stage.** The 500× speedup the brief hoped for
> is not available, because the two stages do not extract the same thing.

They are still worth capturing — 219 to 743 cues of free dialogue transcription is content
this system has never had, and `clip_library` already carries `subtitle_text` for it. It is a
**new signal**, not a substitute.
