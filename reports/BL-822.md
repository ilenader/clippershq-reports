### CORRECTION — that rewrite ran with the real lock FREE

I wrote `filelock.file_lock(filelock.lock_path_for(MASTER))`. `file_lock()` appends `.lock`
itself, so this locked **`master_leads.csv.lock.lock`** — a path no other writer touches —
while the 58k-row rewrite of the actual master proceeded unprotected. I reported it as done
"under the cross-process lock". It was not.

No damage: master was verified byte-identical for all 58,324 surviving rows immediately after
and again at the end of the round, and no other writer touched it in the window. But the
safety property I claimed was absent, and a concurrent appender would have been silently
clobbered. Another agent independently caught this (BL-817/818) and hardened `file_lock()` to
raise `LockPathMisuse` when handed a path already ending in `.lock`, so the mistake is now
unrepeatable rather than merely corrected in my copy.

**Attempt 1 was my bug.** `Select-Object -First N` stops the upstream pipeline once it has N
objects, killing the producing process; the clean log ending with no traceback is the
signature. Attempts 2 and 3 were stopped by the environment, which would not sustain the ~1.7 h
full path.

### Attempt 4 finished, and I reported it as dead

I received a `killed` notification and wrote the item off. The evidence said otherwise and I
should have checked before publishing: master went 41 to 71 `MEME_PAGE` rows at **23:46:32**,
and `spend.json` carries a genuine `repost_finder` entry at the same second for **63 calls,
$0.0378** — that is `run_from_config`'s own metering `finally`, which only runs on a clean
completion.

What actually happened: `run_from_config` appends, meters and persists **internally**, then
returns. My wrapper's summary `print()`s were buffered and never flushed, so the log ended
mid-run and looked truncated. **The absence of my output was not the absence of the work.**

Two consequences I got wrong as a result:

- I claimed the funnel "loses every page it confirmed" on a kill. It does not — it had already
  saved. My reasoning was built on a premise I never verified.
- I manually metered attempt 4's page calls as unmetered, **double-counting $0.0060**. A
  correcting entry (`CORRECTION_killed4_was_not_killed_double_count`, −10 calls) is now in the
  ledger. Attempts 1–3 genuinely died before their `finally` and carry no `repost_finder`
  entry, so those manual records stand.
