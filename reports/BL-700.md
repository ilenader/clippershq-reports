# BL-700 — Only 8.5% of clips are music-dominant. Fingerprinting has no target here. Do not build it.

**Date:** 2026-07-29 · **Type:** Measurement, read-only on pipeline code
**Spend:** **$0.00** — no paid calls were needed. BL-690's URLs were still inside the ~33 h
window (`oe` expiry 2026-07-30 21:12 UTC, 31.6 h remaining), so the saved 106-URL pool was
still drawable from the CDN for free.

---

## The number, plainly

**8 of 94 clips (8.5%) are music-or-non-speech dominant. 77 of 94 (82%) are speech-dominated
dialogue.** Corpus-wide, rhythmic content sits *at or below the level of white noise*, and
tonal content sits about a quarter of the way from noise to a pure synthetic chord.

There is no meaningful population of "dialogue with a music bed underneath" that
fingerprinting could target. **Abandon it before it is built.**

| class (n=94) | count | share |
|---|---|---|
| speech-dominant (`speech_frac` ≥ 0.60) | **77** | **81.9%** |
| mixed (0.30 – 0.60) | 9 | 9.6% |
| music / non-speech dominant (< 0.30) | **8** | **8.5%** |

62% of clips have speech covering **≥80%** of their entire runtime.

## Method — the speech-pause probe

Asking "is there music under this dialogue" head-on is hard. Asking it **in the gaps** is
easy, because speech is intermittent: between words and lines the talker stops.

- **pure dialogue** → gaps fall to room tone / near-silence, no pitch structure
- **music underneath** → gaps stay elevated and keep harmonic and rhythmic structure

Speech segmentation came from faster-whisper VAD, which BL-682 measured as a clean gate
(`no_speech_prob` 0.075–0.152 on real dialogue vs 0.931 on silence). Everything else —
HPSS harmonic ratio, per-frame chroma concentration, tempo salience, spectral flatness,
4 Hz syllabic modulation, spectral-peak persistence — was computed over the whole clip and
over the gaps separately.

Explicit inspectable statistics were chosen over a trained speech/music classifier
precisely so the result could be checked against a spectrogram. That turned out to matter.

## Two negative results about the METHOD, reported before the findings

**1. A bug in my own tonality metric, found and fixed.** The first pass computed entropy of
the **mean** chroma vector. If the music changes chord, the mean flattens and a genuinely
tonal clip scores as noise. Tonality must be measured **per frame** and then averaged. Fixed
and recomputed. It changed almost nothing (gap chroma entropy 0.999 → 0.991), so the
conclusion does not rest on the bug — but the first number was wrong and would have been
wrong in a convenient direction.

**2. Most of my spectral features do not work on this material, and I could not rescue
them.** The three accounts are near-identical on every feature, *including the music page*:

| account | speech_frac | bed_depth_db | tempo_salience | chroma_conc | gap_harm_ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| clipcapture.tv | 0.910 | 4.24 | 0.083 | 0.129 | 0.459 |
| culturemeetsonline | 0.846 | 3.68 | 0.077 | 0.127 | 0.423 |
| **decibeldrops (music page)** | 0.804 | 3.94 | **0.095** | **0.139** | 0.456 |

Spectral-peak persistence — the actual Shazam primitive — failed the same way: clips with
`speech_frac < 0.3` scored **0.710** and clips with `speech_frac ≥ 0.8` scored **0.722**.
The known music-only clip (0.856) was *outscored* by a pure-speech clip (0.952).

**So the fine-grained "is there a bed" question is not answered by these features.** Only
`speech_frac` survived validation, and it survived because it was checked against
spectrograms by eye. The headline number rests on that one validated feature plus the
calibration below — not on the features that failed.

## The calibration that makes the result defensible

A low tonality score is worthless unless the metric can detect tonality at all. Synthetic
controls, same code path:

| control signal | chroma_conc | tempo_salience |
|---|---|---|
| sustained C-major triad | 0.227 | 0.877 |
| chord progression (changes every 2.5 s) | 0.214 | −0.010 |
| 4-on-the-floor kick + bass | 0.000 | **0.765** |
| white noise | 0.095 | 0.112 |
| perfectly flat chroma (1/12) | 0.083 | 0.000 |
| **real corpus median** | **0.130** | **0.084** |
| **real corpus maximum (best of 94)** | **0.173** | 0.418 |

Two things follow, and they are the core of the finding:

- **The median clip's rhythmic periodicity (0.084) is LOWER than white noise (0.112),** and
  roughly one-ninth of a synthetic 4-on-the-floor beat (0.765). There is no beat in the
  median clip. Even the single most rhythmic clip out of 94 (0.418) sits well below a plain
  synthetic drum pattern.
- On tonality the corpus median sits about **27% of the way from white noise to a pure
  sustained chord**; the best clip in 94 reaches 59%. Note the compressed scale — a pure
  synthetic triad only reaches 0.227 under `chroma_stft`, so this metric is blunt. That is
  why it is reported as supporting evidence, not as the headline.

## What the spectrograms actually show

A random sample (fixed seed 694) of 24 clips was rendered as mel spectrograms and read by
eye, plus 14 chosen at the extremes of the feature space. What is visible:

- **Genuine dialogue with true gaps** is the dominant pattern. On clips like R017340
  (`bed_depth 17.27 dB`) and R738529 the spectrum goes **black across all frequencies**
  between phrases. When the gap is black, there is definitively no music bed.
- **Music-only clips are unmistakable when they occur.** Clip 467261 (`speech_frac 0.00`)
  shows dense regular percussive strikes and sustained harmonic bands filling the whole
  duration. Clip 936974 shows sustained horizontal partials. These are the 8.5%.
- **Where a bed does exist it is usually diffuse and broadband** — a continuous haze with no
  harmonic lines (R399329, R723159). That reads as film ambience, crowd, or room tone rather
  than music, and it is consistent with the near-noise tonality numbers.
- **A minority do show a real music bed.** R834212 has an evenly-spaced low-frequency pulse
  train at ~60–120 Hz running the whole clip — a bassline or kick under the dialogue. This
  exists, but it is the exception, not the pattern.

## Does the music dominate, or sit far under the speech?

For the 77 speech-dominant clips:

| | value |
|---|---|
| `bed_depth_db` (speech level − gap level), p25 / median / p75 | −1.0 / **4.0** / 9.0 dB |
| non-speech audio available per clip (median) | **4.1 seconds** |

Both numbers are bad for fingerprinting, for different reasons.

The 4 dB median looks *encouraging* at first glance — it suggests the gaps are nearly as loud
as the speech. It is not encouraging, and this is the subtlest point in the report: when
`speech_frac` is 0.86, the VAD has labelled almost everything as speech, so "the gaps" are
mostly **short pauses inside speech** — breath, plosive stops, inter-word silence — not
sections of exposed bed. The statistic is measured on very little audio and is contaminated
by speech tails. **The speech-pause probe degrades exactly where this population lives.**

The second number is the one that decides it. Chromaprint and Shazam-style matchers need
roughly **5–10 seconds of reasonably exposed music** to return a confident match. The median
clip offers **4.1 seconds of non-speech audio in total**, scattered across dozens of
sub-second fragments, and that audio is mostly ambience. There is nothing to hash.

---

## Verdict

**Do not build audio fingerprinting for this population.**

1. **91.5% of clips are not music-dominant**, and 82% are speech-dominated dialogue.
2. **There is no beat**: median rhythmic periodicity is below white noise.
3. **There is nothing to fingerprint even where a bed exists**: 4.1 s of fragmented
   non-speech audio per clip, mostly broadband ambience.
4. This is consistent with BL-682, which found `music_info` on **0/108** and
   `original_audio_title` = `"Original audio"` on 108/108. These pages repost movie and TV
   scenes; the audio is the scene's own dialogue track. There was never a licensed song in
   there to identify.

The ~8.5% that *are* music-dominant are identifiable cheaply and for free by
`speech_frac < 0.30` alone — no fingerprinting needed to find them, and 8 clips is not a
business case for building a matcher.

---

## Limits — read these before quoting the number

- **n=94, three accounts, all repost/clip pages.** The 8.5% is a property of *this*
  population. A creator-account corpus that adds trending songs over original footage would
  look completely different, and BL-682 already flagged that the 36% music-metadata figure
  may describe exactly that population.
- **The classification rests on one validated feature.** `speech_frac` (whisper VAD) is the
  only feature that survived validation against spectrograms. The HPSS, chroma, peak-
  persistence and harmonic-ratio features **failed to separate a known music page from
  dialogue pages** and were not used to assign classes. This is a real weakness: a quiet
  music bed under continuous speech is a case my tools genuinely cannot see.
- **Therefore the 8.5% is a floor on music-dominance, not a ceiling on music-presence.** The
  honest claim is: *at most a small minority is music-dominant, and no tonal or rhythmic bed
  could be demonstrated corpus-wide.* It is not: *we proved no clip contains music.*
- **Labels were assigned by machine vision on rendered spectrograms**, not by a person
  listening. Nobody has actually listened to these clips, which was the premise of the
  question and remains true. Reading a spectrogram catches sustained harmonics and beat
  grids reliably; it is weaker on quiet, heavily-compressed beds.
- The tonality metric is blunt: a pure synthetic triad reaches only 0.227 under
  `chroma_stft`. The tempo-salience result (median below white noise, vs 0.765 for a
  synthetic beat) is the stronger of the two and is what the verdict leans on.
