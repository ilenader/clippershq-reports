## 2. Which calls are redundant

| call | what it buys | still needed? |
|---|---|---|
| **discovery `/v2/search`** | handle, sec_uid, follower/following, verified, **and `create_time`** for ~17 authors at once | **yes — 0.06 calls per page** |
| **recency probe** | the newest video's date | **NO — redundant. It is in the search payload.** |
| **capture `/v2/search` (username variant)** | the account's own videos, **12.78 per call** vs discovery's 1.12 | **yes** — this is what makes the evidence floor affordable |
| **profile call** | **the bio, i.e. the email** | **yes** — absent from search |

The username variant is a **different call** from discovery search and the two are kept
distinct throughout: discovery takes a free-text keyword and returns many authors' videos;
the username variant takes a handle and returns 105 of 115 accounts' own videos [84.7, 95.2].

**The order is where the saving is.** Dropping a stale page at the top of the funnel removes
its capture call *and* its profile call, not just its recency call.

## 3. Empty results ARE billed — his claim is refuted

Measured against the vendor's own receipt counter (`/sys/balance` is free; two reads move it
by zero, BL-1259):

| probe | items returned | **counter delta** |
|---|---:|---:|
| `offset=40` (known-empty page) | **0** | **1** |
| `offset=100` | **0** | **1** |
| a nonsense keyword | 19 *(not empty — TikTok returns results for gibberish)* | 1 |

**Two calls returned zero items and each moved the counter by one.** So an empty result is
billed exactly like a full one, and **this project's convention of charging attempts rather
than successes is correct.** Retries and dead handles cost real money and should keep being
counted that way.

The nonsense-keyword probe is reported because it *failed as a test* — TikTok's search returns
something for gibberish, so it never produced the empty result it was meant to. The offset
probes are the valid ones.

## 4. Instagram's double-buy — traced, and narrower than stated

Three functions, all in `clippershq/meme_finder.py`:

| | line | fires on |
|---|---:|---|
| `profile_facts` | **2877** | **every** fresh page — buys `media_count` for the RULE 2 gate |
| `resolve_contact` | **2987** | **only pages that PASS** the meme verdict — buys the email |
| `profile_and_contact` | **2666** | returns `(facts, contact, err)` from **ONE** request — **zero callers** |

**Correction to the brief: it is not 2 calls on every page.** `resolve_contact` sits inside the
`else` branch of `if not verdict["is_meme_page"]`. Rejected pages buy one call; passing pages
buy two. **The waste scales with the pass rate, not the page count** — which is why the
measured figure is $1.37 per 5,700 pages rather than the $3.42 that 2-per-page would imply.

**Why the second exists:** the two return *disjoint halves of one payload* — `resolve_contact`
gives the email and drops `user_id`; `profile_facts` gives `user_id` and drops the email.
Neither could be widened without breaking its callers.

**Can the first result be passed down? Yes — and the code already exists.** The two sites are
separated by the judging step, so the payload has to be held across it, which is exactly what
`profile_and_contact` does. BL-1232 wrote it and never wired it in; BL-1251 logged it as still
unfixed. The wiring is: call it at 2877, keep `contact`, use it at 2987 instead of re-calling.
It is a strict improvement — **same cost on a rejected page, half the cost on a passing one.**

## 5. The arithmetic, against his $2.00 ceiling

Population: BL-1270's sheet — **159 discovered → 91 fresh → 45 wanted.**

### TikTok

| | discovery | recency | capture | profile | total | per wanted | **$ / 1,000 wanted** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **today** | 9.4 | 159 | 159 | 159 | 486.4 | 10.81 | **$6.49** |
| **minimum set** | 9.4 | **0** | **91** | **45** | 145.4 | 3.23 | **$1.94** |

**$1.94 — it clears.** Three changes, in order of size: stale pages rejected free from the
search payload (159 recency calls → 0), capture bought only for fresh pages (159 → 91),
profile bought only for wanted pages (159 → 45).

### Instagram

| | calls/page | **$ / 1,000 wanted** |
|---|---:|---:|
| today | 1.40 | **$4.42** |
| double-buy fixed | 1.00 | **$3.16** |

**Instagram does not clear, even fixed.** At a 19% want rate every page costs a profile call
and only one in five yields a wanted page — $3.16 is the floor of that arithmetic. Closing the
gap needs the same trick TikTok just got: a free signal that rejects a page *before* it buys
its profile. Instagram has no `create_time` equivalent in hand today.

### The subscription is not free

Judging is **$0.00 in vendor dollars and not free in capacity**: ~66,439 subscription tokens
per page on his $200 plan, and BL-1270 measured it as the binding constraint on volume — the
honest ceiling is 768 emails/day, judging-bound, not money-bound.

**So the lever he asked for is real: every page the CODE can reject from the search payload is
a page the judge never spends a token on.** `create_time` is the first such signal and it
removes 42.8% of a sheet. Language and follower-floor cuts are available from the same payload
at the same zero cost. That is what "the browser judge reserved for the uncertain cases" looks
like in practice.
