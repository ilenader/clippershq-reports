# BL-819: One honest walkthrough — the system works end to end, and the first thing it tells you to do would delete data

**Date:** 2026-07-31 · **Class:** End-to-end verification · **Spend:** **$0.00** — no paid calls. Read-only on pipeline code. One disposable artefact written: `output/ALL_BOT_READY.csv`, via the app's own free `[B]` builder. Nothing stamped, no outcome recorded, no config touched.

---

## Starting it

`python clippershq/main.py` opens on an ASCII logo, a colour legend, and a thirteen-item menu in plain English with a price beside every paid option — "*Twitch streamers … about $0.38 per 1,000*". Free things say FREE. `[B] BUILD MY SEND LIST` says *"Writes output/ALL_BOT_READY.csv — the ONE file to send from. FREE"*. `[Q]` quits with "bye." It is genuinely readable by someone who has never seen the codebase, and the pricing-in-the-menu decision is the single best UX choice in the product.

But the **first line printed, above the logo, is a warning**:

> `⚠ ./master_leads.csv schema drift: header != current 70-column layout. Run 'python main.py --rewrite-master' to realign it`

The Control Panel (`python clippershq/control.py`) opens on a twenty-item menu covering the same funnels plus campaign management, health check, and per-funnel filters. **Every capability is present and dispatched in both** — the two differ only in organisation. `main.py [6] "Find editors (Instagram / TikTok / both)"` is the same work that Control Panel reaches through `[6] Run a campaign` plus `[9] Find more editors like my best`; Control Panel adds `[7] Health check` and the tuning menus, which main.py folds into `[8] Settings`. Nothing is orphaned in either, and both offer `[B]/[b]` and `[o]/[O]`.

## Building the send list

`[B]` printed:

> `2,949 leads written (+0 vs last build).`
> `YOUR SEND FILE: output\ALL_BOT_READY.csv`
> `2,949 leads | just rebuilt`
> `This is the ONLY file to send from. Every other CSV in output/ is either a subset of it or an old snapshot — sending from a second file just emails the same people twice.`

It names the file, states the exclusivity rule, and reports the delta against the last build — so "are new leads included?" is answered by `+0`, which is the right shape of reassurance. It ran offline in seconds and cost nothing.

**The exclusivity claim is undermined by the directory it writes into.** `output/` currently holds, alongside `ALL_BOT_READY.csv` (23:10 today), seven other send-shaped CSVs written at 19:48 today: `LATEST_BOT_READY.csv` (398 KB), `DIRECT_SEND.csv`, `PANICBABY_SEND.csv`, `ZHUS_SEND.csv`, `DAYLIGHT_SEND.csv`, `IG_DM_LIST.csv`, `CALIBRATION_20.csv`. One of them is called **`LATEST_BOT_READY`**, which to any reasonable user sounds *more* current than `ALL_BOT_READY`. The warning is correct and the naming actively fights it.

## Reading the file

19 columns, 2,949 rows: `email, profile_link, score, intent_score, activity, lead_kind, agency, role, days_since_post, flags, handle, display_name, fandom, fandom_display, fandom_is_character, followers, clip_views, clip_date, rank_median_views`.

**BL-814's fix is really in here.** `median_views` is gone; `clip_views` and `rank_median_views` are separate columns and they genuinely differ per row — @areqaep is `clip_views 4.4M` against `rank_median_views 8.1M`. The ranking number and the messaging number can no longer be confused, which was the whole point.

Then I rendered five rows through the merge sentence, and this is where the walkthrough stops feeling finished:

| # | handle | clip_views | clip_date | clip URL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | @areqaep | 4.4M | 2026-06-14 | **none** |
| 2 | @hoodieguyofficial1 | 18.7M | 2025-10-03 | **none** |
| 3 | @onytitned | 2.9M | 2026-03-26 | **none** |
| 4 | @yaruzi.aep | 2.9M | 2026-03-02 | **none** |
| 5 | @tusk.ae | 826k | 2025-05-03 | **none** |

> *"saw your clip — 4.4M views, from 2026-06-14"*

**The number is real and the date is real. The clip is not identified.** There is no `clip_url`, `clip_permalink` or `matched_video_url` column in the export, and — I checked — none in the master either. The only URL is the profile. So the sentence can name a view count and a date but cannot point at the video, and neither the recipient nor you can check which clip is being referred to. BL-814 made the number true; it did not make the clip *nameable*. This is the direct downstream cost of the BL-753 finding that `video_id` is captured at discovery and never persisted.

Two further things the file shows plainly: **`clip_views` and `clip_date` are filled on 1,532 and 1,531 of 2,949 rows (52%)** — so the specific-clip sentence can only be rendered for about half the list, and the other half needs a different template. And **`role` is 0% filled on all 2,949 rows** — an always-empty column riding along in the send file.

## Tracing one lead

@areqaep, picked because it was row 1.

Master row: `platform tiktok · verdict EDITOR · editor_pct 91 · editor_signals "core_edit, page, business, film" · followers 2,500,000 · avg_views_sampled 4,400,000 · deep_median_views 8,095,740 · deep_posts_checked 10 · matched_video_views 4,400,000 · matched_video_date 2026-06-14 · date_source deep · source_hashtag tt:hashtag:acrossthespiderverseedit · date_added 2026-07-02 00:59:27 · secuid MS4wLjABAAAA… · email from bio ("📩Business: …📩")`.

**Yes, I could explain to that person how I found them:** *"You posted under #acrossthespiderverseedit; we sampled ten of your recent posts on 2 July, your median is 8.1M and the post we matched on 14 June did 4.4M; your business email is in your bio."* Every clause traces to a stored field. `clip_views` in the export is `matched_video_views` from the master — one real video, not the median. The provenance ladder holds.

Two gaps in the same row: **`email_source` is empty** even though the address demonstrably came from the bio, and **23 of 71 master columns are populated** — the other 48, including every outcome column, are blank.

## The outcome path

`[o] → [4] "Mark a whole sent list — stamp every lead in a send file as contacted"` exists in the Control Panel. Reading `control.py:3150` and `outcomes.py:327`: it calls `mark_sent_from_csv(..., dry_run=True)` first, prints the preview (matched / unmatched / ambiguous, with unmatched and ambiguous explicitly *not* stamped), and only then asks `_ask_yes_no("Mark N leads as contacted?", current=False)` — **the confirm defaults to NO**, exactly as the docstring claims. The write path takes a timestamped backup, atomic replace and fsync. I verified all of this by reading; **I did not stamp anything**.

## The clip library

555 clips across 39 accounts, in 22 monthly JSONL files going back to 2023-06. `permalink`, `play_count`, `posted_at`, `clip_pk`, `account_user_id` and `caption` are 100% filled; `media_renditions` and `media_duration_s` 99%.

**Yes, a downstream bot could retrieve the video** — every one of the 555 clips carries an `https://www.instagram.com/reel/…` permalink plus a rendition list.

Two caveats. It is **concentrated**: `loste1980` is 240 clips and `movies.avengers` 101, so two accounts are 61% of the library. And five declared fields are **empty on all 555 rows** — `affect_conflict`, `arousal_audio`, `format`, `speech_frac`, `subtitle_text` — the clip schema's own version of the dead-knob problem BL-778 catalogued for config.

## What surprised me

**The startup warning is wrong, and following it would destroy data.** The master has **71** columns; `writer.FULL_COLUMNS` has **70**. Nothing is missing — the single difference is one *extra* column in the master: **`twitch_login`**. It is populated on **260 rows**, and a grep across all 60 production modules finds **no code that writes it**. So the app greets you with a scary drift warning and tells you to run `--rewrite-master`, which realigns the file to the 70-column layout and would **drop 260 real values** for a column no live code maintains. The honest fix is to either re-adopt `twitch_login` into `FULL_COLUMNS` or export-then-remove it deliberately — not to let a user follow the banner.

**The master grew 938 rows today** — 57,386 at the BL-753 snapshot this morning, 58,324 now — while other agents were working. Worth knowing when reconciling any figure quoted from earlier today.

**2,949 in the send file against 4,217 unique emailable addresses measured in BL-757.** Not a contradiction — the send builder applies gates BL-757's raw count did not — but the two numbers are quoted in different reports without a stated relationship, and a user comparing them would reasonably think something was lost.

**`role` is a column in the send file that is empty on every row**, and `intent_score` is `0` on the traced lead while present as a column. Both are surfaced to the user with nothing behind them.

## Does it feel finished?

**The spine is finished.** Start to send file is one keystroke, free, fast, self-explaining, and the file it produces is honest about what it is. Provenance survives the whole way: I picked a row at random and reconstructed exactly how that person was found, from which hashtag, on which date, with which evidence. The dangerous action — stamping thousands of rows as contacted — dry-runs first and defaults to no. That is a system somebody thought carefully about.

**The last mile is not finished.** You can tell a creator their clip did 4.4M views on 14 June and you cannot show them which clip, because the video id was never stored. Half the send file cannot carry that sentence at all. The output directory contradicts the "one file" rule by containing a file called `LATEST_BOT_READY`. And the first line of the app is a warning whose recommended remedy deletes 260 values.

None of those is deep. All four are visible from the user's chair in the first five minutes, which is the argument for fixing them before anything more ambitious lands.

## Method

Both entry points launched as real processes with input piped and a hard timeout; menus captured verbatim from stdout. `[B]` run through `main.py` exactly as a user would. Export, master, and clip library read with a CSV/JSONL reader, never modified. Outcome path verified by reading `control.py:3150-3175` and `outcomes.py:327-420` rather than executing — deliberately, since executing it is the one action that writes to master. Email local-parts redacted throughout. Master and clip-library figures are as of 2026-07-31 23:10; other agents were writing to the master during this walkthrough.
