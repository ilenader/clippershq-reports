# BL-704: Genre from title+artist — the provably-clean source covers 30% of licensed clips, the source that covers 90% isn't provably clean, and the curated map does not saturate

**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Builds on:** [BL-702](BL-702.md) (title+artist free on ~23% of clips).
**Spend:** **$0.00** — free keyless public endpoints only (Wikidata Action API, iTunes Search API). No pipeline code changed.
**Artifacts:** `scratch/bl703_artist_concentration.py`, `bl703_wikidata_probe.py`, `bl703_weighted.py`, `bl703_itunes_probe.py` and their JSON results.

Tiers: **VERIFIED** (measured here / primary source quoted) · **UNVERIFIED LEAD** · **REFUTED** · **GAP**.

---

## Verdict first

**The budget was never the binding constraint. The licence is.** Both routes with real coverage cost **$0**; what separates them is whether you are allowed to keep the answer.

| route | clip-weighted genre coverage | cost | commercially clean? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wikidata (P136)** | **30%** of licensed clips | $0 | **YES — CC0, no NC clause** |
| **iTunes Search (`primaryGenreName`)** | **90%** (89% namesake-safe) | $0 | **UNPROVEN — see §4** |
| curated artist map | 49% from 4 artists, then a long tail | one-off + upkeep | yes (your own data) |
| MusicBrainz commercial licence | high | **$100–499/mo** | yes, but 50–100× over budget |
| Discogs | unknown | $0 | **probably not** — genre appears to be Restricted Data |

Recommended stack: **curated head + Wikidata tail now, iTunes only if a human clears Apple's terms.** If Apple clears, it is free and triples coverage in one step.

## 1. The measurement that reframes the question: the corpus is mostly tail

**VERIFIED — 74 saved clip payloads, 417 unique clips.**

A first pass reported 48 artists over 311 licensed clips with a suspiciously flat ~13 clips each. That was **my own BL-684 track-feed output** contaminating the corpus — each of those payloads is 12 media objects that use the *same* track by construction. Excluding them (`soundlib_tracks`, `ig_track_by_id`) gives the real shape, and it independently reproduces BL-702's headline:

| metric | value |
|---|---|
| unique clips | 417 |
| audio_type | 283 original_sounds / 98 licensed_music / 36 none |
| **carrying title+artist** | **98 / 417 = 23.5%** (BL-702 measured 23%) |
| distinct artists | 48 |
| distinct tracks | 63 |

**Concentration (artists):** 50% of licensed clips need 5 artists, **80% needs 29**, 90% needs 39, 100% needs 48.

**The number that answers question 5: 38 of 48 artists (79.2%) appear exactly once.** For tracks, 46 of 63 (73.0%).

The hypothesis under test was "if 80% of licensed clips come from 50 artists, a hand-built map beats every service." Measured, **80% requires 29 of the 48 artists seen — 60% of the entire artist set.** That is not a head with a thin tail; it is mostly tail, and it is not saturating: roughly **one new artist per two licensed clips**.

What concentration exists is real but narrow — Apollo Keys (20 clips), Trikstar (14), kowlys+Kowlys (14) — production/library music used repeatedly *inside single accounts*, not a global head. Note also `kowlys` vs `Kowlys`: a curated map needs case normalisation before it needs entries.

**Conclusion: build the curated map for the recurring head (4 artists = 49% of licensed clips, and they are exactly the artists an encyclopaedia will never carry), but it cannot be the whole answer and it is not maintenance-free.**

## 2. Wikidata — provably clean, and honestly weak

**VERIFIED — live probe of all 48 corpus artists via the free keyless Action API** (`wbsearchentities` → `wbgetentities`, P136 genre, P31 to confirm a music entity).

| view | found | carry P136 genre |
|---|---|---|
| **artist-weighted** | 36/48 (75%) | **25/48 (52%)** |
| **clip-weighted** | 40/98 (41%) | **29/98 (30%)** |

**Report the clip-weighted number.** Artist-weighted 52% flatters badly, because Wikidata knows the famous artists that appear *once each* and does not know the library artists that appear *twenty times*:

| artist | clips | Wikidata |
|---|---|---|
| Apollo Keys | 20 | **missing** |
| Trikstar | 14 | **missing** |
| kowlys / Kowlys | 14 | **missing** |
| Hans Zimmer | 2 | genre ✓ |
| Lana Del Rey | 2 | genre ✓ |
| ABBA, Pixies, Billie Eilish… | 1 each | genre ✓ |

The four missing head artists alone are **48 of 98 licensed clips (49%)**.

**Namesake risk, measured:** **14 of 48 artists (29%) returned more than one music-like candidate** — e.g. "Ghost", "Journey", "Passenger", "Pandora", "adore" are all ordinary words. Mitigation that works without a paid identifier: require an exact label match **and** a music-type P31 (or a P136 present) before accepting. Four multi-artist credit strings ("Kendrick Lamar, SZA") resolved only after splitting on the comma — a name-matching failure mode, measured rather than assumed.

**Where the genres are good, they are very good:** Lana Del Rey → indie pop, baroque pop, trip hop, sadcore, dream pop. Where absent, absent.

**Licence: VERIFIED clean.** Wikidata is CC0 with no NonCommercial clause — the one source in this whole search with no legal asterisk.

**Method honesty:** the first run of this probe reported 10% coverage. That was **HTTP rate-limiting misread as "not in Wikidata"** — 37 of 48 names never got a real answer. Fixed with explicit backoff and status capture; the corrected figures are above. A probe that cannot distinguish *429* from *absent* will always under-report coverage.

## 3. iTunes Search — 90%, free, and it covers exactly what Wikidata misses

**VERIFIED — live probe of the same 48 artists** (`/search?entity=musicArtist`, `primaryGenreName`).

| view | found | genre present |
|---|---|---|
| artist-weighted | 43/48 (90%) | 41/48 (85%) |
| **clip-weighted** | 92% | **90%** |
| **exact-name-match only (namesake-safe)** | — | **87/98 = 89%** |

The head Wikidata could not resolve resolves here, because Apple's catalogue is **distribution-based, not notability-based**:

| artist | clips | Wikidata | iTunes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apollo Keys | 20 | missing | **Classical Crossover** |
| Trikstar | 14 | missing | **Pop** |
| kowlys | 10+4 | missing | **Electronic** |
| Pc Platinum Child | 1 | missing | **Hip-Hop/Rap** |

That the namesake-safe figure (89%) is barely below the raw figure (90%) is the useful part: **44 of 48 lookups were exact artist-name matches**, so the 53%-wrong name-matching result from the earlier round does not reproduce here. The difference is that this is a *catalogue* lookup keyed on the exact credited artist string that Instagram itself got from the licensor, not a fuzzy title match across databases.

**Costs of the approach, stated plainly:**
- **Vocabulary is coarse** — 18 distinct values across the corpus, top-heavy (Alternative 8, Rock 7, Pop 5, Electronic 3). Nothing like Discogs' or Wikidata's granularity. "Alternative" is not a mood.
- **Rate limit ~20 calls/minute** (Apple's documented figure). At 23% track coverage that is ~230 lookups per 1,000 clips ≈ 12 minutes. Not a blocker.
- **Multi-artist credit strings fail** — 5 artists / 8 clips returned nothing ("Kendrick Lamar, SZA", "Vishal & Shekhar, …"). Splitting on the comma fixed this for Wikidata and would likely recover most of those 8 clips; **untested for iTunes** — the cheapest remaining improvement.
- Two artists resolved but carried no genre ("Temper City", "Nocturne Dominus").

### 4. The catch — and it is the whole decision

**GAP, and it is not a technicality.** Apple's Search API documentation frames the API around promoting store content: developers may use promotional content *"only to promote store content and not for entertainment purposes"*, previews *"must be streamed only, and not downloaded, saved, cached, or synchronized"*, and the surrounding programme is affiliate-oriented. The documentation I could reach **does not explicitly address whether descriptive metadata such as `primaryGenreName` may be stored** — the storage restrictions it does state are about previews and artwork.

So iTunes is **not refuted** and **not cleared**. It is unadjudicated. Given that this pipeline would *store* the genre in a clip library and use it commercially, that is exactly the clause that matters, and it should be read by a human before adoption. **Do not ship iTunes-derived genre on the strength of this report.**

## 5. The paid options, priced

**MusicBrainz commercial licence — UNVERIFIED LEAD (published tier pages), and far above budget.** MetaBrainz sells support tiers rather than metered lookups: the small-commercial **Bronze tier is described as $100–499/month**; Unicorn (large companies) $2,000+/month; non-profits and public universities free. Against a budget of **$1–2 per 1,000 clips** and ~230 lookups per 1,000 clips, even the $100/month floor needs **50,000–100,000 clips per month** before it reaches $1–2/1,000. It is a database subscription, not a lookup fee. **REFUTED at this pipeline's volume**; revisit only if clip volume grows two orders of magnitude.

**Discogs — probably blocked, and I could not confirm it.** Discogs splits API data into **CC0 Data** and **Restricted Data**, stating Restricted Data **may not be used for any commercial purpose**. The CC0 enumeration quoted in secondary sources is: *release titles, notes, dates, format, track listings, barcodes and other identifiers, credits, versions, URL links, and label/producer/manufacturer/distributor names and contact information.* **Genre and style are absent from that list.** If the enumeration is exhaustive, the one field worth having — Discogs' unusually good electronic/hip-hop taxonomy — is the non-commercial part.

**GAP:** `support.discogs.com` returns **HTTP 403** to automated fetches and `web.archive.org` is unreachable from this environment, so the primary text is unread. **This is the second question a human should settle**, because if genre *is* CC0, Discogs beats everything in this report on taxonomy quality.

**TheAudioDB — UNVERIFIED LEAD.** Free shared test key at 30 req/min; a personal key with higher limits via a **$8/month Patreon tier**. Well inside budget. **GAP: the licence covering redistribution/commercial storage of the community-contributed data was not located** — it needs the same human read as the two above before it counts as clean.

**Others considered and dismissed without probing:** ListenBrainz is MetaBrainz — its genre linkage inherits the same CC BY-NC-SA supplementary-data problem already refuted for MusicBrainz. AllMusic and Genius have no open genre endpoint and restrictive terms. **One lead not pursued and worth a look: Wikipedia/DBpedia infobox genre is CC BY-SA — share-alike, but *not* NonCommercial**, so unlike MusicBrainz's genre links it is commercially usable with attribution, and infobox coverage is typically wider than P136.

## End-to-end, on real numbers

Only **23.5%** of clips carry a track at all. So genre coverage over the *whole* clip corpus is:

| route | genre on licensed clips | **genre on ALL clips** |
|---|---|---|
| Wikidata (clean) | 30% | **~7%** |
| iTunes (unadjudicated) | 90% | **~21%** |
| curated head map (4 artists) | 49% of licensed | ~12% |
| curated head + Wikidata tail | ~60–65% est. | **~15%** |

Whatever is chosen, **genre will be null on most clips**, because most clips are original sounds with no track. That is a property of the corpus, not of any vendor, and the field should be built to be absent by default.

## Open

1. **Apple's terms on storing `primaryGenreName`** — the single decision that swings coverage from 7% to 21% of all clips. Needs a human read.
2. **Discogs CC0 vs Restricted for genre/style** — 403 blocked automated reading; if CC0, it likely wins on taxonomy.
3. **Comma-splitting multi-artist credits against iTunes** — untested, would recover ~8% of licensed clips, free.
4. **TheAudioDB data licence** — $8/mo is affordable; the licence is unread.
5. **Wikipedia/DBpedia CC BY-SA infobox genre** — not probed; the only remaining candidate that is both wider than Wikidata and free of an NC clause.
