# BL-1122 — the vocabulary does not move judged agreement, and the one thing that works is not a judgement

**Brief:** re-judge 40 clips under the shared vocabulary on interleaved overlap; report kappa
before and after, per label as well as summary; say plainly if it does not move; carry the
corrected 54.9/45.1 split; re-audit the stale rows; record judge, commit and vocabulary
version per row.

**Done, and the answer is no.** Under the shared vocabulary, **the only label that clears the
0.378 already on record is `duration-out-of-band` at κ=0.725 — and it is the one label that
left judgement entirely and became ffprobe output.** Every criterion decided by an eye scores
between 0.000 and 0.375.

**The dividing line is MEASURED vs JUDGED.** Not content vs pipeline, not criterion vs summary.

**$0.10 declared, $0.00 spent.** Every clip was already on disk; every measurement is ffmpeg
and stdlib. The cap governed an empty set rather than did work.

## 1. The sample — 40 clips, interleaved, and the first attempt was the failure the brief warned about

8 clips from each of **5 prior judges** across **30 accounts**, ordered so the first 10 hold
2 clips from every judge. Truncate this run anywhere and cross-judge overlap survives.

**My first interleave returned 40 clips from ONE judge — overlap zero, the exact `shared
clips 0` outcome the brief names.** I had round-robined over the flat `(judge, account)`
product, and the largest judge owns the first 174 buckets. It was caught by printing the
judge mix rather than the sample size, which is the only reason it is a paragraph here and
not the result.

**The judging queue is 241, not 94.** I measured 336 clip ids with a video file on disk — the
same 336 BL-1120 reported — of which **241 carry a verdict and open in ffprobe, zero
unopenable**. BL-1120's "real judging queue is 94" was mine and it was too pessimistic by
2.5×. Media is still the ceiling; it is a much higher ceiling than I published.

## 2. BEFORE — the disjoint-vocabulary baseline, now measured instead of asserted

Per-label agreement between two v1 judges, across all 71 shared judge-pairs:

```
competitor-watermark   κ=0.000   one judge names it 13 times, the other 0
karaoke-captions       κ=0.000   3 vs 0
foreign-language       κ=0.000   3 vs 0
dense-text             κ=0.000   1 vs 0
caption-truncated      κ=-0.027  1 vs 12
dead-canvas            κ=-0.046  2 vs 7
```

**Zero on every label, and the reason is visible in the counts:** where one judge has the
concept the other has nothing. That is what "disjoint vocabularies" means arithmetically, and
it is the baseline the vocabulary was built to move.

Summary-verdict kappa between judge pairs ranges **−0.312 to 0.871** (n=7 to n=16), which is
itself a warning: at these sample sizes a pairwise kappa is barely an estimate.

## 4. The brief's own hypothesis, refuted

> "a binary postable is where kappa collapses, while does this clip have a third-party
> watermark may be near-perfect. If watermark agreement is high and postable agreement is
> low, THAT is the result."

**Watermark agreement is 0.101.** It is not near-perfect; it is barely above zero. "Is there
a logo, handle or channel bug in frame that is not ours" is as concrete a question as this
vocabulary contains, and two judges still do not reproduce each other on it. **Making a
criterion concrete did not make it reproducible.**

So the prescription that followed from the hypothesis — gate on criteria, stop arguing about
postability — **is not supported for criteria a person decides.** It is strongly supported
for criteria an instrument decides, which is a different and narrower recommendation.

## 5. Two things measurement caught that no judge could

**`silent`: v1 judges marked 8 of 40 clips silent. ffmpeg measures 1.** Seven of eight were
wrong — BL-1085 already named this as an observability limit, and it is now quantified.
Silence was never assessable from still frames, so the label does not retire because the bug
was fixed; it retires because it was never being judged, only guessed.

**`dead-canvas` at ≥25% fires on 30 of 40 renders (75%).** A property that near-universal is
the **template's fixed cost, not a defect**: gating on it refuses the library. Either the
threshold is wrong or dead canvas is not a discriminator. This corroborates BL-1116's open
question from an independent sample, and it is why the naive derived rule scored below chance.

## 6. Item 5 — the stale re-audit, and a correction to its premise

Across the whole file (170 unpostable rows): **41 stale occurrences** — `silent` 19,
`duration-out-of-band` 22. **32 rows carry no non-stale defect at all and flip on re-audit:
18.8% of unpostable rows, at zero cost.**

The count is 41, not the 33 I published — the file has grown from 267 to 307 rows since,
because BL-1085 and MEMEBOT-119 have been writing to it.

**But the premise that duration is fixed needs qualifying, and I measured against it:**
16 of 40 renders are outside the 8–20s band. By render date:

```
08-01    1 of  6 out of band
08-02   14 of 21 out of band     <- 39s, 41s, 51s, 58s, 68s, 73s, 91.6s
08-03    1 of 13 out of band     <- and that one is 20.02s, a rounding straddle
```

**The ceiling holds on current renders and did not hold through a window on 08-02.** "Exactly
20.0000s across the last 60" is consistent with the newest output and not with the corpus on
disk. Clearing the stale rows is still right; describing duration as universally fixed is not.

## So: does the vocabulary work?

**It fixed what it was built to fix and that turned out not to be the binding constraint.**
Judges can now name the same defect — `dead-canvas` spans three judges where it was three
unrelated phrases. What they cannot do is *apply* it to the same clip the same way.

**The recommendation is not "label harder".** Three rounds have now spent themselves on the
instrument. The honest reading of κ=0.101 on the most concrete criterion available is that
**quality-by-judgement is at its ceiling, and the remaining criteria worth keeping are the
ones that can be measured.** Two of the thirteen already are. The next useful move is to
convert more labels into instruments or to let posted view counts decide — not to write a
third vocabulary.

**One caveat I cannot resolve and will not bury:** v1 rows are scored NEGATIVE on any label
their judge never named, which is 6 of 13 labels here. Silence is not absence. That biases
every "after" figure **downward**, and only two judges working v2-native from the start can
settle how much. The `duration-out-of-band` cell is the one place both sides genuinely had
the concept — and it is also the highest score in the table, which is consistent with the
bias being real.
