## 3. The lesson that made this work — recorded where the next round reads it

BL-1194 wired a 57%-precision positive signal as a **rescue** and made things worse, 71.1% →
69.8%. The conclusion drawn then — *"a positive signal cannot override rejections more
accurate than itself"* — was **too broad**, and it would have stopped this round before it
started.

| wiring | agreement |
|---|---|
| rescue (add picks) | 70.2% → 70.2% — **flat** |
| **requirement (remove picks)** | 69.9% → **75.5%** |

**A rescue ADDS picks and every wrong one costs precision directly. A requirement REMOVES
picks, and only has to be better than what it removes.** The same 64–67% signal cannot
profitably admit pages and can profitably exclude them. That is now a comment on the rule and
an assertion in `tests/test_bl1202_caption_length.py`, not just a line in a report.

## 4. 500 measures better than the 400 he approved — his call, not mine

Sweeping the threshold live on today's data:

| threshold | all: agreement | all: pick accuracy | passes | held-out: agreement | held-out: pick |
|---:|---|---|---:|---|---|
| off | 69.9% | 52.3% | 65 | 61.9% | 45.2% |
| 300 | 74.8% | 64.9% | 37 | 73.0% | 61.1% |
| **400 (shipped)** | 75.5% | 68.8% | 32 | 73.0% | 64.3% |
| **500** | **76.7%** | **76.9%** | 26 | **76.2%** | **75.0%** |
| 600 | 73.6% | 71.4% | 21 | 73.0% | 70.0% |

**500 is better on every metric, in-sample and out**, and 600 is worse — so it is a genuine
optimum, not "higher is always better". It costs six more pages (32 → 26).

**I shipped 400 because that is the number he approved.** Changing an explicitly approved
threshold on my own would be the unilateral product decision earlier rounds correctly refused.
The constant is named and the switch is one line. **If he says the word, 500 is the better
rule.**

## 5. Reconciling BL-1195, which REFUSED this same signal

BL-1195 tested caption length and concluded it **fails out of sample** — the opposite verdict.
Both are right about what they measured, and the difference is instructive:

| | BL-1195 | BL-1199/1202 |
|---|---|---|
| threshold | **500**, its in-sample optimum | 400 |
| out-of-sample n | 50 | 61 → 63 |
| result | 42.9% [21.4%, 67.4%] — fails | 67% [44%, 84%] — cleared |

Re-run today at both thresholds, the picture is **less stable than either round claimed**:

- At n=63 out-of-sample, **≥400 now MISSES the bar by 1.6 points** (67% [44%, 84%] against a
  45.6% bar). It cleared at n=61 and stopped clearing when he added marks and the base rate
  moved.
- **≥500 now CLEARS** out-of-sample at 75% [51%, 90%] — the threshold BL-1195 refused.

**So the honest statement is: the direction is robust and the exact threshold is not.** Every
threshold from 300 to 600 clears on the full 163. The single-threshold precision test flips on
a handful of labels, which is what a ±20-point Wilson interval means in practice. The
**agreement** gain — the thing the rule is for — is large and stable at every threshold from
300 to 600, and that is the number worth trusting.

## 6. What was refused, and the n-gram conflict

is *weaker* than the 64% signal that shipped, because the bar for a rejection is the "he does
not want it" base rate — already **69%** — making 92% a lift of just **1.34×**, and it failed
both held-out splits. **A 64% that beats 31% is a better result than a 92% that beats 69%.**
The shipped rule is the same arithmetic read the right way round: scored as a positive signal
against the 32% base rate.

**The template hypothesis is not resurrected.** 1,252 distinct captions across 161 pages, six

caption**. Within-account repetition swung 89% → 43% across halves. Noise.

**The n-gram conflict: there is no contradiction in production, and BL-1195 agrees with the
record.** Its own report says the mined n-grams scored *"92.0% in-sample and 66–77%
out-of-sample, clearing the base rate on no out-of-sample threshold"* — that is a refusal, and
it matches the memorisation finding exactly. `clippershq/caption_style.py` is **not imported
by anything shipped**, and its `score_page` returns a **count, not a verdict**, so it gates
nothing. Two implementations exist; only one is wired, and the unwired one is measurement code
that reached the same conclusion. Left in place, reported rather than deleted — it belongs to
BL-1195 and it is not wrong, it is just not a gate.
