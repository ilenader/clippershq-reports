## PART 1 — what changed in the schema, and why

### 1.1 Deleted outright

| field | why it is gone, not merely nullable |
|---|---|
| **`mood`** | MTG-Jamendo mood/theme SOTA is **PR-AUC 0.154–0.161 and has not moved since 2020** (trivial baseline 0.032). Essentia's models are trained on **230–446 tracks**, and happy/sad are the weakest of them. Valence is unreachable — Spotify closed audio-features to new applications on **27 Nov 2024**. The decisive fact is not engineering: **human inter-annotator agreement on valence is below the acceptability threshold**, so there is no ground truth to hit. Coverage × accuracy gives **~23% of rows right, ~10% of all rows confidently wrong, and no confidence signal separating them** |
| **`tempo_bpm`** | rides the same licence wall — madmom's accurate beat models are **non-commercial**, Essentia is **AGPLv3/non-commercial**, and librosa (ISC) is the only commercially-free option and the weakest. A BPM right most of the time and silently wrong the rest is the `mood` failure mode again |
| **`drop_ts`** | the only peer-reviewed drop detector scores **F1 = 0.62 at ±5 s** on 100 EDM tracks; ±5 s inside a 20–52 s reel is a quarter to a tenth of the clip. And the premise largely collapses: **67% of clips are `original_sounds`**, where the audio *is* the creator's own export — there is no upstream song at an unknown offset to search |

If a field is ever demanded where `mood` sat, it must be **arousal** — one high-energy-vs-calm bit, validated against hand-labelled real reels first. Never happy/sad. That matches the human-agreement result exactly: arousal is learnable, valence is not.

**Renamed rather than deleted:** any local energy measure ships as **`energy_step_ts`** ("largest sustained energy step within this clip", librosa/ISC), never `drop_ts`. The name is the honesty mechanism.

### 1.2 Added — the audio source that removes video downloads

`progressive_download_url` is present on **100% of all 180 clips**, on **both** the licensed and original branches (an earlier inference that it was licensed-only is refuted). It returns **HTTP 200 with no auth**, supports **`Accept-Ranges: bytes`**, and is **audio-only**.

> **It advertises `Content-Type: video/mp4` and ends in `.mp4`, and that is a lie.** At 67–74 kbps against the reel's own 880 kbps on the same CDN, it is audio. Any code branching on MIME type, or handing the file to a video decoder, will do the wrong thing. **Flagged explicitly because it is the easiest bug in this design.**

Same clip, measured: audio **0.19 MB** vs video **2.26 MB** — **~12×**. At 1,000 clips/day that is roughly **0.5 GB/day instead of 5.4 GB/day**.

### 1.3 `cdn_expires_at` — field kept, urgency deleted

Previously treated as the biggest trap in the design, on the belief that Instagram CDN URLs expire within hours and must be decoded at scrape time or lost. **Measured: ~104 h (≈4.3 days) for audio, ~32 h for video.** Batched, queued and retried fetching is comfortable. The field stays — the URLs are signed and do expire, and a stale one must be refreshed rather than retried forever — but nothing needs building around a same-session deadline.

One adjacent trap kept: **`Cache-Control: max-age` is the CDN's caching hint, not the URL's validity.** Do not read one as the other.

### 1.4 The join key was wrong — the most expensive correction here

```
audio_type == licensed_music   ->  track_id = music_info.music_asset_info.audio_cluster_id
audio_type == original_sounds  ->  track_id = audio_ranking_info.best_audio_cluster_id
```

BL-678 resolved **both** ids for one licensed reel. They returned **different audio entities**:

| id used | resolves to | `clips_count` |
|---|---|---|
| `best_audio_cluster_id` | the reel's **own audio cluster** | **5,584** |
| asset `audio_cluster_id` | the **licensed track** (*The Winner Takes It All*, ABBA) | **837,493** |

**A 150× understatement, delivered silently.** Both ids are syntactically valid `track_id`s. Neither 404s. There is no error to catch — the call succeeds and returns a plausible integer.

This also explains the 100% coverage that the research round had flagged as suspicious. The suspicion was right: `best_audio_cluster_id` is populated on every clip *because it is not a track identifier* — it is the reel's own audio cluster, which always exists. **A field that is never missing is not evidence of good coverage.**

**And a warning this round can add that BL-678 could not.** Replicating its divergence test on this corpus:

| corpus | licensed clips where the two ids differ |
|---|---|
| BL-678, chart-pop reels | **7/20 = 35%** |
| this corpus, Meta Sound Collection production music | **0/59 — identical** |

**Our own data would have shown no symptom at all.** So the rule is *more* important, not less: route unconditionally on `audio_type`, and never test whether the ids happen to match — on the corpus where it matters most, you cannot tell from the data that anything is wrong. The schema persists `track_id_source` so a wrong count stays auditable after the fact.

### 1.5 The hook field, redocumented rather than removed

`highlight_start_times_in_ms` is kept, with four warnings that must travel with it:

1. **It is a suggested START POINT, not a drop or chorus marker.** `instagrapi` takes element `[0]` and feeds it into `audio_asset_start_time_in_ms` — the slot the app's own trimmer writes.
2. **It is TRACK time and cannot be mapped to REEL time.** `music_info` carries no excerpt offset, while **97% of licensed reels are excerpts**. The fields that would allow the subtraction exist only on the original branch, where they are all `0`. The two never co-occur.
3. **It is track-level, not per-post** — 15 clusters, 0 with a differing array. Store once per sound.
4. **The array is not sorted** (38/48 ascending) and the order is undocumented.

Coverage: 81.4% of licensed clips, 0% of original → **~27% of all clips**. It is genuinely useful in one direction only: choosing *which sound* and *where to start it* in a **new** edit, where track time is the correct coordinate space.

### 1.6 Smaller corrections

| change | detail |
|---|---|
| `clips_count` | **VERIFIED passthrough** — exact integer, unmodified by the provider (BL-678, live) |
| formatted count | **never parse it.** Lossy *even in English*: `"5.5K reels"` against 5,584 is a 1.5% error, `"837K reels"` discards 493. **Two** such strings per response |
| `is_trending_in_clips` | added — free trending flag from the track endpoint; null on the clip payload |
| `spotify_track_id` | **partially reverses an earlier write-off.** `spotify_track_metadata` is null on all 59 clip payloads, but the *track endpoint* returns an **exact** Spotify track id. Fill rate unmeasured, n=1 — a lead, not a guarantee |
| `own_library_sound_count` | free and exact, but **always 1 for original audio** (121 clips → 121 distinct ids). Informative only on the licensed branch |
| `view_count` | **banned outright.** Present on 180/180 and literally `0` on all of them — it survives every `is not None` guard. Read `play_count` |
| reel duration | **median 52.0 s, p25 34.4 s** — not 15–30 s. Every CPU/bandwidth figure derived from the old number understated by ~2× |
| endpoint character | `/v2/track/by/id` is also a **discovery feed** — 12 full media objects per page, paginated, with play/like/comment/reshare counts |

## PART 2 — the shape of the data, in one observation

The two example records in the schema doc are real, from the corpus. Put side by side they make the problem obvious:

* the **licensed** record is richly described — title, artist, hook array, track duration — and is **one third** of the corpus;
* the **original-audio** record is **two thirds** of it, and is the one with **712,326 plays**. It has no title, no artist and no hook.

**Any design whose value depends on the licensed branch is designing for the minority of its own data.** That single fact drove more of this schema than any individual measurement.
