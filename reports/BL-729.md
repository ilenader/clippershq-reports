# BL-729: Comments are a noise pit for song ID (5%) and a duplicate for source ID (20%) — close the channel, but keep three free fields it exposed

**Signal density, first line as asked:** of 20 probed clips, **4 (20%) carry a comment naming the SOURCE**, and **1 (5%) carries a viewer identifying the SONG** — 2 (10%) if a self-referential artist post counts. **0 of 20 had anyone ask "what's the song".** Under the 20% bar for the song question. **Close it.**

**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Spend:** **$0.0168** of a $0.05 budget — 28 calls at $0.0006 (ledger `total_spent_usd` 3.0748 → 3.0916, delta exactly $0.0168). spend.json backed up first.
**Scope:** read-only on pipeline code; all new code in `scratch/`. Artifacts: `bl720_spec_and_inventory.py`, `bl720_spec_detail.py`, `bl720_probe.py`, `bl720_analyse.py`, `bl720_fields.py`, `bl720_recheck.py` + JSON, raw payloads in `scratch/bl720_raw/`.

## 1. What is retrievable, and at what cost

**VERIFIED from `_probe_samples/ig_openapi_spec.json` (154 paths) and confirmed live.**

| endpoint | page size | cost | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/v2/media/comments` | **15 / call** | no `x-reqs-cost` override → **1 req = $0.0006** | ✅ works, 20/20 clips |
| `/v1/media/comments` | 20 / call (`amount`) | 1 req | not exercised |
| `/v1/media/comments/chunk` | 15 / call, `min_id`/`max_id` | 1 req | not exercised |
| `/v2/media/comments/replies` | replies to one comment | 1 req | not exercised |
| `/v2/media/comments/infos` | **batch of 10 media** | 1 req | ⚠️ works but see below |
| `/gql/comments` (+`/chunk`) | `sort_order` ∈ {recent, **popular**} | 1 req | ❌ **410 Deprecated** |

Only 13 of 154 operations carry a cost override (8×2, 3×3, 2×1); **no comment endpoint does**, so every comment read is one request.

**Two spec-vs-reality gaps, both paid for:**

1. **`/gql/comments` is dead.** It is the *only* endpoint exposing `sort_order=popular`, and it returns `410 {"error":"Endpoint deprecated. Use /v2/media/comments or /v1/media/comments/chunk"}`. The spec still documents the enum. Cost me 6 calls ($0.0036) to learn. **There is no popularity-sort parameter on any live endpoint.**
2. **`/v2/media/comments/infos` returns `preview_comments: [0 items]`** on 20/20 media, despite the spec summary promising "comment_count, has_more_comments, … and preview_comments" for up to 10 media per request. It does return the counts. **The $0.06/1,000-clip batch path I hoped for does not exist in practice** — the field is present and empty.

**The consolation is real, though:** `is_ranked_comment` is true on **215/221 (97%)** of returned comments, so `/v2/media/comments` already hands back Instagram's *ranked* (top) comments rather than the newest. The lost `sort_order` matters less than it looked.

### Already free: `comment_count`

**VERIFIED across 962 unique media in payloads already bought:** `comment_count` present on **926 (96.3%)**, non-zero on 678 (73.2%), distribution min 1 / p25 4 / **median 13** / p75 46 / max 9,087. **26.8% have exactly zero comments** — paying to read those is pure waste, so volume is a free pre-filter on every clip we own.

## 2–3. What is actually in the comments

20 clips (7 high-view, 7 mid, 6 low), all with `comment_count > 0`, **221 comments read**.

| question | result |
|---|---|
| clip has a comment **naming the source** | **4 / 20 = 20%** |
| clip has a comment **naming the song** (raw matcher) | 3 / 20 = 15% |
| … **after adjudication** | **2 / 20 = 10%** |
| … **strict** (a viewer identifying an unknown track) | **1 / 20 = 5%** |
| someone **asks** "what's the song" | **0 / 20 = 0%** |
| ground-truth slice: licensed clips whose title we already know | 11 clips, commenters named it on 3 |

**The adjudication matters, so here it is in full.** My matcher used the known track title as ground truth — a good design that still produced a false positive:

- ❌ **FALSE** — known title *"The Winner Takes It All"*, matched on the ordinary word **winner**: *"And a winner is just a looser who tried once more"*, *"Still the winner btw"*. Nobody was naming a song.
- ✅ **TRUE** — known title *"Karuppa Kooda Va"*: *"My new hobby: listen to karuppa all day ❤️💥"* and *"OMG @abhyankkar YOU JUST BLEW US AWAY"* — genuine viewer identification of track and artist handle.
- ➖ **TRUE-ish** — known title *"stupid song"*: *"Olivia Rodrigo protecting ballerinas from Timothee Chalamet"*. Names the artist, but the clip **is Olivia Rodrigo's own post** — self-reference, not identification.

**The assumed mechanism is absent.** The premise was that comments are where people ask and answer "what's the song / what movie is this". Across 221 comments on 20 clips, **the question is never asked once**. What people actually write is affect ("❤️", "Bro is Liverpool💥"), not identification. The 20% source figure comes from franchise names appearing incidentally in discussion of a clip that was *already* about that franchise — which the free caption matcher (BL-719, audited 97%) already catches at $0.

### Language — refutes the strong form, cannot settle the question

Script across 221 comments: **Latin 166 (75%)**, none/emoji-only 51 (23%), Telugu 2, Arabic 1, Devanagari 1.

BL-708 found the licensed music skews Indian/Tamil and asked whether the audience matches. **The strong form is refuted — commenters are not writing in Indic scripts.** But script ≠ language: romanised Tamil/Hindi ("Tanglish") is written in Latin script and my detector cannot tell it from English. **GAP: this measures script, not language, and cannot confirm the audience either way.** Answering it needs word-level language ID, not `unicodedata`.

### Sentiment — not readable by this method

202/221 comments (91%) scored neutral on a positive/negative lexicon, 17 positive, 2 negative. That is not a signal, it is a failed measurement: the lexicon does not fit emoji-heavy, multilingual, three-word comments. **I am not claiming comment sentiment is readable, and I did not test agreement with caption valence because there was nothing reliable to compare.**

## 4. The spam problem — smaller than feared, and cheaply filterable

| category | count | share of 221 |
|---|---|---|
| empty text (GIF/sticker comments — `giphy_media_info` on 21) | 23 | 10% |
| emoji-only | 16 | 7% |
| ≤2 characters | 8 | 4% |
| promo / "follow me" phrases | **1** | 0.5% |
| **total noise** | **48** | **22%** |
| **usable text** | **173** | **78%** |

The "Instagram comments are full of follow-me spam" fear did not materialise — **one** promo comment in 221. The real noise is emoji and stickers, which is trivially detectable.

**And the filter is free and strong: `comment_like_count` is present on 221/221 (100%)** — median 3, p90 **1,963**, max **274,745**. Thresholding keeps: ≥1 like → 86%, ≥3 → 55%, ≥10 → 33%, ≥50 → 27%. Two more free fields ride along: `is_liked_by_media_owner` (40 comments — owner endorsement) and `preview_child_comments` (30 comments, 14%, carry inline replies at no extra call).

## 5. Cost, against the measured alternatives

| channel | cost / 1,000 clips | yield | **cost per identified clip** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **comments** (`/v2/media/comments`, 15 ranked) | **$0.60** | 5% song / 20% source | **$12.00 per 1,000 song-IDs** |
| Gemini audio | $0.88 | (assumed high) | ~$0.88–1.00 |
| AudD fingerprinting | $2.40 | (assumed high) | ~$2.40–3.00 |

Comments are the **cheapest per clip** of the three and the **most expensive per answer** by 4–14×. For the song question specifically, AudD at $2.40/1,000 clips beats comments at $12/1,000 identifications, and Gemini beats both. For the source question, the free caption matcher already covers it.

---

## Verdict: noise pit for song ID, duplicate for source ID. Close it.

- **Song identification: 5% strict, 10% loose. Under the 20% bar. Closed.** Not because comments are noisy — 78% of them are usable text — but because **people do not use comments to identify music**. The demand the channel was premised on (0/20 asking) is not there.
- **Source identification: 20%**, but it names franchises that the $0 caption matcher already finds, on clips that were already about that franchise. No new coverage for money.
- **Keep three things this probe found, all free:**
  1. `comment_count` on 96.3% of owned media — 26.8% are zero, so it is a free "is anyone talking about this" pre-filter.
  2. `comment_like_count` on 100% of comments — a real quality filter if comments are ever read for another purpose.
  3. `/v2/media/comments` returns **ranked** comments (97%), so no sort parameter is needed despite the deprecation.
- **Do not build on `/v2/media/comments/infos`** until someone re-tests `preview_comments`; it is advertised and empty.

## Two mistakes of mine, recorded

1. I read `like_count` per comment; the real field is **`comment_like_count`**. My first pass therefore reported *no* like data at all rather than erroring — a silent null, the exact failure class this project keeps finding. Corrected above (100% present).
2. My ground-truth song matcher accepted ordinary words from a title ("winner" for *The Winner Takes It All*), which inflated the song figure from 5% to 15% until adjudicated. Title-token matching needs a stop-list of title words that are ordinary English.

## Open

1. **Language vs script** — 75% Latin does not mean 75% English. Needs word-level language ID before the audience question from BL-708 can be answered.
2. **`preview_comments` empty** — spec says it exists; 20/20 say otherwise. Possibly account/permission dependent.
3. **Reply threads** — 14% of comments carry inline replies free; `/v2/media/comments/replies` was never called. If a "what's the song" answer exists anywhere it is likelier in a reply than a top-level comment, and that is untested.
4. **A high-comment-count stratum** — every clip here had ≤9,087 comments; whether identification appears on clips with 100k+ comments is unmeasured.
