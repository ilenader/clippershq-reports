# BL-1140 — paste the links, name the song, get the videos

**One sentence:** The operator can now paste 30–60 Instagram links into the dashboard,
choose the song per batch or per link, and get finished pairs — the matcher is bypassed
entirely, every refusal names its measurement, and **28 real pasted links produced 4 videos
and 24 refusals for $0.0168**, which is the first time the two broken stages have been out
of the path.

**Spend: $0.0180 of a $0.30 cap** — 30 billed Instagram calls, one per link plus two probe
calls. Every other leg is local and free: ffmpeg, Silero VAD, RapidOCR, the render. No
vision call, no model call, no matcher.

---

## 0. The precondition that shaped the whole design

`tools/claim.py takeable dashboard/server.py` → **`BL-1139 holds it`**, and while this round
ran that file went to ` M`. Ten rounds share one working tree and one git index, so the
endpoints are **not** in `server.py`. They are an `APIRouter` in `dashboard/paste_routes.py`,
mounted by `dashboard/paste_app.py`, which imports the real server and splices the routes in
**front of** its `StaticFiles` mount at `/`.

That splice is not a flourish. `server.py`'s own comment says the mount is registered last
because Starlette matches in order; `include_router` appends, so appended routes would each
be a 404 served from disk — which looks exactly like a route nobody wrote.
`tests/test_paste_batch.py::TheRoutes` asserts every paste path sits before the mount index,
and that `install()` is idempotent.

**Handover:** when `server.py` is free, move two lines into it just above `STATIC_DIR` and
delete `paste_app.py`. Nothing in `paste_routes.py` changes.

Run it today with:

```
python dashboard/paste_app.py --port 8788
```

## 1. One paid call per link, and it took a probe to find

A pasted URL carries a shortcode. `clip_media.retrieve` needs `<pk>_<user_id>` **and** a page
carrying `video_dash_manifest`. The obvious wiring — resolve the id, then walk the account's
view-sorted clips pages until the clip turns up — costs 1 + N calls per link and **cannot
reach** a clip that is not in the first pages at all.

Two paid calls settled it ($0.0012):

| endpoint | status | shape | manifest |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/v2/media/info/by/url` | 200 | `{media_or_ad, status}`, 2 keys | **no** |
| `/v1/media/by/url` | 200 | 29 keys incl. `id`, `code`, `user.pk` | **yes, 11,337 chars** |

`/v2/media/info/by/url` is the one the provider's own summary recommends, and it is the wrong
one here. The `/v1` media is handed to `clip_media.retrieve` as a **one-item envelope**
(`{"stream_rows": [media]}`, built to `clip_walk._is_media`'s contract), so the rendition
ladder, the `&amp;oe=` unescape and the xHE-AAC audio mux are the shipped code and not a
second implementation. **One billable call per link. The rendition and audio GETs are CDN and
unbilled.**

### The refusals, across both batches (24 of 28)

| reason | n | what was measured |
|---|---|---|
| `speech` | 19 | VAD over the shipped 20 s at 0.10, **or** a subtitle band on ≥25% of frames |
| `unreadable_text` | 2 | OCR block score / frame disagreement, quoted per clip |
| `no_caption_card` | 2 | text on moving picture, so a strip would leave a seam |
| `could_not_resolve` | 1 | deleted, private, an ad, or a bad link |

The 19 speech refusals split three ways, and the split is the argument for having built the
gate with two terms and an explicit unmeasured case:

```
VAD over the shipped 20 s   13   1.00 .996 .932 .769 .535 .465 .363 .217 .201 .170 .149 .113 .101
subtitle track only          4   0.91  0.82  0.64  0.27  of frames carry a low band
unmeasured                   2   the audio would not decode — see §6
```

**Four clips were caught by the subtitle term alone** — quiet audio, but a caption track
saying something specific on 27–91% of frames. That term is the operator's own stated
mechanism ("a talking clip carries its OWN on-screen subtitles") and without it those four
would have shipped with a song over a conversation.

**No clip was refused for an empty Instagram caption**, and it cannot be:
`tests/test_paste_batch.py::EmptyCaptionIsNotARefusal` runs an empty, a blank and a `None`
caption through the whole path and asserts a finished video each time. The library gate
refuses `no caption` and that wrongly refused 17 clips; the caption is the reposter's
post-box text, it is never drawn, and the burned-in text is the hook.

* **Every output equals its source's dimensions** — no added canvas. `white_frame` has no

* **Both halves double-click open.** The originals arrived as **AV1 + xHE-AAC** and **VP9 +

  h264 + AAC-LC and the claim is re-read off the shipped file, never off the command.

**The chosen song is actually under it.** Correlating each render's first 8 s against the

Four panels on a new **Paste** tab, second in the tab order because it is the tab he opens to
do the work.

* **The form** — textarea, song dropdown, hook dropdown (each hook shows its window and his
  own marking note), cap field. **The poll never rewrites it.** `setHTML` already refuses a

## 6. What this found that nobody had connected

### The no-speech gate auto-refuses the undecodable-audio class

Two of batch 1's twelve came back `speech_unmeasured`. Both **muxed audio successfully** and
both fail `clip_media.audio_decodable`:

```
al.ora940       muxed=yes   decodable=False
cineframesoul_  muxed=yes   decodable=False
```

MEMEBOT-052 measured that class at **35.5% of clips** (Instagram serves xHE-AAC; ffmpeg 8.0's
USAC decoder is partial). `no_speech.refuse` treats unmeasured as **refused** — correct by its
own doctrine, since a clip nobody could listen to cannot be asserted clean under a rule that
says *none*. Nobody had multiplied the two facts together: **roughly a third of all clips are
refused by the speech gate without ever being measured.** It cost 2 of 12 here (17%).

That is not a bug to patch blind. It is a supply number the operator should have: the honest
options are (a) accept it, (b) find a decoder, or (c) let an undecodable clip through on the
subtitle term alone — which is his call, not mine.

### `clip_motion.looks_static` has a call site now

BL-1136 found it with **zero callers**. This is its first one, and it exists because a render
once shipped as a still photograph of an album cover with a fight song under it while every
check in the chain asked whether the picture had been *altered* and none asked whether it
*moves*. It refused nothing across these 28 links — the gate is armed and these clips all
move.

## 7. What did not happen, and what I got wrong

* **`dashboard/server.py` was not touched.** It is BL-1139's and it went dirty mid-round.
* **`config.json` untouched:** 162 keys, 5 campaigns, `7a029ee5447cddd8` (compact separators)
  and `8e02f8d6f6307ae8` (default) — one object under two spellings, both reproduced.
* **`songs.json` untouched.** Rotation is never advanced by a page read.
* **The clip library was not written to**, and is not read by the paste path.
* **I got the estimate's audience wrong first.** My first version computed it in the browser.
  A cap the browser computes is a cap the browser can edit, so `plan()` is server-side and
  `run()` re-derives it from the same function rather than trusting what was posted back.
* **The links are mine, not his.** He supplied none, so I sourced 28 from the library's
  permalinks. Batch 2's 16 were **pre-filtered on the library's own `speech_frac`**, which is
  a luxury he will not have when pasting from his phone — so **4 of 16 is an optimistic
  ceiling, and 4 of 28 (14%) is the honest headline.**
* **One batch at a time.** A second `POST /api/paste/start` while one is live returns 409.
