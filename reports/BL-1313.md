*(`tools/commit.py` refused the `.bak` — `.gitignore:53` matches `*.bak` — and that refusal is

around, and the git-history copy is the stronger guarantee anyway.)*

**2. `ground_truth/NON_TEXT_JUDGE_INPUTS.json`** — a declared registry of judge inputs that are
not text, and the marked handles each names. **A declaration, deliberately, not OCR:** OCR adds
a failure mode — a missed line reads as *clean* — to a check whose entire purpose is that a
clean reading must be earned. A declaration is auditable by one person opening one file.

**3. `tools/brief_leakcheck.py::guard_scoring` now consults it**, merging an asset's handles

| `guard_scoring(clean_brief, ["memwithsm"])` | **VOID** |
| the same call with `assets=[]` (text channel only) | **not void** |

**If those two ever agree, the registry has stopped doing anything**, and a test requires them
to disagree.

**4. `tests/test_bl1313_non_text_inputs.py`** — 15 tests, green.

### What this does NOT close, said plainly

**The card still shows four real grids.** Removing the text closes the channel a checker can
verify; a judge that had seen the card could in principle still match one of those grids by eye.
That is unfixable while the card teaches from real pages — so the four handles **stay in the
registry** and must be excluded from any scored set, redaction or not.

### The one worth handing back

**Three independent guards went red on one thing: a refutation header naming the marked page
the veto recovered.** Writing *"it recovers one page (`armour__mindset`) and breaks eight"* is
true, useful, and exactly what a refutation should say — and it trips every leak check in the
tree. **Marking a brief as refuted must not name the marked page it recovered.** BL-1308 has
already resolved it by redacting the handle; the general rule is worth keeping.

### And the guard caught me

**`test_silent_zero_shape.py` found a hole I had created minutes earlier**, and it was right.
My first `non_text_inputs()` caught `(OSError, ValueError)` and returned `[]` — so a missing or
corrupt registry produced **the same value as "no assets are declared"**. The docstring above
it promised *"never fatal, always loud"* over code that was silent. **A round whose whole
subject is a check that could not see its input had written a check that could not see its
input.**

It now raises `NonTextRegistryUnreadable`; `missing_ok=True` is the only route to `[]`, a
corrupt registry raises even then, and `guard_scoring` does not swallow it — a scoring pass
that cannot verify its non-text inputs stops rather than proceeding. **My first test asserted
the opposite behaviour and has been rewritten to assert this one, with the reason recorded in
the test.**
