## 2. Templates — tested as templates, and his number is not there

Normalisation is deliberately blunt: lowercase, strip @handles, #tags, URLs and punctuation.
Two captions differing only by the page's own handle **are** the same template — that is the
copying he is describing.

### Cross-account: the same caption on different accounts

| | |
|---|---:|
| distinct normalised captions (≥4 words) across 161 pages | **1,252** |
| captions used by **2+ different accounts** | **6** |

He said he had seen ten to twenty distinct recurring templates. **Across 161 pages there are
six**, and the rule built on them is carried almost entirely by one:

| accounts sharing it | wanted | caption |
|---:|---:|---|
| **8** | 7 | *"v stepped into the crowd taking in live performances at vogue world…"* |
| 2 | 2 | *"jujutsu kaisen 2018 action fantasy supernatural ongoing storyline…"* |
| 2 | 1 | *"one of the greatest cult comedies is finally back super troopers 3…"* |
| 2 | 0 | *"keeping the energy high"* |
| 2 | 0 | *"popular streamer … designated as the chief inclusivity…"* |
| 2 | 2 | *(Japanese One Piece announcement)* |

As a rule it looks good in-sample — fires 14, 10 wanted, **71% [45%, 88%]**, lift 2.30× —
and **8 of those 14 fires come from one caption.** That is not a template family; it is one
viral source post being reposted, which is the same shape as the n-gram classifier that
memorised its pages. A signal resting on a single string is a signal that expires when that
post does.

### Within-account: the same caption pasted on its own posts

| repeats | fires | wanted | precision | 95% CI | lift | in-sample |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---|
| ≥2 | 30 | 17 | 57% | [39%, 73%] | 1.82× | clears |
| ≥3 | 17 | 10 | 59% | [36%, 78%] | 1.89× | no |
| ≥4 | 15 | 9 | 60% | [36%, 80%] | 1.93× | no |

And it **falls apart on the splits** (§4): 89% on one half, 43% on the other. That is noise
with a confidence interval drawn round it.

## 3. Caption length — the thing nobody had scored

The record reported 605 characters on pages he wants against 109 on pages he does not, a 5.5×
gap, never tested as a rule. On the larger merged set:

| | he wants | he does not | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| mean of per-page mean caption length | **609** | 270 | 2.26× |
| median of per-page means | **592** | **131** | **4.5×** |

The 5.5× shrinks to 2.26× on means because a handful of very long captions sit on pages he
rejects; on medians the gap is 4.5× and intact. The signal is real and the original figure was
somewhat flattered by its smaller sample.

**As a positive signal** (long caption → he wants it), in-sample, against a 38.6% bar:

| threshold | fires | wanted | precision | 95% CI | lift | rejects |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| ≥200 | 78 | 39 | 50% | [39%, 61%] | 1.61× | 83 |
| **≥400** | 50 | 32 | **64%** | **[50%, 76%]** | **2.06×** | 111 |
| ≥600 | 36 | 24 | 67% | [50%, 80%] | 2.15× | 125 |

**As a rejection** (short caption → he does not want it) the numbers look far better and are
not: <100 chars gives **92% precision [79%, 97%]** — but the bar for a rejection is the
"he does not want it" base rate, which is already **68.9%**. So 92% is a lift of only **1.34×**
and its interval overlaps the base. **A 92% that beats a 69% base rate is a much weaker claim
than a 64% that beats a 31% one**, and quoting the first without the second is exactly how two
earlier filters were oversold.

## 4. The memorisation guard — and it eliminates almost everything

Every number above is in-sample and proves nothing alone. Two held-out splits: a random
alternation, and — the honest one — **derive on the 100-page sheet, score on the 61 pages he
marked later**, which tuned nothing and were drawn on different days by different discovery.

**A rule is only reported as shipping if it clears on BOTH held-out halves.**

| rule | random held-out | sheet held-out (61) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LONG caption ≥400 (positive)** | 67% [48%, 81%] **clears** | 67% [44%, 84%] **clears** | **SHIPS** |
| LONG caption ≥600 (positive) | 73% [52%, 87%] clears | 69% [42%, 87%] **fails** | refused |
| SHORT caption <100 (reject) | 95% [78%, 99%] clears | 92% [67%, 99%] **fails** | refused |
| SHORT caption <150 (reject) | 93% [78%, 98%] clears | 94% [74%, 99%] **fails** | refused |
| within-account repeat ≥2 | 43% [24%, 63%] **fails** | 80% [49%, 94%] clears | refused |

**One rule out of five survives.** The ≥600 variant looks better in-sample and is more
overfitted — the extra 200 characters buy nothing but a smaller sample. The within-account
rule swings from 43% to 80% between halves, which is the clearest picture of noise in this
report.

## 5. What wiring it would do — measured before anything was wired

The record's warning is specific: a 57%-precision positive signal was wired to **rescue**
pages and made things worse, 71.1% → 69.8%. So all three wirings were simulated.

**On all 161 labelled pages:**

| variant | agreement | pick accuracy | picks |
|---|---|---|---:|
| current filter | 70.2% [62.7%, 76.7%] | 51.6% [40%, 63%] | 64 |
| A — rescue pages failing only `format_share` | 70.2% | 51.5% | 68 |
| B — rescue any rejection | 71.4% | 52.4% | 82 |
| **C — REQUIRE a long caption to pass** | **76.4% [69.3%, 82.3%]** | **68.8% [51%, 82%]** | **32** |

**On the 61 held-out pages:**

| variant | agreement | pick accuracy | picks |
|---|---|---|---:|
| current filter | 62.3% [49.7%, 73.4%] | 43.3% [27%, 61%] | 30 |
| **C — REQUIRE a long caption** | **75.4% [63.3%, 84.5%]** | **64.3% [39%, 84%]** | **14** |

**The rescue forms are flat and the requirement is a large win.** That is the round's most
useful finding and it corrects the lesson the record had drawn: the earlier failure was not
"positive signals do not work as overrides", it was **rescues add wrong picks while
requirements remove them**. A signal at 64–67% precision cannot profitably admit pages, and
can profitably exclude them.

**Two things stop me calling this proven.** The intervals overlap — 76.4% [69.3%, 82.3%]
against 70.2% [62.7%, 76.7%] is not a separated result at n=161, and neither is the held-out
pair. And **it halves the picks**: 64 → 32 overall, 30 → 14 held out. The direction is
consistent across both splits and both metrics, which is the most that can honestly be said.

## 6. The independence that made this worth doing

**Re-measured on this larger set: of 997 posts carrying both, 969 (97.2%) have a drawn line
that appears nowhere in their caption.** The caption really is a separate surface from the
text every other rule judges, so a caption signal is new information rather than the same
signal counted twice. That was the premise of the whole exercise and it holds.

And the surface discipline is intact: this is a **page-level** signal about authorial habit.
It does not touch the standing rule that **promo and language are judged on the drawn text
only**.

## 7. Ship or refuse

**SHIP** — `mean caption length ≥ 400 characters` as a **positive page-level signal**,
64% precision in-sample, **67% on both held-out splits**, lift ~2.1×. It is the first caption
signal here to survive a held-out test.

**REFUSE** — the template hypothesis as stated. Six shared captions across 161 pages, one of
them carrying 8 of 14 fires, and within-account repetition swinging 43%→80% between halves.
His instinct that repost pages caption differently is **right**; the mechanism is not a
shared template library, it is that their captions are **assembled** — credits, catalogue
lines, hashtag blocks — where a creator writes a sentence. Length is what that leaves behind.

**RECOMMEND, DO NOT WIRE** — variant C, as an operator decision, because it trades half the
volume for a large accuracy gain and that is his call, not mine. The exact patch is one
clause in `judge_page`; the file is dirty with another round's work and I will not touch it.
