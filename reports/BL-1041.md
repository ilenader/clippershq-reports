## 2. Stage 1 — the time box fired, and the metering survived the kill

```
declared: target 3 · 3 tags x 1 page · HARD CAP $0.15 · TIME BOX 900 s
result:   exit 124 — KILLED AT 900 s mid-run, in the paid account-gate phase
          3 paid pages, 22 clips scored free, 9 candidate accounts
          $0.0036 metered · 0 new pages (killed before any confirmed page was saved)
```

**This corrects my own BL-1000 runbook text.** BL-1000 ran *the same 3 tags at 1 page each*
in **145 s** and I wrote that the tag bank is the wall clock. It is not. This run surfaced
**9 candidate accounts against 4**, and each candidate costs a paid fetch plus frame CV over
its last 12 clips. **The account gate is the clock; the tag bank only influences how many
candidates reach it.** Budget stage 1 by candidates, not by tags.

### The kill cost nothing, and that is the measurement

`timeout` sends a signal that runs neither `finally` nor `atexit`. The spend still reached
the ledger — **six rows of one call each, written roughly three minutes apart**:

```
2026-08-03 12:11:46  REPOST_FINDER  repost_finder  calls 1  usd 0.0006
2026-08-03 12:14:47  REPOST_FINDER  repost_finder  calls 1  usd 0.0006
2026-08-03 12:17:21  REPOST_FINDER  repost_finder  calls 1  usd 0.0006   ...
```

Three minutes apart is far past the meter's 30-second stale window, so **every row was
written by the CLOCK bound, not the 25-call bound.** BL-914's tiktok run was killed on a
timeout and wrote $0.0000 against a vendor-measured $0.0668; that is the failure this bound
exists for, and this is the first time I have seen it save a real kill rather than a
synthetic one.

a function of scale — my prices were small relative to the cap. The *direction* reproduces;
the *number* does not, and quoting mine as a general figure would be wrong.

### The bounded correlation

```
lag budget +/- 0.5 s
pairs with both a planned and an applied start : 258
TRIVIAL (planned start < 0.25 s — proves nothing) : 12
NON-TRIVIAL                                       : 246
  ... applied lands within budget                 : 197 = 80.1%
worst lags: 66.6 s, 41.8 s, 40.5 s — all on a planned start of 99.567 s
```

Both failure modes the brief names are excluded by construction: the search is **bounded**
at ±0.5 s so it cannot find a spurious alignment the way an unbounded one scored 0.94 on an
absent track, and planned-start-≈0 records are counted **separately** so a 0.00-matches-0.00
cannot flatter the number.

### Ceiling provenance on real renders

```
42  applied=True   source='config.yaml transform.duration_ceiling.ceiling_s'
15  applied=False  source='config unreadable'
201 (absent — predate the field)
```

**All 15 of the `applied=False` renders still finish at exactly 20.00 s.** The ceiling held;
what degraded was the *record* of why. That is precisely the difference the field exists to
make, and without it those 15 would be indistinguishable from coincidences.

### Watching five found something the suite cannot

One of the five — Batman Arkham Knight game footage — carries a **burned-in pixel-art
watermark in every frame** and a **Portuguese caption**. `clip_postable.classify()` returns
**`postable=True`** on it. Two misses in a gate I shipped in BL-988 and which *is* correctly
wired into the render path:

- its `vision_on_screen_text` is the **string `'None'`**, not a null — the watermark term had
  nothing readable to match, and my "emptiness is never evidence" rule does not cover a
  *literal* `'None'`;
- the caption's function words are `os` and `da`, which are not in the non-English stopword
  list. The term's 93.5% recall was measured on an enriched sample; **this is what the other
  6.5% looks like in the wild.**

Every caption defect this project has shipped was found by a human sampling frames while the
suite was green. That held again.
