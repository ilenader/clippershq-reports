# BL-1144 — the corpus fallback is unreachable, the proxy is clean, and one premise was wrong

**2026-08-05. $0.1074 spent against a declared $0.20 cap (54%). Three videos rendered and
verified from their own pixels. `scratch/songs.json` untouched, campaigns unchanged.**

Three things were left over from BL-1141. Two are now closed and measured. The third — the
walk cap — could not be done as briefed, and the reason is the most useful thing in this
report, so it comes first.

---

## 1. The walk cap: the briefed premise is wrong, and it changes what is possible

The brief says 103 of 219 walked clips were *"never looked at ... clips already paid for,
sitting in the library"*. Two halves of that do not hold, and the difference decides whether
the recovery is free or paid.

`scratch/bl1133_walk.py:186` slices `walked[:MAX_GATE_PER_TAG]` — **and the CDN download is
INSIDE that loop, at line 197.** So the capped clips were never downloaded, never gated, and
their `clip_id`s were never written down: `clips_detail` only ever recorded the clips that
were gated. They are not in the library, they are not on disk, and **there is no list of
them**. Recovering those specific clips is not possible at any price; a fresh uncapped walk
would find different clips and cost page calls.

What *is* on disk is 114 clips the two walks did download and gate — 17 stored, **97 gated and
refused**. Those 97 are the valuable free population, because the gate that refused them is
exactly the one BL-1141 fixed: `bl1133_walk.py:194` calls `MF.classify(caption=...)` with no
`line=`, judging the reposter's caption while the renderer draws the source's own words.

**So I re-gated all 97 under the fixed code, on real media, for $0.00** (520s). The result is a
clean null, and it is worth more than a win would have been:

```
97 re-gated
  86  refused: no static card to cover
   5  format OK, refused: speech_unmeasured
   2  format OK, refused: speech_in_shipped_window
   2  format OK, refused: subtitle_track
   1  refused: TOO-LONG (on its own line)
   1  refused: line unreadable
--- the caption->line fix, isolated ---
  10  clips whose real line could be read
   9  THE CAPTION REFUSES AND THE LINE ADMITS  <- the fix works: 5 TOO-LONG, 3 HASHTAGS, 1 PROMOTIONAL
   9  of those, still fail the no-speech gate
   0  ship-eligible
```

**The fix flips 9 of the 10 measurable cases — and recovers zero supply, because the
file-level gates bind harder.** BL-1141's 766-clip figure was measured on the SELECTION gate
over library metadata, which never runs the no-speech test on the shipped window or looks for
a static card; both of those run at render time on the file. A selection-stage gain of 766 is
not a supply gain, and this round is the first to measure the difference on real bytes.

**A measurement bug I made and had to fix.** The first version of this instrument `continue`d
on the speech refusal, so all 97 died before the format gate and the fix was never once
exercised. A refusal that short-circuits the measurement you came to take is the instrument's
bug, not the pipeline's. It now records both verdicts so the two questions stay apart.

### Should the cap move?

**Yes, and it is free — but there is nothing in production to move.** `clip_runner`'s walk
loop takes every media it pulls and calls `clip_record` on it; there is no per-tag gate slice
in shipped code. The cap existed only in two closed rounds' scratch scripts. Priced this
round: the library-side gate is **0.65 ms/clip** and the media gate about **3 s/clip**, so the
103 would have been ~5 minutes of CPU and $0.00 in gate time — but the pages that would list
them are paid. **The next walk round should set the cap high enough not to bind and say what
it set**; I did not edit the closed rounds' scripts, because changing a finished round's
instrument silently invalidates the numbers it published.

## 2. The corpus fallback is now unreachable — and the joinability story is not what it looked like

**The premise checks out.** Re-measured rather than trusted: `load_song_corpus` emits **17**
tracks, and **0 of 17** resolve to any of the store's 4 song paths — `track_*.json` metadata
ids against `song0N.mp3`. Disjoint by construction, so `hook_key` is `None` on every corpus
render and `outcome_loop` can never attach an outcome.

**But "parked clips still ship on the LRU corpus" was already false.** `pick_song` has carried
a `_parked_by_matcher` guard since BL-1127: a clip the matcher READ and refused returns
`SONG_NONE` with *"LRU rotation NOT used — a gap is correct and a forced match is a defect
(spec 7)"*. The corpus tier was reachable only where the matcher **never got to decide**: no
store, an unreadable store, a store of placeholder paths, or **a clip with no vision label**.

That last one is live, and a probe reaches it today. So the change is:

> **While a usable store exists, `pick_song` never returns a corpus track. It returns a
> `store_repeat` instead** — a real store song and hook, carrying a real `hook_key`, labelled
> `store_repeat` and never `matched`, with `confidence: "none"`, `needs_review: True`, and the
> matcher's own failure reason on the record.

A repeat is cheaper than it looks: rotation is seeded from the ledger and each song carries
five hooks, so the same song twice is usually two different **windows** — and the window is the
unit `should_bias` actually measures. A corpus track contributes to no window, ever.

**Spec 7 is deliberately not re-opened.** A clip the matcher *parked* still gets no song at
all. Putting a store song on it is the forced match two batches were rejected for, and
`tests/test_corpus_fallback_unreachable.py::SpecSevenIsNotReopened` fails if that changes.

### What it is worth, measured honestly

| unjoinable `ok` renders, folded by record | 52 of 384 = **13.5%** |

| … of which `explicit` | 11 (caller-named, no hook by design) |

**No `store_repeat` record exists on the ledger yet, and I am not going to imply one does.**
This round's three renders were all spec-7 parks (`tier: none`), so the new tier was never
exercised by a real render — only by unit tests and by direct probes of `pick_song`. It fires
on the 10 no-vision gated clips, and none of those reached the render stage in this batch. The
claim "it carries a joinable key" is proven at the function, not end to end.

**Joinable did not go to 100%, and it cannot while spec 7 stands.** The corpus route is closed,
but the dominant unjoinable case is now a clip that was honestly refused a song. Making *those*
joinable means overruling spec 7 — a different decision from the one I was asked to make, and
yours. The brief's "joinable 100%" figure is reachable only through that door.

### The first draft of this fix was wrong, and the existing suite said so

I made the repeat unconditional. That ate a case MEMEBOT-067 had deliberately built: the
diversion into the corpus is correct **exactly when the corpus track can carry evidence**, so
a joinable corpus track is a real choice and must not be replaced by a forced repeat.
`test_clip_pipeline.py::test_matched_tier_also_honours_the_no_repeat_set` and the module
self-test both went red on it. The repeat is now taken only when the corpus has nothing
joinable to offer — which on the live corpus is always, but is written out as a condition so
that adding a store song to the corpus restores the old behaviour on its own. **Neither of
those two tests needed editing**, which is the signal that the fix is right rather than the
test stale.

`tests/test_pipeline_join.py::TheCorpusTierCannotJoinByConstruction` DID need editing, and it
had asked to be: *"if that is now intended, this test is the place to say so."* Its structural
claim — a corpus track produces a key the store cannot hold — is unchanged and still asserted;
only the route to a corpus pick moved, so it now takes that route with **no store**, the one
case where the corpus remains the right answer.

**A live crash found on the way.** `pick_song`'s own corpus check read `t["track_id"]`, and 2
of the 19 raw rows in `bl691_corpus.json` are `{file, status}` stubs. Production is safe —
`load_song_corpus` normalises — but that read sits **inside the matcher's `try`**, so a
`KeyError` there is swallowed and reported as *"song_library unusable"*: a real match
discarded and blamed on the matcher. Hardened, and the handler now carries the exception's own
words instead of only its class name, which is what let me find it at all.

## 3. The proxy is clean, and the errors go the cheap way

`servable_by` orders the batch on `vision_on_screen_text`; the renderer reads
`read_source_line`'s 5-frame consensus. **100 clips, both run, $0.0876.**

The sample is **stratified and must be read as such**: only 7 of 592 gated clips are
proxy-servable, so 100 uniform draws would contain about one and say nothing about the
permissive direction. All 7 were taken plus 93 random unservable, and the two strata are
reported separately with Wilson intervals. An overall "agreement %" across a stratified sample
would be a fact about the sampling design, so it is not quoted.

```
proxy said SERVABLE   :   7   real agrees   4  =  57.1%  [25.0, 84.2]
proxy said UNSERVABLE :  93   real agrees  93  = 100.0%  [96.0, 100.0]
```

**The direction is the answer, and it is the good one.**

* **Zero conservative errors in 93.** The proxy never once hid a clip the real line would have
  matched. Upper bound on that rate at this sample size: **4%**. The ranker is not throwing
  away good clips before anything looks at them — which was the expensive possibility.
* **All 3 disagreements are permissive**, and none of them is a wrong *line*: 2 clips have no
  static card to cover and 1 was unretrievable. The proxy read the text correctly; the file
  could not be used. Cost of each: one wasted retrieval, $0.0006–0.0012, and an honest park.

A real-OCR failure counts as "not servable" here rather than as a missing value, because that
is what the renderer does with it — it refuses the clip.

## 6. Ledger writes are idempotent now

`append_record` was a plain append, so re-running a round, resuming after a crash, or a retry
that re-appended added a second copy of a row already on the file. `(render_id, rev)` is the
natural key — `rev` 1 is the `pending` line and 2 the terminal one — and the check runs
**inside the existing lock**, so two processes cannot both find the row absent.

A record with **no `render_id`** — an outcome line, which shares this file — has no key here
and is appended unconditionally. Absence of a key must never become a silent refusal to write,
and `tests/test_ledger_kinds.py` asserts both halves.

**Measured on the live ledger: 1,151 rows fold to 616 records, and the naive row-sum reads
$0.7656 against $0.4080 folded — 1.88×**, inside the 1.3–2× the brief predicted, because
`cost_usd` is written on both the pending and the terminal row.
