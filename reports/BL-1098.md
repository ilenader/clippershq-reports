# BL-1098 — the classifier failed twice, reading caught it twice, and I am not publishing the number

**Brief:** classify the top 30 accounts by what their burned-in text is (HOOK / PLOT /
WATERMARK-ONLY / MIXED) with per-account hit rates; re-attribute the 10 failures; act at
the walk layer; state the honest limit; decide `--override-text`.

**I do not have a trustworthy account classification, and I am reporting that instead of
the numbers I produced.** Both passes of my classifier were wrong, both were caught by
reading the output rather than the counts, and the second was wrong in a way that would
have looked *more* convincing than the first.

**$0.10 declared, $0.00 spent.** Read-only throughout.

---

## What the field rule got right (item 2)

This part holds and is worth keeping:

```
ocr_gate_text          RapidOCR, trustworthy where present     281 clips
vision_on_screen_text  present on 1,926 — but WRONG 100% of the time it says EMPTY
                       (11 of 11), so blank means "not looked at", never "no text"
```

So the reader is `ocr_gate_text` → `vision_on_screen_text` → **UNKNOWN**, and UNKNOWN is
excluded from the denominator rather than counted as a negative. Across the top 30 accounts
that is **956 clips, of which 218 (22.8%) are UNKNOWN** — a page that was never OCR'd must
not become a page "with no hooks".

That rule survived both failures because it is about *absence*, not about *vocabulary*.

## Failure 1 — a narrow regex, and a headline that read as a finding

The first classifier matched `POV:` / `wait for it` / `nobody talks about` framing. It
produced:

```
verdicts: {'MIXED': 22, 'INSUFFICIENT': 8}
top-30 clips: 956 | in PLOT/WATERMARK-ONLY accounts: 0 (0.0%)
```

**Zero PLOT accounts, zero WATERMARK-ONLY.** Tidy, quotable, and an artifact — 90% of every
account had landed in `NEITHER`. Item 7 says read a sample before trusting a count, so I did:

```
"I wasn't ready to cry this hard today"
"pls this is so embarrassing for the interviewer"
"Bender really has no chill at all"
"The fact that it cuts to Scarlett Johansson (his wife)"
```

Those are hooks. They are the **dominant** hook family on these pages, and my regex had no
vocabulary for first-person reaction framing. **The classifier was measuring its own word
list, not the accounts.**

And one genuine PLOT line was sitting in the same bucket:

> `"Batman and Superman switching costumes to trick Lex Luthor"`

## Failure 2 — the widened regex matched nothing at all

I widened HOOK to cover the reaction family and re-ran. Every account came back **HOOK 0**.
Testing the pattern directly against a literal:

```
'POV: the friend driving'   HOOK=False -> NEITHER
```

It fails on the one string the *original* pattern was built around. The widened regex is
broken outright — not narrow, **inert**.

**This is the more dangerous failure of the two.** A pattern matching nothing yields
`NEITHER: 100%` and the same clean `0 PLOT / 0 WATERMARK-ONLY` verdict table as before. Had
I run only the second pass, the output would have agreed with the first, and two agreeing
runs read as corroboration. They were two different bugs producing one wrong answer.

## So: no classification, and no re-attribution

- **Item 1 — not delivered.** The per-account table exists in
  `scratch/bl1098_accounts.json` and I am explicitly telling you not to use it.
- **Item 3 — not attempted.** Re-attributing the 10 failures by account requires a
  trustworthy account label. Attributing them with a broken classifier would have produced
  the very thing the brief asks me to test *for*: a story that fits.
- **Item 4 — not acted on.** Deprioritising accounts at the walk layer is a real,
  cheap lever, and it is exactly the kind of change that must not be driven by an
  instrument that failed twice in one sitting. `clippershq/clip_walk.py` and
  `repost_finder.py` are free and untouched.

**Item 5 anticipated this.** It says: if the classification cannot be tied to postability,
report the classification alone. My position is one step further back — the classification
itself is not sound, so there is nothing to report but the method and the failures.

## What a working version needs

Not a regex. The families are semantic — first-person reaction, comparison, curiosity gap,
third-person narration — and my two attempts show a keyword list either misses most of them
or silently matches none. Either:

- **hand-label 100 burned-in texts** and measure any classifier against them (there is no
  labelled set for this task, which is why both failures were invisible to the code); or
- **use the model already paid for** — one classification call per *account sample*, not
  per clip, at 30 accounts × 15 clips.

Neither is expensive. Both were out of reach once the round had been spent on an instrument
that did not work.

## The honest limit, restated

Only **91 of 2,728 clips (3.3%)** have ever been hand-judged; per-account quality ranking
has already come back at **chance** at that n; **17 of 20 stored fields flip sign between
audits**. Even a *correct* classification could not have been tied to postability with the
labels available. That was true before this round and is unchanged by it.

## What I got wrong

- Shipped a keyword classifier for a semantic task and let it produce a headline.
- Widened it without a fixture, breaking it completely; only a direct test on a literal
  string revealed it.
- **The near-miss worth recording:** two independent bugs produced the *same* clean verdict
  table. Agreement between runs is not evidence when both runs share an untested instrument.
