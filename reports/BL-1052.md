## 1. Every path that decides a duration

| decider | constant it reads | derived or hardcoded |
|---|---|---|
| `clip_pipeline.gate` (admission) | `MIN_DURATION_S` = **7.626** | **DERIVED** — `(RENDER_FLOOR_S + RENDER_FLOOR_MARGIN_S) × RENDER_MIN_SPEED` |
| `clip_pipeline` ceiling | `MAX_DURATION_S` = 90.0 | hardcoded, and it is the *outer* bound, not the rule |
| `edit.py` finished-file floor | `config.yaml transform.duration_floor.floor_s` = **8.0** | the **source of truth** |
| `edit.py` trim budget | `target = floor + 0.20` | read **by AST** from edit.py itself |
| `config.yaml transform.speed.min` | **0.93** | the source of truth; **< 1.0 LENGTHENS** |
| `duration.py` | forbids loop and freeze | no duration literal of its own |
| ranker / `run_batch` / walk / brief | — | **none carry a duration literal** |

**One constant per decision, and the derivation is real.** `RENDER_FLOOR_S`,
`RENDER_FLOOR_MARGIN_S` and `RENDER_MIN_SPEED` are declared once in `clip_pipeline.py` as
*mirrors* of memebot's config, and `tests/test_clip_pipeline_entrypoint.py` binds them:

- `assertEqual(float(floor), CP.RENDER_FLOOR_S)` — **numeric**
- `assertEqual(float(speed_min), CP.RENDER_MIN_SPEED)` — **numeric**
- `assertAlmostEqual(margin, CP.RENDER_FLOOR_MARGIN_S, places=6)` — **numeric**, and the
  margin is resolved by **walking edit.py's AST** for `target = floor + <Constant>`

**The prefix-matching defect the brief warns about is closed.** Nothing compares by string,
so 0.25 and 0.2999 can no longer pass where 0.20 is meant. I verified this by reading the
assertions, not by trusting the docstring.

**Why 7.626 < 8.0 is legal and not a second floor:** 0.93 is *below* 1.0, so the slowest
configured speed **lengthens**. A 7.626 s source finishes at **8.20 s**.

## 4. Pad-then-slow, and the overflow guard

**The ordering holds.** `edit.py` attempts zero trim first, and only then walks the speed down
to the configured minimum — *"the speed is walked down to the configured minimum, which
LENGTHENS the video"*, and *"NEVER LENGTHENS BY LOOPING OR FREEZING — `duration.py` forbids
both"*. A fade alters no pixels; a speed change alters every frame. That ordering is intact.

**The guard fires on a planted overflow.** A 480-character caption forced into a 600 px ×
2-line box:

```
returned 2 line(s):
   'OVERFLOWING'
   'OVERFLOWING…'
GUARD FIRED: clipped to the box, picture untouched
```

`_ellipsize_to_box` *"ALWAYS returns lines that fit — this is the function that makes overflow
structurally impossible."* It ellipsizes rather than re-centring, so the 146 px slide under
the caption band cannot recur through this path.

## 5. Two permanent tests

**`tests/test_duration_floor.py` — 13 tests.** Asserts the derivation is intact, that the
slowest speed lengthens, that the shortest admitted source still clears 8.0, that no admitted
duration in the whole range can fail the render floor, and — the rule itself — that **no
finished video is under 8.0 s**, measured by ffprobe, with an unmeasurable file reported
rather than passed.

**`tests/test_no_hardcoded_duration.py` — 8 tests, AST-resolved.** Flags a float literal only
when it is *compared against a duration-named thing*. Proven to fire on a planted
`clip_duration_s < 8.0` and on the gate-regression shape `media_duration_s >= 7.5`, and proven
**not** to fire on a comment, an assignment (`VISION_BACKOFF_S = (2.0, 8.0)`), arithmetic
(`20.0 * math.log10(r)`) or a non-duration name. **Comments do not exist in an AST**, so the
false positive that flagged a comment explaining a correct decision cannot recur by
construction. It also asserts it scanned >20 files, so an empty result cannot mean "never
looked". **The live tree is clean: no second duration literal.**

## What I got wrong

**I watched the wrong output directory, and it nearly produced a false "nothing rendered".**
Both my harness and the first version of the permanent test globbed `memebot/meme/out/`. A
batch rendered fifteen videos and my measurement saw zero. Had I trusted it, I would have
reported the floor as unproven with fifteen conforming files on disk.

**My first version of the permanent test was red on 20 historical files** and I nearly shipped
a guard that fails on arrival — which this project has repeatedly shown gets disabled within a
day. The fix was not to widen the threshold but to separate *what this pipeline produces now*
from *what an older one left behind*, and to pin both.
