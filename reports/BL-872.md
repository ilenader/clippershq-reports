**VERIFIED** — and it left no `.claims/BL-899-TEST.json` behind, which matters: a refused claim must not poison its own id for the retry.

### Refusing, not warning — and why that does not contradict the tool's design

`claim.py`'s stated constraint is that it must **never block**, because *"a gate that cannot clear in a busy tree gets skipped, and skipping it silently is worse than not having it."* That constraint is about **contention**: another round holding your file is information, and you may proceed.

A malformed path is a different thing. It is not a fact about the tree; it is a mistake in the claim, and proceeding cannot resolve it — the claim sits in the registry looking like coverage while matching nobody, for the whole round. So it is refused **at write time, when the round is one keystroke from fixing it**, rather than discovered by whoever collides with it later. `--allow-new-dir` covers the honest case of a round that will create a directory, as a flag rather than a silent allowance, so the intent is on the record.

### The other half: `start` now prints what was *stored*

```
  claimed -> .claims\BL-870.json
  5 path(s) registered individually:
      tools/claim.py
      tests/test_claim.py
      ...
```

The comma bug was invisible for as long as it was **because the caller saw its own arguments echoed back and never the registry's version of them.** Printing the stored form is how a future shape-bug gets noticed in one glance instead of two rounds.

## 2. CORRECTION — the disjoint-labelling premise had already moved

MEMEBOT-024's measurement (mine, earlier today) was **7 of 2,003, 0.03× independence**. The instruction built on it was that "the two passes have been selecting disjoint populations by accident."

**VERIFIED at this round's start — that was no longer the state:**

| | clips | vision | speech | **both** | vs independent | targetable |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MEMEBOT-024 (earlier today) | 2,003 | 419 (20.9%) | 985 (49.2%) | **7 (0.3%)** | **0.03×** | — |
| **this round, before the pass** | 2,003 | 1,042 (52.0%) | 985 (49.2%) | **458 (22.9%)** | **0.894×** | 527 |
| **this round, after the pass** | 2,003 | **1,500 (74.9%)** | 985 (49.2%) | **784 (39.1%)** | **1.063×** | **201** |

**What the 0.03× actually was:** not two programmes avoiding each other, but a snapshot taken when vision had covered only a fifth of the library and had not yet reached the speech-labelled region. As vision labelling ran on, the two converged on their own to ~random (0.894×). **The structural-disjointness reading was wrong, and it was mine.**

The instruction survives the correction, with a smaller prize: targeting still converts every labelled clip into a fully-answered one instead of ~three-quarters of them, and it costs the same per clip. After the pass the overlap sits **above** independence (1.063×), which is what deliberate targeting looks like.

**Attribution, stated honestly:** vision rose by 458 clips during this round but **only 102 of them are mine** — BL-849, BL-854 and BL-864 were labelling in parallel throughout. The overlap rose 458 → 784 (+326); my 102 are ~31% of that. **GAP: I cannot separate the rest.**

## 5. Recorded for the next clustering round (items 4 and 5)

Both findings are now in `scratch/memebot024_clusters.py` — the file the next clustering round will actually run — rather than only in a report.

**THE FORM-VERSUS-SUBJECT RULE**, in the module docstring:

> The first phrase set was seven SUBJECT families and put **50.4%** of the labelled comedy pile in `unsorted`. The instinct there is to add more synonyms for "funny". That instinct is wrong: the missing family was not a subject. **The biggest comedy family is a FORM** — a near-still face with a caption doing the joke. The vision model describes what is *on screen*; the joke is in the overlaid text, and no comedy vocabulary reaches it. It was found only by dumping all 66 unplaceable scenes and reading them.
>
> **The rule: when a cluster pass leaves a large `unsorted` bucket, read the bucket before extending the vocabulary. A high unsorted rate is evidence the AXIS is wrong, not that the word list is short.**

## Limits

- **The headline premise was already stale when I was given it, and the correction is mine to own** — I published the 7-of-2,003 figure this morning in MEMEBOT-024 and read it as structural disjointness. It was a snapshot of an in-progress walk. The lesson is narrow and worth keeping: **an overlap statistic measured against a moving denominator says as much about when you looked as about what is true.**
- **Only 102 of the +458 vision labels are this round's.** The overlap improvement is real but shared with three concurrent rounds, and I cannot attribute the rest.
- **The pass is 102 clips, not the whole 527-clip targetable pool.** 201 remain targetable. Finishing them costs roughly $0.32 and ~70 minutes at this round's measured rate.
- **`$0.001583` per labelled clip is 2.3× BL-864's figure and is not decomposed.** 15 paid failures account for ~1.15× of it; the rest is unexplained.
- **Two unranked vision runners still exist** and one is live. `select()` is confirmed live only for the runner this round used.
- **The claim-tool fix cannot help a round that does not run it.** It is still advisory by design, and every guard in this file's history has been defeated by not being called rather than by being wrong.
- **`validate_paths` refuses on a missing parent directory**, which is stricter than the repo has been. If that turns out to annoy rounds into skipping the tool, the correct response is to loosen it to a loud warning — not to leave it strict and watch adoption fall. **GAP: no evidence yet either way.**
- **The clustering re-run used a library four other rounds were appending to.** Counts will differ on the next run and the proportions are the durable part, not the absolute numbers.
