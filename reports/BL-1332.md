## The answer, first

**It was luck on the part that mattered most, and the part that was judgement has never been
shown to beat doing nothing.**

Three findings, in order of how much they should change his mind:

1. **69 of 238 rejections — 29.0% — were made without looking at the page.** They were cut on
   an evidence floor: fewer than 3 of the page's own videos could be retrieved. **51 of those
   69 saw exactly ONE video. 18 saw two. Not one reached three.**
2. **Scored against his own marks, that floor does not clear the do-nothing baseline.** It is
   right 83.3% of the time **[55.2, 95.3]**, against a base rate of 63.0% **[53.2, 71.8]**. By
   this project's own shipping test — the interval's lower bound must exceed the base rate's
   upper bound — **0.552 does not clear 0.718.**
3. **Of all 238 rejections, only 41 (17.2%) cite a content rule that has ever been demonstrated
   to reject correctly against his verdict.** The rest rest on rules that are untested,
   untestable, or contradicted.

**And the floor is not free.** On his own marked set, **1 in 6 pages under the evidence floor is
one he WANTS** (2 of 12, 16.7% [4.7, 44.8]). Applied to the 69 cut blind in BL-1327, that is an
estimated **11.5 wanted pages thrown away unseen, 95% interval [3.2, 30.9]**. The page he called
perfect was not a fluke of the tab — it is what a 1-in-6 loss rate looks like when you sample it.

## 3. The kept side

His review of **24 of the 125** kept pages: 16 perfect or exactly right, 5 acceptable, 3 clearly
wrong.

| definition | k/n | precision | 95% Wilson |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **lenient** — perfect + acceptable count as correct | 21/24 | **87.5%** | **[69.0, 95.7]** |
| **strict** — only perfect / exactly right counts | 16/24 | **66.7%** | **[46.7, 82.0]** |

**Both are reported because quoting only the first is how this project has gone wrong before.**
And a caveat that matters: 24 of 125 is a **19.2%** sample that **he chose**, not a random draw.
The intervals above treat it as random, which is the generous reading.

### Which rule admitted the three failures

He named the kinds, not the pages: a **streamer-clipping account**, a **low-effort page**, a
**meme-compilation page**. Matching those kinds against the judge's own written reasons across
all 125 kept pages — attribution to a rule, never to a handle:

| kind | kept pages of that kind | rule that admitted them |
|---|---:|---|
| streamer / podcast clipping | 15 | **C17** (edit-page clause) + **C19** (one qualifying tile) |
| low-effort / random dump | 13 | **C19**, with **D4 unable to fire** |
| meme compilation / countdown | 4 | **C17** + **C19** |

**The mechanism is the same in all three, and the judge said so itself.** `D4 low_quality` is
the rule that should have caught them — and it is the chip the judge **cited most often while
keeping** these pages (6 mentions in the streamer group, 8 in the low-effort group). It
considered the rule and the rule could not bite, because **D4 requires that NO recognisable
subject be present**. A streamer compilation, a countdown of viral clips and a well-formatted
random dump all have recognisable subjects. One judge wrote it plainly: *C26's measurable form
of "random junk" is no text at all.*

**So `low_quality` has collapsed into "no drawn text", and a low-effort page with drawn text
passes straight through.** That is the whole of his kept-side complaint, and it is a rule-shape
problem, not a judgement lapse.

## 4. What is actually measured, and what has merely survived

**The flat answer "the content rules are untested" would be wrong, and the exception is worth
more than the rule.**

### Demonstrated to reject CORRECTLY — one rule, marginally

**C26, "no drawn text at all" (BL-1307).** Fired on **10 of 100** of his independent TikTok
marks; he had rejected **10 of 10**. Precision **100% [72.2, 100.0]** against the 63.0% [53.2,
71.8] base rate — **it clears, by 0.4 of a percentage point.** Its whole gain is **out of
sample**: 3 fires and nothing recovered in-sample; 7 fires and 5 recovered held out. The same
signal reimplemented in code (`clippershq/text_rule.py`) is 100% on two disjoint sets with zero
wanted lost — but it only reaches **~1.2% of his real intake**, because his own C17 says an edit
page needs no written line.

### Demonstrated to reject INCORRECTLY — one rule

**The `dialogue` veto.** Scored **+3** on the 623-mark corpus and shipped as brief v9. Re-scored
on his 100 independent marks it was **−7** (v8 92/100 → v9 85/100; recovers 1, breaks 8), and as
a control it fires 22 of 100 at **63.6% [43.0, 80.3]** — *below* the base rate. It is now locked
out by `tests/test_bl1307_veto_refused.py`. **This is the single most valuable measurement on
the reject side, because it is the one that came out negative.**

### Shipped without clearing, and since contradicted

**C27, the animal rule.** 8 of 8 correct, 100% **[67.6, 100.0]** — its own round recorded that
67.6 sits **4 points below** the 71.8 bar and shipped it anyway on his twice-stated preference.
BL-1322 later found `animals_present=true` **kills 6 of his 37 wanted pages** (16.2% [7.7,
31.1]). **It fired 9 times in BL-1327 and should be treated as contradicted pending one sentence
from him.**

### Never scored against his verdict on the reject side

**D1 `creator`, D2 `not_english`, D3 `business`, D4 `low_quality`.** The per-rule reject table
that appears to score them (BL-1216 — including a tempting `not_english` at 14 fires, 14/14) is
computed on **the 623**, where the label is his GOOD/BAD button on the judge's own verdict. That
is the identity that produced the veto's +3. **It has never been re-scored against his
independent marks.**

### Cannot be scored at all

**The view floor** — no per-page average-view figure exists for his 100 marked pages in any
dataset; lifetime likes are not the per-video average the rule uses. **The language rule** — no
per-page language field on his marked set. Both have fired in production and **neither has ever
been checked against his verdict, once.**

### What that means for BL-1327's reject side

| what the 238 rejections rest on | pages | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| **C26 — the one rule demonstrated to reject correctly** | **41** | **17.2%** |
| D1 (52), D4 (43), D2 (25), D3 (18) — never scored on his marks | ~131 citations across 131 rejects | — |
| C27 — shipped without clearing, since contradicted | 9 | 3.8% |
| evidence floor — scored here, **does not clear** | 69 | 29.0% |
| recency gate — n=5, does not clear | 21 | 8.8% |
| view floor + language — **unscoreable** | 17 | 7.1% |

**At most 17.2% of the reject side rests on a rule ever shown to work.**

### What would actually test the rest

Three things, in order of cost:

1. **Record the chips as typed fields, not prose.** D1/D2/D3/D4 are written as sentences at run
   time and are recoverable only by grepping English. Emit them as booleans per page and every
   future run becomes scoreable for free.
2. **Mark the pages the floors killed.** The 69 blind cuts are the cheapest ground truth
   available — they already have contact sheets. **Put those 69 in front of him as a sheet.**
   That single act converts the largest untested block on the reject side into a measurement,
   and it directly answers whether ~12 wanted pages were lost.
3. **Capture the two missing fields** — per-video average views and a per-page language call —
   on his 100 marked pages. Until then the view floor and the language rule cannot be defended
   or retired.
