Every one of those 38 rows carries **`machine_assembled: true`**. `positives(human_only=True)`
returns **0**. That flag is the most important field in the store: `ground_truth/` exists
because a human deciding cannot be regenerated, and a row a script picked by keyword is the
opposite of that.

## 2. What they have in common — three numbers, never one

"45 of 50 have X" sounds decisive and can be worth nothing. If X is also in 88% of the
library then X does not pick your clips out, it picks out *clips*. This project has made that
error twice under different names: `content_genre` is "70.8% from two accounts" (BL-847), and
`genre_mood_map` supplied 75 of one song's 156 matches off film-recommendation captions before
MEMEBOT-022 emptied it.

So every row carries **his share, the library's share, and the lift**:

```
property                                     his  library    lift  verdict
vision term: players                         18%       0%   43.9x  STRONG
vision term: jersey                          10%       0%   31.3x  STRONG
vision term: player                          10%       1%   17.9x  STRONG
vision term: field                           13%       1%   12.5x  STRONG
vision term: game                            16%       4%    3.8x  WEAK
format: portrait (taller than wide)           --      n/a     n/a  UNMEASURED
format: has burned-in text                    --      n/a     n/a  UNMEASURED
format: the picture MOVES (not a still)       --      n/a     n/a  UNMEASURED
vision term: soccer                          26%       0%   62.7x  CIRCULAR
vision term: smallville                      13%       1%   15.7x  COINCIDENCE
audio: class is music-only                   45%      54%    0.8x  COINCIDENCE
```

`players / jersey / player / field` are real: football-adjacent, and **not** the words the set
was assembled on. `audio: class is music-only` at 45% against a library base rate of 54% is
the table doing its job — a property that looks shared and carries **negative** information.

### Three defects the table found in itself, in order

**1. Judging a phrase by its share proposed an EMPTY rule.** 38 football clips split across
`soccer` 26%, `football` 18%, `world cup` 18%, `fifa` 16% — because they are synonyms. Against
a 60% share floor every one failed, the proposed rule was empty and **GAINED 0**. Phrases are
OR'd into a rule, so a phrase is judged on **lift** and the *set* is judged on its **union
coverage**: these 7 phrases together appear in **21 of the 38**. No single phrase can answer
that question and the first version asked every phrase to.

**2. "Nobody looked" was reported as 0%.** The three `format:` rows came out
**"has burned-in text: 0% of his, 100% of the library"** — not merely wrong, exactly backwards.
Nothing had been fetched, so no example could answer, and a share computed over an answerable
count of zero is 0% and reads as a measurement. `UNMEASURED` is now its own verdict, distinct
from COINCIDENCE, which means *we looked and it is not a signal*.

**3. Lift alone proposed `smallville` as STRONG at 15.7x.** Smallville is not football. Five of
its hits were one account posting Smallville clips whose vision text mentions a game. A term
must now also appear across **≥3 accounts** with **≤60% from any one**, and the page prints the
reason inline: *"39.9x lift but only 3 accounts, 71% of the hits from @randomly_curious.official
— that is a page's house style, not the campaign."* Removing those artefacts moved GAINED from
**77 to 46**.

A fourth was found by the suite rather than the run: **a term appearing in ZERO library clips
got `lift = None` and fell through to COINCIDENCE** — the strongest possible signal ranked as
the weakest. The live library happens to contain a few of every football word, so only a test
with a synthetic library could see it. Add-one smoothing: never having seen a term in N clips
does not establish a rate of 0, it bounds it below 1/N.
