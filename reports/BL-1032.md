## 1. The 16× discrepancy — resolved, and it is not resolution

### The instrument bug I hit first, and had to fix before measuring anything

The first probe timed the *same* 720×1280 frame at **33.12 s → 14.59 s → 12.35 s** across three
"resolutions" that were all no-ops. Two faults at once: the frames are natively 720 wide so
"full/1080/720" were byte-identical, and the first `readtext` call pays lazy model init. Timing
that as a resolution effect attributes warm-up to pixels — which is a plausible way to publish a
16×. The harness now warms the reader on a synthetic image (uncounted) and marks any scale wider
than the frame as `noop`.

### The controlled repeat — one image, eight reads, one warmed process

```
rep 1  24.54s     rep 5   6.04s
rep 2  10.88s     rep 6   4.81s
rep 3  13.05s     rep 7   4.58s
rep 4  14.26s     rep 8   5.13s

min 4.58s   median 8.46s   max 24.54s   -> 5.4x spread on IDENTICAL input
```

Elsewhere in the same run that same file read in **128.35 s**. **Total observed range on one
frame: 4.58 s – 128.35 s, 28×.** This tree had 9–10 rounds in flight throughout, all running
Python, ffmpeg and test suites; easyocr on CPU contends with every one of them.

**So BL-1002b (7.53 s) and BL-1027 (120.8 s / 136.1 s) are not in conflict.** Both are honest
samples of a distribution whose spread is larger than the discrepancy they appeared to disagree
about. The 16× needed no explanation beyond the box it was measured on.

### The cost curve, such as it is

6 clips × 4 scales, warmed, scored against BL-1027's own extracted text (free ground truth from a
different round — not this script scoring itself).

| scale | n | median s | min | max | no-op? | median token recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| full | 6 | 14.94 | 6.72 | 52.76 | 0/6 | **1.00** |
| 1080 | 6 | 15.98 | 7.30 | **128.35** | **6/6** | 1.00 |
| 720 | 6 | 11.41 | 4.08 | 73.48 | **6/6** | 1.00 |
| 540 | 6 | 8.94 | 2.46 | 24.64 | 0/6 | **0.95** |

**The min-to-max column is the result.** Every row spans an order of magnitude; the medians
differ by less than the within-row spread. A curve whose error bars swallow its own signal is not
a curve.

> **Off-brief:** the brief asked for ≥20 clips. I measured **6**. Once one image had been shown to
> vary 28× against itself, more clips would have bought more samples of the load and no more
> information about the cost. The 6-clip table is reported for completeness, not as the finding.

## 2. The knee — there isn't one, and here is why

Two independent reasons, either sufficient:

**(a) There is almost no headroom to downscale.** Native source resolution across 40 clips:

| resolution | clips |
|---|---:|
| **720×1280** | **28 (70%)** |
| 1080×1920 | 10 (25%) |
| 360×640 | 1 |
| 1078×1234 | 1 |

BL-1027's lead — *scale the frame before OCR* — assumed 1080×1920 inputs. **70% of the library is
already 720 wide.** For those clips "downscale to 1080/720" is a no-op, and the only real
reduction available is 540.

**(b) 540 loses text.** Token recall against BL-1027's full-resolution reading: **3 of 6 clips lose
tokens at 540** — recall 0.91, 0.50 and **0.43**. One of those is a Portuguese burned-in subtitle,
which is exactly the population this whole effort exists to reach.

So the only affordable-looking scale is the one that stops reading the thing we are trying to
read, and its time saving (median 8.94 s vs 11.41 s) sits well inside a 5.4× noise band.

**AFFORDABILITY IS REFUSED.** At the observed 4.58–128 s spread, a 2,728-clip sweep is somewhere
between **3.5 and 97 CPU-hours**. That is not an estimate with error bars; it is a range that
contains both "run it tonight" and "do not run it at all", and no amount of re-measuring on this
machine narrows it. Per item 2, I stop here rather than start a sweep I cannot price — the same
standard BL-1004 applied when it refused a 0.23-precision gate against a 0.80 bar.

**What would change the answer:** a quiet machine (measure with nothing else in flight), or a GPU,
where easyocr's detector is 10–50× faster and load contention stops dominating. Neither is
something this round can conjure, and both are cheap to check before anyone re-opens this.

## 5. The pattern limit, recorded

The watermark term matches **`@handle` and `domain.tld` only**. A bare word — `PRIMECUTTV`,
`SCENESTING`, `ALPHANATIV` — is invisible to it **however clearly OCR reads it**. BL-1027 read
PRIMECUTTV on three clips and the gate could not act on any of them.

**This is a pattern limit, not an eyesight limit,** and it is not re-opened here. BL-1015 measured
every widening against 35 frame-labelled clips and refused them all: bare-brand **43.8%**
precision; +ALL-CAPS **50.0%** at 14.3% recall; ALL-CAPS+CORNER **0% recall** — because OCR does
not preserve case on faint marks (`ALPHANATIV` reads as `alphanatv`, `4LPHAnATIV`, `alpmAMAT`).
`tests/test_clip_postable.py` asserts the refusal, so it cannot be landed without deleting the
measurement.

**The consequence, stated plainly:** even a free, perfect OCR sweep would not let this term act on
a bare-brand watermark. Cost is the blocker for the *field*; the pattern is a separate blocker for
the *term*, and clearing one does not clear the other.
