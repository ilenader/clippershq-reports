# BL-1180 — the OCR was not misreading the type, it was looking in the wrong half of the frame

**The operator's verdict was "the OCR needs to be on a higher level — it's not doing a good job
at all", and BL-1177 had already filed the cause as *"OCR fragments on stylised type, 5 of his
own 37"*. That diagnosis was wrong, and two of the three fragments it named are not OCR errors
at all.**

I hand-read all 82 clips off the frames — his 37 example reels and the 45 pages actually
delivered — and scored the shipped reader against what is on screen. Of its 13 failures:

| | |
|---|---:|
| the poster's line sat **below the top-45% band** and was thrown away unread | **11 of 13** |
| an actual misread of stylised type | **2 of 13** |
| *separately:* text **invented** on a clip carrying no poster line at all | **6 clips** |

`GTANyTC` and `ADEGA` — the two fragments BL-1177 quoted as stylised-type failures — are the
engine correctly reading **a T-shirt and a bar sign**, on two clips that carry no burned-in
line whatsoever. Nothing was misread. Text was manufactured for a clip that had none.

It was a **crop** failure and a **refusal** failure. It was never an eyesight failure.

Every figure here came from `.venv\Scripts\python.exe`, **Python 3.11.9**. **Cost: $0.00** —
all 357 frames were already on disk from BL-1177 and re-reading them is local CPU.

> **A note on the id.** This round began as BL-1178. A concurrent round already held that
> prefix and **overwrote my scoring script mid-run**. Everything moved to `bl1180_`; the two
> cached OCR passes survived only because their filenames did not collide. The check this repo

## 1. The corpus, and why it is 82 and not 77

| population | n | frames each | what it is |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| his example reels | 37 | 6 | the format he says he wants |
| **delivered** pages | 45 | 3 | what the filter actually judged and shipped |
| **total** | **82** | | **357 frames** |

The brief said "40 delivered pages"; the shipped CSV (`exports_meme.../meme_pages_20260807.csv`)
has **45 rows**, so all 45 are used. The two populations are **never pooled** — the reels are
hand-picked stylised memes and the pages are whatever the crawl found, so one accuracy number
over both describes neither.

**Ground truth is a human reading the picture.** Every clip's middle frame was rendered to a
labelled contact sheet and read by eye. The nine clips whose middle frame showed no line were
then viewed at *every* frame before being labelled empty — *"no text on the frame sampled"* and
*"no text on this clip"* are different claims and only the second is a true negative. Labels
were written down **before** any variant was run: `scratch/bl1180_labels.json`.

## 2. What the shipped reader scores, per text style

`exact` = normalised string match. `near` = one or two characters out — still a correct
hook-vs-synopsis judgement — and it is **not** folded into the headline number.

| style | n | exact | near | partial | **FAIL** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| plain (white-on-black / black-on-white) | 56 | 33 (58.9%) | 9 | 6 | 8 (14.3%) |
| card / tweet screenshot | 9 | 1 (11.1%) | 4 | 2 | 2 (22.2%) |
| **outlined** | 8 | 1 (12.5%) | 2 | 2 | **3 (37.5%)** |
| **coloured** | 4 | 0 (0.0%) | 0 | 2 | **2 (50.0%)** |
| shadowed | 1 | 1 (100%) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **on a busy background** | 22 | 3 (13.6%) | 4 | 3 | **12 (54.5%)** |
| **line sits below the band** | 12 | 1 (8.3%) | 0 | 0 | **11 (91.7%)** |
| app UI screenshot | 2 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 |

*Styles overlap — an outlined line on a busy background is both — so the rows do not sum.*

**Overall: exact 35/72 = 48.6%, FAIL 13 = 18.1%, and text invented on 6 of the 10 clips that
carry no line.** Reels 67.6% exact and zero failures; delivered pages **31.6% exact, 34.2%
failures**. The filter was reading his own examples well and the pages it shipped badly.

That `lower` row is the whole story: **91.7%**. `TOP_FRACTION = 0.45` is a *guess about where
the line is*, and on 12 of 82 clips the guess is wrong and the line is discarded unread.

## 3. The two levers everyone reaches for first — both measured, both dead

Rather than argue about them, I cached every block with its box and score from two engine
passes over all 357 frames, so every post-processing rule could be scored on identical
evidence for free.

| lever | result |
|---|---|
| **frame downscale** — `scale=720:-2` before OCR | **a no-op.** `ffprobe` says the source videos are already 720×1280, and RapidOCR's detector runs `limit_type: min, limit_side_len: 736`, which scales the *short* side **up**. There was never a downscale to remove. |
| **detector resolution** 736 → 1440 | **worse: 58.3% → 51.4% exact**, and it invents more text, not less. |
| **confidence threshold** 0.30 … 0.70 | **inert** — 37–38 exact across the whole range. |

Higher resolution is the obvious fix and it is the wrong one: more pixels find *more background
signage*, which is the failure mode that was already hurting.

## 4. What actually moved it, run apart so each change is credited honestly

Two things changed, so the 2×2 was run in full (n=82, `scratch/bl1180_decompose.py`):

| crop | merge | exact | FAIL | invented |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| top-45% band | exact-mode | **35 (48.6%)** | 13 (18.1%) | 6 |
| top-45% band | medoid | 37 (51.4%) | 15 (20.8%) | 6 |
| **largest paragraph** | exact-mode | 39 (54.2%) | 8 (11.1%) | 7 |
| **largest paragraph** | **medoid** | **42 (58.3%)** | 7 (9.7%) | 7 |

**The crop is the bigger lever.** Instead of guessing *where* the line is, ask *what* it is:
the poster's hook is the **biggest type on the frame**, because it is the thing that has to be
read mid-scroll — the clip's own subtitles, its watermark and any app UI are all set smaller.
Ranked by `median glyph height × √characters`; height alone hands the frame to a three-letter
watermark, character count alone hands it to a wall of subtitles. Both failures were observed.

**The merge is the medoid, not the mode.** Six frames that each slip a *different* single
character agree on nothing under exact equality, so the shipped rule returned an arbitrary one
of them and reported a confidence of 1.

## 5. Multi-frame agreement — and the assumption it broke

"Burned-in text does not move, so agreement across frames is free confidence." That holds for a
poster's hook. **It is false for a clip carrying rolling subtitles**, where two frames read

```
"We stepped in. And dropped the Fat N*gga Tek."
"We pulled up to the savannah in a matte black Range Rover."
```

and **both are correct reads of the frame they came from**. The engine did not fail. There is
no single line to return.

So agreement is not a statement about the *engine* — it is a statement about the *clip*:

| agreement across frames | clips | FAIL rate | clips carrying no poster line |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1.00 (every frame) | 56 | **1.8%** | none present |
| 0.67 – 0.99 | 6 | 16.7% | none present |
| ≤ 0.33 (no consensus) | 19 | **55.6%** | **all 10, and 7 of 7 inventions** |

The separation is clean enough to be the refusal rule, so the threshold sits between them at
**0.66** — on a 3-frame page that reads "at least two of the three agree".

## 7. Speed and cost, at p50 and p99 — never a mean

Per frame, RapidOCR only (the frame is already extracted), n=357:

| | min | p50 | p90 | p99 | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| stock detector | 0.18 s | **0.62 s** | 1.21 s | **1.97 s** | 2.27 s |
| detector at 1440 | 0.79 s | 1.70 s | 3.77 s | 5.26 s | 5.40 s |

Under **deliberate** contention, the same 48 frames at 1 worker and at 4 processes:

| workers | p50 | p90 | p99 | max/min |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1.15 s | 2.33 s | 3.44 s | **10.0×** |
| 4 | **2.20 s** | 4.38 s | **7.47 s** | **15.5×** |

Contention costs **1.9× at p50 and 2.2× at p99**, while throughput roughly doubles
(0.7 → 1.5 frames/s). **Quote the 4-worker p99 for a sweep, because a sweep *is* the 4-worker
run.** A single frame varies 10–15× against itself, which is exactly why no mean is published.

At 3 frames a page, a 220-page run is **≈ 11 min of OCR at p50 and ≈ 25 min at p99**, and it is
**$0.00** — a local model on a CDN download.

## 9. What it still cannot read, and what a page with unreadable text should do

**It refuses 20 of 82 clips (24%), and that is the deliberate half of the fix.** A wrong hook
sends a page to the wrong side of a filter silently; a refusal is visible and can be re-read.

| the 20 refusals | |
|---|---:|
| carry **no poster line at all** — correctly withheld | 10 |
| would have been a fragment or a wrong line | 6 |
| **were reads that were actually right — the price** | **4** |

The four it gives up: `her Liked a message. 28w`, `Mandir Jaaun`, `"do u have a..."`,
`THAT HE WAS WEARING`. All four are short lines on clips whose *other* frames carry different
text, so the clip cannot be told apart from a rolling subtitle without reading it.

**The two it still gets wrong**, both on delivered pages, both multi-panel layouts rather than
stylised type:

- `bookedwithlilly_` — two drawn lines of similar size; it returns the lower one.
- `captain_india616` — a four-panel meme; it returns `MCU fans right now` and stops.

**Coloured type is the honest remaining weakness**: 4 clips, 3 refused, 1 partial, **0 exact**.
Yellow-on-water and dark-red-on-cream are still not read. n=4 is too small to quote a rate
from, and it is named here rather than averaged away.

**The handling.** `top_text()` returns `text=None` and never `""` — the same distinction
`clip_ocr.gate_text_for` already makes. `""` is a *measurement* that the clip carries no line;
filing "I could not read this" as "there is nothing here" is exactly what let six clips be
judged on background signage. `reason` is always populated, so a run reports *why* it declined
instead of showing a blank column.

**The one thing not done, and why.** Wiring this into the live funnel means editing
`clippershq/meme_finder.py`, which **a concurrent round is modifying right now** — it is `M` in
the working tree and is not mine. The call is one line:

```python
import frame_text
res = frame_text.top_text_for(pngs)          # 3+ frames
post["top_text"] = res["text"] or ""
post["top_text_unreadable"] = res["text"] is None and bool(res["raw"])
```

A post with `top_text_unreadable` should count as **unknown** in `judge_page`, not fall through
to the caption — BL-1177's whole finding is that the caption is the wrong string.
