# Assisted clip review — brief

**Follows:** BL-662 and BL-663
**Status:** proposal
**Respects:** BL-518 (no automatic status change) and BL-521 (no clipper ever
sees machine suspicion) as hard constraints, not preferences

---

## 1. There is a live problem, and it comes before everything else

BL-663 measured `rule_shadow_decisions` at 17:47 UTC today: **7 rows, and every
one has `captionPresent = false` and `soundIdPresent = false`.**

The write path deployed at 12:58 and is firing correctly. But the evaluator is
receiving no caption and no sound id, so all 7 rows returned `cannot_evaluate`.
The shadow is accumulating rows that decide nothing.

Nothing proposed below works until that is diagnosed. This is not a scheduling
question — the system is running right now and producing empty verdicts.

**Diagnostic, per BL-663:** trace the submit `raw` for one TikTok, one
Instagram and one YouTube clip and confirm what `extractClipMetadata` actually
receives. Two known candidates: the Instagram nested sound path
(`clips_metadata.music_info.music_asset_info.id`) that the current extractor
does not reach, and whether the submit `raw` is a summary rather than the full
provider item. The `skipHikerOverlay: true` question from BL-662 is unresolved
and may mean Instagram captures nothing at all.

## 2. What we want to build

A note attached to every pending clip, written by the evaluator, read by the
reviewer.

The note says what the machine found, what it could not check, why it thinks
what it thinks, and — where relevant — a draft reply the reviewer can edit and
send.

**The bot decides nothing.** It writes notes. This is not a step toward
auto-rejection; it is worth building even if auto-rejection never happens.

## 3. Why this shape, for this platform specifically

**The time is worth having.** Clip review costs 14.9 owner-hours a month (76%
of measurable owner time), plus 17.0 hours from the one scoped reviewer
carrying 52.6% of decisions. Roughly 32 hours a month across ~2,300 clips. A
note that pre-gathers the facts and pre-writes the rejection text takes a bite
out of that without touching a single decision.

**Auto-rejection is a long way off and may never arrive.** R-2 measured 11.05%
false rejection on the caption gate, and found humans approve 85% of clips that
break the hashtag rule. BL-663's arithmetic is the part to hold onto: at ~2,300
clips a month across 127 clippers, **even a 1% false-rejection rate is about 23
honest clippers wrongly rejected every month.** Notes deliver value now without
waiting on a number that is 2 to 3 months away at best.

**Undo does not rescue a bad gate.** Rejections can be reversed in seconds, and
89 owner overrides happened in the last 30 days, so the mechanism works. But it
only fires when someone notices. A clipper who shrugs and stops submitting
generates no override and no signal. Reversibility lowers the cost of a caught
mistake and does nothing for an uncaught one.

**78 of 86 production rule lines are `HUMAN_ONLY`.** The rulebook is mostly
judgement by design. A system that only spoke about machine-checkable rules
would be silent on most of what reviewers assess — which is why the note lists
human-only requirements for the reviewer rather than ignoring them.

## 4. What the reviewer sees

```
BOT NOTE   [likely_reject]   confidence: high
Would reject on REQUIRED_SOUND.

MACHINE CHECKS
  [FLAG] REQUIRED_SOUND: Sound id is 111222333, campaign requires 76219603...
  [ ok ] REQUIRED_CAPTION_TOKENS: Caption contains every required token.

FOR YOUR EYES (no data can settle these)
  - Edit must feel high energy and well paced

MY THINKING
  This is an exact comparison against data the platform stamps on the post,
  not an interpretation of anything.
  Before acting on it: an original-sound variant carries its own per-post id,
  and a short link that failed to resolve reads as no sound at all.

DRAFT REPLY  (edit before sending, nothing is sent by me)
  | Thanks for the submission. This one can't be approved as-is.
  | The clip isn't using the campaign sound - please repost with the sound
  | linked in the campaign brief.

  Advisory only. No status, earnings or payout touched.
  The clipper sees none of this.
```

Four things earn their place:

**Failure modes printed beside certainty.** BL-663 requires that a check is
only called certain alongside what would make it wrong. The note prints that
line every time a certain rule fires, so a reviewer never sees confidence
without its caveat.

**Human-only rules surfaced, not hidden.** They are 90% of the rulebook.

**What could not be checked.** A clip whose sound was unreadable and a clip
whose sound was wrong are entirely different situations, and collapsing them is
how automated review goes bad.

**The draft reply, in clipper language.** Reviewers currently type rejection
reasons by hand. The draft never contains raw ids — a 19-digit sound id is not
actionable to a clipper.

## 5. Where it fits, and what not to collide with

```
campaign-rules.ts       verdicts                     EXISTS
rule_shadow_decisions   storage                      EXISTS (live 12:58 today)
 -> the note layer      reviewer-readable output     MISSING - this proposal
```

`campaign-rules.ts` is not being replaced. The note layer consumes its verdicts
and adds presentation.

Three existing systems to stay clear of:

- **BL-599 peer-rank** — an independent bot-detection signal, fires on 3.2%,
  ranks for the owner, surfaced in the fraud-review panel. Different signal,
  different surface. Do not merge them.
- **BL-650 screenshot reader** — inert, `OCR_SPACE_API_KEY` unset, accuracy
  never measured. Out of scope.
- **`fraudScore`** — R-5 proved it has no predictive power (19.0% vs 19.5%
  reject). Nothing may be gated on it, and the note must never cite it.

## 6. Build order

**Step 0 — diagnose `captionPresent = false`.** Blocking. Everything else is
theoretical until metadata reaches the evaluator.

**Step 1 — persist what is already fetched.** TikTok `music.id`, caption,
hashtags, duration, `createTime`; Instagram caption, `taken_at`,
`video_duration`, and the nested sound id once extraction is fixed. No new
vendor call. Add TikTok short-link resolution — 69% of stored links are `/t/`
short links that return 500 and are unreachable without it.

**Step 2 — run the evaluator on every submit,** not only campaigns carrying a
`rulesJson`. Where a campaign has no rules, the note should say so plainly
rather than being absent; "no machine-readable rules on this campaign" is
useful information to a reviewer.

**Step 3 — store the note text** alongside the verdict. Per BL-662, extend
`rule_shadow_decisions` or add a sibling table; do not put it on `clips`.

**Step 4 — render it** in the admin review queue. Owner and reviewer only, no
clipper-facing route, consistent with BL-521.

**Step 5 — add `REQUIRED_SOUND` to a few TikTok campaigns.** There are
currently zero in production, so the one genuinely machine-certain check
generates no data at all.

**Step 6 — let it run,** and compare note against human decision. That
comparison is the false-rejection rate, accruing from normal work rather than
from a dedicated study.

## 7. Attached

`reviewer_note.py` — the note layer, runnable and tested. It parses the real
`rulesJson` shape, emits the existing verdict vocabulary (`would_reject` /
`would_pass` / `cannot_evaluate` / `rank_review`), and composes the reviewer
note. No database, no network, no dependencies.

`python reviewer_note.py --demo` covers eleven cases including **today's live
case** — nothing reaching the evaluator — plus wrong sound, missing caption
token, a zero-width character hidden inside a required hashtag, an unresolved
TikTok short link, YouTube where the sound rule cannot exist, an Instagram
caption edited after posting, both certain rules failing at once, a campaign
with no reviewer scoped, a campaign with no `rulesJson`, and malformed
`rulesJson` proving it yields zero rules rather than a rejection.

Port `compose_note()` and its vocabulary. The evaluator half is only there so
the file runs standalone.

## 8. Open questions

1. What is actually reaching `extractClipMetadata` at submit? (Step 0)
2. Does Instagram capture even the caption, given `skipHikerOverlay: true` and
   Apify retired?
3. Short-link resolution at submit, or a backfill for the existing 69%?
4. Extend `rule_shadow_decisions` or add a sibling table for note text?
5. **Policy:** four campaigns have no reviewer scoped (BAD BITCH ANTHEM x2,
   Deja Shoe, CROCS). The note flags this, but who receives those notes?
6. **Policy:** on YouTube the sound can never be verified. Should a YouTube
   clip be eligible for "nothing flagged" without it?

## 9. Out of scope

- No automatic approval or rejection. `RULES_AUTO_REJECT_LIVE` stays off.
- No change to earnings or payout paths.
- Nothing bot-written rendered to a clipper.
- No new provider spend beyond what submit already does.
- No vision, frame sampling, or OCR in this phase.
