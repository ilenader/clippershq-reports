# BL-766 — Cause found: the run asked for 1,000 leads from a seed pool that holds ~452. The overflow came from related-artist expansion, whose artists carry a social link 17 points less often. Not a regression — a volume-driven source shift, and it is reproducible on demand

**Date:** 2026-07-31 · **Type:** Investigation, READ-ONLY · **Spend:** **$0.0000** — free SpotifyScraper reads only; no paid call, no write to `master`, `spend.json` or any module. Campaigns SHA `8e02f8d6f6307ae8` unchanged.

## Answer

**Cause identified, with a mechanism that reproduces live.**

| | measured today | matching run |
|---|---|---|
| **seed-playlist artists carry a handle** | **93.5%** (n=93, 95% CI 86.6–97.0) | 27 Jul run: **92.8%** — 0.7 pt apart |
| **related-expansion artists carry a handle** | **76.6%** (n=64, 95% CI 64.9–85.3) | — |
| difference | **17.0 pt**, z = 2.89, **p = 0.0038** | |

**The 30 July run asked for 1,000 leads. The seven seed playlists contain 599 unique artists, of which 75.6% clear the listener gates — about 452 in-band artists, before the country, language and dedup gates remove more.** A 1,000-lead target is **2.2× the entire seed pool**. The overflow could only come from related-artist expansion, and expansion artists carry a social link 17 points less often.

The mixing model closes it: an observed 81.1% sits at **27% seeds / 73% expansion**, implying ~267 seed-derived leads — comfortably inside the 452-artist pool. The 27 July run, at 304 leads, fits *within* the seed pool (67% of it) and therefore scored the seed rate almost exactly.

**This is not a platform change, not a code regression, and not a fetch failure. It is the funnel running out of seed and walking into a different population.**

## What I ruled out, and how

**Code change — ruled out.** `parse_external_links` and `email_finder._detect` were last modified **23 July** (`31eb13c`), before both runs. The only commit in the window touching Spotify at all was `39528d1` (28 Jul 13:47), which edited `listenbrainz.py`, `main.py` and 8 lines of `control.py` menu text.

**Config change — ruled out as a cause.** Comparing the timestamped backups across the window, exactly two settings moved:

| day | change |
|---|---|
| 28 Jul | `spotify_use_listenbrainz` **False → True** |
| 29 Jul | `spotify_concurrency` 6 → 3 |

`min_listeners` (50,000), `max_listeners` (3,000,000), `spotify_expand_depth` (2) and the seed list were **identical across the whole window**.

**Budget exhaustion — ruled out.** Paid coverage of discovered handles was essentially the same in both runs: **251 calls / 282 handles = 89.0%** on 27 Jul versus **704 / 811 = 86.8%** on 30 Jul. The run did not stop paying.

**A read failure — ruled out, twice.**

*Re-probe.* I took 40 leads recorded with **no handle** on 30 Jul and re-read their `external_links` today through the shipped parser: **0 of 40 now yield a handle.** A control of 20 leads recorded *with* a handle: **20 of 20 still do.** Perfect separation — the links are genuinely absent, not missed.

*Paired test.* 125 artists appear in **both** sheets. For those same artists the handle rate was **82.4% on 27 Jul and 81.6% on 30 Jul** — a 0.8 pt change, with exactly **one** artist differing (MALIA). The reader gives the same answer on the same input across both runs.

**ListenBrainz — real but too small to be the cause.** It was genuinely switched on between the runs, and it is a new population. But the enabling commit's own measurement was *65 releases → 63% joined → **13 in band*** on a Tuesday. Thirteen artists cannot move a 1,000-lead run by 11.7 points. It contributes to the shift; it does not explain it.

## The strangest part, and an honest split

The brief asked me to explain the steepest drop landing on the **largest** artists, or say I cannot. Decomposing by band separates two different effects that BL-758 had reported as one:

**Handle discovery fell fairly uniformly across every band** — 50k–100k −15.9 pt, 100k–250k −11.1, 250k–500k −9.0, 500k–1M −7.5, 1M–3.1M −10.6. No size concentration. Within the 30 Jul run, large artists actually had the *best* handle rate (87.2% at 1M+ versus 73.1% at 50–100k).

**Email-given-a-handle is where the large-artist anomaly lives** — 50k–100k −2.0 pt, 100k–250k **+8.4**, 250k–500k −11.6, 500k–1M −11.5, **1M–3.1M −19.3**.

So the −24.2 pt email-fill drop in the top band is handle (−10.6) and conversion (−19.3) compounding, and **the size-specific part is the paid email step, not discovery.**

**The leading explanation is consistent but not proven.** Expansion surfaces established, label-managed acts — the no-handle examples it returned include Max Dean (2,774,462), Gaskin (1,335,539), Mija (101,940). Those accounts are typically agency-run, which plausibly suppresses a public email on the Instagram profile. **I did not measure email availability by source, so I am flagging this as the likely mechanism rather than claiming it.** That is the one part of the brief I cannot close.

## Correcting BL-758

BL-758 concluded *"not depth — the audit should alarm, not excuse."* That was **half right, and the half it got wrong matters.**

- **Right:** the original hypothesis, that a bigger run reaches *smaller* artists and quality follows size, is refuted. The size mix barely moved (24.0% → 24.9% under 100k) and fill fell across bands.
- **Wrong:** I concluded from that it was therefore *not* a volume effect. It is — via **discovery source**, not artist size. Running past the seed pool changes *where leads come from*, and that is invisible if you only look at listener size.

So the correct audit verdict is **neither "alarm" nor "expected decline"**: it is *"the target exceeded the seed pool; 73% of this run came from expansion, which yields handles 17 points less often."* That is an actionable statement about a knob the operator controls.

## Limits

- **The population comparison is measured today, not on 30 July.** Playlists rotate and related-artist graphs move; I am inferring the July state from the July artifacts plus a current measurement of the two populations. The 0.7 pt agreement between the measured seed rate and the 27 Jul run is strong corroboration, not proof.
- **n = 93 and n = 64** after in-band filtering. Significant (p = 0.0038), but the CIs are wide — the true gap is somewhere between roughly 5 and 28 points.
- **The 27% / 73% seed-versus-expansion split is inferred from a two-population mixing model**, not counted. No per-lead source existed on 30 July. It is consistent with the independently measured pool capacity, which is why I trust the direction, but it is an estimate.
- **The large-artist email-conversion drop is explained by hypothesis only** — label-managed accounts suppressing a public email. Not measured.
- **ListenBrainz's exact contribution to the 30 Jul run remains unmeasured** and cannot be recovered.
- **The first version of my population experiment returned zero artists for all seven playlists** because `PlaylistTrack` wraps the real track one level down; the shipped `discover_fn` already handles both shapes. I reran using the module's own `_artist_ref` helper so the measurement matches what the funnel actually sees.
