# BL-1185 — the band reader is deleted, the rule survives it, and coloured type was never the problem

**The fix existed and nothing called it.** `clippershq/frame_text.py` landed in BL-1180 measured
at 48.6% → 61.3% exact and 18.1% → 3.2% failure, and then sat there while **two separate copies
of the top-45% band reader** kept producing every `top_text` in the funnel. This round deletes
both, puts the whole producer path in a shipping module, and guards it so a third copy is a red
suite rather than another round of the same bug.

Then it re-measured the thing nobody had re-measured — **the rule itself** — and put a real
sample under the one open question, coloured type.

Every figure here came from `.venv\Scripts\python.exe`, **Python 3.11.9**. **Cost: $0.00** — all
3,961 frames were already on disk. No billed call was made.

## 1. What was actually still cropping, and it was not `meme_finder`

The brief said `meme_finder` still calls its own top-45% reader. **It does not — it has never
read a frame.** `meme_finder.py` only ever *consumes* `top_text`. The band lived in two scratch
harnesses, and the second was written from the first:

| file | had | called by |
|---|---|---|
| `scratch/bl1177_reels.py` | `TOP_FRACTION = 0.45` + `top_text_of` | 4 harnesses |
| `scratch/bl1181_ocr.py` | its own `TOP_FRACTION = 0.45` + `top_text_of` | BL-1181's page run |

Both constants are **deleted** and both functions now delegate. The extraction half moved too:
`frame_text.extract_frames` and `frame_text.read_video` mean the whole path — mp4 in, poster's
line or a refusal out — lives in `clippershq/` instead of in a directory `scratch_gc` empties
every 36 hours.

**`tests/test_no_band_crop.py`** enforces it repo-wide: no file may compare a block's `y`
against a fraction of frame height, and `TOP_FRACTION` may not be redefined. One exemption,
by filename and with its reason: `bl1180_score.read_band_SUPERSEDED`, which exists so
BL-1180's 48.6% / 18.1% stays reproducible from cached blocks rather than being a published
number with nothing behind it. A second test proves no `top_text` producer can reach it.

## 3. Coloured type: n=4 was a small-sample artefact

BL-1180 reported coloured type as the remaining weakness at **n=4, 0 exact**. Four clips is not
a rate, and this project has had to retract that shape of claim before.

**Method.** An ink-saturation detector runs on the pixels of the paragraph `frame_text`
*actually chose* (via the new `largest_paragraph_blocks`, so the measurement cannot drift from
the rule that ships). Validated first against BL-1180's 82 hand-labelled clips: it caught **3 of
3** clips a human tagged coloured, against a median saturation of **4** for everything else.
Then swept over **463 clips / 1,851 frames**.

**The detector finds candidates. It does not decide accuracy** — all 31 were hand-read off
contact sheets, and **only 12 of 28 gradable candidates are genuinely coloured type (43%)**. The
rest are white or dark ink that the detector caught off a coloured *background*. Reporting 31 as
the sample would have overstated it three-fold.

| coloured type, hand-graded | n=12 |
|---|---:|
| exact | 3 (25%) |
| near — a character or a lost space | 4 (33%) |
| partial | 4 (33%) |
| **outright failure** | **0** |
| **good enough to judge (exact+near)** | **7 (58%)** |

**Coloured type is not unreadable.** The clip BL-1180 named as the archetype — yellow on
shimmering water — is `#26` here, and it now reads **exactly**.

**And the refusal is already doing the work.** Across all 463 clips:

| chosen line's ink | n | refused |
|---|---:|---:|
| coloured | 91 | **60 (65.9%)** |
| monochrome | 229 | 74 (32.3%) |

Coloured type is twice as likely to be refused, and the ones that survive the agreement rule
fail **zero** times. **So: not fixed, not refused — already correctly handled.** Adding a
blanket refusal for coloured clips would throw away readable lines to solve a problem the
measurement says does not exist.

### What is actually left, with its number

The real residual defect is not colour, it is **lost word spaces in display caps** —
`WHOISTHEGREATESTPASSER`, `THECOMMUNITYDECIDES`, `Howitfeelswhen`. It affects **33 of 286
returned lines (11.5%)**.

It looks alarming because `is_hook` matches on word boundaries (`\bwhen\b`). So I measured it:
re-splitting every suspect run and re-judging **flips 0 of 33 hook verdicts** — the anchors and
the trailing colon that rule 1 fires on sit outside the run. **It is cosmetic for the filter and
is not worth a dictionary-based splitter.** Recorded so the next round does not spend on it.

## 4. Speed — p50 and p99, and the 10.9s figure is a throughput, not a latency

The whole per-post stage (ffmpeg extract + OCR), one process per worker, 12 posts × 3 frames:

| workers | per post p50 | p90 | p99 | of which ffmpeg / OCR (p50) | throughput |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | **7.78 s** | 10.61 s | **12.15 s** | 2.78 s / 5.31 s | 0.11 posts/s |
| 4 | **17.58 s** | 20.45 s | **22.38 s** | 5.54 s / 11.46 s | 0.22 posts/s |

Contention costs **2.3× at p50 and 1.8× at p99**, while throughput doubles. A single post varies
**2.4×** against itself in one run — which is why no mean is published.

**Reconciling the 10.9 s/page ceiling.** From the frames themselves, a real page is
**5.9 posts × 4 frames = 23.7 frames**. At 4 workers that is ~24 frames of work per page, which
cannot be 10.9 seconds of *latency* — it is 10.9 seconds of **wall clock per page with four
workers running**, i.e. a throughput figure. Both are true and they are not interchangeable:

- **throughput** (what a sweep budget needs): ~11–20 s/page at 4 workers
- **latency** (what someone waiting on one page experiences): **~104 s/page at p50, ~133 s at p99**

The ceiling number in circulation is the first. Anyone quoting it as "a page takes 11 seconds"
is out by roughly an order of magnitude.

## 5. The engine, and the duplicate cv2 — it is a landmine, not a fire

| | |
|---|---|
| resolves to | `.venv\Lib\site-packages\rapidocr_onnxruntime\__init__.py` |
| distribution | **`rapidocr-onnxruntime` 1.4.4, installed** |
| `scratch/bl1061_pkgs` | present, **254.2 MB, NOT on `sys.path`** → **safe for `scratch_gc`** |

**The duplicate is real and measurable:**

```
opencv-python           5.0.0.93    owns  59 files    <- `import cv2` gives this one
opencv-python-headless  4.13.0.92   owns 103 files
                        49 files claimed by BOTH, including cv2/__init__.py
```

`clip_ocr.OCR_INSTALL_HINT` says `--no-deps` is load-bearing precisely to prevent this. It
happened anyway.

**Does it cause a problem? Not today** — a live probe reads a frame correctly through it, and
every number in this report was produced across it. **But the 49-file RECORD overlap means
`pip uninstall` of *either* provider deletes `cv2/__init__.py` and breaks the other, taking the
OCR engine with it.**

**I did not repair it, deliberately.** The repair is
`SAFE_PIP.bat uninstall opencv-python` followed by
`SAFE_PIP.bat install --force-reinstall opencv-python-headless==4.13.0.92 --no-deps`, and if it
goes wrong `import cv2` is dead — with **six rounds in flight**, three of which touch OCR or the
dashboard. That is the operator's call to schedule, not mine to take mid-round.

`tests/test_ocr_environment.py` pins the situation instead: it asserts the engine resolves
inside the venv, that the provider set is exactly those two names, and that a frame still reads.
It is deliberately **not** red on the duplicate — a test that is red for a known, documented,
deferred reason teaches people to ignore red.

## 6. What is still not wired, and why

**The refusal does not reach `judge_page`.** `_post_text` falls back to the **caption** when
`top_text` is empty, and a refusal currently arrives as empty — so a page whose drawn text
could not be read is judged on the caption, which is the exact string BL-1177 proved is the
wrong one. The call is three lines:

```python
res = frame_text.top_text_for(pngs)            # 3+ frames
post["top_text"] = res["text"] or ""
post["top_text_unreadable"] = res["text"] is None and bool(res["raw"])
```

…and a post with `top_text_unreadable` should count as **unknown**, not fall through.

**`clippershq/meme_finder.py` is claimed by BL-1182, which is live.** Editing it would collide
with a round mid-flight. This is the second consecutive round blocked on that file. It needs to
be done by whoever holds it, or after it lands.
