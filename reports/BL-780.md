## Buyer fit — read, not assumed

I read real channel names and titles in every category. The ones that pass do so because the streamers behave like people who want distribution:

> `PyriteKat` — "CHAOS SHUFFLE! :) | 18+ | DROPS | !commands **!ig !TikTok !YT**"
> `itsEMS` — "🚨DROPS ON + NEW UPDATE🚨 PARTNER CELEBRATION STREAM AND GIVEAWAYS **|!Tiktok|**"
> `badgeforge` — "🔴 LIVE | Dead by Daylight | **Road to 100 Followers**"

Individual streamers, growth-seeking, already pushing short-form socials, and playing games that produce clippable moments. That is the clipper's customer.

**Art is confirmed the wrong buyer, and it is the trap it was said to be.** 60 in band — the second-highest raw count of any category measured — and 59 survive the trap filters, so a volume-only ranking puts it near the top. Reading it settles it:

> `Lux_arts_` — "studies and art grind - **!novel !patreon !artbook**"
> `Suitedx` — "Sketching Sesh | **KDM Comission | Get your comic!!**"
> `WispurrPines` — "**Artfights** - 70/100"

These are illustrators selling commissions and Patreon tiers. Their product is the artwork; clips of someone drawing do not sell it. **High volume, clean data, wrong customer** — which is exactly why fit has to be a separate factor and not inferred from supply.

The same logic demotes three more with respectable counts: **Animals** is institutions (Edmonton Valley Zoo, The World Bird Sanctuary, a cat-sanctuary cam) rather than personal brands; **Music** is largely 24/7 DMCA-safe playlist bots (`LeekBeats`, `RelaxBeats`); **ASMR** monetises direct-to-fan through Patreon and runs reruns overnight.

**League of Legends is the one recommendation to take with a caveat.** It has the third-highest raw band (85) but **16 traps — the most of any category** — because official broadcasts and watch parties dominate the middle of the viewer range (`LoLPacificEN`, `YamatoCannon` LCK watch party, `kiittwy` LPL co-stream). It still scores 30.5 after removing them, but it is the category where the filters are doing the most work, and it should be reviewed after a real run rather than trusted on this measurement.

### The language gate does leak

**BL-726 reported 0 leaks in 2,669 streams. That result is tautological and it does not hold.** BL-726's own method note explains why: the client-side re-check "validates the identical ISO code the API already filtered on." It cannot detect a streamer who *sets* their stream to `en` and then broadcasts in German.

My machine scan flagged 31 of 682 (4.5%). **I hand-checked all 31, and 19 are false positives** — `[AUS]`, `[OCE]`, `[SG/EN]` are *region* tags on English streams, and kaomoji or `【】` brackets are not language evidence. **Nine are genuine**, three are multilingual and arguable:

## Item 6 — the accounting is worse than 7.7×, and the channel is not stored

Confirmed: `platform == 'twitch'` gives **69**; `email_source` starting with `twitch` gives **534**; something mentions twitch on **693** rows. **Platform undercounts by 7.7×.**

The deeper problem is that **master has no Twitch column at all** — 66 columns, none of them the channel. Twitch leads are stored under the IG or TikTok handle they resolve to, and the originating login is discarded. Consequences:

- **"Which Twitch channels have I already contacted?" cannot be answered.** Only 19 twitch.tv logins are recoverable from master, and only as incidental text inside bio/link fields.
- Re-running a category re-discovers the same streamers. Dedup still catches them at the email/handle stage, which is **before** the paid resolve — so **no money is wasted** — but supply accounting is impossible and the 2.8% overlap figure above is the best available estimate rather than a fact.
- One column (`twitch_login`) would fix it permanently. **Not changed here.**

## Limits

- **Single snapshot at a low-traffic hour** (Friday 13:14 CEDT). The general-chat category returned 1,128 English streams against BL-726's 2,302 on a Wednesday evening — so every count here is roughly **half of peak**. The *ranking* should be stable; the absolute ceiling is understated.
- **Contactable rate is carried from BL-726 (88.4%), not measured per category.** It is a constant in the score, so it does not affect the ranking at all — the ordering is effectively clean-supply × fit. A category could have systematically worse contact yield and this would not show it.
- **Buyer fit is my judgement**, expressed as a 0.1–1.0 multiplier from reading roughly five channels per category. It is the least objective number in the table and it drives the recommendation. Art at 0.1 versus Fortnite at 1.0 I am confident about; Travel at 0.6 versus Retro at 0.6 is a guess.
- **Trap detection is regex over titles and names.** The rebroadcast and org counts are *not* hand-verified and will contain false positives at a similar rate to the language scan before I checked it — treat 8.1% and 3.7% as upper bounds. Only the 1.3% language figure was verified case by case.
- **The 2.8% already-in-master figure is unreliable in both directions** — substring matching over-matches short logins and misses streamers saved under an unrelated handle. It is the best test available because no Twitch column exists.
- **16 of 60 candidate categories measured.** The remaining 44 contribute the tail of the 1,270 figure and were not re-checked.
- **No run was executed.** These are discovery counts, not delivered leads; the funnel's own gates (adult, followers, panels) will remove more.
