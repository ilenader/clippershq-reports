### The mechanism, proven on the clip where his own card is measurable

| | rows |
|---|---|
| **our strip** | 167 to 837 |
| **his own white card** | 420 to 837 |
| **his ink** | 455 to 837 |

**253 px of white above where his own card even begins.** That is not clear space inside his
caption panel. It is white painted over what was his picture, which is exactly what he
described: *"he's literally making it fill out the whole top of the screen, from the video
to the top."*

### Why, in one sentence

`measure_label_card` finds the card by the property that **it does not move while the picture
does**, and the letterbox bars do not move, and a static dark region of picture does not move
either. So the card runs to the top of the frame. `label_strip_from_card` then lowers the top
by `LABEL_TOP_CLEAR_FRAC = 0.20` **of that card**, and 20% of an inflated card lands nowhere
near his text.

The comment above that constant states the assumption in as many words:

> *WHAT IS UNCOVERED IS HIS OWN CARD, NOT PICTURE. ... what shows above it is the empty upper
> part of his own caption panel.*

On @theclipbuzzz that is false by 253 px. **The fraction is not the wrong value; it is a
fraction of the wrong thing.**

### The patch, why it works, and why it is not shipped

Kept at `scratch/bl1214/clip_pipeline_PROPOSED.py`. It adds a ceiling on the clear space,
proportional to his own type and capped in pixels, applied before the existing coverage
clamp so that clamp still wins:

```python
ink_h = max(1, int(ink_band[3]))
clear = min(LABEL_MAX_CLEAR_PX, int(round(LABEL_CLEAR_OF_INK * ink_h)))   # 40 px, 0.28
off   = max(off, ink_top - cy - clear)
off   = min(off, max(0, ink_top - cy - LABEL_INK_SAFETY_PX))              # unchanged
```

On the measured clip it does the right thing: **strip top 167 becomes 415**, which is 40 px
above his ink and 5 px above where his own white card starts. The bottom edge does not move,
so the seam stays shut.

**It fails `tests/test_label_offset.py` on three of five fixtures, and the failure is
informative rather than incidental.** That test computes

```python
clear = strip_top - card_top
ratio = clear / strip_height          # must be 0.20 to 0.30
```

which under the old policy is the identity `0.2ch / 0.8ch = 0.25` for every card ever
measured. It is not measuring breathing room around his text; it is measuring how much of the
card was dropped, and **no policy that drops more of an inflated card can satisfy it.** With
the patch those fixtures read 135%, 226% and 342%.

So the fix needs that test rewritten to measure clear space above **his ink** rather than
below the card's top, and it needs a re-render to confirm on frames. That is one round's
work and it is not this one. I reverted `clippershq/clip_pipeline.py` to HEAD byte for byte

### A note on the measuring tool, because three versions of it were confidently wrong

`scratch/bl1214/strip_measure.py` finds the strip by rows that are achromatic, polarised to
white or black, and mostly white, bounded below by the first row holding real colour. Three
earlier tests all produced clean-looking numbers and all of them were wrong: "most of the row
is near-white" cut the band off at the first glyph and reported zero padding below on all
seven clips; "both side margins are white" cut it off at the widest line of text; the median
failed the same way, because 79 px bold type covers more than half of its own rows. The
numbers in section 1 come from the fourth version, and the tool writes a debug PNG per clip
so the band can be looked at rather than trusted.
