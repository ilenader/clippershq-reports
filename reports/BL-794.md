## Item 3 — the character-name decision, and my reasoning

**Recommendation: keep the raw tag and change the sentence to `#{fandom}`. Do not map, do not classify.**

I tried to build the classifier first and the data refused it. Token length looked like the obvious signal — characters are short first names, titles are long run-together strings. It does not hold. Across 520 distinct tokens, the short bucket contains real titles and character names in roughly equal measure:

| short tokens that ARE titles | short tokens that are CHARACTERS |
|---|---|
| frieren 418 · bleach 380 · titanic 264 · hyouka 252 · erased 230 · anohana 225 · encanto 218 · arcane 174 · aot 163 | shoko 224 · mikasa 211 · frances 191 · nicole 176 · robin 169 · kanade 165 |

Any length or shape rule misclassifies both directions. So the three options resolve like this:

- **Map character → franchise.** Rejected. It requires knowledge we do not have, and a *wrong* map is worse than no map: telling an editor "saw your Twilight edits" when they edit a different Bella is a false statement about their work, where a neutral phrasing is merely bland. It would also need permanent curation of a 520-token vocabulary that grows every run.
- **Emit a title/character flag.** Rejected on the same evidence — I cannot populate it honestly. A flag that is wrong 30% of the time is worse than no flag, because the template would trust it.
- **Change the sentence.** Adopted. `saw your #holly edits` is grammatical and true for *every* row, character or title, because it stops claiming the token is a title and states what it actually is: the tag they post under. That is also how editors describe their own work.

**It fixes the run-together problem for free.** `#AcrossTheSpiderVerse` is unremarkable as a hashtag — running words together is the convention, not a defect. The 23% that read badly as prose read normally as tags.

I still built `fandom_display` (item 6) for prose use — `AcrossTheSpiderVerse` → `Across The Spider Verse` — from a table of the multi-word titles actually measured on the block. It is a second column, not the default, and unlisted tags are title-cased but never guessed at, because splitting without a dictionary turns `frieren` into nonsense.

## Part 2 — the DIRECT sort

**Before:** `(_ACT_RANK, -quality_score, -editor_pct, -deep_median_views)`. All three tiebreakers are clipper metrics and all three measure **0 on every one of the 1,236 DIRECT rows**, so the key produced **1 distinct value** and the order was activity-rank then input order.

**The trap I avoided, per your warning.** Sorting on monthly listeners alone would have repeated the bug in a new place: only **488 of 2,614** client rows carry it (18.7%), so 81% would tie at zero and fall straight back to input order. The ladder is built so every row lands somewhere meaningful:

| rank | signal | fill on client rows | why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | activity | 100% | shared with clippers; a dormant lead cannot reply |
| 2 | release smart-link | 241 | BL-789's live-prospect signal — an artist keeps one up while promoting |
| 3 | email quality | 168 | personal > agency > role > unknown |
| 4 | monthly listeners | 488 | real reach where it exists |
| 5 | **date_added, newest first** | **100%** | **this is the one that replaces "input order"** — a row with no other evidence still sorts by freshness of discovery rather than arbitrarily |

**Result: 1 distinct sort key → 184.** All 100 of the top 100 carry at least one piece of evidence; 288 of 1,236 (23.3%) carry any.

## Limits

- **The `#tag` phrasing is a recommendation, not a measurement.** No reply data exists, so "reads better" is my judgement. It is reversible — the raw and display forms are both in the file.
- **`fandom_display` covers 23 hand-listed multi-word titles.** Anything outside that list is title-cased only, so a long unlisted run-together tag still reads as one word in prose. The `#` form is unaffected.
- **The client ladder is unvalidated as a predictor.** It orders by evidence that *should* correlate with willingness to buy; nobody has sent to these rows, so the correlation is 0-measured. Only the tie-breaking improvement (1 key → 184) is a fact.
- **77% of DIRECT rows still carry no evidence** and are ordered by `date_added` alone. That is better than arbitrary but it is recency, not quality.
- **`Melhores filmes e series` and 14 other page-names** still greet a page as a person. Detecting that needs a different signal than string cleaning.
- **`email_quality` is populated on 168 of 2,614 client rows**, so tier 3 of the ladder is inert for 94% of them.
- **Master was not modified**, so the raw `niche` values and the unfixed `display_name` strings remain in it; only the export is cleaned.
