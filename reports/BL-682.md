# BL-682 — Mood from text, not audio: the owner is right, and the reason is stronger than the premise

**Date:** 2026-07-29 · **Type:** Deep research, read-only · **Spend:** $0.00 (no paid API calls)

Evidence tiers used throughout: **VERIFIED** (measured in this round) · **UNVERIFIED LEAD**
(documented elsewhere, not confirmed here) · **REFUTED** (checked, premise is false) ·
**GAP** (needs access or spend not available).

---

## Verdict first

**Text beats audio for this task, and it is not close.** That was the owner's hypothesis and
the evidence supports it — but for a different and better reason than the one proposed.

The premise was: transcribe *lyrics*, look up *music genre*, infer mood. What the data
actually shows is that on repost/meme pages there is **no music to look up** (licensed track
metadata: 0/108) and Whisper is not transcribing lyrics at all — it is transcribing **movie
and TV dialogue**. Dialogue is a far richer mood signal than lyrics ever would have been.

Ranked by what they are actually worth on this population:

| Signal | Coverage | Cost | Worth alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Caption text** | **108/108 = 100%** | $0 (already extracted) | **Strongest.** Median 908 chars |
| **Whisper dialogue** | 108/108 audio available | ~1.6s CPU / 30s clip | Strong, and it is *dialogue* |
| **OCR overlay text** | high on meme layouts | already built | Moderate, complementary |
| **Track title → genre** | **0/108** | n/a | **Collapses. Do not build.** |
| Audio mood (acoustic) | n/a | n/a | No honest source (BL-678) |

## What was measured

108 unique posts (deduped by `pk`) across 3 real clip/meme accounts — `clipcapture.tv`,
`culturemeetsonline`, `decibeldrops` — from 9 saved probe payloads. All numbers below are
measured on that set unless marked otherwise. Local stack: `torch 2.11.0+cpu`, 12 cores,
**no GPU**, `ffmpeg 8.0`, and — already installed — `whisper 20250625`, `faster_whisper 1.2.0`,
`transformers 5.12.1`, `onnxruntime`, `ctranslate2`.

Two prior findings re-confirmed at larger n:

- **`play_count` present on 108/108 = 100%.** The earlier n=15 result holds at n=108. The
  codebase's "views hidden on ~60% of accounts" belief stays refuted.
- **`caption.text` non-empty on 108/108 = 100%.**

## 1. Whisper — REFRAMED, and it works better than expected

### The audio is free and genuinely audio-only

**VERIFIED.** `clips_metadata.original_sound_info.progressive_download_url` is present on
**108/108 = 100%**. Its sibling `dash_manifest` declares exactly one AdaptationSet:
`contentType="audio"`, `mimeType="audio/mp4"`, `codecs="mp4a.40.5"` (HE-AAC). There is no
video track in it.

**VERIFIED — the size advantage is better than the ~12× assumed.** Live HEAD requests
(HTTP 200, URLs still valid at time of test):

| duration | audio | video | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 58.0s | 669 KB | 9.13 MB | **13.6×** |
| 89.0s | 1.13 MB | 20.09 MB | **17.8×** |
| 45.0s | 426 KB | 9.11 MB | **21.4×** |

### CPU speed — acceptable, with tiny the clear pick

**VERIFIED**, `faster-whisper`, `compute_type=int8`, 6 threads, measured on 6 real clip audios
(302s of audio total):

| model | real-time factor | **per 30s clip** | model load |
|---|---|---|---|
| **tiny** | 0.05 | **~1.6s** | 9.2s |
| base | 0.18 | ~5.4s | 15.4s |

Both are cheap next to the OCR pass already in the pipeline (measured 1.34 s/image in the
prior round — a 12-frame clip costs ~16s of OCR, ten times the `tiny` transcription).

Variance is real and worth budgeting for: one 58.7s file took 26.6s on `base` (RTF 0.45).
Noisy, music-heavy audio costs several times the median.

Accuracy is "gist-correct, entity-wrong". `tiny` returned `"KILLY CIPRIL"`, `"Carl Togo"`,
`"Zara is your own dog"`; `base` improved but still gave `"Zoro is your undogsus"`. **For mood
classification the gist is sufficient. For anything keyed on names, titles, or quotes it is
not** — do not use Whisper output to identify *which* film a clip is from.

### Does it hallucinate on instrumental? — the answer is reassuring, with one caveat

This was flagged as the question that matters most, so it was tested directly rather than
cited.

**VERIFIED**, four synthetic inputs (30s each), both models, with and without VAD:

| input | tiny, no VAD | base, no VAD | either, `vad_filter=True` |
|---|---|---|---|
| pure digital silence | **`"you"`** | **`"you"`** | *(empty)* |
| sine + tremolo + echo | *(empty)* | *(empty)* | *(empty)* |
| pink noise | *(empty)* | *(empty)* | *(empty)* |
| bass + brown noise (EDM-like) | *(empty)* | *(empty)* | *(empty)* |

Hallucination occurred **only on pure digital silence**, on both models, and
**`vad_filter=True` eliminated it 4/4**.

There is also a clean confidence separation usable as a second gate:

| | `no_speech_prob` | `compression_ratio` |
|---|---|---|
| real dialogue (6 files) | 0.075 – 0.152 | 1.37 – 1.87 |
| the silence hallucination | **0.931** | **0.27** |

A `no_speech_prob > 0.5` drop rule separates cleanly on this data.

**GAP, stated honestly:** synthetic tones are not real instrumental music. Real scored
music — orchestral swells, reverb-heavy pads, vocal samples — is a harder case than a sine
wave, and Whisper's well-documented failure mode (emitting subtitle boilerplate like
"Thank you for watching") is most severe on near-silence and long-form audio. The
mitigation stack (`vad_filter=True` + `no_speech_prob` gate) is sound, but its behaviour on
real instrumental tracks is untested here.

## 2. Caption and OCR — the strongest signal, and richer than assumed

**VERIFIED**, n=108:

- Non-empty on **108/108 = 100%**
- Length: min 438, **median 908**, mean 899, max 1502 characters
- Hashtags on 101/108 · emoji on 58/108 (emoji are among the strongest short-text affect cues)

The example in the brief — *"when she leaves you on read"* — undersells what is actually
there. On these accounts captions are **structured metadata blocks**:

```
Title: Two and a Half Men
Created by: Chuck Lorre, Lee Aronsohn
Genre: Sitcom, Comedy
Language: English
Original Release: 2003–2015
Cast: ...
```

**`Genre:` appears in 34/108 captions; `Title:` in 33/108.**

**But the critical qualifier: this is account-templated, not universal.**

| account | captions with a `Genre:` field |
|---|---|
| clipcapture.tv | **34/36 (94%)** |
| culturemeetsonline | 0/36 |
| decibeldrops | 0/36 |

It is all-or-nothing per account, driven by whether that page uses a caption template. That
is operationally *good news*: templating is detectable at the account level from a handful of
posts, and where present it yields free structured metadata across the entire back catalogue.

Genres stated: Comedy 17, Drama 15, Sitcom 11, Fantasy 7, Action 7, Adventure 5, Crime 3,
Superhero 3, Romance 2, Comedy-Drama 2, Dark Comedy 1, Family 1, Music 1, Musical 1.

**These are content genres, not music genres — which is what an editing bot actually wants.**
A clip library is indexed by "Comedy / Sitcom / Fantasy", not by "dance-pop".

### Published accuracy for text emotion — be modest about fine-grained

**UNVERIFIED LEAD** (literature, not measured here). On GoEmotions (58k annotated comments,
27 emotion classes), the original fine-tuned BERT baseline reaches **macro-F1 0.46 /
micro-F1 0.51**. Later work reports higher figures on reduced label sets and different splits
(DeBERTa-v3 ~87% accuracy / 0.86 macro-F1), and large general LLMs do *worse* on the strict
27-class task (Gemini 39.9% accuracy, macro-F1 0.363).

The honest reading: **fine-grained 27-way emotion is genuinely hard (~0.46 macro-F1);
coarse valence — positive / negative / neutral — is a much easier problem and reliably lands
far higher.** Ship coarse buckets. Do not ship a 27-way mood wheel.

**GAP:** no published benchmark was found specifically for *Instagram meme-clip captions*.
GoEmotions is Reddit comments. The transfer is plausible (both are short informal English)
but unmeasured.

## 3. Track title and artist — REFUTED

The brief assumed track/artist on ~36% of clips, "near-certain when present". On this
population it is **0%**.

**VERIFIED**, n=108:

| field | result |
|---|---|
| `clips_metadata.music_info` | **0/108 = 0%** |
| `clips_metadata.music_canonical_id` | 0/108 |
| `original_sound_info.original_audio_title` | `"Original audio"` on **108/108** |
| `clips_metadata.audio_type` | `"original_sounds"` on **108/108** |
| `original_sound_info.ig_artist` | **the posting account itself, 108/108** |
| `additional_audio_info.additional_audio_username` | `null` throughout |

`ig_artist` was verified by pk-equality against `item.user.pk` — it is the uploader, not a
musician. There is no music attribution anywhere in the payload.

**This is structural, not a sampling accident.** Repost pages rip existing footage and upload
it as original audio; a page posting a Game of Thrones scene does not attach a licensed
Instagram music track. The 36% figure may well hold for *creator* accounts adding a trending
song over their own footage — it does not describe the accounts this tool targets.

**Consequence:** the MusicBrainz / Last.fm genre lookup has nothing to look up. Combined with
BL-678's licensing findings (MusicBrainz genre associations are CC BY-NC-SA, not CC0;
Last.fm and Deezer are both explicitly non-commercial), **this branch should be dropped
entirely.** It is licence-encumbered, coverage-poor, and — since the useful genre is *content*
genre, already free in the caption — aimed at the wrong target.

**Caveat:** n=3 accounts. A wider sweep would pin down whether 0% generalises, but the
mechanism is clear enough to act on.

## 4. Fusion

### What the literature says

**UNVERIFIED LEAD** (not reproduced here):

- On CMU-MOSEI, the largest multimodal sentiment benchmark (3,228 videos, 1,000+ speakers),
  **text is the best-performing single modality**. Reported fusion results cluster around
  **80–88% binary accuracy**, with weighted-F1 often much lower (0.51–0.74) once class
  imbalance is respected.
- A recurring, well-documented failure mode: models **"learn to rely almost exclusively on the
  statistically dominant modality (typically text) and ignore acoustic and visual streams."**
  Naive fusion tends to *become* the text model while costing more.
- In music-specific work, **angry and calm are classified better by audio; happy and sad
  equally well by audio or lyrics**, and the best-performing valence model was "a relatively
  balanced average of unimodal predictions."

### What that implies here

The cleanest principled split maps modalities onto the two axes of affect:

- **Text (caption + dialogue + OCR) → VALENCE** (positive/negative). This is where semantics
  live, and text is strongest on exactly this axis.
- **Audio → AROUSAL** (calm/intense). Energy, tempo and dynamics are acoustic properties;
  they survive even when no lyrics or music metadata exist.

**Realistic accuracy, stated conservatively:** coarse three-way valence from caption text
should be solid; a fused fine-grained mood label should not be trusted much past the
**~0.45–0.55 macro-F1** range that fine-grained text emotion achieves on clean benchmarks —
and this data is messier than a benchmark. Anyone promising 85% on a 27-class mood wheel is
quoting a binary-sentiment number.

**Design recommendation:** do not build a learned fusion model. Given that text dominates and
naive fusion collapses onto text anyway, a **rule-based, per-field pipeline with explicit
provenance** delivers most of the value at a fraction of the complexity — each mood output
carries which signals produced it and whether they agreed.

## 5. The sharp question: sad lyrics over upbeat music

**Which signal should win: text, for the perceived-mood field.**

The music-psychology literature is direct on this. In studies of deliberately conflicting
lyric/melody pairs, **"the lyrics appeared to influence mood to a greater extent than the
melody"**, and specifically *"the semantic content of lyrics was more influential on mood than
the vocal expression of lyrics."* When words and music disagree about valence, listeners
follow the words.

That is doubly true here, because on this population the text is not lyrics — it is
**dialogue plus a human-written caption**. A caption is an *authored statement of intent*
about the clip. It should outrank an acoustic inference.

**Does the disagreement itself carry information? Yes — and it is arguably the most valuable
derived field in the whole design.**

Sad-text-over-happy-music is not noise; it is a *recognised affective category*. The
literature on this exact combination is titled, near enough, "pleasure generated by sadness:
the effect of sad lyrics on the emotions induced by happy music" — the mismatch reliably
produces bittersweet, nostalgic, ironic, or poignant affect. For a meme/edit bot that is a
distinct and highly desirable bucket: it is precisely the register of nostalgia edits, "we
were happy then" cuts, and comedic-tragic juxtaposition.

**Concrete recommendation — store three fields, not one:**

| field | source | note |
|---|---|---|
| `valence_text` | caption + dialogue + OCR | the primary mood signal |
| `arousal_audio` | acoustic energy/tempo | independent axis, always available |
| `affect_conflict` | boolean/score, set when the two disagree in sign | **the bittersweet/ironic flag** |

Collapsing these into one `mood` string destroys the most interesting signal you have. Keep
them separate, and let the bot query on the conflict.

## 6. What cannot be filled reliably — say it plainly

- **A single fine-grained `mood` label.** No honest source, and no honest accuracy number.
  BL-678 established there is no usable free acoustic mood source (AcousticBrainz was
  withdrawn by its own maintainers as *"not of high enough quality to be useful for much at
  all"*, and is frozen at 2022; Spotify audio-features closed to new apps in Nov 2024).
  Nothing in this round changes that. Leave the field empty rather than guess.
- **Music genre.** 0/108 track metadata. Drop it.
- **Film/episode identification from Whisper output.** Entity accuracy is too poor
  (`"Zoro is your undogsus"`). Use the caption's `Title:` field where templated instead.
- **Whisper behaviour on real instrumental music.** Untested; only synthetic tones were tried.

## 7. Recommended build order

1. **Caption NLP first.** 100% coverage, $0, already extracted, median 908 chars. Coarse
   valence + emoji features. Detect caption templating per account and parse `Title:` /
   `Genre:` / `Created by:` where present — free structured metadata across a whole catalogue.
2. **Whisper `tiny` with `vad_filter=True` and a `no_speech_prob > 0.5` drop rule.** ~1.6s per
   30s clip on CPU, audio is 13–21× smaller than video, 100% coverage. Treat output as
   *dialogue*, not lyrics.
3. **Keep valence and arousal as separate fields, and store the conflict flag.**
4. **Do not build** the music-genre lookup, a learned fusion model, or a single fine-grained
   mood label.

The honest headline: **text is stronger than audio for this task, the strongest text is the
caption you already have for free, and the music-metadata branch of the design should be
abandoned rather than optimised.**

## Method and limits

- All measurements are on **3 accounts / 108 posts**. Coverage figures (100% play_count, 100%
  caption, 0% music_info) are consistent across all three accounts, but three accounts is a
  small sample for a structural claim.
- Whisper benchmarks are **6 audio files, 302 seconds total**, single machine, CPU int8.
- The hallucination test used **synthetic** audio, not real instrumental tracks.
- Literature figures are cited as reported by their sources and were **not** reproduced.
- No paid API calls were made. Audio downloads were free public CDN fetches. No code changed.
