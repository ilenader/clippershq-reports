# BL-1545 — Instagram works, and it is 3.1x CHEAPER per address than TikTok

**Round:** BL-1545 · **Cap:** $2.00 · **Spent:** $0.1340 (194 billed requests at $0.00069064)
**Platform:** Instagram. No model, no pictures, no video. One billed call per hashtag page;
**the bio is free.** Touches neither funnel.

---

## THE PARAGRAPH AT THE TOP

**Yes, Instagram works, and it is cheaper than TikTok.** 18 hashtags, 3,589 accounts touched,
**137 net-new addresses, 132 passing every deliverability check** — for **$0.104** in 52
minutes. That is **$0.79 per 1,000 passing addresses against TikTok's $2.47 fresh and $3.70
on a re-walk: 3.1x cheaper.** Worth running at scale — with one real caveat below.

**The reason is the thing the round was sent to measure.** Instagram's hashtag payload
carries no biography, so the fear was a paid profile call per account at $0.00069064 — a
floor of roughly $23 per 1,000 addresses, 6–9x worse than TikTok. **It does not need one.
The free profile page carries the bio on 76.5% of accounts that have one, and no rate
ceiling appeared down to a 0.25-second gap.** The only billed call is the hashtag page
itself, and one page yields ~26 new accounts.

**The caveat is the clock, not the money.** The free bio is one HTTP round trip per account
— a median of 0.77 s — so Instagram runs at about **0.9 seconds per account against TikTok's
0.085**. It is 3x cheaper and roughly 10x slower. For a fixed budget Instagram wins; for a
fixed afternoon TikTok still wins.

The workbook is at `%USERPROFILE%\OneDrive\Desktop\clipper_emails_ALL.xlsx` and now holds
**2,007 addresses — 1,870 TikTok and 137 Instagram.**

## 1. THE PRICE OF A BIO — THREE ROUTES, MEASURED

The test buys the profile once per handle and treats **that** bio as ground truth, then asks
each free route for the same handle and checks whether the ground-truth text appears in the
body. **It searches for the VALUE, not the field name** — on TikTok a probe once searched
`biography` where the field was `signature` and reported 0 of 25 when the truth was 86.2%. A
value search cannot make that mistake.

**DENOMINATOR: 25 handles; 17 of them returned a bio from the paid call (68.0%).**

| route | carries the bio | 95% Wilson | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **free PROFILE PAGE** | **13 of 17 = 76.47%** | [52.74, 90.45] | **$0.00** |
| ...of which via `og:description` | **0 of 17** | [0.00, 18.43] | — |
| free `/embed/` | **0 of 17** | [0.00, 18.43] | $0.00 |
| paid profile call | ground truth | — | $0.00069064 |

**Two surprises in that table.** The bio is *not* in the `og:description` meta tag — it comes
out of the page's embedded JSON. And the `/embed/` route, which is the free route this
project already uses for grids, **carries no biography at all**. That zero is trustworthy
because the profile page found the same bios on the same handles in the same run: the matcher
is proven, not assumed.

**The pages are real, not shells.** A shell is constant; a real page varies with its subject.
Profile bodies ranged 629,460–856,520 bytes (spread 227,060) and embed bodies 227,673–300,531.
Both vary, so both are real content — the embed simply does not contain a bio.

**What only the paid call buys:** the contact button. 51.03% of all Instagram addresses ever
collected came from that button and are provably absent from the bio. On this sample the
paid-only contact fields appeared on 1 of 25. **That is the reason to buy a profile — not the
bio.**

## 2. THE RATE CEILING — THERE ISN'T ONE, AND THE MISSES ARE DEAD ACCOUNTS

Instagram's wall on this surface is a per-IP *rate*, so the question was requests per hour.
Four arms, gentlest first, 20 requests each, a request counting only if the body carries a
content marker:

| gap | content | rate |
|---|---|---|
| 2.00 s | 17/20 | 85% |
| 1.00 s | 20/20 | **100%** |
| 0.50 s | 18/20 | 90% |
| 0.25 s | 17/20 | 85% |

**That is not a rate curve** — a limiter degrades as the gap shrinks, and here the fastest arm
was not the worst. So the competing explanation was tested directly: **take the content-less
handles and ask again slowly.** A rate-limited handle recovers; a dead account never does.

> **0 of 5 misses recovered at a 4-second gap, while 10 of 10 known-good handles kept
> working.** The misses are private, deleted or renamed accounts. **No rate ceiling was found
> at any pace tested down to 0.25 s** — at least 14,400 requests/hour from one IP, and the
> run used a deliberately conservative 0.30 s.

The control for the control matters here: had the known-good handles also stopped, the IP
would have been degraded and the whole comparison void. They didn't.

## 7. THE SPREADSHEET — AND 66 ADDRESSES THAT HAD GONE MISSING

`%USERPROFILE%\OneDrive\Desktop\clipper_emails_ALL.xlsx`, the desktop the **shell** reports.

- **Sheet "Emails": 2,007 rows** — **1,870 TikTok + 137 Instagram**, parser-counted.
- **Sheet "No email": 24,241 rows.**
- Three columns: Profile link · Instagram · Email.

**How Instagram rows handle column 2:** for an Instagram account the profile link *is* the
Instagram link, so column 1 carries `instagram.com/<handle>` and **column 2 is left blank
rather than repeating it**. The domain in column 1 is what tells you which platform a row came
from. **No cross-platform link is invented** — of 26,897 bios in BL-1544, the 18 carrying an
`instagram.com` URL all belonged to accounts with *no* address. Creators publish a link **or**
an address, not both.

**⚠️ AND THE WORKBOOK HAD SILENTLY LOST 66 ROWS.** It read back 1,804 TikTok addresses where
BL-1544 reported writing 1,870, and 15 of BL-1544's own 767 were absent. I did not diagnose
the cause and I am not going to guess at one. **The fix was to stop treating the workbook as
its own source**: it is now rebuilt from the per-round artefacts — BL-1541's CSV, BL-1542's
CSV, BL-1544's and BL-1545's `addresses.jsonl` — with the count stated per source, and the
file is read back after writing and the row count compared. **All 66 are restored.** That
read-back assertion is the protection; the rebuild is just this round's repair.

## 8. WHAT YOU GOT WRONG

**I nearly shipped a workbook that had quietly dropped 66 of his addresses** — and I only
caught it because the row count I read (1,804) disagreed with the number the previous round
had printed (1,870). Had BL-1544 not stated its count, the loss would have been invisible and
permanent. Two lessons, and the second is the one that generalises: a deliverable rebuilt from
itself accumulates its own losses, and **a written file is not verified until it has been read
back**.

**My own verdict string printed unfilled format placeholders.** The pace-control probe ended
with `%d` and `%.1f` literally in its output because I put the arguments on the wrong
statement. The numbers were correct in the data file and in the lines above it, but the
sentence a reader would quote was broken. A print statement is an instrument too.

**The brief's premise about `og:description` was wrong, and I nearly inherited it.** It said
the description tag carries the biography. It carries 0 of 17. The bio is in the embedded
JSON. I only found that because the probe recorded *where* the match came from rather than
just whether there was one — had I checked only the meta tag I would have reported the free
route dead and recommended the paid call, which would have made Instagram look 30x more
expensive than it is.

**I hand-rolled a payload reader the repo already owns — the exact mistake my own module
docstring warns about.** `tests/test_envelope.py` caught `ig_bio.sections_of` as a new
depth-blind endpoint reader and offered me an exemption. I took the other option and routed
the slot extraction through `envelope.read()` instead, which turned out to be **strictly
better code**: driven on a payload carrying all three containers it returns 3 of 3, which is
the very "misses 4 of 33" failure my hand-written container tuple existed to prevent. Two
lines in the same file already said "a second copy would drift" about the TikTok unwrapper.

**And the fix introduced a worse defect than the one it cured.** Rewriting the section COUNT
at the same time, I made it read only the top-level key — but the live payload nests sections
under `data`, so **it returned 0 on every real page**. A denominator that is always zero is
worse than no denominator, because it looks like a measurement. Caught by driving it on a
live payload rather than on my synthetic one, which of course had the key at the top. The
same drive found a second one: `envelope.read({})` hands back the dict *itself* as a single
item, so an empty page reported one phantom slot. Both now have regression tests.

**The extractor was restructured AFTER the run that produced the published numbers**, which
means the committed code is not the code that generated them. Rather than assert they agree,
one live call ran both versions over the same payload: **30 slots and 28 distinct authors
from each, author sets identical.** The figures stand, but they needed that check and I would
not have thought to run it if the restructure had happened an hour earlier.

**And the first reading of the pace test looked like a rate limit and was not.** 85/100/90/85
across shrinking gaps is noise, not a curve, but it is exactly the shape a tired reader
rounds off to "degrades under load". The discriminating test cost four minutes and reversed
the conclusion.

## 9. SAFETY

Nine files backed up sha256-verified before any spend — config, spend, master, all five seen
stores and the tag ledger — paths from one round constant, body found BY SHAPE. **All five
corruption controls fired on planted damage**, including the index-qualified fingerprint that
sees the deletion of one of a duplicated pair where a natural-key set is blind.

**The cap was proved at the INSTAGRAM unit.** Lifted `reserve()`'s real source by AST and
drove it: funded allows and the meter advances **by $0.00069064 and not by TikTok's
$0.000600** — the two differ by 15.1% and this round is the one where the default is right
and the TikTok constant is the leak risk. The ceiling raises, $0.00 raises, the meter does not
advance on either refusal, and under 8 threads the locked counter stopped at exactly 50 of 50.
The proof wrote nowhere.

**Nothing latched. No seen store was written.** The Instagram tags occupy their own ledger
namespace (`ig:<tag>@v1`) so they can never collide with a TikTok tag of the same spelling.
An absent bio is `unknown`, never `empty`. **No Python process was killed. No `--no-verify`.**

`docs/FACTS.md` was checked at round start: +212 rows of a +2,000 limit, so no re-stamp was
needed and none was made.

**The suite:** `FAILED -- 25 red of 475 suite(s)`, **zero new red**, attributed per suite
name and never by subtracting totals. 475 suites / 10,688 checks against 473 / 10,653 — +2
suites and +35 checks, of which **one suite and 22 checks are mine**; the rest belongs to
another round in flight, which is why a suite count is a moment and not a property.
