## 1. Repaired rows are marked — and the marker immediately said something unwelcome

Two fields, declared in `CLIP_FIELDS` so `field()` can actually read them (the trap `speech_frac` sat in for a whole round):

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| `vision_parse_repair` | **which rung** worked: `clean` · `fence` · `timestamps` · `closed_braces` · `dropped_tail` |
| `vision_parse_lossy` | **the response was TRUNCATED** — `beats` is short and `on_screen_text` may be partial because the model stopped early, not because the clip had less in it |

The rung is recorded rather than a bare boolean, because *"repaired"* does not say **how much was guessed at**. Stripping a fence changes nothing about the content; reinterpreting `1:24` as `84.0` changes a value; dropping a trailing element silently shortens a list. Those are three different confidences.

### VERIFIED, and it is the finding of the section

**Every one of the 30 live responses this round came back fence-wrapped.** `repair=fence` on 12 of 12 in the verification pass and 18 of 18 in the token probe.

> **`repaired` is therefore true on essentially every row and carries almost no information.** A single boolean would have shipped a flag that is always on. **`lossy` is the field a consumer must branch on**, and it was 0 of 30 once the ceiling was raised.

**Existing rows: 1,778 of 1,790 labelled rows carry NO marker** — they were written before it existed. That is the honest count. It cannot be backfilled: the marker describes *how the response was parsed*, and the responses were not kept (§5). **A zero there would have been fabricated.**

## 2. The token ceiling, set from an uncensored sample for the first time

My own BL-892 evidence had two of eleven observations sitting at precisely 2000.

So this asked 18 clips with `max_tokens=8000` and recorded where the model stopped on its own. **None hit 8000**, so the distribution is uncensored:

A higher ceiling costs *nothing* on the 17 of 18 responses that stop below 2000, and buys headroom only for the beat-heavy tail that is currently being truncated and repaired.

The library's own beat distribution corroborates the shape: p50 **7**, p90 **20**, max **56** beats per row — with a 95-beat response observed live, which is exactly the population that blew the ceiling.

## Limits

- **`$1.84/1,000` on 12 clips is not a better estimate than BL-892's `$1.62` on 24.** Small n, and this pass ran against a library four rounds were writing. The rate is not the finding; the token distribution is.
- **1,778 labelled rows can never be classified.** The marker is not backfillable because the responses were not kept, and the log that did keep them kept only 400 characters.
- **The 5,000 ceiling rests on 18 observations.** None hit 8,000, so the shape is real, but p95 from n=18 is a wide interval and a 200-beat clip would still truncate — the difference is that it would now be *marked* `lossy` rather than silently short.
- **`MAX_TOKENS` was changed in two runners, not four.** `bl872_label.py` and `bl895_finish.py` are held by live rounds and still request 2000.
- **`fix_timestamps` is an interpretation, not a repair.** It assumes `1:24` means minutes:seconds. On a clip under a minute that is certainly right; on a two-hour source it could be hours:minutes. **GAP: not tested against a long-form source.**
- **The `_PARSE_META` module-level dict is safe only because these runners are strictly serial.** Any future parallel runner will interleave and mis-attribute the rung. It is a deliberate shortcut around `parse()`'s one-value contract and it should become a return value if concurrency ever arrives.
- **I broke `bl867_deep_label.py` mid-round with a bad patch and could not `git checkout` it — it is untracked.** I repaired it by hand and it compiles, but that is exactly the "written, run live, never committed" hazard BL-880 reported, landing on me. It is still untracked.
