## The half that is still missing, demonstrated

BL-867's taxonomy had **three** classes. BL-937's has **two**. `unparseable` falls into `content` and still retires permanently:

```
record_failure(rec, "unparseable response") x2
  ->  vision_attempts = 2  ->  at ceiling  ->  never selected again
  ->  vision_last_error_kind = 'content'
```

**A parser bug is being filed as a verdict on the clip.** BL-867 measured what that costs: 4 clips retired this way were re-opened, **3 were labelled immediately and none was still unparseable**. One carried **67 beats** — so it had *also* truncated at the old `max_tokens=2000`. Two separate tooling limits produced a single tombstone, and neither was a fact about the video.

**The fix is specified, not landed** — `scratch/bl939_tooling_spec.md`. The shape: a third `vision_last_error_kind` value plus a `PARSER_GEN` integer stamped at failure time, and one branch in `should_skip()` that re-opens a tooling retirement whose recorded generation is older than the current one. The counter still increments, because the call did cost money and `vision_attempts` is also the record of that; the re-open belongs in the *skip decision*, keyed on generation, not in the counter. BL-889's shared parser and BL-904's token raise would each have been a bump, and each would have freed exactly the clips it fixed — with no sweep and no scratch runner.

## Rule 8 written to `docs/TESTING.md`

> **A validation set drawn from what your filter already accepts cannot test the filter.**

With the worked case: BL-865 validated `estimated_pages()` against clips a previous pass had labelled at `max_pages=1` — clips that are on page 1 **by construction** — scored 93.9% against a 95.2% baseline, and concluded the axis was immaterial. The population that could have falsified it was the one the estimator called **deep**, and it was never sampled. BL-921 sampled it: **0 of 12** behaved as the docstring promised.

The rule ships with two tells (*ground truth described as a previous run's success*; *high accuracy against an equally high baseline*) and the correction that hides it — a multiplier that reconciles the total while leaving every individual decision wrong. It sits beside the fixture rule as section 8, and cross-references rule 4.
