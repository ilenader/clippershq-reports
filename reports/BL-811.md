## 7. The hypothesis, stated honestly

**At recruiting's measured 4–8% reply rate, 20 sends expects 1 to 2 replies.**

**20 is far too small to compare variants and the pilot must not be read that way.** With 10 per
arm, a 4–8% base rate produces roughly 0–1 replies per arm. Any A-vs-B difference you observe —
including 1–0, which will look decisive — is noise. Distinguishing a 5% message from a 10%
message at 80% power needs several hundred sends per arm. **The variant field exists so the data
is there later, not so this run can call a winner.**

**What 20 sends can actually establish — the mechanism:**

1. **Deliverability** — do messages reach real inboxes? 20/20 have live MX; if bounces appear
   anyway, the MX check is insufficient and that is worth knowing before 1,600 sends.
2. **The stamp** — does `record_outcome` write `date_sent` to the right row, under the lock,
   without corrupting a 58,325-row master? Never once exercised in production.
3. **The follow-up** — does `touch_number` survive, and can a second touch be targeted without
   re-sending to touch 1?
4. **Reply handling** — when a reply arrives, is there anywhere for it to go?

**A zero-reply run is not a failed run.** At this size, 0 replies is an ordinary outcome
(roughly a 20–44% chance if the true rate is 4–8%). Failure is a bounce, a stamp that writes to
the wrong row, or a reply with nowhere to land.

## Limits and corrections

- **BL-790's block boundaries do not reproduce.** It described rows 1–1,352 spanning 673,973
  down to 663 median views. Today: **2,949 rows**, top median **8,095,740**, and row 1,352 has
  a median of **0**. Master has grown to 58,325 rows. The *shape* holds — the list sorts by
  reach within the ALIVE/personal bucket, which is what selection needed — but the numbers are
  from a different corpus. I selected against today's list.
- **The full list is not globally monotonic**, and should not be: activity and role dominate the
  sort key ahead of reach, by design. Rows 1–20 are all ALIVE and strictly descending.
- **The MX data is 14 days old** (`bl763_mx.json`, another round's artifact). I did not
  re-resolve. All 20 are major free providers, so staleness risk is negligible here — it would
  not be for custom domains.
- **`matched_video_date` ranges from 2025-05-03 to 2026-06-14.** Four of the 20 reference a clip
  over a year old. Still true, less immediate.
- **Nothing was sent.** No mail client was touched, no message transmitted, `spend.json`
  unchanged, master unchanged.
- **The character-vs-title list is hand-built** from the tags encountered while filling 20. It
  is not a general classifier and will miss cases in a larger run.

## Method

Built the ranked list in memory with `all_bot_ready.build()` (read-only on master) rather than
reading the on-disk `ALL_BOT_READY.csv`; the two matched exactly at 2,949 rows. Joined each
ranked row back to its master row via `writer._first_email` to recover `display_name`,
`source_hashtag` and `matched_video_views`. Fandom = the discovery tag with its `tt:hashtag:`
prefix and any `edit(s)/amv` suffix stripped. Names cleaned of emoji and separators, falling
back to the handle per the template. Safety checks called the shipping functions —
`role_policy.kind`, `role_policy.looks_agency`, `bio_parser.is_junk_domain`,
`writer._activity_of` — not re-implementations. The CSV was written atomically
(tmp + fsync + replace), read back, and every `match_key` resolved through the real
`outcomes.match_indices()` against live master. Zero paid calls, $0. The only file written
outside the scratchpad is `output/CALIBRATION_20.csv`.
