## Step 1 — schema fix, 60 → 62 columns

BL-727 found `user_pk` missing. **There were two drops, not one.** `append_found_to_master` guards every write with `if col in fieldnames`, and neither `user_pk` **nor `source_tag`** existed in the master header — so both were discarded silently. That is why all 30 pre-existing MEME_PAGE rows carry no tag, and why per-tag yield could not be reconstructed from the master at all.

Both added to `writer.FULL_COLUMNS` and mapped in `append_found_to_master`.

### The migration refused itself once, and that was correct

`writer.migrate_master_csv` ran first and **failed verification**: 1,347 rows differed outside the two new columns. Not corruption — its docstring says it "fills sensibly: … `instagram_url` reconstructed from the handle", and that is exactly what it did on rows where `instagram` was set and `instagram_url` was blank. Benign, documented, and still a violation of the standard set for this round: *nothing may change except the two new empty columns*.

So the master was **restored from the migration's own backup** and re-done with a strict append-only pass: `csv.reader` → append two empty fields → `csv.writer`. Field values are untouched by construction — never parsed, reformatted or reconstructed. Not line-based, because bios carry embedded newlines and only the csv module handles those correctly.

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| columns | 60 | **62** |
| data rows | 56,906 | **56,906** |
| rows differing outside the new columns | — | **0** |
| new cells non-empty | — | **0** |

> **Note on the expected row count:** the brief said 56,177. The master actually held **56,906** before this round — other workstreams have appended since that figure was taken. The invariant verified is *unchanged across the migration*, which held exactly.
>
> **Note on "byte-identical":** a csv round-trip may renormalise quoting, so byte-identity of row text is not a property any csv rewrite can guarantee. The invariant asserted is **field-value identity, position by position**, which is the one that matters and which passed on all 56,906 rows.

### One test had to be narrowed, and why

`test_outcomes.py::test_outcome_columns_are_the_tail_of_full_columns` failed: appending after the outcome block broke its assertion that outcomes are the literal tail.

The test's own comment states its purpose — *"if someone reorders/removes, this bites"* — and `outcomes.py` says *"Order matters only for the CSV layout."* Tail position is incidental, not functional. Meanwhile every column block in `writer.py` is appended last **by design**, so existing columns keep their byte positions; honouring "outcomes must be the tail" would have forced an insert mid-header, shifting the outcome columns and breaking the very discipline the comment appeals to.

The assertion was narrowed to **contiguous and in order**, which preserves the stated purpose (reorder/removal still bites) without forbidding future appends. Rationale recorded inline at the test.

## Step 3 — the honest ceiling

Extrapolating from the pilot's own numbers, a full sweep at the current `pages_per_tag=2` across all 24 tags:

| | |
|---|---|
| clip slots | 1,440 |
| candidates surfaced | ~414 |
| **confirmed pages added** | **~216** → ~258 total |
| paid calls | ~462 = **$0.28** |
| **wall clock** | **~3.7 hours** |

| target | more needed | candidates | pages to fetch | time | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 140 total | 98 | ~188 | 22 of 48 | **1.7 h** | $0.13 |
| **200 total** | **158** | **~303** | **35 of 48** | **2.7 h** | **$0.20** |
| 215 total | 173 | ~332 | 38 of 48 | 2.9 h | $0.22 |

**200 fits the bank without raising `pages_per_tag`.** 35 of the 48 available pages, at the measured 52% confirm rate. There is no need to scrape exhausted tags, which was the explicit thing to avoid.

**So the honest answer is 200 — but the price is 2.7 hours of wall clock, not the $0.20.** Cost is a rounding error; time is the whole constraint, and it is ~9× the largest run this pipeline has done.

Two caveats that push the realised figure **down**, not up:
- The seen-cache already holds 312 posts and grows every run; late tags will return more already-seen clips than the pilot's fresh ones did.
- 4 of 23 candidates (17%) were dropped for non-English captions. That rate applies across the sweep and is already inside the 52%.

**Recommendation:** run to 200 in **two or three sessions of ~1 hour each** rather than one 2.7-hour block — the finder is resumable through the seen-cache and master dedup, so a session boundary costs nothing and a crash three hours in costs everything. If a single unattended block is preferred, 140 in 1.7 hours is the safer commitment.

**Widening the bank** was not needed for 200 and so was not costed in depth; at the pilot's ~0.287 candidates/clip and 52% confirm, each additional tag at 2 pages is worth roughly **9 more pages for ~$0.012 and ~9 minutes.**
