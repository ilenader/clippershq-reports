## Why it didn't move — and this is the actual finding

The premise was that those clips are "unlabelled by a table that only matches exact hashtags". For the low band that was true and text matching helped (+7.1 points). For the high band it is **not a labelling problem at all**.

The most common proper nouns across all unlabelled high-usage clips are `Sai Abhyankkar`, `Anirudh`, `Dhanush`, `TVK`, `Argentina`, `Travel`, `Thank`, `Love` — Tamil music-industry names, places and ordinary words. **There is no franchise in these captions because there is no franchise in these clips.**

That is the structural result: **a sound is popular precisely because it is content-agnostic.** A million-use sound is used by divers, dads, gyms and jewellery shops. Franchise vocabulary cannot label content that has no franchise, and no amount of table-building changes that.

### Ceiling test — how much could a bigger vocabulary ever buy?

I re-scanned the 23 unlabelled high-usage sounds with a deliberately generous net of ~50 further properties (Two and a Half Men, Friends, The Office, Game of Thrones, Attack on Titan, John Wick, Oppenheimer, …). Hits: **2 sounds**, one via "two and a half men" and one via the word "friends" — and *friends* in ordinary prose is almost certainly a false hit.

> **Ceiling: 7/27 = 25.9%**, counting the dubious one. Realistically 15–18%.

Even an unboundedly larger franchise table does not reach the 40% bar. This is the number that closes the question rather than deferring it.

## The labeller, and its false-positive guard

Matching is done on **prose with hashtags stripped**, so a text hit can never be the hashtag being counted twice. Three tiers:

- **STRONG** — distinctive multi-word name (`ryan gosling`, `ghost rider`, `peter parker`). Accepted anywhere.
- **WEAK** — single token (`naruto`, `spiderman`, `gosling`). Needs ≥1 corroborating signal: proper-noun casing, subject position (first 60 chars), or within 30 chars of a content cue (edit/scene/clip/movie/series/trailer/arc/…).
- **AMBIGUOUS** — token that is also an ordinary word (`marvel` the verb, `joker`, `one piece` the swimsuit). Needs proper-noun casing **and** a second signal.

A match failing its tier's requirement is dropped, not downgraded — a guess with a confidence score is still a guess.

### False-positive audit — all 34 text-only matches adjudicated (30 requested)

| verdict | n | rate |
|---|---|---|
| correct franchise | 33 | 97.1% |
| **hard false positive** | **1** | **2.9%** |
| borderline (franchise right, not franchise *footage*) | 5 | 14.7% |

**The one hard false positive is instructive:**
> *"This is "Walter White" ☢️😁😎🌱 Alocasia Cucullata Banana Split in a 250 mL Griffin beaker with leca and moss."*

A **houseplant named Walter White**, in a beaker, captioned by a plant collector. It cleared the STRONG tier because "walter white" is a distinctive two-token name — exactly the rule that is supposed to be safe. Multi-word distinctiveness is a good guard, not a perfect one.

**The 5 borderline cases** are Spider-Man merch and fan art — *"SPIDERMAN 🕷️ T SHIRT"*, *"Spiderman Realistic Painting"*. The franchise label is correct; the clip is not franchise footage. If the downstream question is "what property is this clip about" they are true positives; if it is "is this a clip of the property" they are wrong. **That distinction should be decided before this is used, not after.**

**One methodological weakness found:** some creators paste bracketed comma-separated keyword lists without `#` — *"[Spiderman art, Spiderman brand new day, mcu, marvel, sony, Doomsday, peter parker, spidey, trending…]"*. My "prose" assumption treats these as sentences. They are hashtag stuffing wearing prose clothes, and they inflate cue-adjacency evidence. A production version needs to detect and discount them.

### The gain is smaller than 34 suggests

The 34 text-only matches collapse to **17 distinct caption texts**. One Ghost Rider template accounts for 13 of them:

> *"Ryan Gosling as Ghost Rider feels like one of those castings that people accepted before it was even announced…"*

repeated verbatim across 13 clips — a copy-paste caption farm. The honest gain is **17 distinct clips**, not 34.

## Concentration — still not tested

BL-716 measured 12/12 concentrated on a median of 3 labelled clips per sound, and asked whether that survives more labels per sound. **It could not be re-tested here, because coverage did not rise.**

In the high band, 4 sounds carry any label at all, all 4 concentrated (top franchise ≥50%), and the **median labelled clips per sound is still 3** — identical to BL-716. Only **one** sound reached ≥4 labelled clips. The concentration finding remains as thin as it was; this round neither confirmed nor weakened it.

## What I would do instead

1. **Do not buy more sounds for characterisation.** The band that matters stays at ~15% with a ceiling near 26%.
2. **Note the inversion, because it is useful:** the map works where sounds are *small* (57% coverage under 1,000 uses) and fails where they are *big*. If the editing bot's question is "find a niche sound that fits this fandom", the low band is where to look. If it is "characterise the trending sound", that is not reachable this way at any table size.
3. **Keep the text labeller anyway** — it is free, it is 97% accurate on franchise identity, and it lifted clip-level labels 8.2% → 11.5% and the low band by 7 points. It just cannot do the job that was being funded.

## Open

1. **Franchise-identity vs franchise-footage** — 5 of 34 matches are merch/fan art. Which one the bot needs is undecided.
2. **Bracketed keyword dumps** parsed as prose; needs a discount rule.
3. **Concentration at depth** — still open, still blocked by coverage, and no longer cheaply answerable from this corpus.
4. Whether low-band sounds (57% labelled) are worth a dedicated pass is now the more promising question, and it was not measured here.
