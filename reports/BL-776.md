## The finding: the optional-field hazard does not exist here

BL-772's warning was that a canary asserting account-level optional fields "fires constantly and gets switched off within a week" — measured on Instagram, where `lat`, `lng`, `location` and `video_subtitles_*` appear on one account and not two others.

**Across all six newly-baselined endpoints, there are ZERO optional paths.** Every field path present in one sample is present in all three:

| endpoint | schema paths (in all 3) | optional (in some) | **canary set** (present AND populated in all 3) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Spotify `get_artist` | 45 | **0** | 34 |
| Spotify `get_playlist` | 179 | **0** | 166 |
| Twitch panels | 14 | **0** | 10 |
| Google Play `app()` | 47 | **0** | 39 |
| Google Play `search()` | 15 | **0** | 13 |
| MusicBrainz url lookup | 23 | **0** | 15 |

**Why the difference matters.** HikerAPI returns raw Instagram private-API JSON, where an absent optional field is simply missing from the document. These five dependencies are either documented APIs or library-wrapped scrapers that normalise into a **fixed structure** — the field is always present, sometimes null. So:

> **The optional-field hazard is specific to the raw-mirror providers (HikerAPI, LamaTok), not a general property of this project's dependencies.** For these five funnels a canary may assert the full schema safely. For Instagram it must not.

That is the opposite of what I would have assumed from BL-772, and it makes the canary for these five both simpler and stricter than planned.

### The distinction that still matters: present vs populated

Schema count exceeds canary count everywhere (Spotify artist 45 → 34). Those are fields **present but null** in at least one sample — `world_rank` is the clearest case, present in all three artist payloads and null in all three, confirming BL-761's 0/60 measurement. The rule that follows:

- assert **presence** on the schema set,
- assert **non-null** only on the canary set,
- assert nothing on a field that is null in any baseline.

Canary-safe top-level fields, for the record — Spotify artist: `id, name, monthly_listeners, followers, external_links, biography, top_tracks, singles, uri, url, images, share_url`. Twitch panels: `display_name, followers, panels, socials`. Google Play `app()`: 39 including `developerEmail`, `developerWebsite`, `realInstalls`, `minInstalls`, `free`, `offersIAP`. MusicBrainz: `id, resource, relations`.

## The two zero-byte samples — deleted, and recorded

`tt_web_itemlist_davidfilmsss.json` and `tt_web_itemlist_zain.vfx.json` were both 0 bytes: a failed capture frozen as a baseline.

**Deleted, not recaptured.** The reasoning, recorded in the manifest:

- They capture TikTok's **web** itemlist endpoint, which is **not a live dependency** — the TikTok path runs through LamaTok / TikHub.
- Their sibling `tt_web_profile_*.html` files are **1,485 bytes each — a challenge/block page**, not a profile. The whole `tt_web_*` capture attempt failed at the time; these are its residue.
- Nothing in `clippershq/`, `tests/` or `scratch/` reads them by name (verified).
- A 0-byte file *presented as a baseline* is worse than no baseline: a future comparison reads "no fields" and could conclude the endpoint had lost every field.

`MANIFEST.json` now carries a `removed[]` block with the file, the reason, the endpoint, the timestamp and `recaptured: false`, so the fact of the failed capture is not lost by deleting the artifact.

**0 zero-byte files remain.** The sweep also surfaced 14 small-but-legitimate captures (MusicBrainz bodies are ~700 b, Twitch panels ~1.7 k, the balance probes 81 b) — listed, not touched, because small is not the same as empty.

## The size assertion — in place for baselines, still missing in the pipeline

**In place.** `scratch/bl773_capture.py::save()` refuses any capture that is structurally empty (`None` / `{}` / `[]` / `""`) or serialises below **200 bytes**, prints the refusal, and does not write. Every capture this round passed it (**17 saved, 0 refused**), and the one MusicBrainz 404 that failed was a genuine "not in the database" answer, re-captured deliberately as its own 404 sample.

**Still missing, and I could not fix it this round** — the brief was read-only on pipeline code:

| path | current guard | what is needed |
|---|---|---|
| `clippershq/ig_client.py::_save_sample` | `if ... data is None: return` | also refuse `{}` / `[]` / a body under N bytes |
| `clippershq/probe.py::_dump` | **none** — writes whatever it is handed | same |

`_save_sample` guarding only `data is None` means an empty `{}` writes as a "sample" exactly as before. **This is the mechanism that produced the two zero-byte files, and it is still live.** Three lines in each file. Flagged in `_probe_samples/README.md` under "Known gap" so it is not lost.

## Honest limits

- **Three samples is the stated minimum, not a guarantee.** Zero optional paths across three samples does not prove a fourth account would add none — it proves the hazard did not appear at the sample size the brief specified.
- The Spotify and Google Play samples come through **library wrappers** (SpotifyScraper, google-play-scraper), so the shape recorded is the *library's* normalised structure. A change in the underlying web page that the library absorbs would not show up here — the canary would catch a library-visible break, not every upstream break.
- **Twitch panels were captured from three large channels** (shroud, pokimane, sodapoppin). Panel structure on a 50–1,000-viewer channel — the funnel's actual target band — may differ; two of the three samples are near-identical in size, which is weak evidence of variety.
- The MusicBrainz third sample is Taylor Swift, a far larger artist than the funnel's targets. Its `relations` block may be richer than a typical lead's.
- **HikerAPI and LamaTok remain unbaselined at the 3-sample standard**, so the highest-risk dependency in the project still cannot be shape-checked — that is unchanged by this round and is the open decision above.
