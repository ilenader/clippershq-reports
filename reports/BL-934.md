## 2. The cost model, corrected where it is quoted

The comment block above `DEFAULT_MAX_PAGES` is what every cost model has been reading. It said 76.4% page 1 / 15.5% page 2 / 8.1% deeper and framed the cap as a coverage-versus-price dial. It now says what BL-921 measured:

> Retrieval meets **two populations that behave oppositely.** Clips that **exist** are found on page 1–2, essentially always — 24 of 24 at `max_pages=12`, including 12 the estimator called unreachable. Clips that are **gone** cost *exactly* `max_pages` and return nothing.
>
> So raising the cap does not find more clips. It raises the bill on dead ones, from $0.0012 each at cap 2 to $0.0072 at cap 12. **`DEFAULT_MAX_PAGES` is a waste ceiling, not a coverage dial**, and the depth splits and the $5.36/1,000 deep-clip figure both had the variable pointing the wrong way.

The old splits are named as superseded rather than deleted, because they were produced by the estimator this round replaced and someone will otherwise re-derive them.

## 3. Early deletion detection — wired, not just written

**The feed is sorted by views, which makes absence provable on page 1.** If a clip outranks the weakest entry on the page just read, it must already have appeared in that ordering. It did not, so it is not in the feed.

It needs **no extra call** — the page is already paid for — and it is exactly the inference the chronological estimator could never make.

**VERIFIED, on a paging fixture at `max_pages=12`:**

| | pages billed | |
|---|---:|---|
| without `clip_views` (old behaviour) | **12** | walks the whole cap |
| with `clip_views=5000` (outranks the floor, absent) | **1** | **92% cut on a dead clip** |
| with `clip_views=50` (ranks below the floor) | 12 | keeps walking — correct, it belongs deeper |
| clip actually present | 1 | unaffected |

On BL-921's residual run — 42 pages for 9 clips, two of them burning 12 pages each — this takes **42 pages to 20**, a 52% cut on that run.

**Conservative by construction.** Unknown views, an unreadable page, a tie at the floor, or a clip ranking below it all keep walking. **A false "deleted" costs a clip forever; a false "keep walking" costs one page.** The asymmetry is deliberate and is pinned by a test.

`clip_views` defaults to `None`, so every existing caller behaves exactly as before and opts in by passing the number it already holds.

## Limits
