## 1. The wire is closed, and it is proven by moving the drop

`hook_chain` was written, tested, documented — and had **ZERO shipping callers**. Verified
still true at the start of this round: the only references were its own definition, a
docstring in `add_song.py` saying so, and unit tests. Every `place_at_s` this project has
ever computed was a number in a ledger that never reached a pixel.

Three call sites now exist, one per layer:

| layer | file | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| plan | `clippershq/drop_placer.py` | finds the action second, picks the hook, returns `(ss, to, delay)` |
| produce | `clippershq/clip_pipeline.py` → `build_render_config` | writes `ambient_bed.hook_chain` |
| **consume** | `memebot/scraper/edit.py` | runs it as the bed leg **and drops the input-side trim** |

That last one is the half that matters. `edit.py` was doing the window with
`-stream_loop -1 / -ss / -t` on the ambient **input**; the chain does `atrim`, `asetpts`,
`aloop` and `adelay` in the **filter**. Leaving both would cut the window twice and land the
placement where neither half chose, so the input-side path is now an `else`.

### The A/B — same clip, two planned seconds

```
A  planned 3.0s  ->  drop HEARD at 3.00s   (error 0.00s, corr 0.999)
B  planned 9.0s  ->  drop HEARD at 9.03s   (error 0.03s, corr 0.17)

THE DROP MOVED BY 6.03 s. The planned difference was 6.0 s.
```

Measured by cross-correlating the output's 40–250 Hz band against the song from the exact
`atrim` start the render used. **The measurement itself had to be fixed twice**, and both
fixes are the point:

* the first version sampled the output from t=0, and arm B opens with 1.17 s of `adelay`
  silence — correlating silence against music returned a confident-looking 0.061 from noise.
  It samples from t=5 s now.
* the first wiring attempt read `amb_cfg` in a function 1,200 lines from where that variable
  exists, so both arms died with `name 'amb_cfg' is not defined`. The chain now travels
  through `_LAST_ROLLED_VALUES`, which is how every other per-render ambient value already
  reaches that function.

## 2. The placer, re-measured — 2.02x, not 3.35x

**The hit rate reproduces. The lift does not.**

Scored against the same 28 hand labels, on the **video** — I paid $0.0114 to re-fetch the 19
clips whose media `scratch/`'s sweep had taken, because the first pass scored on the
preserved contact sheets and those turned out to be a **degraded proxy**: sheet and video
picked the same second on only 22 of 28, and the sheet hit 10 where the video hit 12.
Measuring the shipping signal on the worse of two sources is the mirror image of inheriting
someone else's lift.

| tolerance | hits | rate | density-matched baseline | **lift** | 95% CI on the lift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ±0 s | 7/28 | 25.0% | 7.2% | **3.47x** | [1.76, 6.01] |
| **±1 s** | **12/28** | **42.9%** | **21.2%** | **2.02x** | **[1.25, 2.87]** |
| ±2 s | 14/28 | 50.0% | 33.6% | 1.49x | [0.97, 2.01] |

**42.9% is exactly the inherited hit rate. The inherited lift was 3.35x against a 12.8%
baseline; mine is 2.02x against 21.2%.** My baseline is computed exactly — not sampled — over
the same candidate seconds the placer is allowed to return, per clip. A tighter baseline
makes a smaller lift, and the smaller one is the one I can defend.

It clears the ~1.5x bar at ±1 s and at ±0 s. **At ±2 s it does not** — the CI includes 1.0 —
because widening the tolerance inflates the baseline faster than it inflates the hit rate.
The signal is a one-second event and it should be read at one-second resolution.

**On the 9 'high' confidence labels: 5 of 9 at ±1 s.**

## 3. Four of your twenty-one windows can ever place

This is the finding that reframes the request, and it arrived in the first measurement of
the round — free, before anything was built.

`song_library.place_at_detail` refuses when `clip_len < 2 × hook_len`. At the 20.0 s ceiling
that means **any hook longer than 10.0 s can never place on anything this pipeline ships.**

```
windows marked            : 21
length range              : 9.24 – 26.93 s
PLACEABLE on a 20.0s clip : 4 of 21
placeable on an 8.0s clip : 0 of 21

sng_0001  0 of 5   *** NONE ***
sng_0002  4 of 6   -> h1, h2, h3, h4
sng_0003  0 of 5   *** NONE ***
sng_0004  0 of 5   *** NONE ***
```

**All four are `sng_0002`** — the song MEMEBOT-019 measured as having essentially no clip
supply. So under that rule, "pick the hook that fits" is not a selection problem; it is a
supply problem, and three of your four songs could never have a drop placed at all.

### The rule is guarding a different design

`place_at_detail` places the hook to **play once, in full, inside the clip** — so it needs
room for the whole hook after the start point. What you asked for is **alignment**: the drop
instant lands on the action and the hook **loops** to fill, with whatever runs past the end
cut by `-shortest`. Under that model the hook's length does not have to fit; only the drop
instant has to land inside the clip.

**So `place_at_detail` is untouched.** `drop_placer.plan()` is a second, named answer to a
different question, and every placement records what the strict rule *would* have said:

```
"place_at_s": 7.0,
"strict_place_at_detail_ok": false,
"strict_place_at_detail_reason": "clip 8.0s is shorter than twice the 14.89s hook —
   place_at_detail refuses (it places the hook to play ONCE IN FULL inside the clip)"
```

The README prints both, per pair. Nobody has to guess which rule answered.

**THE ASSUMPTION THIS RESTS ON, so you can check it by ear: the drop is at the START of each
marked window.** Your notes name what a window contains — *"the best beat drop"*, *"this is
just a warm up"* — and a window is marked from its beginning. If a window's drop is really
3 s in, every placement using it is 3 s late, and the fix is one number per window in your
own file.

### Picking among h1–h5

After the drop lands at `action_s` there are `clip_len − action_s` seconds of video left, and
the hook should carry them. So: prefer a hook that **covers** that remainder (the loop seam
falls outside the payoff), then the **least-used** (rotation, derived from the ledger, never a
stored counter), then the shortest. When none covers, take the longest and **say** the seam
will land inside the payoff.

## 4. "Could not place" is not "placed at 0.0"

`place_at_s` is **`None`** on every refusal path, and `placement_refused` carries the reason
as a separate field. From the shipped ledger:

```
paste_DHETr2HtxFc  ok              place_at_s=3.0    hook=h1  placement_ok=true
paste_DLfI_3YNUrL  failed:refused  place_at_s=null   hook=—   placement_ok=—
```

0.0 is a real second — a drop on the first frame. A refusal that returns it is
indistinguishable from a success, and that exact shape produced a false 588-of-588 result
here once. Eight tests in `tests/test_drop_placer.py::CouldNotPlaceIsNotZero` pin every path.

### Does the drop land where it was planned?

**13 of 13 measurable rows within 0.25 s. Median |error| 0.00 s.**

Both traps the brief names are avoided by construction:

* **Correlate against the APPLIED window.** The reference is the `atrim` start parsed out of
  the render's own config, not the marked hook and not `fit_window`'s widened one — that
  mistake once read 11 of 20 renders as missing their song when 20 of 20 had it.
* **A plan of 0.00 matches a lag of 0.00 trivially.** 0 rows were planned at 0.00, so none
  needed excluding — and the exclusion is implemented and reported either way.

3 rows measured at correlation < 0.30 (the 4 s sample landed in a quiet or heavily faded
passage) and 4 could not be measured at all; both counts are stated rather than folded into
the accuracy figure.

### Is the planned second the RIGHT second?

Against the hand labels, restricted to the **17 clips whose labelled second falls inside the
shipped 20 s window** — the other 3 name a second the output does not contain, so comparing
them would be scoring the ceiling, not the placer:

**7 of 17 within 1 s (41%), 9 of 17 within 2 s (53%)** — consistent with the 42.9% measured
independently in §2.

### What reading every frame found that the numbers did not

The misses cluster on one shape: **clips built as HOLD → REVEAL.**

```
#13 @reeelcollabs  planned  2s   label 11s   "wait for it" — the baby reveal after a belly montage
#16 @cultureh0f    planned  3s   label 10s   the body in the coffin opens her eyes and screams
#22 @songss        planned  9s   label 15s   the crowd appears in the doorway
#27 @songss        planned  1s   label 17s   camera change out of the sustained arms-raised hold
```

**Motion peak finds the first big change. On a "wait for it" clip the first big change is the
setup, and the payoff is the last one.** The clear hits (#9 Emma Stone's scream, #12, #15
"you dumbass", #19) are all clips whose biggest change *is* the payoff.

I tested the obvious fix rather than asserting it: take the **last** second at or above a
fraction of the maximum, instead of the argmax. On the 19 clips available to that pass:

```
argmax (shipped)      5/19   1.41x
last >= 90% of max    7/19   1.97x
last >= 80% of max    6/19   1.69x
last >= 50% of max    4/19   1.13x
```

**Two hits on nineteen clips is not a result.** It is suggestive, it is the right next
experiment, and it is not shipped. Four placer attempts have already been closed here by
numbers; a fifth guess dressed as an improvement is worth nothing.

## 7. What I got wrong

* **I declared `outputs_drop_20260805_151235` and the run wrote `outputs_teach_…`** — `--teach`
  forces its own prefix. I amended the claim to the real path rather than leaving a
  declaration pointing at a directory that does not exist, and added a `--prefix` flag so the
  next batch can name its own folder.
* **The first wiring died on `amb_cfg` being out of scope**, in both A/B arms.
* **The first correlation sampled from t=0** and scored a placed render at 0.061 — measuring
  silence against music and reporting the noise floor as a result.
* **The A/B first picked a source that could not render at all** (no caption card), which
  would have reported the wiring as broken when nothing about the wiring had been tested. It
  walks until it finds one that labels, and skips lines over 90 characters.
* **The render path added one ffmpeg call per second of clip to the test suite**, turning a
  3-second suite into a 2-minute one before I patched the measurement out of the fakes.
