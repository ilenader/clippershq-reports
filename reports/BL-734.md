## 1. OpenRouter → Gemini — VERIFIED, and the arithmetic self-checks

Primary source: OpenRouter's own machine-readable pricing API,
`https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/google/gemini-3-flash-preview/endpoints`.

Gemini exposes a **separate `audio` price field**, distinct from `prompt`. That is the field that matters, and it is what the baseline was actually billed on.

|---|---|---|---|
| `google-vertex/global/flex` | **$0.50** | $1.50 | **$0.45** |
| `google-vertex/global` | $1.00 | $3.00 | **$0.89** |

Mirrored on `google-ai-studio` at the same three tiers.

**The standard tier computes to $0.89 and the owner measured $0.88.** That is the strongest validation available in this report: it independently confirms both the 800/31 token counts and that the baseline was billed at the standard $1.00/M audio rate. Working, so it can be checked: `800 × $1.00/1e6 + 31 × $3.00/1e6 = $0.000800 + $0.000093 = $0.000893` → **$0.89 per 1,000**. Flex halves both rates → **$0.45**.

### Signup — the criterion that disqualifies everything else

> "We accept all major credit cards, AliPay and cryptocurrency payments in USDC. We are working on integrating PayPal soon."

What *is* verified is that OpenRouter documents no tax-ID requirement, and that a USDC path exists which bypasses card-issuer identity checks entirely.

## 2. The honest caveat on Flash Lite

**Cost is verified. Quality is not.** Flash Lite is a smaller model, and nothing in this round tested whether it describes *music* as well as Gemini 3 Flash — genre, energy, vocals-present, build/drop. A 5.5× saving is only a saving if the answers are still right.

The cheap way to settle it: run both over the same 30-50 clips with the same enum schema and compare agreement. At $0.16 and $0.45 per 1,000, a 50-clip bake-off costs under three cents. **Do that before switching to Flash Lite; switch to Flex on Gemini 3 Flash immediately, because that is the same model at half price and carries no quality question at all.**

## 3. Everything that failed, and why

### Groq — REFUTED on capability
Groq's audio surface is `/audio/transcriptions` with `whisper-large-v3` and `whisper-large-v3-turbo`. Transcription only; there is no audio-understanding LM. Fast and cheap at the wrong task.

### OpenAI GPT Audio — REFUTED on cost
Per OpenRouter's model page for `openai/gpt-audio`: text $2.50/$10 per 1M, and audio **"$32 per million input tokens and $64 per million output tokens."** At OpenAI's documented ~10 audio tokens/second, a 58 s clip is ~580 tokens → `580 × $32/1e6 = $0.0186`/clip = **~$18.56 per 1,000**. Roughly 21× the baseline and 9× the hard ceiling.

### Alibaba DashScope (Qwen-Audio) — DISQUALIFIED on signup
"International signup via Alibaba Cloud requires KYC (Know Your Customer verification)", and the free tier "requires a Chinese phone number for verification". International Visa/Mastercard works at the Singapore endpoint, but KYC is precisely the identity burden the brief rules out. Not priced — the gate fails first.

### Cyanite / Sonoteller — DISQUALIFIED on sales model, despite being the best capability fit
These are purpose-built for exactly this. Cyanite returns "BPM, key, mood, genre, sub-genre, voice, gender, instruments, energy level" — genre, energy and voice-presence natively, no prompt engineering. Sonoteller returns genre/mood/instruments.

But Sonoteller states API "pricing is customized based on the user's specific needs and volume of analysis, with interested parties encouraged to contact the Sonoteller team directly for a quote", and Cyanite sells through AWS Marketplace. Contact-sales with no public price is the opposite of what an individual with a card needs, and AWS Marketplace drags in an AWS account with its own billing and tax surface. **GAP:** no public price exists to compute, so they cannot even be ranked on cost.

### Mistral Voxtral — viable on price, but the product signal is wrong
**Voxtral Small (v25.07)** is the only audio-*understanding* variant; Mistral's own model overview calls it "Our first model with audio input capabilities for instruct use cases."

Everything Mistral has shipped since is transcription or speech synthesis: Voxtral Mini Transcribe 2 (v26.02), Voxtral Mini Transcribe Realtime (v26.02), Voxtral TTS (v26.03). **The understanding variant has not been updated in about twelve months.** Read that as a direction-of-travel signal before building on it.

**No price is published** on `mistral.ai/pricing` or the docs model overview — GAP for the direct API. Priced instead via **DeepInfra**, which hosts both:

| model | $/minute | **$/1,000 clips @58 s** |
|---|---|---|
| Voxtral-Mini-3B-2507 | $0.00100 | **$0.97** |
| Voxtral-Small-24B-2507 | $0.00300 | **$2.90** — over the hard max |

DeepInfra's own copy says both "excel at speech transcription, translation and audio understanding."

**GAP:** DeepInfra lists these under *automatic-speech-recognition*, priced per **minute**. Whether the chat-with-audio understanding path bills at that same per-minute rate or at token rates on a different endpoint is not stated. The $0.97 assumes the former. Even if it holds, it is 2× Flash Lite Flex and 6× dearer for a 3B model against Gemini's.

**Mistral signup is genuinely friendly**, for the record: billing needs "either an individual or company name, address, and VAT number **if applicable**" — individuals explicitly accommodated, VAT optional, card and Google Pay. If Gemini were unavailable, this is where I would look next.

### Replicate, Together, Fireworks, Hyperbolic — GAP, not cleared
- **Replicate**: billing is pay-as-you-go on compute seconds with prepaid-credit or arrears, and the docs mention no business entity or tax ID — the *signup* side looks fine. But the model search page is client-rendered and returned no parseable HTML, and no search surfaced a hosted audio-understanding model (Qwen2-Audio, Audio Flamingo, LFM2-Audio) with a public price. **Capability unconfirmed, price uncomputable.**
- **Together AI** advertises multimodal including audio; **Fireworks** advertises "text, image, and audio models". Neither yielded a primary page naming a specific audio-*understanding* model with a price.
- **Hyperbolic**: nothing found on audio at all.

I am reporting these as GAP rather than inferring numbers. They are the loose ends of this round.

### Hugging Face Inference Providers — a payment path, not a discount
Successor to the retired Inference API; routes to third-party providers "at pass-through pricing with no markup". Free tier $0.10/month of credits, PRO $9/month gets $2.00 plus 2M credits, then pay-as-you-go at provider rates. **GAP:** could not confirm any *audio-text-to-text* model is served through it; HF's own backend is described as CPU-bound tasks (embeddings, classification, small models). Because pricing is pass-through, the economics are just the underlying provider's — it adds a way to pay, not a lower price.

## 4. The local route — all three open items CONFIRMED

### (a) Use ggml-org 2.0, not LiquidAI 2.5 — CONFIRMED
`huggingface.co/api/models/ggml-org/LFM2-Audio-1.5B-GGUF` contains three files: `.gitattributes`, `LFM2-Audio-1.5B-Q8_0.gguf` (~1.17 GB), and `mmproj-LFM2-Audio-1.5B-Q8_0.gguf`. **Not gated.** LFM2 architecture, 128,000 context.

`huggingface.co/api/models/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-Audio-1.5B-GGUF` ships exactly four runners:

```
runners/llama-liquid-audio-android-arm64.zip
runners/llama-liquid-audio-macos-arm64.zip
runners/llama-liquid-audio-ubuntu-arm64.zip
runners/llama-liquid-audio-ubuntu-x64.zip
```

**No Windows build.** BL-720's warning holds exactly as written — take the ggml-org 2.0 repo, which needs no vendor runner.

### (b) LFM Open License $10M threshold — CONFIRMED verbatim
> "Threshold" shall mean annual revenue of 10 million United States dollars ($10,000,000) or more."
> **§5(a)** "The rights granted under this License for Commercial Use are conditioned upon You or Your Legal Entity not exceeding the Threshold."
> **§5(b)** "Any Commercial Use of the Work or a Derivative Work by a Legal Entity that exceeds the Threshold is not licensed under this Agreement."

**Commercially usable below $10M annual revenue; not licensed above it.** A business fact for the owner to confirm, not a technical one — but for an individual with no registered company it is not a live constraint.

### (c) Mellow — still dependency-blocked, no change
`raw.githubusercontent.com/soham97/mellow/main/requirements.txt` still pins **`numpy==1.23.5`**, `torch==2.0.0`, `transformers==4.46.3`. These are exactly the downgrades pipguard refuses, numpy 2.x being this repo's ABI floor. **Mellow stays parked.** Its MIT licence — code and ungated weights — remains the cleanest of any candidate in this report; the blocker is purely its pins, and it has not moved in the year since.

## 6. Limits of this round

- **No account was created and no payment was attempted**, so every signup finding is documentation-derived. The tax-ID conclusion for OpenRouter is "not documented as required", which is weaker than "confirmed not required".
- **No model was called.** All prices are computed from published rates against the owner's measured 800/31 token profile. The $0.89 ↔ $0.88 agreement is what makes that arithmetic trustworthy; it does not make the *quality* of any untested model known.
- **Replicate, Together, Fireworks and Hyperbolic were not cleared** — capability unconfirmed from primary sources rather than refuted.
- **Voxtral's per-minute rate may not apply to the understanding path**; that single uncertainty is what separates $0.97 from unknown.
- **No accuracy benchmark was run for anything**, local or hosted. Every cost figure in the ranked table is firmer than every capability claim in it.
