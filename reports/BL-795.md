## Item 4 — one nuance to add, and it does not rescue the tool

The brief's hypothesis was that `vocals=none` would score high and `vocals=speech` low, giving a usable tool with a documented boundary. BL-763 tested this by *true* speech-dominance and found the `vocals` field carries no signal. I cut it the other way — by **the model's own `vocals` answer** — which is the cut a production gate would actually use, since it has no ground truth at runtime:

| model said | n | gradeable | ok | rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `singing` | 5 | 4 | 4 | **100%** [51, 100] |
| `none` | 8 | 7 | 3 | **42.9%** [16, 75] |
| `speech` | 2 | 0 | — | both unverifiable |

**The direction is the opposite of the hypothesis** — instrumental clips scored *worse* than sung ones — and the speech bucket is empty of gradeable rows, so the proposed boundary cannot be drawn even in principle here. With n = 4 and n = 7 and overlapping intervals this is not a finding to build on; it is one more reason not to. It does not change the verdict, and I would not have reported it as support for shipping if it had pointed the other way at this sample size.

## Item 1 — the headline number does not exist, and cannot

Stratum B is *defined* as clips with music and **no declared track**. All 20 rows confirm it: not one carries a title. There is no ground truth, no transcript, and the grader cannot hear audio, so no accuracy and no confidence interval can be computed for the stratum the brief calls the headline. What exists is a distribution:

```
pop 7 · orchestral-cinematic 5 · none 4 · rock 2 · hip-hop-rap 1 · electronic-edm 1
```

**Four of the 20 answered `none` — "no music at all" — in a stratum selected for having music.** That is a 20% false-negative on the simplest question in the schema, though with n=20 and the reproducibility problem above it is better read as more noise than as a measured error rate.

## Verdict — unchanged: CLOSE IT

The brief's rule was *above ~80% ships, below ~60% closes it, in between say so.* Strict accuracy is **33.3%**, below the floor. Lenient is **66.7%**, in the middle band. Ordinarily that would mean "say so and state what n would settle it" — BL-763 gives that answer: **120–150 gradeable clips**.

**But the sample size question is downstream of a problem that makes it moot.** An accuracy of 66.7% measured once, on a labeller whose genre answer reproduces 40% of the time, is not an estimate of anything stable — re-running the same 12 clips would move it. Collecting 130 more clips would buy a tighter interval around a number that is not measuring a fixed quantity. **Reproducibility has to be fixed first (seed pinning, or a vote across N passes), and only then is n worth spending.**

Ship declared-only; leave genre null on the ~77% with no declared track. That is what BL-763 concluded and the raw data still supports it.
