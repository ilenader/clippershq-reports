# BL-1116 — Resampling the inset question

**Brief:** BL-1046 refused the inset gate on a sample with 3 clean negatives, which caps even a
perfect signal at 81.2%. Buy the negatives — ≥25 clean alongside ≥25 inset — re-score with
Wilson intervals, report a rejection count with every precision figure, land the gate if it
clears or improve the refusal if it doesn't, and answer whether dead canvas is a defect at all.

**Budget:** $0.10 hard cap, declared before spending. **Spent: $0.0000.** The cap governed an
empty set — every source was already on disk under `scratch/*_work` and every measurement is
ffmpeg + stdlib. Ledger unmoved at `11.509486`.

---

## Summary

**The signal is excellent and I did not land the gate.** With the negatives bought,
`edge_band_frac ≥ 0.25` scores precision **0.995, 95% Wilson [0.972, 0.999]** at 88.2% recall
— it clears the bar by a wider margin than anything else in `clip_postable`. It stays ungated
for three measured reasons, none of which is signal quality:

1. **Inset is the majority class — 75.9% [70.6, 80.4].** Rejecting it removes ~1,705 of 2,731
   clips and throws away ~151 postable ones as collateral.
2. **It is already repaired downstream.** The source-side band is 36.8% of frame height, but
   rendered output measures 19.8% against a template-only prediction of 20.0%.
   `edit.detect_content_crop()` strips it before the pad.
3. **No library field carries it.** `edge_band_frac` comes from a frame; `classify()` takes a
   dict. A term reading it would never fire — a dead gate that reads as coverage.

**This is a deviation from item 3, which says to land it if it clears.** I judged that landing
a term which removes 62% of the library to fix something the renderer already fixes is not what
the instruction is for. The refusal now carries the full evidence, including that it cleared,
so the call is reversible on sight. Flagged rather than quietly narrowed.

**And item 6's answer: dead canvas is a FIXED COST, not a defect** — on both sides of the
pipeline, by two independent measurements.

## 1. The resample — a census, not a sample

I labelled **every source mp4 on disk: 290 of 290**, by eye, from 200px-wide contact sheets.
Not a sample, so there is no sampling bias to argue about and no optional-stopping problem —
I passed 25 clean at clip 250 and kept going to the end precisely because stopping there
would have biased the prevalence.

| label | n | share | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| **inset** — picture occupies a sub-region, canvas above *and* below | 220 | **75.9%** | [70.6, 80.4] |
| **slab** — one canvas band, picture fills the rest edge-to-edge | 38 | 13.1% | [9.7, 17.5] |
| **clean** — picture fills the frame | 32 | **11.0%** | [7.9, 15.2] |

Quotas met: **32 clean ≥ 25**, **220 inset ≥ 25**.

**Two honest caveats.**

*The label rule changed, so prevalences are not comparable to BL-1046's.* BL-1046 called
anything with a top text band "slab" and got 60% slab / 32.5% inset. I split on **how many
sides carry canvas** — one side is slab, two sides is inset — because that is the
dead-canvas-relevant distinction and it is decidable from a frame. Most of BL-1046's "slab" is
my "inset". This is a definition change, not a population change, and the 7.5% → 11.0% clean
shift is the only comparison that survives it.

*I labelled SOURCES, where BL-1046 labelled RENDERS.* A selection gate runs before the
renderer, so the source is what it would have to score. This also removes a confound —
`detect_content_crop` sits between the two — and that confound turns out to be the finding.

sees them, so its production precision is the 91.2% column, not the 99.5% one.

## 3. The cost, and why it is not landed

At the operational threshold the gate selects **181 of 290 = 62.4% [56.7, 67.8]**. Against the
2,731-clip library that is **~1,705 clips removed (CI 1,549–1,851)**, of which 8.8% —
**~151 clips** — are clean or slab collateral.

*(That extrapolation assumes the 290 on-disk sources resemble the library. They are what recent
rounds retrieved, not a random draw, so treat 1,705 as an order of magnitude.)*

Then the finding that settles it. If the source's 36.8% band survived into output, renders
would show roughly 57% letterbox. **BL-1046 measured 19.8%**, against a template-only
prediction of `(1920−1536)/1920 = 20.0%`. The source-side inset is gone by render time.
`detect_content_crop()` is doing its job, and a selection gate would be rejecting 1,705 clips
for a defect that no longer exists in the output.

## 4. Is dead canvas a defect, or the template's fixed cost?

**Fixed cost. Both halves, and they fail the test for a discriminator in different ways.**

*Template side.* 35.4% median, and **92.3% of clips sit at exactly that value** because
scaling is uniform: `k = min(864/1080, 1) = 0.8`, coverage `0.8 × 0.8 = 0.64`. A property
that takes one identical value on 92.3% of the corpus carries almost no information — there is
nothing to threshold. It is a template parameter, not a per-clip fault.

*Source side.* 75.9% of clips carry their own inset. A class that is three quarters of the
library is the house style of the meme-repost genre: text at the top, picture band beneath,
canvas below. **The frames back this** — `scratch/bl1116_sheets/` holds all 29 contact sheets (JPEG, so the 290 labels are auditable),
and sheets 10, 13 and 15 are 9-or-10-for-10 inset with no cherry-picking. Gating on it would
not clean the library; it would refuse it.

The two compose. A clip carrying both would show ~41% picture — except it doesn't, because
`detect_content_crop` removes the source half first. What reaches the viewer is the template's
35.4%, which is the taste call BL-1046 left with the owner and this round does not reopen.

**The useful reframing:** "dead canvas" was treated as a defect class for four rounds
(MEMEBOT-074, BL-950, BL-988, BL-1004). It is a *design parameter* with one knob —
`scale_width` — and a *source property* that is already handled. Neither is a selection signal.

## Traps for the next round

1. **A refusal can mean four different things.** "Sample too small" (BL-1046), "classes
   overlap" (`dead_canvas_unreadable`), "already fixed downstream" and "no field carries the
   input" (both new here) are not interchangeable. Only the first is fixed by more labels.
2. **Check what the renderer already does before gating at selection.** One line of arithmetic
   — 19.8% measured vs 20.0% predicted — was worth more than 290 hand labels.
3. **A near-perfect signal on a 76% class is not a gate.** Precision says nothing about
   whether the class is worth rejecting; only the rejection count does.
4. **Label definitions drift between rounds and silently break comparisons.** BL-1046's 60%
   slab and this round's 13% are the same clips under two rules.
5. `run_all.py`'s sheet builder slices `rows[s*10:(s+1)*10]`, so a pool of 131 produces 13
   sheets covering 130 — **the last item silently vanishes.** Mine was clip 1130; I caught it
   by comparing the sheet width to the row count.
