## 1. WHAT WAS BUILT

A judging loop that drives a logged-in Chrome, opens an Instagram profile, reads the **burned-in
text off the grid thumbnails**, and returns a verdict with a written reason naming the
disqualifier that fired. 211 pages judged across four runs, every verdict `flush()`+`fsync()`ed
to disk at the moment it was produced.

The central mechanical discovery is that **the drawn text is legible in the profile grid**. That
removes the 17-hour wall: BL-1239 measured 69.9 s/page unloaded, 95.2% of it frame reading.
This judge reads the same text off cover frames the browser has already rendered.

## 2. PART 1 — THE 16 EXAMPLES, STUDIED BEFORE ANY RULE WAS WRITTEN

Full study: `scratch/bl1240_reference_study.md`, written and timestamped **before** the first
line of judging logic. Every figure read off the live profile header.

### What they share
1. **A written line sitting on a strip above a borrowed clip** — 14 of 16. The clip is always
   someone else's footage.
2. **First-person and relational voice** — `when…`, `POV:…`, `me…`, `bro…`. **"bro" appears on
   11 of the 16 pages**, the most frequent content word in the corpus.
3. **Borrowed, recognisable footage** — 7 of 16 anime-dominant, 6 film/TV, 3 mixed.
4. **Nobody talks to camera.** Faces recur constantly, but they are film and TV characters.
5. **The page follows almost nobody** — 13 of 16 follow fewer than 25 accounts. Counts:
   0,0,0,0,1,1,1,2,4,13,14,24,95. The cleanest structural page-not-a-person tell in the set.
6. **English on the video** — and the caption is irrelevant.

### Where they differ, and it matters more than where they agree

**Follower count spans 4 → 22,300,000. Median 13.35K. Nine of sixteen are under 20K; three under
400; two under 10.** The 874-address harvest was ordered by follower count into the 250k–1M band.
**Exactly one of his sixteen examples would ever have been reached by that ordering.** That single
comparison is the whole diagnosis, and it needed no code.

**Line length is not a constant** — 3 words ("Me trying to sleep.") to 34 words. Any 90-character
hook ceiling cuts a large share of his own examples.

**`wasted` is a genuine outlier and I am not smoothing it over.** 22.3M followers, all-caps viral
news headlines, many stills. It shares the structural tells weakly and the *format* not at all.
It is consistent with his written "*it's ok if there is a couple of meme clips*", but used as a
positive template it readmits the entire news-aggregator population. **Treated as a capability
case, not a format template** — and flagged as the one place his 16 pull against each other.

**Two of the sixteen cannot be judged from the grid at all.** `lowkeyfunny69` (7 followers) has
13 identical textless chrome-skull covers; `legend47.x` reports **0 posts** with content only in
the Reels tab. Any cover-frame judge is blind on these. Stated up front, not discovered later.

### The three properties that destroy the shipped filter
- **"Reel creator" is in four of the sixteen bios** — `voidsenpai.fx_`, `carnine90`, `lawxliet1`,
  `loserdeity`. The `creator_page` rule reads the bio and hunts that exact word.
- **The captions are adversarial.** `memwithsm` posts 900-character AI film-encyclopedia captions.
  `sukuna._meme`, `legend47.x` and `lowkeyfunny69` paste the *same unrelated trending blurb* — a
  Japanese ONE PIECE PV announcement, or an English BTS/Vogue-World paragraph — onto every video.
  **841 of the 874 were judged on exactly this material.**
- **Bio promotion is on the best examples** — "dm for paid promotion", "Paid promotions & page
  shoutouts are OPEN. DM 'BLUE'". His rule is explicit: judge promotion on the drawn text only.

Scored against his own marks with the correct semantics — `mark` means *was the filter's verdict
correct*, not *is this page good*:
`want = (filter_verdict=="PASSED") XOR (mark=="BAD")`.

Three disjoint 50-page sets, **zero overlap**, all drawn from handles he has personally marked.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | v1 | A (50) | **77.6%** | [64.1, 87.0] | 76.0% | 56.0% [42.3, 68.8] |
| 2 | v2 | B (50) | **80.0%** | [67.0, 88.8] | 80.0% | 56.0% [42.3, 68.8] |

**Combined, 147 scoreable of 150: 80.3% [73.1, 85.9].** Recall on WANT rose 77.3 → 77.3 → 86.4.
**Iteration 3 is the first whose lower bound (70.4) clears the base rate's upper bound (68.8).**
The shipped filter scores 68.9% accuracy on the full 623.

Resolved agreement improved monotonically, +5.7 points. **Strict agreement plateaued at 80.0%**,
because iteration 3 produced two honest UNSUREs. Both numbers are reported; neither is chosen.

### What changed between briefs — only where he actually wrote something

**v2** (from iteration 1's eleven disagreements): stop firing `topical_aggregator` on sport and
opinion pages (his words: *"this is not promotion this is good… only text on the video"*); narrow
`edit_page` to pure montage (*"It IS a meme / repost page — the format is right"*); fire
`dialogue` harder on visible talking-head footage (two "Too much dialogue" chips).

**v3** (from iteration 2's ten): **the music-niche rule is about whose footage it is, not the
topic.** He wanted `_ravelab_` — *"a rave meme info repoest page, this one is very good"* — and
`technobangersdaily`. A rave/techno page that **reposts borrowed footage with a line on top** is
WANT; one that posts **its own sets, flyers and tour dates** is `business`. Also: narrow
`no_video` to grids that are entirely stills *and* have no reels.

Four of iteration 1's disagreements and five of iteration 2's were on pages where he wrote
nothing. **The brief deliberately did not move to fit them** — that is memorising the old filter.

## 5. THE MEASURED CEILING, AND WHERE IT SITS

Agreement is not uniform. Across all 147 scoreable rows, split by how much he engaged:

| his mark | n | agreement | Wilson 95% |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| chip "A person / content creator" | 15 | **93.3%** | [70.2, 98.8] |
| no reason at all | 80 | 85.0% | [75.6, 91.2] |
| chip "It IS a meme / repost page" | 14 | 57.1% | [32.6, 78.6] |
| **chip "Too much dialogue"** | 8 | **25.0%** | [7.1, 59.1] |

**The residual error is concentrated on "Too much dialogue", where the judge is worse than a coin
flip.** That is not a brief defect. Dialogue is largely an *audio* property and the judge reads
still cover frames — it cannot hear the videos. It is his second-largest rejection reason (63 of
623 marks). Three briefs pushed at it and it did not move.

**To clear ~90% something must hear the audio.** The cheapest route is a speech/music ratio per
post, which `attach_speech` already computes (Silero VAD, `speech_frac`) but which the browser
judge has no access to. Fusing the two is the concrete next step.

A separate and independent measurement of his marking behaviour: **on the 352 marks where he
typed nothing, he said the filter was right 98.3% of the time; where he typed a reason, 40.8%.**
Those silent rows largely echo the filter. *Correction to my own working note mid-round:* I first
read that as the place the judge loses agreement. Across all 150 pages it is not — the judge
agrees with the silent rows 85.0% of the time. The rubber-stamping is real and worth knowing when
weighting his marks; it is not the ceiling. The dialogue chip is.

## 7. PART 6 — TEACHING THE CHEAP FILTER

The browser's drawn lines are the labelled data. Scoring code: `scratch/bl1240_rules.py`.
In-sample = iterations 1+2 (100 pages, real operator labels, base reject 56.0% [46.2, 65.3]).
Out-of-sample = the 61 volume pages — **scored against the browser judge's verdict, not his, since
only 2 of the 61 carry a mark.** That caveat is stated rather than buried.

### Ships — lower bound clears the base rate's upper bound (0.653)

| rule | fires | precision | Wilson 95% |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **`no_text`** — ≤1 usable drawn line | 14 | 1.000 | **[0.785, 1.000]** |
| **`need_opener`** — reject if zero relational openers | 43 | 0.837 | **[0.700, 0.919]** |

Both are **requirements, not rescues**, which is the shape this project has repeatedly found to be
the only one that gains. Measured directly: rescuing browser-rejected pages on the opener lands at
**0.286 [0.117, 0.546]**, *below* the 0.440 base want-rate. Requiring it inside the accepted set
adds +0.011 over control — nothing.

### Does not ship, and the honest reason
Every opener threshold above one fails. `short_lines<40`, all `posts<N` and `followers<N` cuts,
and `commentary` (LB 0.646, missing 0.653 by 0.007) all fail. Eleven further rules fire on fewer
than 12 pages and are **refused on principle** rather than reported as flattering percentages —
that is the exact shape of the signal that went 6-for-6 at 31 labels and collapsed to 53%.

**The composition test decides it, and it says hold.** Running the full stack on top of what the
pipeline already PASSes cuts 13 of 46 — **4 of them pages he wants** — leaving 33 at 0.758
[0.590, 0.872] against the shipped 0.630 [0.486, 0.755]. **The intervals overlap. The stack is not
proven better than what ships.** +0.128 at n=46 is a direction, not a result; separating it needs
roughly 150–200 more marked PASSED pages.

### Three shipped rules are dead on arrival — delete at zero cost
Run over the judge's **verbatim, human-read** drawn lines (OCR error removed, best possible case):

| shipped function | fires on 100 pages |
|---|---:|
| `looks_like_dialogue` (rule `dialogue`) | **0** |
| `caption_is_promotional` (rule `promotional`) | **0** |
| `narrates_action` (film narration) | **0** |

This is not BL-1237's missing `top_text` and not an OCR fault — the regexes cannot match this
vocabulary. `caption_is_promotional` misses `WATCH FULL MOVIE ON YOUTUBE`, `Message Us to Order`,
`SIGN UP FOR PRESALE`. **Repairable** by swapping in the promo/flyer vocabulary; `dialogue` should
never have lived on `top_text` at all and needs re-expressing as talking-head *footage*.

**And `format_share` is confirmed as the weak point BL-1239 saw flip.** Its input `is_hook`
accepts **70 of 100 pages including most of the rejects**; reconstructed at every threshold
(<0.30 → 0.654, <0.50 → 0.647, <0.70 → 0.612, <0.90 → 0.583) **not one clears**. It is too
permissive, not miscalibrated.

### Do not add these disqualifiers to the pipeline
Cross-tabulating the browser's disqualifier against his `want` on the 100 marked pages:
**`dialogue` fires 6 times and he wants 3 of them — precision 0.500, below the 0.560 base reject
rate, i.e. anti-predictive.** All three (`rare.movie.clips`, `dumbhumour`,
`straight_face_challenges`) are **passed by production today**; adding it would destroy live leads.
`topical_aggregator`, `news`, `edit_page` and `no_video` each destroy at least one wanted page.
Only `business` (12 fires, 2 losses) survives with a testable n — and even it does not clear.
