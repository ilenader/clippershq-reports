# BL-763 — Audio genre labelling: CLOSE IT. Non-reproducibility is upstream of accuracy, and no threshold or vote is worth buying.

**Date:** 2026-07-31 · **Type:** Verdict, READ-ONLY · **Spend:** **$0.0000** — no paid call
made. This is an interpretation of BL-752's measurements, not a new benchmark.

---

## Verdict: CLOSE

Ship **declared-only**. Leave genre null on the other ~77%. This joins mood classification
(BL-682) and drop detection (BL-677) as a measured dead end, which is a valid outcome and
should be recorded as one rather than revisited.

The reason is not that accuracy is low. It is that **accuracy is not a meaningful quantity for
this model**, because the model does not return the same answer twice.

---

## 1. The measured numbers, plainly

| measurement | result | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| **genre agreement, music-under-dialogue stratum** | **does not exist** | — |
| genre vs declared track, **lenient** | 8/12 = **66.7%** | [39.1, 86.2] |
| genre vs declared track, **strict** | 4/12 = **33.3%** | [13.8, 60.9] |
| **reproducibility — genre identical on re-ask** | **4/10 = 40.0%** | [16.8, 68.7] |
| reproducibility — *whole answer* identical | **1/10 = 10%** | — |
| speech-dominant clips given a music genre anyway | **11/14 = 78.6%** | [52.4, 92.4] |
| speech-dominant clips correctly called `none` | 3/14 = 21.4% | [7.6, 47.6] |

**The headline you asked for is not computable, and BL-752 was right to refuse it.** The
stratum named "music-under-dialogue" is `B-music-no-title`, and it turns out to be music with
*little* speech — median `speech_frac` **0.10**, range 0.00–0.34. It has no declared track and
no transcript, so there is no ground truth to grade against. On the 6 clips inside it with
`speech_frac >= 0.20` the model returned orchestral-cinematic 2, pop 2, hip-hop-rap 1, rock 1
— a distribution, not an accuracy. Declining to invent a number there was the correct call.

### Accuracy split by whether the model detected speech: also uncomputable, and that is itself the finding

Of the 16 gradeable declared-track clips, only **2** returned `vocals='speech'`, and both are
`unverifiable` (obscure artists). **The gradeable subset is n=0.**

The reason it is n=0 matters more than the gap: **the `vocals` field carries no signal at
all.** It reports "speech" on **28.6%** of genuinely speech-dominant clips and on **28.6%** of
near-speechless ones. Identical rates. You cannot split accuracy by a detector that is not
detecting anything.

## 2. Non-reproducibility, quantified

**6 of 10 clips returned a different genre when sent twice on identical bytes at
`temperature: 0.0`.** Only 1 of 10 returned an identical answer across all fields.

Your framing — neighbouring genres survivable, orchestral versus hip-hop not — is exactly the
right test, and the two documented disagreements both fail it:

| case | pass A | pass B | severity |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Cornfield Chase* (Hans Zimmer, Interstellar) | **hip-hop-rap** | **orchestral-cinematic** | **the exact non-survivable case you named** |
| one B-stratum clip | a named genre | **`none`** | **"there is music" → "there is no music at all"** |

Neither is a neighbouring-genre wobble. The first is piano-and-strings film score labelled as
hip-hop. The second is not a genre error at all — it is the model disagreeing with itself about
whether music is present.

The 5-pass re-test of the Zimmer clip is the clearest picture of the failure mode:

```
pass 1  hip-hop-rap            moderate  no build
pass 2  orchestral-cinematic   intense   build
pass 3  orchestral-cinematic   intense   build
pass 4  orchestral-cinematic   intense   build
pass 5  orchestral-cinematic   intense   build
```

**4 of 5 correct. The failure did not reproduce.** That is the worse outcome, not the better
one. A systematic blind spot can be documented and routed around. A 1-in-5 flagrant error on a
clip the model usually gets right is unpredictable, unfilterable, and invisible unless you
repeat every single call.

A third, independent instability signal points the same way: **24% of the first-pass calls
returned unparseable output** (11 of 45), and a plain re-run recovered all 11 with zero errors.
Same bytes, different behaviour.

**I could not reconstruct the full pairwise distance distribution across all 10 repeat pairs** —
the published asset `bl739_labels.json` did not fetch, and the local `bl722_results.jsonl` has
63 rows with no repeated keys (the consistency pass was recorded separately). So "6 of 10
disagreed" is solid, and the two cases above are documented verbatim, but **I cannot tell you
how many of the remaining four disagreements were mild versus flagrant.** That gap is stated
rather than filled.

## 3. Is there a fix? Priced, and none of them worth buying

| option | cost / 1,000 | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| do nothing | $0.55 | 60% genre churn on re-ask |
| **pin `seed`** | **$0.55** (no change) | **try first — but it is not known to be honoured** |
| lower/alternative sampling params | $0.55 | temperature 0.0 is *already* being sent and is not producing determinism |
| **majority vote over 3 calls** | **$1.65** | **does not fix it — see below** |

**Majority-vote arithmetic, since it is the tempting one.** BL-752's real billed rate is
**$0.55/1,000** (OpenRouter's own `usage.cost`, not the token estimate, which ran 19% high).
Three calls is **$1.65/1,000** — the brief's $0.45→$1.35 figures are from the older estimate;
the billed numbers are 0.55 and 1.65.

Voting would help if errors were independent and rare. Here the base is 40% genre stability,
so a 2-of-3 majority is being taken over a distribution where the modal answer wins only
sometimes. On the Zimmer clip a vote *would* have worked (4 of 5 correct). On the clip that
flipped between a named genre and `none`, a vote decides *whether music exists* by ballot —
and there is no measurement of how often the modal answer is the right one across the corpus,
because that experiment was never run.

**Buying a 3× cost multiplier to paper over an instability whose distribution is unmeasured is
not a fix; it is a hope with a price tag.** If anything is worth spending on, it is the free
option: pin `seed`, re-run the 10-clip consistency test, and see whether determinism returns.
That costs $0.0055 and answers the only question that matters. Everything downstream is
unmeasurable until it does.

## 4. Against the free alternative

| | declared track (`music_info`) | model genre |
|---|---|---|
| coverage | **~23% of clips** | ~100% |
| accuracy | **100% — the platform states it** | 33.3% strict / 66.7% lenient |
| reproducible | **yes, trivially** | **40%** |
| cost | **$0** | $0.55–1.65 / 1,000 |

The model would need to beat *nothing* on the other 77% — the field is currently null there, so
any correct answer is a gain. That is the strongest possible case for shipping it, and it
**still fails**, because a wrong genre is not a neutral substitute for null. It propagates into
tone, era and mood inference exactly as BL-731 found a wrong film title does, and 6-in-10
churn means a clip's stored genre depends on which day it was processed.

**A field that changes when you re-ask it cannot be a library column at any accuracy.** The
66.7% lenient figure is the only measurement inside the 60–80% band, and it is moot:
reproducibility sits upstream of accuracy. An accuracy number computed on an unstable
classifier describes one draw from a distribution, not a property of the tool.

Widening n does not help. Separating 66.7% from an 80% bar at 95% confidence needs roughly
**n = 120–150** gradeable clips — which at $0.55/1,000 costs cents. **Do not spend it.** The
prerequisite is not more samples; it is a model that answers the same question the same way
twice.

---

## 5. What to do

1. **Ship declared-only.** `music_info` gives title and artist on ~23% of clips at platform
   accuracy and $0. Store those. **Leave genre null on the rest** — null is honest, a churning
   genre is not.
2. **One free experiment before closing the file for good:** pin `seed`, re-run the 10-clip
   consistency test. If genre stability goes from 4/10 to 10/10, the accuracy question reopens
   and is worth ~$0.08 to settle properly. If it does not, close it permanently.
3. **Do not buy majority vote.** $1.65/1,000 to average over an unmeasured error distribution.
4. **Record `vocals` as dead.** 28.6% vs 28.6% is not a weak signal, it is no signal, and it
   should not be carried in any schema as though it were.

## Limits

- **This report makes no new measurements.** Every number is BL-752's, restated and interpreted;
  I made no API call and spent nothing.
- **n is small throughout** — 10 consistency pairs, 12 gradeable declared clips, 14
  speech-dominant. Every CI is wide, and the 66.7% lenient figure spans [39.1, 86.2]. The
  *reproducibility* finding is the one that does not need a tighter CI: 6-in-10 disagreement on
  identical bytes is a categorical property, not a rate to be refined.
- **The full disagreement-distance distribution is missing** (see §2). Two cases are documented
  in detail; the other four are known-different but uncharacterised.
- **A standing confound on the declared-track grades**, carried from BL-752: the audio sent is
  the *clip* — licensed track with dialogue and edit mixed over it — not the clean master. A
  divergence from the declared track is not automatically a model error, which is why the
  lenient 66.7% is the fairer figure and the strict 33.3% understates.
- The Zimmer re-test is **one clip**, 5 passes. It establishes that instability exists and is
  flagrant; it does not establish its frequency across the corpus.
