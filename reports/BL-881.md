## 3. `superseded:` — built, not just proposed

A manifest had two states: **still true**, or **red**. A round whose work is correctly removed later had no third option, and its failure message read as a regression — *"this shipped and is now missing"* — when the truth was *"this shipped, was measured, and was deliberately deleted"*.

```
superseded: const clippershq/song_library.py::TIER_TITLE by MEMEBOT-032: 1 right of 13
```

**It asserts the INVERSE of the original claim.** It passes while the symbol is genuinely absent at HEAD, and **fails if it comes back** — because then somebody has re-added a thing a measurement removed, and two records disagree. That is the property a comment cannot have: MEMEBOT-036's comment is correct today and would still read as correct the day `TIER_TITLE` reappeared.

**VERIFIED**, four behaviours, all in the suite (`tests/test_claims_manifest.py`, now 17 tests):

| case | result |
|---|---|
| the real case — `TIER_TITLE`, deleted by MEMEBOT-032 | **PASS** — *"absent at HEAD, as recorded (removed by MEMEBOT-032: 1 right of 13)"* |
| a symbol that is still present (`TIERS`) | **FAIL** — *"is PRESENT again at HEAD … reconcile the record with the code"* |
| no removing round recorded | **PASS**, and the detail says *"no removing round recorded — add 'by <ROUND>'"* |
| malformed (`superseded: nonsense`) | **FAIL closed**, never a silent skip |

It is the mirror of BL-874's rule. That one says a manifest waits for the code and never the reverse; this says a manifest may record that the code moved on **without lying about what shipped or failing forever**. Both keep the manifest subordinate to the tree.

Documented in `verify_claims.py`'s own header, with the one misuse spelled out: it is **not** a way to retire a claim you can no longer support — that is deleting the line.
