## 2. THE PRE-FILTER'S COST — one wanted page in six, and two instruments disagree

Instrument: `scratch/bl1243_prefilter.py` (selftest **18/18**), output `scratch/bl1243_prefilter.json`.

The brief asks how many pages the browser would have approved the cheap filter kills, and says:
*"If that number is large, the pre-filter is not worth it and you should say so."*

The join needs no re-run: `ground_truth/review_marks.jsonl` (**669 rows → 623 handles**, read
from disk, path and count reported before use) already records `filter_verdict` per handle.
Joined against BL-1240's browser verdicts (**213 rows → 211 handles**): **152 handles carry
both.** Browser: **WANT 69 · REJECT 80 · UNSURE 3.**

**Random baseline first, per the project rule.** A filter that PASSES 46.1% of pages kills
**53.9%** of anything by chance.

| instrument | kills browser-approved pages | 95% |
|---|---|---|
| **recorded** `filter_verdict` (historical, what the marks were made against) | **22 of 69 = 31.9%** | [22.1, 43.6] |
| **live re-run of the shipped filter, today** | **12 of 69 = 17.4%** | [10.2, 28.0] |
| random, same pass rate | 53.9% | — |

**Both beat random. They do not agree with each other, and that is the finding.** The live
re-run reproduces the recorded verdict on only **82.2%** of the 152 (differs on 27), because the
recorded verdicts predate several rounds of rule edits. **Quote the live figure forward, the
recorded one when reasoning about the marks, and never average them.**

**What did the killing, live:** `low_views` **6**, `creator_page` **4**, `format_share` **3**,
`too_few_posts` **1**.

### The answer

**17.4% [10.2, 28.0] is large.** Roughly one page in six that the browser wants — the judge the
operator rated "every approve 100% justified" — never reaches it. Named casualties include
`hexamv`, which BL-1242 independently records him wanting.

**`creator_page` and `format_share` account for 7 of the 12**, and both are already on record as
broken: BL-1240 measured `format_share`'s input `is_hook` accepting **70 of 100 pages including
most of the rejects**, clearing at no threshold. Using a filter with two known-broken rules as a
gate in front of a judge that outperforms it inverts the ordering.

**Recommendation: do not gate the browser behind the code filter as it stands.** Run the cheap
filter for *ordering* if throughput demands it — judge its PASSes first — but do not let it
delete anything. A 17.4% loss at the top of the funnel cannot be recovered downstream, and the
supply is the binding constraint (§4).

### The "Reel creator" blind spot — half right, and the half that is wrong matters

The brief warns that `creator_page` *"hunts for the phrase 'Reel creator', which four of his
sixteen favourite pages have in their bio."* Tested directly against the shipped function:

| input | `creator_page` fires |
|---|---|
| bio `"Reel creator"`, neutral handle | **True** |
| bio `"Reel creator \| DM for credit"` | False — repost phrase in bio disarms it |
| bio `"Reel creator. Daily memes"` | False — same |
| bio `"Reel creator"`, handle `memepage7` | False — `meme\|clip\|repost\|daily\|funny\|vids?\|page` in the handle disarms it |
| followers ≥ **300,000** | exempted at the call site |

**The literal string "Reel creator" appears in no `.py` file in this repo.** The mechanism is the
bare word `creator` inside `_SELF_DECLARED_RE` (`clippershq/meme_finder.py:1132-1135`), reached
via `is_creator_page()` at `:1149-1191`.

**And the correction that matters: in this project's data "Reel creator" is usually the Instagram
`category` field, not the bio.** `category` is passed into `judge_page` (`:1290`) and forwarded
**only** to `niche_of(...)` (`:1445`), which is documented *"NEVER a rejection"* (`:768`). **A
category of "Reel creator" fires nothing.** The rule bites on bio text, and only when no repost
signal appears in the bio or the handle.

So the warning is real but narrower than stated — and it still cost 4 wanted pages live.

## 3. CAN THE WRITTEN REASON BE SHORTER? — yes, and length is a difficulty flag

Same instrument. 149 scoreable rows, agreement scored with BL-1240's semantics
(`want = (filter_verdict=="PASSED") XOR (mark=="BAD")`, its own function with seven tests
including the negative case that `mark: GOOD` does not by itself mean wanted). Median reason
**384 chars**; split at the median:

| half | n | agreement | 95% |
|---|---|---|---|
| short (≤ median) | 76 | **89.5%** | [80.6, 94.6] |
| long (> median) | 73 | **71.2%** | [60.0, 80.3] |

**Intervals are disjoint, and longer reasons agree LESS.**

**Confound checked before believing it.** A REJECT has a disqualifier to explain and a WANT does
not, so length might be standing in for the verdict. It is not — the effect survives inside
both:

| verdict | n | mean reason | short-half agreement | long-half agreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WANT | 69 | 408.5 chars | 85.7% | 67.7% |
| REJECT | 80 | 376.0 chars | 92.5% | 75.0% |

**This is not evidence that writing at length causes errors** — the reason is written *after*
the judgement. The defensible reading is that **the judge writes more when the page is hard**, so
length is a **difficulty marker**, not a carrier of diagnostic value.

Two consequences, and the second is worth more than the first: the reason **can** be shortened on
the easy majority with nothing lost — and at 366 median characters it was never the cost anyway
(§1) — while **reason length is a free, already-logged confidence signal**: the long half is
where 71% agreement lives, and it is the half worth re-checking.
