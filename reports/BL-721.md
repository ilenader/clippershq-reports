# BL-721: the pilot is now answerable — 73 targets, and the window question resolves in the pilot's favour

**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Spend:** **$0.** No AudD request made, no account created. CDN audio and local CPU only.
**Follows:** [BL-718](BL-718.md), which refused to run the pilot at n=12.

## 1. The target group is 73, not the projected 52

VAD split run over **every** undeclared clip in the saved corpus (294 with an audio URL; 284 decoded, 10 URLs expired):

| group | n | share |
|---|---|---|
| **music-no-title (target)** | **73** | **25.7%** |
| dialogue-only | 208 | 73.2% |
| silent / unusable | 3 | 1.1% |

**The music rate is 25.7%, not the 17.6% BL-718 measured on n=68.** The small-sample figure was low by roughly a third, which is a useful reminder in its own right: BL-718's 12 targets were not just few, they were drawn from an underestimate. The larger measurement also lands much closer to the ~24% the pilot was originally scoped on, so the original assumption was better than the n=68 check suggested.

## 2. Power at n=73: the pilot can now return a verdict

| outcome | hit rate | 95% CI (Wilson) | against the bars |
|---|---|---|---|
| 29/73 | 40% | 29% – 51% | **decisively below 60%** |
| 36/73 | 50% | 38% – 61% | spans a bar — ambiguous |
| 44/73 | **60%** | **49% – 71%** | **decisively clears 40%** |
| 51/73 | 70% | 59% – 79% | clears 40% |

At n=12 every plausible outcome spanned both bars. At n=73 only a result in the low-to-mid 50s stays ambiguous; a 60% ship signal and a 40% close signal are both now separable. **This is the change that makes the pilot worth running.**

## 3. The window question — settled, and it goes the pilot's way

**The standard endpoint cannot aim.** Its documented parameters are `api_token`, `url`, `file`, `return`, `market` — no offset, no window, no duration. It caps at 10 MB and the docs redirect longer audio elsewhere: *"For longer audio … use the enterprise endpoint instead."*

**The enterprise endpoint can aim, and it does not require an enterprise account.** Quoting the docs directly:

> *"You don't have to have an enterprise account in order to use the enterprise endpoint."*

It accepts exactly the parameters this pilot needs:

- **`skip_first_seconds`** — *"How many seconds of the file the server should skip at the beginning before starting the music recognition"*
- **`limit`** — *"The upper bound for the number of chunks the server will recognize"*
- (`skip` and `every` also exist, for sampling long files)

Billing is **1 request per 12 seconds of audio scanned**. So `skip_first_seconds = <clean-window start>` with `limit=1` fingerprints exactly one 12-second chunk aimed at exposed music, for exactly one request.

**No local trimming and no re-hosting is required.** `progressive_download_url` can be passed straight through as `url`.

### What that costs, at the measured n

Target clips are short — median **23 s**, mean 34 s:

| approach | requests | share of the 300 free tier |
|---|---|---|
| scan whole clip | **269** | **90%** |
| **aimed** (`skip_first_seconds` + `limit=1`) | **88** | **29%** |

**3.1× fewer requests.** 88 covers all 73 targets plus a 15-clip positive control. Crucially it leaves headroom: a full second-pass retry on every failing target is 161 requests, still only **54%** of the free tier. Scanning whole would leave none.

Extrapolated at one aimed request per clip: **$5.00 per 1,000** at list, **$2.00 per 1,000** at volume.

## 4. A new constraint the aiming data exposes

AudD's chunk is **fixed at 12 seconds**. The clean windows are not always that long:

| clean window | clips | share | consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≥12 s | 49 | **67%** | chunk sits entirely in music |
| 5–12 s | 21 | **29%** | chunk will include some speech |
| <5 s | 3 | 4% | no usable window |

Median clean window is 18.5 s (min 3.5, max 179.1), and 96% have at least 5 s. But only **two-thirds of targets can be given a fully speech-free 12-second chunk.** For the remaining third the fingerprinter will be working against partial dialogue overlap — which is exactly the condition this pilot exists to measure, so it should be *recorded per clip and reported as a stratum*, not hidden in the headline. Per-clip window start, length and the top three gaps are persisted in `scratch/bl719_targets.json`.

## 5. VAD cost: BL-718's 4.15 s/clip was inflated by model load

This run: **284 clips in 768 s = 2.70 s/clip**, and the per-clip figure fell monotonically as the run progressed (4.47 → 3.61 → 3.22 → 2.95 → 2.76 → 2.70), which is model load and warm-up amortising over a larger batch. At 1,000 clips/day that is **45 minutes**, not the 69 BL-718 projected. Still above the original ~1.6 s estimate.

Audio fetch: 216 newly downloaded in 120 s, **10 of 294 URLs expired** (3.4%) — the ~33 h window is holding but no longer perfectly.

## Recommendation

The pilot is now correctly sized and the request budget is settled. What remains is the one thing that is not mine to do: **the AudD account.** 300 free requests, no credit card, but creating it means accepting the vendor's terms, which is the account holder's decision.

If you create it and hand me the token, the run is:

1. **positive control first** — 15 clips with declared titles, aimed the same way. If AudD cannot name tracks we already know the names of, nothing downstream means anything.
2. **73 targets**, one aimed request each, stratified by clean-window length so the ≥12 s and 5–12 s groups report separately.
3. hand-check 15 hits; a confident wrong answer is worse than a miss.
4. verdict against the 40% / 60% bars, on the ≥12 s stratum primarily.

Total: 88 requests, 29% of the free tier, with retry headroom.
