## 1. The @-handle floor — and why lowering it is not the fix

The shipped rule is `@([A-Za-z0-9._]{3,})`. Every variant, measured on **2,728 library clips**:

| variant | clips flagged | Δ vs shipped | top account's share of the new | catches `@nm`? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `{3,}` (shipped) | 108 | — | — | no |
| `{2,}` | 126 | **+18** | **66.7%** (`clipsvaultex` ×12) | yes |
| `{2,}` + not-an-English-word | 126 | +18 | 66.7% | yes |
| `{2,}` + must-contain-a-digit | 108 | +0 | — | **no** |
| `{1,}` | 126 | +18 | 66.7% | yes |
| **`{2,}` + acronym-aware `foreign()`** | **110** | **+2** | — | n/a — see below |

MEMEBOT-108's +18/12 reproduces exactly. But the whole library contains only **four** distinct
two-character tokens, so this is hand-answerable rather than statistical:

| token | × | account | verdict |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `@cv` | 12 | `clipsvaultex` | **C**lips**V**ault — its own sign-off |
| `@nm` | 4 | `nextmovies2o` | **N**ext**M**ovies — one clip reads `@nextmovies2a` / `@nm` |
| `@MC` | 1 | `movyclipz` | **M**ovy**C**lipz — self |
| `@TC` | 2 | `kyroxr.x`, `fun.exa` | **genuinely foreign** |

**16 of the 18 clips the lower floor adds are an account abbreviating itself.** Lowering the floor
alone would be wrong 89% of the time.

### This corrects the brief's premise for `@nm`

The brief states `@nm` "was read by OCR and lost in the regex", implying a third-party mark the
matcher should catch. It is `nextmovies2o` signing its own video — **the term is right to ignore
it.** MEMEBOT-108 recorded that video's defect as *"NextMovies fake-tweet brand card … plus an
`@nm` corner mark"*; the objectionable part is the fake-tweet card, which is a different, ungated
shape. Catching `@nm` would have been a false positive dressed as a fix.

### What landed

A substring test cannot separate these, because `"cv"` is not a contiguous run of
`"clipsvaultex"`. **An ordered-subsequence test can**: c…v appears in order inside clipsvaultex,
n…m inside nextmovies2o, and `TC` matches neither `kyroxr.x` nor `fun.exa`. Bounded to
`_ACRONYM_MAX = 3`, because above that almost any short string is a subsequence of a long account
name and a generous bound would quietly stop the term firing at all.

## 2. A second matcher bug, pre-existing, on the term's own cited example

`domains = [d.split(".")[0] for d in _DOMAIN.findall(t)]`

On `www.OutstandingScreenplays.com` that returns **`"www"`**, which `foreign()` then strips to the
empty string and never flags. **`www.OutstandingScreenplays.com` is one of the four marks this
term's own `why` text cites as the reason it exists.** Confirmed present at HEAD; found by writing
a test case straight from that docstring. Fixed by stripping `www.` before taking the label.

### Combined cost of both fixes

**107 → 115 clips (+8), 0 previously-flagged clips lost.** Every addition hand-checked:

**8 of 8 are genuinely somebody else's mark. 0 false positives, 0 regressions.**

## 3. The bare-brand rule and five narrowings — REFUSED, with numbers

The SCENESTING shape is a bare brand: no `@`, no TLD. Position is not in
`vision_on_screen_text` — it is a flat string — so the 35 frames BL-1002b already extracted were
**re-OCR'd with `detail=1`** for per-block boxes and scored against **BL-1002b's hand labels**,
read off those same frames before any score existed. The 7 third-party positives are ALPHANATIV ×5
and TRILLERTV — exactly the SCENESTING shape.

| rule | TP | FP | FN | TN | precision | recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| bare brand (any non-account token) | 7 | 9 | 0 | 19 | **43.8%** | 100.0% |
| + ALL-CAPS | 1 | 1 | 6 | 27 | **50.0%** | 14.3% |
| + CORNER (top/bottom 18%) | 1 | 3 | 6 | 25 | 25.0% | 14.3% |
| + ALL-CAPS **and** CORNER | 0 | 0 | 7 | 28 | — | **0.0%** |
| + ALL-CAPS and CORNER and narrow | 0 | 0 | 7 | 28 | — | **0.0%** |

**Nothing clears 80%. Nothing ships.** The bare-brand rule's 43.8% on 35 clips is consistent with
the 245-clip/9% blow-up MEMEBOT-108 measured library-wide.

**Why the narrowings fail is the interesting part.** Adding ALL-CAPS drops recall from 100% to
14.3% and adding CORNER takes it to zero — because **OCR does not preserve the case of a faint
corner mark**. BL-1002b read ALPHANATIV as `alphanatv`, `4LPHAnATIV`, `alpmAMAT` and `Lphanat`.
A rule keyed on `.isupper()` is keyed on something the reader destroys. The signal is in the
pixels; the *matcher* fails — and so does every narrowing built on properties the OCR does not
carry.

`tests/test_clip_postable.py` now asserts the refusal (`SCENESTING` must NOT flag), so a future
round cannot land the bare-brand rule without deleting a test that carries the measurement.

## 5. Content safety — blocked on OCR coverage, and no keyword filter built

MEMEBOT-098 saw 1 of 30; MEMEBOT-108 saw **4 of 30**. Checked against the field a keyword rule
would have to read:

| account | defect | field |
|---|---|---|
| `weareassassin` | crucifixion, visible blood | **EMPTY** — visual only |
| `acerhodes2` | medical gore, bloodied corpse | **EMPTY** — visual only |
| `wisecon` | ethnic slur burned into the subtitles (*"Fuck that norteno up"*) | `"Forced to commit a brutal prison murder to join the…"` |
| `thomasabg` | triple murder plus burned-in profanity | `"Arthur Fleck snaps and kills three men on the subway"` |

**A keyword filter on `vision_on_screen_text` would catch 0 of 4.** Two have an empty field. The
other two have a field that *describes the scene* — it does not quote the burned-in words, so the
slur a filter would key on is not in the string it would search. **No keyword filter was built,**
which is the brief's instruction and also what the measurement independently says.

This is blocked on OCR coverage, not on a rule.
