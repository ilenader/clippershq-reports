## 3. THIRTY MESSAGES READ VERBATIM — AND THEY FOUND TWO DEFECTS

30 rendered and read across both audiences: 10 ALL/clipper, 10 ALL/client, 10 MEME/clipper.
Full text in `scratch/bl1250_messages.md`, every address redacted at the **write** point.
Tiers seen: REACH 12, NAMED 10, FULL 8; name sources: display 15, `handle_at` 13, display_first 2.

### DEFECT 1 — the greeting is a raw Instagram handle

That last one is literally the defect family the brief names. Measured across the whole

### DEFECT 2 — the follower count is an unformatted integer
```
I found you through your edits on short-form. That's 9575159 followers.
That's 56262520 followers
```
**831 of 874 meme messages — 95.1%.** Zero in `ALL_BOT_READY`. The FULL tier formats
properly ("That's 2.5M followers and an 8.1M median"); the REACH tier prints the raw digits.

**831 messages carry both defects at once.**

**One cause for both.** `MEME_PAGES_BOT_READY.csv` has **no `display_name` column**, so
every row falls to `name_source=handle_at` and tier REACH (839 of 874 REACH). The defect is
in the *file's shape*, not in the copy — which is why the suites are green.

### AND A HARD BLOCKER — the TikTok file cannot render at all

`render()` raises `ValueError: lead_kind '' is not routable to a message` on **20 of 20**
tested. It also has **no `date_sent` and no `touch_number` column**, so even if it rendered,
the stamp could not land on it.

## 5. THE HONEST EXPECTATION, AND WHEN TO STOP

**Expect ~0.81% bounce, 95% interval [0.37, 1.76] — on the informative subset only.**
That figure is **inherited, not re-measured here**, and I am labelling it as such.

**Naive SMTP acceptance of 97.2% is meaningless** and must never be quoted as deliverability:
a catch-all domain accepts every address, including ones that do not exist.

**What I did re-measure, today, against BL-962's stored per-domain table (`scratch/bl962_catchall.json`,
551 domains, no mail sent):**

| | rows discriminating | catch-all | **never measured** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ALL_BOT_READY (3,978) | 84.2% | 7.5% | 8.3% |
| **MEME_PAGES (874)** | 52.5% | 1.4% | **46.1%** |
| TIKTOK (115) | 82.6% | 0.9% | 16.5% |
| **union (4,967)** | **78.6%** | **6.3%** | **15.2%** |

By distinct domain: of 1,286 domains in the live set, **55.1% have no catch-all measurement
at all.** So on the primary file roughly 8 in 10 bounces will be informative; **on the meme
file nearly half the rows sit on domains where silence means nothing.**

### STOP IMMEDIATELY IF
- **any batch of ≥200 shows more than 2.0% hard bounces** — that is above the measured
  interval's upper bound of 1.76% and into reputation damage;
- **any single batch shows ≥5%** — stop mid-batch, do not finish it;
- **two consecutive batches both exceed 1.76%** — the point estimate has moved, not noise;
- **any bounce arrives from a domain `suppress_mx.json` marked MX_OK today** — the table is
  wrong and every downstream suppression decision is suspect.

On a 500-address batch, expect ~4 bounces and treat 9 as the ceiling of normal.
