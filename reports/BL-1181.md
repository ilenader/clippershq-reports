## 2. RULE 1 — THE VIEW GATE. THE PINNED FLAG IS A LIE; POSITION IS NOT

Probed live first (`scratch/bl1181_probe.json`, 4 calls, $0.0024). The vendor **does** return
`timeline_pinned_user_ids` — and it is `[]` on every item, on every account. `is_pinned` is
absent entirely. **The flag is unusable.**

What is usable: Instagram serves pinned posts at the **head** of the response and the rest in
reverse-chronological order. So a post in the first three positions whose `taken_at` is older
than something behind it is pinned. It is falsifiable — on a page with no pins the API order
*is* date order and the rule returns nothing. **It fired on 38 of the 80 pages, 90 posts in
all.** Pinned posts are not an edge case.

### The page you said averages ~200 views

`21.prio` — delivered to you, and this is its actual feed:

| pos | date | plays | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 2023-01-24 | **27,113,227** | PINNED |
| 1 | 2022-12-29 | **15,984,906** | PINNED |
| 2 | 2023-01-03 | **6,177,064** | PINNED |
| 3–11 | 2026-05-07 → 05-16 | 350, 430, **231**, 532, **252**, 483, 699, 737, **300** | |

**446 average excluding pinned. 4,927,817 including them — an 11,049× inflation.** Three posts
from 2022–23 carrying 49.3M plays were being averaged with nine recent posts running 231–737.
Your "~200" is posts 5, 7 and 11. `blitzedbuddy` is the same shape: 38.85M on one pinned post,
162–2,478 on everything since.

Pinned posts are now excluded from the view average **and** from the format share — a pinned
post is by definition the page's best ever, so counting it in either asks the wrong question.
The exclusion happens before the latest-ten slice, or three pins eat three of the ten slots.

`judge_page` now reports the inflation rather than hiding it:

> `average 446 views over 9 non-pinned post(s) older than 48h, below the 2000 floor;`
> `3 pinned post(s) excluded, which alone read 4927817`

The 48h grace you asked for is kept: a post younger than that is excluded, not counted as a
zero, so ten posts in the last hour cannot kill a page. A photo post carries no `play_count` at
all and is excluded as unknown rather than counted as zero.

## 3. RULE 2 — MINIMUM POSTS, AND WHY YOUR OWN EXAMPLES CAP IT AT 13

You asked for a floor justified from your examples. **The justification is uncomfortable and
you should see it.** The 33 accounts behind your 37 example reels:

| | media_count |
|---|---|
| minimum | **0** — `v4mp3rit409`, which you picked a 940,603-play reel from and which now reports zero posts |
| next lowest | **13** — `edm_whale`, 11,513 followers |
| then | 32, 48, 57, 98, 99, 125, 174, 257 |
| p10 / median | 32 / 401 |

So **13 is the highest floor your own examples permit**, and it rejects your twelve-post page
by exactly one post. Anything higher cuts a page you chose yourself. `buzz_lightning_mcqueen`
— 12 posts, 113 followers — is the page you meant, and 13 removes it.

The floor sweep is in the report data; it climbs (12→25.0%, 100→33.3%, 200→41.7%) but only by
throwing away most of the supply, and above 13 it is no longer derived from anything you
showed me.

**`media_count` also undercounts**, measured on 3 of 80 pages: `_neinaramwa` reports 1 against
10 posts actually served, `_iydys._` 2 against 6, `brad.da.rad` 7 against 11. A floor on the
raw field would reject a page for having fewer posts than we are literally holding, so the
floor is applied to `max(media_count, posts served)`. All four still fall.

This rule costs a billed call: `media_count` exists only on the profile payload, and
`user_medias` caps its page at 12 items (probed — asked for 24, got 12). So the gate order
changed: **profile first**, and a page under the floor is rejected before it buys its post
list. The bio and follower count that call returns are the ones contact resolution was going
to fetch anyway, so a page that passes costs exactly what it used to.

## 4. RULE 5 — THE NICHE, AND WHY IT NEVER REJECTS

You said `casinodubs` is "correctly meme format but gambling", and asked for a niche per page
so you can exclude categories rather than have your taste hard-coded. So the niche is

Niches across all 78 judgeable pages: general 28, film/tv 18, relationship 5, music 5,
football 3, kids/family 3, animals 2, anime 2, gambling 2, basketball 2, and one each of
travel, gaming, religious, motivational, tech, food, cars, sports.

**Caveat, stated plainly: the niche labeller is the weakest thing in this round.** It is
keyword scoring, and it put `21.prio` in `animals`. Treat the column as a hint to sort by, not
a fact. It costs nothing and rejects nothing, so it is safe to ship in that state — but do not
build an exclusion list on it without spot-checking.

## 5. WHAT EACH RULE ACTUALLY REMOVES

Over the 62 delivered pages, every rule evaluated independently — `judge_page` no longer
short-circuits, because a first-to-fire design cannot answer this question at all (a page
killed by language *and* views counts once for language and zero for views).

| rule | removes | of them BAD | **GOOD lost** |
|---|---|---|---|
| RULE 3 format must dominate | **22** | 20 | 2 — `1bornfire1`, `britishmemes` |
| RULE 1 view gate, pinned excluded | **5** | 5 | 0 |
| RULE 2 minimum total posts | **4** | 4 | 0 |
| RULE 4 media / news pages | **3** | 2 | 1 — `1bornfire1` |
| RULE 6 film & TV promotion | **3** | 3 | 0 |
| RULE 7 English only | **0** | — | — |

**Format share does nearly all the work.** The view gate and the post floor are clean — nine
removals between them, not one of them a page you'd have wanted. Rule 7 removed **nothing**:
this population is not foreign-language, it is English pages with junk captions, and the four
Indic-script pages the previous round leaked were in a different sample.

### Two defects the labels exposed, both fixed

1. **`is_news_report` read only the drawn line**, so it could not fire on `799_media` — the
   page you *named* for that rule. Its drawn lines are ordinary ("What an incredible shot");
   what makes it a news page is that every caption is a `Facts: ●` bulletin. A rule that
   cannot fire on the example it was written from has not been implemented, only spelled.
2. **A frame word alone made a narration a hook.** "When a sold out stadium knew every word to
   a song they just made 3 hours ago" scored HOOK purely on "when", on a wholesome-reporting
   page. The frame now needs an anchor, a colon, or brevity — below the 69-character median of
   your 37 examples. All your own frame lines survive.

### One fix tried and measured back out — this is the important one

Treating `Facts:` / `Interesting fact:` / `Did you know` captions as reporting looked obvious
and made rule 4 fire on 7 pages. **Three of the seven were pages labelled GOOD**, and precision
dropped at every single threshold. The reason: `cartoon` (1.25M followers) and `brainfuel.meme`
open their captions with the *identical* boilerplate as `799_media` and `brainnourishment`. It
is algorithm-gaming caption spam common to both populations and **it does not separate them.**
Reverted, and recorded in the code so nobody re-adds it.

## 6. THE SWEEP — PRECISION, INTERVAL AND REJECTION COUNT AT EVERY THRESHOLD

**Base rate = the precision of what you actually received = 11/62 = 17.7% [10.2, 29.0].**
To ship, a threshold's lower bound must exceed **29.0**.

| t | passed | rejected | GOOD | precision | recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 47 | 15 | 10 | 21.3% [12.0, 34.9] | 91% |
| 0.15 | 44 | 18 | 10 | 22.7% [12.8, 37.0] | 91% |
| 0.25 | 38 | 24 | 10 | 26.3% [15.0, 42.0] | 91% |
| 0.35 | 31 | 31 | 9 | 29.0% [16.1, 46.6] | 82% |
| 0.45 | 26 | 36 | 8 | 30.8% [16.5, 50.0] | 73% |
| **0.55** | **16** | **46** | **6** | **37.5% [18.5, 61.4]** | 54% |
| **0.60** | **13** | **49** | **5** | **38.5% [17.7, 64.5]** | 46% |
| 0.65 | 7 | 55 | 2 | 28.6% [8.2, 64.1] | 18% |

| 0.90 | 2 | 60 | 1 | 50.0% [9.5, 90.5] | 9% |
| 0.95+ | 0 | 62 | 0 | nothing passed | 0% |

**No threshold clears.** The best lower bound anywhere in the sweep is 18.5 at t=0.55, against
a base-rate ceiling of 29.0. The point estimates rise — 17.7% → 38.5% is a real doubling — but
at t=0.60 that estimate rests on **13 pages**, and thirteen pages cannot distinguish a filter
from luck. The high-t rows read 40% and 50% on **five and two pages**; that is why the
rejection count sits beside every row.

This is the same shape as the previous three attempts and I am not going to dress it
differently: **the filter is probably better and cannot yet be shown to be better.**

## 7. WHAT I'D DO NEXT, IN ORDER

1. **Ship rules 1 and 2 now, on their own.** Nine removals, zero good pages lost, no threshold
   involved and nothing to calibrate. The view gate alone would have kept `21.prio`,
   `blitzedbuddy`, `__anonymous_memer_`, `aimemeguy` and `4.udear` out of your inbox.
2. **Do not ship a format-share threshold yet.** Set it to 0.35 as a demotion/sort key rather
   than a cut, so nothing is thrown away on a number that cannot be defended.
3. **The measurement needs ~200 labelled pages, not 80.** At n=13 passing, no interval will
   ever clear. The other live round found discovery runs at 27.5 new handles per billed call,
   so supply is not the constraint — labelling is, and OCR at ~26s/page is the clock.
4. **Two false negatives are worth your eye**: `_10.eren` and `celluloidrealm` were both
   rejected by the shipped filter and are both, I think, pages you'd want — English drawn hooks
   under Japanese caption spam. If you disagree with either, tell me and the standard changes.
5. **The caption is now actively adversarial.** Roughly a quarter of this sample carries
   copy-pasted spam captions (the same One Piece PV block, the same Vogue World paragraph)
   bearing no relation to the video. Judging the drawn line is not merely better, it is the
   only thing that still works.
