## The one thing to do before sending anything

**`send_identity.json` does not exist.** Only `send_identity.example.json` and
`send_identity.json.example` are on disk. Every message therefore refuses:

```
ValueError: operator terms not filled in: SENDER_NAME, RATE, TURNAROUND,
PAYMENT_TERMS, REPLY_TO. Copy send_identity.example.json to send_identity.json
and fill them, or pass allow_placeholders=True to preview the copy unsent.
```

That guard is working exactly as designed — it is BL-1091's fix, after 60 of 60 messages went
out carrying `[[SENDER_NAME]]`. **Nothing can be sent until that file is filled**, and no
amount of rebuilding changes it.

**But the send guard says the files are fine, and both statements are true.**
`clippershq/send_guard.py` reports:

```
ALL_BOT_READY.csv        rows 4270  sampled 60    OK
MEME_PAGES_BOT_READY.csv rows 874   sampled 60    OK
TIKTOK_PAGES_BOT_READY.csv rows 123 sampled 60    OK
0 of 3 file(s) NOT SENDABLE                                   exit 0
```

It passes because `_operator_for_checking()` hands `render()` a **stand-in operator**
(`SENDER_NAME="GUARD"`). That is a defensible design — the guard checks the *file*, not the
environment — but the consequence is worth knowing: **a green send guard does not mean he can
send.** It means the rows would render if an identity existed.

If whatever sends these uses the body as-is, the recipient sees `Subject: …` as the first line
of the email and the real subject is blank. Worth confirming against the sending tool before
the first batch.

**C. Several pages are being pitched the wrong offer.** The clipper pitch — *"ClippersHQ pays
editors to cut short-form"* — went to:

| page | followers | address domain | what it looks like |
|---|---:|---|---|
| an official artist account | 101k | the artist's own domain | an artist, i.e. a **client**, not an editor for hire |
| a record label | 16k | the label's own domain | a label, and an `info@` role inbox |
| two very large meme pages | 1.9M, 2.2M | gmail | pages that size do not take editing work |

**D. Greetings often use the handle as if it were a name** — *"Hi thenadyamoore,"*, *"Hi
mustbemeek,"*, *"Hi vfx.edwinn,"*, *"Hi sunaevii,"*. Milder than `Hi @handle,` but still not a
person's name. Note the logic is principled: `@juniat35` correctly fell back to *"Hi there,"*

## 3. The abort threshold

**The baseline is 0.81% [0.37, 1.76]** (measured). The rule is computed against **1.76%**, the
interval's upper bound, so it does not fire on an ordinary bad day.

| batch | expected bounces | **WARN at** | **STOP at** |
|---:|---:|---|---|
| 25 | 0.2 | 3 (12.0%) | **3 (12.0%)** |
| 50 | 0.4 | 4 (8.0%) | **5 (10.0%)** |
| 100 | 0.8 | 5 (5.0%) | **6 (6.0%)** |
| 200 | 1.6 | 8 (4.0%) | **9 (4.5%)** |
| 300 | 2.4 | 10 (3.3%) | **12 (4.0%)** |
| 500 | 4.1 | 15 (3.0%) | **17 (3.4%)** |
| 1,000 | 8.1 | 26 (2.6%) | **29 (2.9%)** |

A STOP count would occur by chance on a healthy list **less than 1% of the time**. WARN is the
5% tail. **Above all of it, a hard ceiling of 2% sustained across a day means stop regardless**
— that is the level at which mailbox providers begin to act, and the damage is not recoverable.

**Why the SMTP figure cannot be used instead.** Naive SMTP acceptance is 97.2% and it is
meaningless: a catch-all domain accepts every address, including invented ones. **44% of
domains and 9.5% of rows are catch-all, and 46.1% of the meme file sits on domains never
catch-all tested.** Acceptance is not deliverability. The only true signal is what bounces
after sending, which is why the rule above is expressed in bounces and checked per batch.

**Practical form:** send the first batch at **50**. If 5 bounce, stop. If fewer than 4, go to
100, then 200. Do not go past 200 in a day until a full batch has come in under WARN.
