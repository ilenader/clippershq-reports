## 2. Hand-read verdicts, 20 real rows per funnel

**GOOGLE PLAY — junk for this purpose, and worse than the table suggests.**
20 of 20 were app-company inboxes: `su..@miniclip.com#9a90e7`, `su..@openai.com#8cdaab`,
`of…@microsoft.com`, `gp..@supercell.com#5412e3`, `su..@x.ai#3bcb00`,
`pf..@abcfinancial.com#782f5c`. Nobody selling clip editing wants to email
`su..@openai.com#8cdaab`. This confirms the earlier Microsoft/Supercell verdict and extends it —
it is not a few bad rows, it is the whole funnel.

**And my own item-1 table overstates it.** `role_policy` called 58 of these "personal"
because it keys on **domain type**, not on the local part — so a company domain that is not a
known free/role provider reads as personal:

```

fr…@garena.com           an..@ubisoft.com#5eaa70
ca..@king.com#96cd0d     wo…@azurgames.com
```

**77 of 131 GP addresses (59%) have an explicit role local part** (`support`, `help`, `info`,
`contact`, `gp-info`, `…support`). Hand-reading puts the genuinely personal count at roughly
2 in 20. **This is a live defect in `role_policy.quality()`, not just a Google Play problem** —
any funnel that surfaces company-domain addresses will be scored too generously.

**KICK — highest personal rate (94.6%), real streamers**: `re…@hotmail.com`,

**IG_CRAWL — highest-quality volume-per-row.** 82% carry an email, all `EDITOR` verdict, real

**TT_HASHTAG — real editors, but 95% unreachable by email** and the same bios recur:

### One thing I have to raise, because it changes how the ranking should be read

The two largest funnels — `tt_hashtag` (54,524) and `ig_crawl`/`ig_hashtag` — find **video
editors who advertise that they sell editing**. If the business sells clip-editing services,
these are peers or competitors, not buyers. Twitch, Kick and Spotify find people who *need*
clips cut. I have not assumed which model is right, and I am not treating it as a defect —
but "best leads" resolves very differently depending on the answer, and nothing in master
records that intent. **Worth settling before optimising the mix.**

## Limits

- **No outcome data exists**, so "quality" here is contact-shape, not response. A 58% personal
  rate is not evidence anyone replies. Until `date_sent` is populated this ranking cannot be
  validated, and I would not spend on the strength of it alone.
- **Two of the three largest funnels are unpriced** because their rows predate the spend
  ledger. The ranking is therefore complete only for Twitch, Spotify, Kick, email_finder and
  repost_finder.
- **Hand-reading was 20 rows per funnel** — enough to catch a systematic pattern like Google
  Play's, not enough for a precise rate. The GP correction (52 → 2) is extrapolated from
  20 rows and should be treated as directional.
- `role_policy.quality()` over-counts company-domain addresses as personal. Every "personal"
  figure in item 1 inherits that bias; it bites hardest on Google Play and is negligible on
  Twitch/Kick, where addresses are mostly free-provider.
- The editor-vs-buyer question in item 2 is unresolved and materially affects which funnel is
  "best". It is a business question, not a data one.

## Method

All figures come from a single streaming read of `master_leads.csv` (57,386 rows, 62 columns,
mtime 2026-07-30 17:01, unmodified by this round). Funnel attribution used `source_hashtag`
with `platform` as fallback for legacy bare-hashtag rows. Email classification used the
repo's own `role_policy`, with its limits measured and reported above rather than assumed.
`crossdedup --report` was verified read-only before running. Spend came from `spend.json`.
No credential was read or printed, no paid call was made, and no file was written.
