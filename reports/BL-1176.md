# BL-1176 — the filter was worth nothing, the merge had no caller, and 17% is two populations

**The meme-page filter, measured against 60 hand labels made from real caption text, scored
25.0% precision [8.9, 53.2] against a base rate of 26.5% [16.2, 40.3] — 1.5 points BELOW
selecting every page.** It is now 85.7% [48.7, 97.4], rejecting 42 of 49 judgeable pages
instead of 37. That is the round's first number.

The second is that **300 emails was never reachable for $5.00 from hashtag discovery**, and
the reason is not the filter: the 17.0% email rate was measured on the 288 curated repost
accounts, and hashtag discovery returns a different population. Measured this round on the
funnel's own output: **1 of 28** resolved hashtag pages carried an email, 3.6% [1.0, 17.6].
The same instrument on known repost accounts returned **16.2%**. Two populations, one number,
and the plan was sized on the wrong one.

Every figure here came from **`.venv\Scripts\python.exe`, Python 3.11.9**. Bare `python` is
3.13.14 and lacks the packages; it was used only for the stdlib-only claim readers.

## 1. The filter — hand-labelled first, then measured

**The standard was written and frozen BEFORE any page was read**
(`scratch/bl1176_labelling_standard.md`), because a standard written after seeing the data
describes the data instead of testing the filter. One amendment (A1) was added at page #43 and
applied to all: a page with fewer than three judgeable captions is UNJUDGEABLE.

**A1 removed a filter failure from the precision number, so it is reported separately rather
than allowed to help quietly.** Page `kaifff_009__` was passed by the old filter at
`meme_share=1.00` on a sample of ONE caption. Excluding it flatters the filter; the underlying
defect — no minimum sample size — is a finding in its own right and is now fixed.

60 pages pulled, 545 non-empty captions of 558, **$0.0378 / 63 billed attempts**.

| | pages |
|---|---:|
| pulled | 60 |
| UNJUDGEABLE (9 with no caption, 2 with one) | 11 |
| **judgeable** | **49** |
| hand-labelled GOOD | 13 |
| **base rate** | **26.5% [16.2, 40.3]** |

### The result

| filter | passed | rejected | TP | FP | precision | recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| OLD, as shipped (t=0.15) | 12 | 37 | 3 | 9 | **25.0% [8.9, 53.2]** | 23.1% |
| top-line fix only | 39 | 10 | 13 | 26 | 33.3% [20.6, 49.0] | 100.0% |
| **+ grammar gate (shipped)** | **7** | **42** | **6** | **1** | **85.7% [48.7, 97.4]** | 46.2% |
| pass everything | 49 | 0 | 13 | 36 | 26.5% [16.2, 40.3] | 100.0% |

The full sweep at every threshold, with rejection counts, is in `scratch/bl1176_score.json`.
**t=0.05 is the only threshold whose lower bound (48.7) clears the base rate's upper bound
(40.3).** t=0.10 reads 100.0% and is two pages: an interval of [34.2, 100.0] that contains the
base rate is not evidence. t=0.00 rejects nothing and scores exactly the base rate — the trap
this round was warned about, reproduced here for the record.

### Why it was worth nothing, in three mechanisms

1. **`TOO-LONG` in the denominator.** `judge_page` fed the WHOLE caption to
   `meme_format.classify`. `genzepedia` posts "POV: Your ex is a brand. Which one are they?"
   and scored **0.00** — ten of its twelve captions classified TOO-LONG because the hook is
   followed by more text.
2. **A follow-CTA poisoned a real meme page.** All twelve `gym_fit_vibes` captions are hooks
   ("He started wearing dark baggy hoodies… He started his villain arc.") and all twelve
   classified PROMOTIONAL, because each ends "💪 Follow @gym_fit_vibes for more". Score: 0.00.
3. **`classify` is a short-line detector on captions, not a hook detector.** It returns
   MEME-HOOK for `"GSM = fabric weight."`, `"Sateen vs Percale."` and
   `"Happy Thursday from Kobe!"`. It was measured at 100% precision on 60 CLIPS, where the
   population is already memes and it never had to tell a hook from any other short sentence.

Also found: `judge_page` called `classify(caption=…)` with **no `headline_fn`**, while
`clip_pipeline` passes `edit.caption_headline`. `meme_format._headline`'s fallback "only ever
makes this module STRICTER" — and that fallback was in force here.

### What changed

`hook_line()` takes the TOP TEXT — the operator's own criterion — after dropping furniture
lines (tag blocks, follow-CTAs, credit lines, dot padding). A **page-level promo veto** rejects
outright at ≥15% advertising captions: `noare.inc` is a bedsheet brand whose caption six reads
"Summer SALE is live both on website and amazon. Link in Bio." and it passed at 0.33, because
the old sieve only ever demoted the single caption it fired on. A **minimum sample** of 3.
And a **hook-grammar gate** derived from the operator's two examples, not fitted to the 60 —
the same discipline `meme_format.HOOK_MAX_CHARS` records for itself.

Order is load-bearing and getting it wrong cost seven tests: PROMOTIONAL must be reached before
the grammar gate can demote the same caption to a generic OTHER, or "use code MEME10" counts
as zero adverts on a page made of them.

**The one surviving false positive** is `ilove_geun6`, a Korean fitness journal — sincere and
emotional, which the definition allows, but long-form rather than hooks. A genuine borderline.

## 2. The merge — and the caller that did not exist

`crossdedup.reconcile` merged the provenance tag and dropped the email, exactly as briefed.
`fill_missing_email` now carries it, under three refusals: never overwrite an existing address;
never adopt one that already identifies somebody else (that would put one address on two master

**But fixing it was not enough, and that is the more important half.** `meme_finder.run_funnel`
**never wrote master at all** — it wrote an export, a seen-set and a checkpoint and stopped. A
correct helper with no call site measures as zero. `_append_to_master` is new.

Proven on a copy of the real 61,051-row master (`scratch/bl1176_merge_proof.json`):

| | OLD | NEW |
|---|---:|---:|
| emails written to master | **0** | **25** |
| appended / merged | 0 / 25 | 0 / 25 |

publishes most often. The address still reaches the export; it does not reach master by this

## 3. The three instrument failures

**The wrapper — clean.** 545 non-empty captions of 558. `_media_of` already unwraps
`{"media": {…}}`; it was reused, not reimplemented.

**The username-for-id swap — never fired.** 72 of 72 fresh candidates carried a numeric
`user_id`. Worth noting anyway: `run_funnel` line 589 does `uid = rec.get("user_id") or h`,
which silently falls back to the handle and counts the empty result as an error.

**The quadratic regex — THE BRIEF'S PREMISE WAS WRONG, and it mattered.** "Both live paths are
capped but the regex is not" — there was **no cap anywhere in `bio_parser`**. Measured:

| n | seconds | vs half |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2,000 | 0.0034 | — |
| 32,000 | 0.8697 | 4.06x |

~4.00x per doubling over five doublings, and **`parse_bio` on a 250,000-character input took
57.46 SECONDS**, holding the GIL. This funnel is a new caller. `MAX_BIO_CHARS = 10_000` now
bounds all three extractors: **57.46s → 0.087s**. An Instagram bio is 150 characters, so the
bound is 66x the real maximum and cannot alter a real result.

## 4. What I broke, and how it was caught

**I overwrote BL-1175's export.** `_write_export` writes only what the process holds in memory
and the filename is date-stamped, so the verification run replaced a 71-row file with its own
40 rows. My checkpoint-union was already written to prevent exactly this and **did not fire**:
the row is nested under `payload` and I read `rec.get("handle")` on the envelope, so it matched
nothing, silently.

Caught by checking the file immediately after the run rather than trusting the code. Nothing
was lost — the checkpoint is the durable record — and rebuilding from it produced **226 pages,
not 71**: BL-1175's own export had the same defect and had already stranded 115 pages it had
paid for.

## 5. The run — 41 emails, not 300, and the reason is a population not a budget

**$0.2934 of $5.00 was spent. The remaining $4.71 was NOT spent, because the measurement says
it would not buy the target and the round would rather say so than prove it slowly.**

| leg | attempts | $ | result |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| caption pull for labelling | 63 | 0.0378 | 60 pages, 545 captions |
| 10-page verification | 50 | 0.0300 | 40 judged, verdicts real |
| **known repost accounts** | **287** | **0.1722** | **41 emails, 14.3% [10.7, 18.8]** |
| hashtag discovery | 89 | 0.0534 | 77 judged, 4 passed, **0 emails** |
| **total** | **489** | **$0.2934** | **41 emails** |

### Why 300 was not reachable

The plan sized 300 emails at ~1,765 pages resolved on a **17.0%** rate. That rate was measured
on the 288 curated repost accounts. Two things break the extrapolation:

1. **The population is exhausted.** There are 288 known repost accounts and this round
   resolved **287** of them. There is no 1,765 to draw from at 14.3%; the whole pool yields
   ~41, which is what it yielded.
2. **The other population does not carry emails.** Hashtag-discovered pages resolved at
   **1 of 32 — 3.1%**, against 14.3% for curated pages. #dailymemes returns small personal
   accounts with no business address.

And hashtag discovery is **supply-capped as well as low-yield**: 8 hashtags × 50 accounts
returned **133 distinct accounts**, 56 already seen. The run stopped at 89 of its 500 allowed
attempts because it ran out of fresh candidates, not budget.

> **So the binding constraint is not the rate limiter and not the $5.00. It is the number of
> curated meme pages in existence in this system.** Reaching 300 means running
> `repost_finder` to grow that population first; resolving harder cannot do it.

### Where the 41 went

`41 offered -> 7 appended, 34 merged by identity`, master 61,051 -> 61,058 rows.

**30 of the 250 handle-only `repost_finder` rows now carry an email, from 0.** That is the
merge fix delivering in production, and it is the number this round exists for.

### The send list did NOT grow, and that is the next blocker

3,947 before, **3,947 after** — computed by rebuilding from the pre-append master backup, not
from a remembered figure.

All 38 emailable `meme_pages` rows are cut at `send_list.py:339`, the **verdict gate**: a
`lead_kind=clipper` row must carry a `BOT_READY` verdict, and this funnel produces none. They
join **1,249 already-paid emailable clipper leads** cut for the same reason
(`LAST_BUILD_STATS['cut_verdict'] = 1249`).

**No verdict was invented to move that number.** A verdict is a quality judgement about an
account and writing one this round did not measure would be exactly the fabrication this
project keeps refusing. The addresses are in master and in the export; making them mailable is
a product decision about whether a meme page is a `clipper` at all.
