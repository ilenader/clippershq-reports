# BL-731 — Source-footage ID from a frame: the model never declines, is wrong 20% of the time, and the free route is licence-blocked

**Date:** 2026-07-30 · **Type:** Measurement, read-only on pipeline code, output in `scratch/`
**Spend:** **$0.0006** (one `/v2/user/clips` call to widen ground truth, metered as
`probe:filmid_groundtruth`). Gemini ran on the free tier at **$0.00**. Budget was $0.10.

---

## Verdict first

**Leave the field null.** A vision model reading one thumbnail got the title right 8 times in
10 — and got it *confidently wrong* the other 2, while **declining zero times out of ten**,
with an explicit decline option in the schema and a prompt that told it declining was the
correct answer when unsure.

A 20% wrong-answer rate is far above the "few percent" threshold the brief set. And because
the model never declines, there is no subset you can keep: the wrong answers are not flagged,
not low-confidence, and not separable from the right ones.

The free route — Whisper dialogue matched against subtitles — is **not blocked on accuracy.
It is blocked on licensing**, before any matching question arises.

---

## 1. The pilot

**Ground truth is free and exact.** Templated accounts print `Title: <name>` in their own
caption (BL-694), so the account itself says what the clip is. 21 clips with a declared Title
and a downloaded thumbnail: 9 from the three saved gql envelopes, 12 more from one paid
`/v2/user/clips` page. One was discarded because the account itself declined
(`(Unknown / Short Clip Scene)`), leaving **20 gradable**.

`gemini-2.5-flash` is retired (404). Used **`gemini-3-flash-preview`**, structured output,
`temperature 0.0`, one 640x1136 thumbnail per request, schema:

```
identified : yes | no          <- a real decline path
title      : string ('unknown' when declining)
media_type : film | tv_series | unknown
confidence : high | medium | low
evidence   : what was recognised
```

The prompt closed with: *"DECLINING IS THE CORRECT ANSWER WHEN YOU ARE NOT SURE. A wrong
title is far worse than 'unknown' — it silently corrupts a database. Do not guess from genre,
mood, film stock, or a vague resemblance."*

15 sent, **10 usable responses**, 5 lost to the free tier (2 `ReadTimeout` at 90 s, 3
`UNAVAILABLE`). Median latency **23.3 s** per thumbnail.

## 2. Result — reported separately, never blended

| outcome | n | share |
|---|---|---|
| **correct** | 8 | **80%** |
| **confidently wrong** | 2 | **20%** |
| **declined** | **0** | **0%** |

### The two wrong answers, in full

| declared truth | model said | confidence | its stated evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1923** | **Beast** | **high** | "the CGI lions and the night-time setting from the film's climax" |
| **House of the Dragon** | **The Last Kingdom: Seven Kings Must Die** | medium | "Characters Aethelstan and King Constantin in battle attire" |

The second is the BL-719 houseplant pattern exactly: it did not merely pick a wrong title, it
**invented two specific character names** as corroboration. The first is worse in a different
way — *high* confidence, and a plausible-sounding visual justification ("CGI lions") for a
Taylor Sheridan period western.

### Confidence does not separate right from wrong

| | high | medium |
|---|---|---|
| correct (8) | 4 | 4 |
| wrong (2) | 1 | 1 |

**A confidence gate buys nothing.** Filtering to `high` only would keep 5 answers, one of
which is the "1923 → Beast" error — a 20% error rate, unchanged.

### The decline path was never used

This is the finding that settles it. The option existed, was explicitly described, and was
never taken in 10 attempts. The model has no working sense of its own ignorance on this task,
so there is no threshold, no gate and no prompt tweak that converts it into a safe field. It
will always answer.

## 3. Cost, if it did work

Measured from `usageMetadata`, median per thumbnail: **1,290 prompt tokens, 783 output,
2,073 total.**

At Gemini 3 Flash Preview's current direct rate ($0.25/M input, $1.50/M output):

| | per 1,000 clips |
|---|---|
| input 1.29 M tokens | $0.32 |
| output 0.78 M tokens | $1.17 |
| **total** | **~$1.50** |

Against the **$0.88/1,000 measured for audio**, vision is **~1.7x more expensive** — for a
field that cannot be trusted. Wall-clock is also poor: 23.3 s median per thumbnail.

**Free-tier note:** 20 requests/model/day, and a third of this run failed on it. Any real
pilot needs paid quota, which changes the arithmetic from $0 to the figures above.

## 4. The free route — blocked on licence, not accuracy

BL-682 measured Whisper as "gist-correct, entity-wrong" (`"Zoro is your undogsus"`). The
question was whether a garbled line could still be fuzzy-matched against a subtitle database.

**It cannot be done commercially.** OpenSubtitles' own legal page states its subtitle
documents "may not be freely distributed and used for **non-commercial**, scientific and
educational purposes" and asserts copyright protection over commercial use. The OpenSubtitles
REST API is the only large-scale subtitle search that exists.

The obvious fallback — the ~1.5 M subtitle, 62-language research corpus used for dialogue
model pre-training — is **derived from the same source** and carries the same non-commercial
research restriction. No CC0 or CC BY subtitle corpus of film/TV dialogue surfaced.

This is the third time this pattern has appeared: BL-678 found MusicBrainz genre associations
CC BY-NC-SA and Last.fm/Deezer explicitly non-commercial. **The free metadata for exactly the
fields this library wants is consistently non-commercial.**

So the fuzzy-match question is moot, and was never reached. For the record, the prior on it
was poor anyway: matching needs a distinctive multi-word span, and BL-682 showed the entity
words — the distinctive ones — are precisely what Whisper `tiny` corrupts.

## 5. Verdict

**Cheapest route to a film/TV title on non-templated clips: there isn't one worth taking.**

| route | hit rate | wrong-answer rate | cost / 1,000 | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| caption template (`Title:`) | 100% where present | 0% | $0 | **use it** — but only ~37.5% of pages |
| Gemini vision on thumbnail | 80% | **20%, never declines** | ~$1.50 | **do not ship** |
| Whisper + subtitle search | untested | untested | n/a | **licence-blocked** |

**Recommendation: leave `title` null on non-templated clips.** A wrong title is not a
degraded field, it is an actively poisonous one — it propagates into genre, tone, era and cast,
which is the whole reason the brief wanted it. Two of ten library rows silently claiming the
wrong show would corrupt every downstream decision keyed on them, and nothing in the output
marks which two.

**What to do instead:** BL-703's `content_genre` already gets 91.5% genre and 59% franchise
from the discovery hashtag, free, and genre is what BL-703 established a clip library actually
indexes on. That is the field to lean on. Title is a nice-to-have that is currently
unobtainable safely.

## Limits — read before quoting the 80%

- **n = 10 usable responses.** The 80/20 split has a 95% CI of roughly 49–94% / 6–51%. The
  *direction* is clear and the zero-decline result is unambiguous, but the point estimates are
  soft.
- **All ground truth is one account** (`clipcapture.tv`), which posts mainstream US film and
  TV — the *easiest* possible case for a vision model. On anime, foreign-language or obscure
  footage the hit rate should be expected to fall and, given zero declining, **the wrong-answer
  rate to rise correspondingly.** This is a best case, not an average.
- **One frame per clip.** A thumbnail is Instagram's chosen cover frame. Sampling several
  frames would likely help the hit rate; it would not fix the refusal-to-decline, which is the
  blocking problem.
- 5 of 15 requests failed on the free tier and are excluded; if failures correlate with
  image content the surviving 10 are not a random sample.
- **No prompt-variant sweep was run.** It is possible a different phrasing induces declining.
  Given the model ignored an explicit, emphatic instruction to decline, I would not expect much
  — but it is untested.
- Pricing is the published direct rate as of this measurement; third-party providers were
  quoted at up to 2x.
