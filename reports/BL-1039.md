## 1. THE ONE NUMBER: 4 of 21 hooks can EVER place, and it is arithmetic

Two constants that were never considered together make most of the hook library ineligible:

* `song_library.place_at_detail` refuses when `clip_len < 2.0 × hook_len`
* `duration.CEILING_S = 20.0` trims every finished video to at most 20 s

Therefore a hook longer than **10.0 s can never place on any video this pipeline will ever
produce** — not on a long clip, because none survives the ceiling; not on a short one,
because of the doubling rule.

```
  hooks marked          21   (9.24 s to 26.93 s, median 16.79 s)
  hook_len <= 10.0 s     4   <- 9.24, 9.61, 9.62, 9.81
  the other 17 can never place, at ANY clip length

  clips >= 2 x shortest hook (18.48 s) : 14 of 28
  (clip, hook) pairs that can place    : 50 of 588  (8.5%)
  clips with at least ONE placeable hook: 14 of 28
```

After re-marking hooks to ~4-second cores: **28 of 28** clips can place (a 4.0 s core needs
8.0 s of clip, which is exactly `FLOOR_S`, so it is the largest core that fits a
floor-length video). At 5.0 s cores: 23 of 28. At 6.0 s: 19 of 28.

## 2. Re-derived independently, and it caught my own error first

Restating MEMEBOT-116's table would make the decision single-sourced, so
`scratch/bl1039_before.py` scores the 28 labels again from the labels and `song_library`'s
own functions, then diffs. First run: **3 of 6 cells disagreed** (6.40 vs 5.70, 7.00 vs 6.00,
3.60 vs 3.47) while **every within-2s count agreed exactly**.

That signature — counts agree, medians move — is a summary-statistic bug, not a data
disagreement. `errs[n // 2]` is the **upper-middle** value for even n, not the median.
`statistics.median` reproduces MEMEBOT-116 exactly. **Their number was right and mine was
wrong; now 6 of 6 agree.**

## 3. The applied-placement instrument, built against its three known traps

`scratch/bl1039_corr.py` measures whether a placement actually landed, by envelope
cross-correlation against the **applied** window.

* **The zero-plan tautology.** MEMEBOT-116 scored 5 rows "APPLIED" whose plan was `0.00`; a
  plan of zero matches a lag of zero for free. Worse — **`place_at_detail` returns `0.0` when
  it REFUSES**, so the rows most likely to score a spurious success are exactly the ones
  where placement was impossible. `score()` counts only non-zero plans and reports what it
  excluded.
* **The marked-vs-applied window.** `fit_window` widens the marked hook; correlating against
  the marked window once read 11 of 20 videos as missing their song when the truth was 20 of
  20. This uses `applied_start_sec`/`applied_end_sec`.
* **A peak is not a match.** Verified against a measured null distribution rather than a
  guessed threshold — see below.

```
  planted 0.00 s -> measured 0.00 s   peak 17.57   PASS
  planted 3.00 s -> measured 3.00 s   peak  8.70   PASS
  planted 5.00 s -> measured 5.00 s   peak  7.97   PASS
  planted 7.50 s -> measured 7.50 s   peak  7.28   PASS
  unrelated audio -> peak 2.27, below the floor    PASS (must not clear it)

  tautology guard: 7 rows in -> 5 excluded (zero plan), 1 excluded (noise), 1 scored, 1 applied
```

## 4. What the next round needs, so the wiring is one measured step

`scratch/memebot024_hookproof.py` already measured the working ffmpeg form, and it is **not**
one line:

> `-stream_loop -1` and `aloop` are **mutually exclusive**. With an infinitely looped input,
> `atrim` emits its window then consumes and discards upstream forever: the graph never
> reaches EOF, `-shortest` never fires, and ffmpeg hangs. `edit.py` sets `-stream_loop -1`
> unconditionally today.

The form that works: no `-stream_loop`, no input-side `-ss`/`-t`, the window in the filter via
`hook_chain`, and an **output-side `-t` equal to the video duration** (because `-shortest`
does not bound a filter-generated infinite stream either).
