**Date:** 2026-07-30 · **Type:** Retention + audit + fix · **Spend:** **$0.0000** (three live runs; every paid surface was covered by dedup or free paths)

Follow-up to [BL-726](BL-726.md), which measured all of this and could not audit any of it because `tw.run` discarded the rows.

**Ownership gate:** no funnel process; newest repo write 27.6 min old at start. Backups `backups/{config.json,spend.json,master_leads.csv}.bak_20260730_105352`.

## The headline

Retention was supposed to be plumbing. It immediately falsified the filter it was built to audit.

| | |
|---|---|
| adult keyword drops in the audited run | **6** |
| of those, matched `nsfw` | **5** |
| of those, chat rules *prohibiting* NSFW | **4 — false positives** |
| genuinely adult keyword drops | **1** (`findom`, self-described) |

`nsfw` was in `ADULT_KEYWORDS_STRONG` — a standalone trigger. It is now a corroborator. **This is the third time this exact failure has been found in this filter** (`18+` and `adult only` were demoted on 2026-07-28 for the same reason), and the first time it was caught by evidence rather than by someone remembering.

Suites **50/50, 2,235 checks**. Campaigns SHA **`8e02f8d6f6307ae8`** unchanged.

## 1. Dropped rows are retained, with the signal AND the text it matched

`stats["dropped"]` and `stats["quarantined"]`, deliberately separate from `results` — they never reach the sheet or the master. Each dropped row carries login, reason, signal, category, title, viewers, followers, the quarantine lists, and **`matched_context`**: up to 60 characters either side of the match, tagged with which surface it came from.

That last field is the one that mattered. `keyword:nsfw` on its own is not reviewable — the token reads identically in "come see my nsfw content" and in "no nsfw comments". The surrounding sentence is the entire difference between an auditable filter and a number, and BL-726 had only the number.

`find_contact` was already recording `adult_signal`, `quarantined_handles` and `quarantined_sites`; only the caller's view was missing. Three fields (`category`, `title`, `display_name`) were added to the dropped row because the filter matches against them and you cannot review a match without seeing what it matched in.

An earlier run of the same categories caught a sixth: @linny and @da_rach3l on `paid-content:fansly.com` and `aggregator:isSensitive`, both **REAL**.

**Four unambiguous false positives.** Three of the four are grammatically *negative* — the word appears next to "no", "not permission", "NOT allowed". A streamer stating a boundary was being classified as selling the thing they were prohibiting.

### The fix, and its bound

`nsfw` moved from `ADULT_KEYWORDS_STRONG` to `ADULT_KEYWORDS_AMBIGUOUS` — a corroborator that never drops alone but still counts when an adult domain, adult category, or another strong keyword is present. Verified:

```
keep   No spam, creeping, overly nsfw comments
keep   This is not permission to post NSFW content
keep   NSFW topics are NOT allowed
keep   NO serious NSFW comments about Shin
DROP   keyword:findom     I started findom on twitch
DROP   keyword:onlyfans   come see my onlyfans
DROP   domain:onlyfans.com    (nsfw title + an OF link — corroborated)
```

All four false-positive strings are pinned verbatim in `tests/test_twitch_audit_trail.py`, so this cannot regress a fourth time.

**On the keywords the brief named:** `spicy`, `sexy`, `hot tub`, `thicc` **did not fire once** across three live runs. They are unproven, not exonerated — no evidence either way, and they were left alone rather than demoted on suspicion.

## 4. The website scrape — 59% of fetches removed, provably losslessly

BL-726 measured 682 fetches for 86 channels and assumed depth on real sites. **It is not that.** Instrumenting every fetch (host, path, bytes, seconds, emails-yielded) on a 430-fetch run:

| surface | fetches | with an email | yield | seconds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| x.com / twitter.com | 140 | 4 | 2.9% | 260.6 |
| real websites | 120 | 4 | 3.3% | 128.0 |
| **twitch.tv (+subdomains)** | **118** | **0** | **0.0%** | 115.0 |
| aggregators (linktr.ee) | 33 | 12 | **36.4%** | 78.6 |
| **facebook.com** | **19** | **0** | **0.0%** | 33.6 |

Broken down by path, the cause is unambiguous: **121 of the 140 x.com fetches and 105 of the 118 twitch.tv fetches were the contact-path list** — `x.com/contact`, `x.com/imprint`, `twitch.tv/booking`, `twitch.tv/legal`. The scraper was walking Twitter's and Twitch's own corporate pages looking for a streamer's email. Zero returned one.

Meanwhile all 4 x.com emails came from **profile** pages (`/Malek04Official`, `/Raulmesajr`, `/Alinity`, `/notqiya`) — a different surface entirely.

### The fix, and the mistake I made first

`_targets()` now returns **just the page itself** for a handle surface, never the contact-path list. The page is still fetched (that is where the 4 emails were); the 10 corporate legal pages are not.

**My first attempt guarded `site_contacts` only, and the live re-run still showed 70 contact-path walks on twitch.tv.** `enrich_link`'s hop-2 reaches `_targets` directly and bypassed the guard. `_targets` is the single chokepoint that generates a contact-path walk, so the rule belongs there — one rule, one place. A test pins that hop-2 path specifically.

### Losslessness, proven deterministically rather than by A/B

The live A/B was confounded: the second run delivered 96 results against 64 (Twitch supply moves minute to minute), so wall time was not comparable and I am not going to claim it was. Instead the before-run's complete fetch log was replayed through the new code:

```
before-run fetches      : 430
now eliminated          : 254 (59%)
fetch-seconds saved     : 400.5 of 615.7 (65%)

email-yielding fetches  : 20
of those eliminated     : 0        <-- LOSSLESS
```

**Every one of the 20 fetches that produced an email still happens.** Eliminated by host: x.com 80, www.twitch.tv 69, twitter.com 41, twitch.tv 26, facebook 17, safety.twitch.tv 10 — **zero emails between them.**

The final run surfaced two more: `yt3.googleusercontent.com` (23 fetches) and `lh7-us.googleusercontent.com` (10), Google's avatar CDNs, being contact-path-walked because `gstatic`/`googleapis` did not match them. Added to the junk list.

**Page-size cap:** already in place from BL-715 and working — median fetch 22.9 KB, max exactly 250,000, i.e. the cap truncating. **Distinct-host cap:** added as `MAX_SITE_HOSTS_PER_LEAD = 3`, but reported honestly as a **safety valve that does not bind** — 27 distinct hosts across 40 channels. The explosion was paths on the wrong hosts, not hosts.

## 5. The viewer floor — and a premise that had already changed

**`min_viewers` was 10, not 50.** BL-726 reported it as 50 and awaiting a decision; the backups show it moved 50 → 10 between `config.json.20260729_221639.bak` and `config.json.20260730_101628.bak` — another agent applied BL-726's most aggressive option.

So setting it to **20 as instructed is a raise, not a lower**, and by BL-726's own table it *reduces* combined in-band supply from ~673 to ~482. The instruction named 20 explicitly and 20 is BL-726's stated conservative recommendation, so 20 is what is set — but if the goal was more supply, 10 already delivered it. **Flagged for a decision; trivially revertible.**

**Delivered at floor 20, live:** 120 channels considered → **89 delivered results, 31 with an email**. `max_viewers` unchanged at 1,000.

One thing that got worse and I cannot yet explain: **timeouts rose from 3 to 23** in the floor-20 run. That run also took 759 s against 156 s. The lower-viewer population may include slower/thinner sites, or it may be network variance on a single run — one sample cannot separate those. It is not caused by the scrape fix (fetch count fell in the same run). Worth one more run before trusting the floor-20 timing.

## Limits

- **`spicy` / `sexy` / `hot tub` / `thicc` never fired.** Unproven, not cleared.
- **@onelittlepiranha is a judgement call.** *"I could talk about 18+ topics that are NSFW"* is a content warning from a talk streamer, not a seller — counted as a false positive, but a stricter operator could disagree. It now passes.
- **`is_sponsor_url` rejected nothing** in three runs, so it is neither vindicated nor implicated.
- **The wall-time A/B is not usable.** Live supply moved between runs; the 59%/65% reductions are deterministic replay, which is the stronger claim, but the end-to-end second-count is not.
- **The timeout jump (3 → 23) is unexplained** and rests on one run.
- **Floor 20 vs 10 is a supply decision, not a quality one** — BL-726's liveness proxies were indistinguishable down to 10, and buyer fit at either floor remains untested.
- Three runs, one time of day. Twitch supply is time-dependent.
