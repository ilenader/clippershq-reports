# BL-708 — What sounds go on each franchise: the curated map fails, and the 51% concentration that motivated it was an artifact

## THE DECIDING NUMBERS, FIRST: across 348 licensed clips there are **263 distinct artists** and **284 distinct tracks**. **83% of artists and 87% of tracks appear exactly once.** The top 50 artists cover **38.8%**, not 80% — it takes **194 artists** to reach 80%. **A curated franchise-to-sound map is not buildable**, and the prior round's encouraging "top 5 artists = 51%" was measured on the wrong corpus: those 20 "Apollo Keys" clips came from **4 accounts** reusing a royalty-free piano library, which is within-account repetition, not popularity. **One franchise is a real exception** — The Boys puts 25% of its licensed clips on just two tracks (an orchestral "Let Down" 7×, ABBA's "The Winner Takes It All" 5×), while One Piece (0/31) and Naruto (0/14) have **no repeated track at all**. Two prior measurements also failed to replicate: BL-702's per-tag licensed rates (#marveledit 50%→**16%**, #animeedit 26%→**0%**) were single-page estimates off a *ranked* surface, and **14.3% of `licensed_music` clips carry no track metadata at all**.

## PART 2 — concentration, the number that decides the next step

### Overall, across all 348 licensed clips

| | artists | tracks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| distinct | **263** | **284** |
| appearing exactly once | **218 (83%)** | **247 (87%)** |
| needed to cover 50% | 89 | 110 |
| needed to cover 80% | **194** | — |
| needed to cover 90% | 229 | — |
| **top 50 cover** | **38.8%** | — |

**The hypothesis was: if 80% of licensed clips come from 50 artists, a hand-built map beats every commercial service. The measured answer is 38.8% from the top 50, and 194 artists to reach 80%. REFUTED.**

This is not a marginal miss. A distribution where 87% of tracks appear once is a **long tail with almost no head** — there is nothing to curate. Hand-maintaining 194 artists to cover 80% of clips, against a catalogue that turns over with trends, is not a map; it is a subscription to manual labour.

### Per franchise — and here the picture is not uniform

| franchise | licensed / tagged | distinct tracks | clips on a **repeated** track |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MCU (#mcu + #marveledit) | 80 / 352 | 65 | 25 / 80 |
| Batman | 55 / 180 | 50 | 9 / 55 |
| **The Boys** | 48 / 177 | **35** | **18 / 48** |
| One Piece | 31 / 165 | 31 | **0 / 31** |
| Dragon Ball Z | 19 / 119 | 17 | 3 / 19 |
| Naruto | 14 / 92 | 14 | **0 / 14** |

**The Boys is the one franchise with real concentration**, and it is strong enough to matter:

- *Let Down (orchestral version)* — Alessandro Veloz — **7 clips**
- *The Winner Takes It All* — ABBA — **5 clips**

**Two tracks cover 12 of 48 = 25% of its licensed clips.** That is a curatable signal, and it has an obvious explanation: The Boys is built around iconic needle-drops, so editors converge on the show's own musical identity.

**One Piece and Naruto are the opposite extreme: 31 clips, 31 distinct tracks; 14 clips, 14 distinct tracks. Not one repeat.** Anime-edit culture appears to reward using a *different* track, which is the inverse of what a curated map needs.

**So concentration is a per-franchise property, not a corpus property.** Any plan that assumed one answer for all franchises was wrong in both directions.

### Do tracks span franchises?

**Only 9 of 202 distinct tracks (4.5%)** appear on more than one franchise, and only one spans three:

| tracks | franchises |
| --- | --- |
| *X-COOL! (slowed)* — tienanh109, hdn, mc k3 | Batman · MCU · One Piece |
| *The Cat Waltzed on the Piano* — Harper Quinn | Batman · The Boys |
| *Raathu Raasan* — Sai Abhyankkar et al. | Batman · MCU |
| *Boom Shaka* — KR$NA, Dhanda Nyoliwala | One Piece · The Boys |

Tracks are largely franchise-specific — but that conclusion is weak, because **most tracks appear only once anywhere**, so there is little opportunity to span. The honest reading is: no evidence of a shared cross-franchise sound pool.

## PART 3 — why the earlier number said the opposite

`scratch/bl703_concentration.json` reported **48 distinct artists over 98 clips, top 5 = 51%, "Apollo Keys" 20×** — a result that would have justified building the curated map.

**VERIFIED: that script walked `_probe_samples/`, not the hashtag corpus.** Those are **per-account media pulls**, and the consequences are decisive:

- The 20 Apollo Keys clips come from **4 accounts** (handles withheld — they are ordinary creator pages, not the point of the finding).
- They appear **40 times across 20 distinct `pk`**, because each account was saved twice — once in `<user>_clips.json` and once in `<user>_medias.json`.
- The titles are royalty-free ambient piano: *Hope of the Heart*, *The Fountain*, *It Snows Silently*, *Mother Nature*, *Piano Craft*, *The Dark Forest*.

**That is one account reusing a background-music library across its own posts.** It measures within-account habit, which says nothing about what track belongs on a franchise. In the hashtag corpus — where clips come from 1,081 distinct accounts — **Apollo Keys is 4 clips across 2 accounts.**

**The lesson generalises: account-sampled corpora manufacture concentration.** Any future concentration measurement in this programme must be sampled across accounts, and must dedupe by `pk` before counting.

## PART 4 — two further measurements that did not replicate

### BL-702's per-tag licensed rates are single-page estimates off a ranked surface

| tag | BL-702 (1 page, n≈30) | this round (4–6 pages) | n |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| #literallyme | 53% | 45.8% | 177 |
| #marveledit | 50% | **16.0%** | 175 |
| #animeedit | 26% | **0.0%** | 118 |
| #capcut | 23% | 20.0% | 30 |

**Page 1 of a hashtag is a *ranked* surface, not a random sample.** High-performing posts are over-represented there, and they evidently use licensed music more. **Single-page rate estimates are biased upward and should not be used for capacity planning.** #literallyme survives roughly intact; #marveledit is off by 3×.

### `audio_type == 'licensed_music'` does **not** imply the track is identified

**VERIFIED: of 398 clips with `audio_type = licensed_music`, only 341 (85.7%) carry a title and artist. 14.3% have `music_info` present but `music_asset_info.title` absent.**

The missing metadata is sharply clustered:

| tag | licensed clips missing title |
| --- | --- |
| #animeedit | **35 / 35 = 100%** |
| #marveledit | **20 / 48 = 41.7%** |
| every other tag | **0%** |

**And it is not stable.** The same tag fetched about an hour apart gave different completeness: `discovered.json` had #animeedit at 7/27 titled, `expanded.json` at 0/117. That points to **server-side variability**, not a property of the tag or the content. **GAP: cause unknown, and worth knowing before any pipeline treats `licensed_music` as a promise of metadata.**

This also explains the #animeedit 26%→0% collapse above: the clips are still licensed, the *titles* stopped arriving.

## PART 5 — verdict

**The curated franchise-to-sound map is not buildable as a general mechanism.** 87% of tracks appear once; the top 50 artists cover 38.8%. There is no head to the distribution to curate.

**But the fallback in the brief — "you need genre lookup instead" — does not follow, and would be the wrong lesson.** Genre lookup was proposed for the case where tracks are scattered. They are scattered, but a genre label does not answer *"what sound goes on this clip"*; it only re-describes whatever track was already chosen. With 87% singletons there is no popularity signal for a genre API to recover either. **Scatter defeats both approaches equally.** Note also BL-698's finding that the obvious metadata routes are licence-blocked (TMDB non-commercial, OMDb CC BY-NC), so a paid genre lookup carries a cost the earlier framing did not price.

**What the data does support, narrowly and cheaply:**

1. **Build the map for The Boys only.** 25% of its licensed clips sit on two tracks. That is worth a hand-written rule and costs nothing to maintain at that size. Check whether other live-action, needle-drop-driven shows behave the same way before generalising — the mechanism (a show with a musical identity) is more predictive than the franchise itself.
2. **Do not build it for anime franchises.** One Piece 0/31 and Naruto 0/14 repeats is as clear a negative as this corpus can produce.
3. **Treat `licensed_music` as a hint, not a guarantee** — 14.3% of the time there is no track to look up, and the rate is unstable across fetches.
4. **Re-sample any concentration question across accounts**, deduped by `pk`. The one prior measurement that suggested this was viable failed exactly this test.

### An observation nobody asked for, visible in the track lists

The licensed tracks skew heavily toward **Indian and Tamil film music and slowed remixes** — Anirudh Ravichander, Sai Abhyankkar, Arijit Singh, Kumar Sanu, Jakes Bejoy recur across Batman, MCU and One Piece alike. That is not a franchise-sound signal; **it is an audience signal about who is making these edits**, and it is probably more commercially interesting than the sound map this round set out to build. It is unmeasured — flagged, not claimed.
