## PART 0 — the owner's question, answered first

**On the books, it is money the budget has RELEASED, not money it still holds. But paying it would double-spend NOTHING in practice, because every campaign that holds real money here is frozen, so no other clipper can ever claim the freed room. It is "created" by the accounting and "reserved-and-unclaimable" in reality, and the gap between those two only becomes a real double-spend if one of these campaigns is ever un-paused.**

In plain English: when these clips were live, their earnings counted against the campaign's clipper pool, so the budget was genuinely charged for them. The moment each clip was flagged "video unavailable," its earnings **dropped out of the pool's spend calculation** and the room re-opened. So the platform's own accounting no longer reserves this money; on paper the room is free for someone else to earn. That is proven below from the cap code, not guessed.

The reason paying it is nonetheless safe today is that the campaigns holding it (somesome, GainzAlgo, WinGram, STRAENGE) are all paused or finished, so their existing clips cannot earn into the freed room and no new clips can be submitted. The freed room just sits there, claimable by nobody. Paying the 24 clippers hands them money that is, in practice, still theirs and theirs alone. The only way it becomes a real double-charge is if the owner later reactivates one of these campaigns and new clips fill the room that these retired earnings used to occupy while the retired clippers are also paid.

## PART 0 (proof) — the pool cap EXCLUDES videoUnavailable, proven from code

The whole question reduces to one thing: does the code that ENFORCES the budget cap still count these retired clips, or does it drop them like the display endpoint does? It drops them. Traced, not inferred:

1. **The L1 hard lock reads committed spend from `getCampaignBudgetStatus`.** `src/lib/clip-earnings-writer.ts:196-197`: `const { getCampaignBudgetStatus } = await import("@/lib/balance"); const status = await getCampaignBudgetStatus(current.campaignId);`. The gate then throws if `status.spent` is already over budget (`:214`, `:235-236` `projected = status.spent + delta; if (projected > status.budget) throw`). So `status.spent` IS the number the money core enforces against.

2. **`getCampaignBudgetStatus`'s clipper aggregate filters `videoUnavailable: false`.** `src/lib/balance.ts`, the `earningsAgg`: `db.clip.aggregate({ where: { campaignId, isDeleted: false, status: "APPROVED", videoUnavailable: false }, _sum: { earnings: true } })`. That aggregate is `spent` for the clipper side, and it is also `clipperSideSpent`, which feeds `clipperHeadroom = max(clipperPoolCap - clipperSideSpent, 0)`.

3. **Therefore a retired clip's earnings are invisible to both the total-budget gate and the BL-162 clipper-pool clamp.** The instant a clip flips `videoUnavailable = true`, its earnings leave `status.spent` and leave `clipperSideSpent`, so `clipperHeadroom` GROWS by exactly that clip's earnings. The pool literally re-opens that room to the next clipper.

This is the same asymmetry BL-642 found on the display endpoint (`/api/campaigns/spend:63` clip side excludes `videoUnavailable`, `:94` agency side includes everything), now confirmed on the ENFORCEMENT path as well. **The cap enforcement excludes the retired clips. The room was released.** So mechanically, paying these earnings is spending against a pool that has already re-opened that headroom to others. Whether that is a real double-spend depends entirely on whether "others" can actually earn it, which is PART 1.

**None of the $390.50 has been paid. Paying it is not a double payment.** Measured per affected clipper: lifetime paid versus lifetime earned including the retired clips.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|

| Clippers whose lifetime paid EXCEEDS lifetime earned (incl. retired) | **0** |
| Dollars already overpaid among the 24 | **$0.00** |

Examples: `3a8763` earned $1,329.31, paid $1,313.86 (the $15.45 gap is exactly their phantom); `bc64d4` earned $994.82, paid $993.36 (their $0.94 phantom). The phantom is the still-owed remainder in every case.

**Overlap with BL-627's overpaid group: none.** BL-627 found 5 clippers holding $142.59 more than they earned (clamped at $0, unrecoverable). **Zero of those 5 are among these 24** — the two groups are disjoint. The overpaid clippers already took more than they earned; these 24 have taken less. So paying the phantom neither touches the BL-627 over-hold nor risks paying anyone twice.

## PART 3 — effect on campaign spend and the owner's share

**Paying the phantom changes no campaign's recorded spend and breaches no budget** (payouts are not a term in `getCampaignBudgetStatus.spent`, PART 1). It moves money from the platform to the clipper; it does not re-charge the campaign on the books.

**The owner was already credited on this same work, and that is a real asymmetry worth naming.** The owner's share is derived from clipper earnings and is accrued into `agency_earnings` at the time the clip earns. Those rows still exist on the retired clips:

| STRAENGE | $2.06 |

So on the very clips whose clippers cannot withdraw ~$390.50, the owner has **~$2,363 of accrued share still on the books** (and in the legacy `spent` display, that owner accrual is still counted, since the agency aggregate at `balance.ts` is unfiltered — the BL-164 cap-math figure `ownerSideSpent` does exclude it). The platform kept the owner's cut of this work while the clippers' cut became unwithdrawable. That asymmetry does not by itself decide anything, but the owner should see it: **the work was already good enough to credit him for; refusing the clipper's matching share is the harder position to defend.**

## PART 4 — the growth problem

The daily `retire-dead-clips` cron (BL-590) adds to the phantom every day. Confirmed from live data (BL-655's split, re-stated): of today's $390.50, **$383.61 is the one-off BL-584 event and $6.89 was added by the cron over the six days since**, i.e. **~$1.15/day**. The cron retires only newly-dead Instagram clips (a handful a day, mostly cents), so the payable gap grows far slower than the clip count.

| Horizon | Added by the cron at ~$1.15/day | Phantom, if nothing changes |
|---|---|---|
| Today | — | **$390.50** |
| 1 month | ~$34.50 | **~$425** |
| 3 months | ~$103.50 | **~$494** |
| 12 months | ~$419.75 | **~$810** |

Two honest caveats on the projection. It **decelerates**: the big backlog was drained by BL-584, so the cron now catches only the trickle of freshly-dead videos, and the true 12-month figure is likely below $810. And it is **step-risk, not just drip-risk**: a single second mass-death event like 2026-07-18 (a whole campaign's Instagram clips taken down at once) would add hundreds in one day, dwarfing a year of the cron. The argument for deciding the policy now is not the $1.15/day; it is that a daily job keeps silently converting earned money into unwithdrawable money, and the next bulk retirement will make the number jump.

## PART 5 — verdict and options, priced

**Verdict: the money is RELEASED on the books (the cap enforcement excludes it, proven), but reserved-and-unclaimable in practice, because every campaign holding real money here is frozen. Paying the 24 double-spends nothing today. It only becomes a real double-charge if the owner reactivates one of these campaigns and lets new clips fill the freed room while also paying the retired clippers — and even then only STRAENGE is at its cap edge, for $1.71.** None of it was ever paid, none of these 24 are overpaid, and the videos are genuinely gone.

### (a) Fix the display so the 24 stop seeing it
* **True cost: $0**, and it makes the site honest immediately.
* **What it hides:** if the owner believes these clippers earned the money (they did, for real views on real work, and it was withdrawable until an automated job flipped a flag), this option buries a real debt rather than paying it.
* **Mechanics:** add `videoUnavailable: false` to `clipWhere` at `api/earnings/route.ts:64`, matching the gate. Display only; nothing in the database moves.

### (b) Pay it and keep deleted-video earnings payable
* **True cost: $390.50 of money the pool has RELEASED, not reserved** — so economically it is closer to "created" than "already set aside", though because the campaigns are frozen no other clipper can claim the room, so no actual double-spend occurs. **Plus the ongoing ~$1.15/day** (about $425 in a month, ~$810 in a year, decelerating), and the step-risk of the next mass retirement.
* **What must be proven before shipping:** apply the rule change across all five gate sites consistently, confirm no campaign is reactivated with the freed room still open (STRAENGE is the one at-cap case, $1.71), and confirm the owner accepts paying earnings the pool no longer reserves.
* **Only partly reversible** once a payout settles, so it needs sign-off first.

### (c) The better middle option: pay it as a one-time settlement, then fix the display, and leave the rule alone
Because the money is released-but-unclaimable and the campaigns are frozen, the owner can **pay these 24 clippers their $390.50 as a deliberate one-off** (they earned it, none was double-paid, the videos are confirmed dead), **and separately fix the display bug so the number stops growing on screen and no future clipper is shown unwithdrawable money.** This pays the real debt without permanently changing the withdrawal rule to "deleted videos always pay" — which is the part that carries the post-farm-delete exploit risk and the open-ended daily cost. It settles the known $390.50 on its merits and closes the display leak, without committing the platform to pay every future deleted-video dollar.

**Recommendation: (c).** It is the only option that both pays the clippers what the evidence says they are owed and stops the bug from recurring, without signing the platform up to an unbounded future liability. If the owner wants the simplest thing and is comfortable never paying deleted-video earnings, (a) alone is defensible because the code's consistent rule already says "do not pay gone clips" and this sample confirms the clips are genuinely gone. What is NOT defensible is (a) done quietly as if it were only a rendering fix: it would erase a $390.50 debt the owner has now been shown is real. **Paying would not breach any recorded budget and would not double-pay anyone; the honest catch is only that the pool has already re-opened the room, so it is fresh spend rather than a release of reserved funds.**

## What could not be measured

* **Whether the clippers *should* be paid** is the owner's policy call, not a data question; the data says they earned it, none was paid, and the videos are gone.
* **A full live re-probe of all ~562 retired clips** was not run; 12 of somesome's 331 were sampled (unanimously 404), and BL-584 re-probed the whole set at retirement time.
* **Behaviour on reactivation** (whether a future top-up would let the freed room be re-earned while retired clippers are paid) is a forward scenario, not a current state; it is the one path by which "released" becomes a real double-spend, and STRAENGE is the only campaign currently close to its cap.
