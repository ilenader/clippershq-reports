## 3. The depth recommendation, with the arithmetic

### The model

```
pages(depth)  = ceil(depth / 11.6)                     11.6 clips per page, measured
calls/account = pages x 1.16                           1.16 measured at one page, EXTRAPOLATED
              + 0.84 if the handle needs resolving     2.00 - 1.16, a ONE-TIME cost per page
$/call        = 0.0006
clip bytes    = $0.0000                                CDN, from the walk's own manifest
```

The 1.16 figure was measured on one-page walks; applying it per page is the likeliest reading
and it is not a measurement. `estimate()` says so in its own output rather than letting the
number pass as one.

### What it costs — five accounts, all already resolved

| depth | pages | calls | **cost** | this-year clips | renderable | local gate time | $ per renderable |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 3 | 17 | $0.0104 | 66 | 6.6 | 0.2 h | $0.0016 |
| 58 | 5 | 29 | $0.0174 | 153 | 15.3 | 0.4 h | $0.0011 |
| **100** | **9** | **52** | **$0.0313** | **265** | **26.5** | **0.7 h** | **$0.0012** |
| 200 | 18 | 104 | $0.0626 | 529 | 52.9 | 1.4 h | $0.0012 |
| **500** | **44** | **255** | **$0.1531** | **1,323** | **132.3** | **3.4 h** | **$0.0012** |

Yield columns use this run's measured rates: 52.9% of walked clips are from this year, 10.0%
of gated this-year clips come out renderable. The projection assumes the ungated ones behave
like the gated ones; the gated ones were the *top by play count*, so treat it as the
optimistic end.

### The three things that fall out of it

**1. Money does not decide this.** Depth 500 across five accounts is **$0.15 of a $0.50
cap**. $ per renderable is FLAT at $0.0012 from depth 58 upwards — going deeper buys more
clips at exactly the same unit price. There is no depth in the practical range where the
walk becomes expensive.

**2. The route decides it.** The same harvest with the bytes re-fetched per clip
(`clip_media.retrieve`, $0.00065/clip at 98.9%):

```
depth 100   walk $0.0313 + bytes $0.3286 = $0.3599    under the cap, barely
depth 500   walk $0.1531 + bytes $1.6431 = $1.7962    3.6x OVER the cap
```

Taking the bytes from the manifest the walk already returned is what makes any depth
affordable. It is the single most valuable line of code in this round and it is BL-1133's,
not mine.

**3. Deep pages are not older pages, and that is the fact that changed my answer.** I
expected the this-year share to fall with depth — page 9 holds the account's 9th-best clips
of *all time*, so a long-running page should spend its deep pages on old material. Measured
across all five accounts:

```
page       p1     p2     p3     p4     p5
this yr  25/60  31/60  34/60  36/60  28/51
share      42%    52%    57%    60%    55%
```

It goes **up**, not down. Within these pages, view rank and recency are not correlated, so
the deep pages are as current as the shallow ones. Nothing about depth degrades what you get.

### The recommendation

**Depth 200 — 18 pages per account — and gate everything from this year.**

* $0.063 for five pages. 13% of the cap.
* ~529 this-year clips, ~53 renderable.
* **1.4 hours** of local gating, which is the number that actually binds.

Not 500: it triples the wall clock for the same unit price and the same yield per clip, and
two of these five pages do not *have* 500 this-year clips to give — solidshampooz ran out at
51. Not 100: it leaves supply on the table at zero extra unit cost.

**It is set on the page and it is yours.** The box is `Pages per account`, and pressing
Estimate reprices the whole table without spending a cent.

**One caveat on going wider rather than deeper.** The manifest URLs the free byte route
depends on carry an expiry: measured at **104 hours** — 4.3 days — from the walk. Gating
deferred beyond that costs the retrieve route. This build gates in the same run and does not
store the manifests, so "walk deep now, gate the rest next week" is not available yet. It is
a small change and it is not in this round.

## 4. The append lock, and a defect I shipped and then caught

The append goes through `clip_library.append_many`, which holds the per-shard file lock and
allocates the rev **inside** it. Lock-free appending was measured losing rows (205 with, 200
without) and 236 clips once collided at equal rev with the loser's fields becoming
unreachable while the store still looked healthy.

**The first version skipped any survivor already in the library.** Four of five survivors
were already stored, so four sets of freshly measured `ocr_gate_text`, `text_bands`,
`text_top_band`, `motion` and `speech_frac` were computed at ~9.3 s each and thrown away.
Three of those rows still read `hook=None ocr=None` afterwards — the store looked untouched
because it was. The skip was protecting against a problem `append_many` had already solved.

Fixed, and re-run over the same measurements at **zero paid calls**
(`scratch/bl1139_store.py`):

```
3911624936214765064   present rev 7 -> 8   filled []                                     clobbered []
3897224896835906067   present rev 3 -> 4   filled [ocr_gate_text, text_bands, top_band,
                                                   motion, speech_frac, hook]            clobbered []
3844262390238406343   present rev 1 -> 2   filled []                                     clobbered []
3864191840015228097   present rev 3 -> 4   filled [ocr_gate_text, text_bands, top_band,
                                                   motion, hook]                         clobbered []
3852599855512982386   present rev 3 -> 4   filled [ocr_gate_text, text_bands, top_band,
                                                   motion, hook]                         clobbered []

rev collisions involving a row this wrote: 0
```

Our `rev` is 1, below anything stored, which `append_many` reads as the staleness signal it
is: fill the gaps, overwrite nothing. **16 empty fields filled, 0 values clobbered.**

(The store carries 319 rev collisions in total, all pre-dating BL-919's fix. None involves a
row written here. Not this round's to clean up, but it is a real number and somebody should.)

## 5. The cost estimate, before running, with the cap binding

The estimate is a **separate, free endpoint** — not a flag on the spending one. You can ask
it what depth 500 would cost as many times as you like and it makes no vendor call. A
dry-run flag on a spending endpoint is one typo away from a bill.

```
  BEFORE YOU SPEND
  --------------------------------------------------------------
   accounts pasted     : 5  (3 already resolved, 2 need a pk lookup)
   depth               : 58 clips walked per account
   pages per account   : 5  (at 11.6 clips/page)
   billed calls        : 31  (29.0 walk + 1.68 resolve)
   ESTIMATED COST      : $0.0184   ($0.0037 per account)
   clip bytes          : $0.0000  (CDN, from the manifest the walk already returned)
   the route NOT taken : $0.1906  if the bytes came from clip_media.retrieve
   CAP                 : $0.50   OK, $0.4816 left
```

**The cap has two walls and neither is decorative.**

* **Pre-flight**: the estimate against the cap, refusing without one call.
  `--cap 0.01` against a $0.0184 estimate → `REFUSED BEFORE SPENDING: nothing was spent`,
  zero calls made. Proven live and in the suite.
* **Mid-run**: `reserve()` runs **before** every request and is never refunded — the vendor
  bills attempts, not successes. Proven in the suite against an injected fetcher: a budget
  with room for 3 calls makes exactly 3, and the 4th request is never made.

The suite also asserts the property that makes the first wall sufficient: **the estimate is
a strict upper bound on the walk**. If that ever stops holding, the cap silently becomes
advisory, so it is a test rather than an assumption.

**Charge-on-attempt, checked against the vendor.** Two runs, in-process counter 27 + 27 = 54
calls = $0.0324. The ledger's own flush records `BL-1139/harvest_accounts 27 calls $0.0162`
and `HARVEST/harvest_accounts 27 calls $0.0162`. Independent agreement, both times.

## 6. Two things found on the way that cost money

### The `1ltaken_at` trap — 27 billed calls to rediscover

The raw `/gql/user/clips` envelope prefixes some scalars with a type tag: `taken_at` arrives
as **`1ltaken_at`**, `device_timestamp` as `1ldevice_timestamp`. My first `posted_epoch` read
the plain name, got None on every clip, and `best_of_year` correctly excluded all of them.
Five accounts reported `this_year 0` across 291 walked clips.

**The failure was silent and plausible.** "These pages posted nothing in 2026" is a sentence
you can believe. What caught it was the library: thedylanseek has 29 of its 30 stored clips
in 2026, which cannot be true at the same time as zero.

`clip_library.mget` has solved this since the library was built, and now gets called instead
of the rule being re-derived. Pinned in the suite against the **real saved envelope**, not a
hand-built dict — a hand-built dict is exactly what the broken version would also have passed.

### 13 of 258 stored accounts disagree with themselves about their own user_pk

The 1.16-vs-2.00 saving assumes "we already have the pk". Measured today:

```
planet_mim      44827640302 x27    16567545488 x1
smoovereactss     971774209 x29       38862652 x1
ashoraif        38606524336 x26    57069443543 x2   48255546494 x1
```

A stored pk is a **candidate, not an address**. Taking the majority silently would be right
about 95% of the time and wrong in a way that costs a whole walk: the wrong pk does not
error, it **walks a different account** and files every clip under the handle you pasted. So
ambiguity is treated as absence and resolved for the 0.84, and the saving is claimed only
where the library is unanimous. Both ambiguities in this run resolved to the majority
(2 of 2) — which is evidence the majority is usually right and not evidence it is safe.

### And one about @shivnosekai's survivors

Both of its survivors carry transliterated Hindi burned into the frame
(`Apne notice Kiya hoga GOKU KI LADAI...`). `clip_postable.is_non_english_caption` reads the
**caption**, not the pixels, so a page that writes English captions over Hindi hooks passes.
Not wrong — the gate does what it says — but you should see them before deciding, which is
why the folder shows the text and not just the frame.

## 10. What I would do next

1. **Decide the speech question.** 87% of refusals are speech. Either the rule stands and
   the paste box needs pages that post silent memes, or the rule bends. The scorecard can now
   tell you which pages are which before you spend, which it could not do this morning.

   thrown away at the end of the run. Storing it turns "walk deep now, gate later" from
   impossible into free.
3. **Backfill the 319 rev collisions.** Pre-existing, not this round's, and every one is a
   row whose fields are on disk and unreachable.
