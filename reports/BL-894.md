## First, the number that changes the question

**The renderer would not choose 30 clips today. It can choose 18.**

| | |
|---|---|
| renderable on RAW library rows | **242** |
| renderable **through `dict_of`** — what the renderer actually does | **18** |
| lost at the boundary | **224** |

`clip_pipeline.dict_of()` passes five fields — `clip_id`, `franchise`, `content_genre`,
`valence_text`, `duration_s` — and no vision fields, so the tier that produces 202 of 209
matches cannot fire inside the pipeline. MEMEBOT-043 found this; I am confirming it from the
renderer's side and reporting what it does to the *selection*, not just the count.

And the selection is the finding: **`franchise_mood_map` has 15 entries and every single one
routes to `hype`. `genre_mood_map` and `valence_mood_map` are both empty.** So through
`dict_of` the only tier that can fire is franchise, and every franchise points at one mood.

**All 18 clips are blockbuster movie scenes. All 18 get song04. It is structurally impossible
for this pipeline to select anything else.**

material away.

## The ranking's one surviving term

**`rank_score` is play count. Exactly, for 18 of 18** — every clip's rank equals
`log10(play_count)` to within 0.001. There is nothing else in it.

**The provisional sub-10-second bonus fires zero times**, because not one of the 18 is under
ten seconds. The n=24 finding it came from has never applied to a single clip this pipeline
would render.

Does the top-ranked clip look better than the median? **No.** #1 is a 43-second Troy recap;
the median (#10, cineframesoul_) is a 65-second Batman/Joker dialogue. Both are competent,
both are forgettable, and the 17× difference in play count between them measures *how big the
account was*, not how good the clip is — BL-850 already found account identity explains 78.7%
of view variance. The ranking is sorting by a number that mostly describes the poster.

## The song matches

The premise was that 206 of 234 route to song04. **Through the pipeline it is 18 of 18 —
100%.** Not because the matcher prefers song04, but because `franchise_mood_map`'s 15 entries
all say `hype` and the other two maps are empty. There is no routing decision being made.

Judging the fit of ten of them: a hype drop suits **#2 (Cap vs Cap)**, **#9 (Maximus's
monologue)** and **#12 (the truck flip)** — all three are build-and-payoff action. It is
actively wrong on **#3** (a man on a sofa describing script pages), **#5 and #8** (quiet
arguments), **#10 and #17** (philosophical dialogue), **#18** (a four-second reaction) and
**#7** (essay narration). **Roughly three of ten fit.** The other seven would have a beat drop
land over people talking.

## The verdict

**If you posted the top 10 as-is, you would look like a low-effort movie-clip farm.** Ten
recycled blockbuster scenes, most of them 40–100 seconds, all with the same hype track,
including one that is a talk-show interview. The page would read as automated within a week.
It would not get you banned and it would not embarrass you — it would just be invisible, which
for a repost page is the same outcome.

**The single change with the most leverage is the `dict_of` boundary — but not for the reason
it is usually given.** It is normally framed as a count problem (18 → 242). The real cost is
that the five fields it passes make *franchise* the only usable signal, and franchise exists
only on movie clips. Fixing it does not just multiply the pool by thirteen; it is the only way
the system can ever select a clip that is funny rather than licensed.

Ranking second, and it is cheap: sorting by `log10(play_count)` inside a library where account
identity explains 78.7% of view variance means the ranker is largely re-sorting the accounts.
Normalising views against the account's own median would at least make it a *clip* score.

Sourcing is **not** the problem — the pages already post 5M-view comedy and it is already in
the library. Songs are not the problem either; four is thin, but three of them never get
offered a clip. **The material is there. The pipeline cannot reach it.**

## Honest limits

- **I did not watch a single video.** None of the 18 is on disk and retrieval costs money,
  which this round was not permitted to spend. I judged from vision descriptions, on-screen
  text, captions and — for #1, #3, #5, #9, #13, #17 — the live Instagram post. For the six with
  no vision description I said "cannot judge" rather than guess.
- **Six of the eighteen I could not judge at all**, which is itself the finding: the pipeline
  selected clips nothing has ever looked at.
- **The like counts are partly broken.** Five clips report exactly 3 likes against six-figure
  view counts. I used them for nothing except to flag them.
- **Item 3 compares against each page's posts *in the library*, not against its true top
  posts.** The walk is capped, so a page's real best may never have been collected. The
  direction is safe — the library already contains 5.3M-view comedy the system cannot select —
  but the ranks (#6 of 11, #4 of 24) are ranks within what was walked.
- **"Would a 100k page post this" is my judgement, not a measurement.** Another reader might
  rate the Maximus monologue a yes on nostalgia alone. I have shown the reasoning per clip so
  the disagreement can be specific.
- **`dict_of` is being changed right now** — MEMEBOT-042 holds `clip_pipeline.py`. Everything
  here is stamped at commit `116deab` and the 18 will change the moment that lands.
- **I did not test what the selection would look like with vision fields passed through.** That
  needs the fix, and this round was read-only.
