## 5. The judge against itself: does its own reason name its own disqualifier?

The defect: the judge **saw correctly and decided wrongly** — *"exactly the C8 pattern the
operator keeps chipping"*, *"she makes these animations"* — WANT anyway. Nobody had measured
the rate.

### The population, and the bug I had to fix in my own scanner first

**708 WANT-verdicts-with-a-written-reason across 288 files.** Getting that number right took
two corrections to my own tool, both of them the exact failure this section is about:

1. `scratch/bl1280_seedprobe_verdicts.jsonl` is a JSON **array** with a `.jsonl` name. Parsing
   it line-by-line yielded **zero records and zero WANTs for a 40-verdict file, with no
   error.** The parser now has to actually produce something before its answer is believed.
2. Three rounds used an API harness whose row is billing telemetry — `cost_usd`,
   `prompt_tokens`, `lane` — with a **duplicate `verdict` at the top level and the real record
   nested under `record`**, where `reason` lives. My walker stopped at the telemetry row and
   read `reason` as absent. `output/bl1260_tiktok_run/verdicts_combined.json` therefore
   reported **0 WANT-with-reason when the true figure is 77** — the single largest WANT block
   in the repo, silently dropped from a measurement whose subject is silent failure.

Before those fixes the population was 160. After them it is 708. **A scan reporting a rate
over the wrong denominator is exactly as wrong as the defect it is hunting**, and both bugs
produced a confident zero rather than an error.

### A prose scanner cannot answer this on its own

The text prefilter flagged **191 of 708 (27.0%)**. Most are the opposite of a defect — the
judge doing the bookkeeping C24 demands:

> *"One tile is a speaker-labelled 'Bro:/Me:' exchange so it does not count toward the
> qualifying total, but under C20 five qualifying tiles is far past the gate of one."*

That names a disqualifier **and is correct**, because there are two kinds:

* **tile-level** (v8 C20 rule 3 — third-person commentary, tweet-cards, brand ads,
  speaker-labelled dialogue): removes one tile from the count. Naming it and passing is right.
* **page-level** (C21, C22, C25 steps 1–6): ends the page. Naming it and passing is the defect.

**And the line moved between eras.** Under v4 and earlier, `dialogue` was a **page-level
veto** — C3: *"fire `dialogue` and REJECT, even if the remaining tiles are perfect"* — so the
same sentence that is correct bookkeeping under v8 is a contradiction under v4. Every record
was judged against the ruleset in force when it was written.

### The answer

Two methods, each where it is valid. For the 281 v8 records and 159 tt2 records the three
counts are structured fields, so the check is **arithmetic** (`scratch/bl1285_consistency.py`):
a WANT with `qualifying_tiles == 0`, with `still_tiles >= 7`, or carrying a non-null
`disqualifier`. For prose-only records, **all 123 pre-v8 candidates were read and classified**
by four independent agents (0 left unadjudicated).

| adjudication of the 140 read | n |
|---|---:|
| **TRUE_CONTRADICTION** | **31** |
| TILE_LEVEL_BOOKKEEPING (correct — C24 asks for it) | 45 |
| NEGATED (the reason clears the thing) | 52 |
| AMBIGUOUS | 12 |

| era | WANTs with a reason | true contradictions | rate | Wilson 95% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **pre-v8** (v1–v7, six rulesets) | 535 | **31** | **5.79%** | [4.11%, 8.11%] |
| **tt2** (current, TikTok) | 76 | **0** | **0.00%** | [0.00%, 4.81%] |
| **v8** (current, Instagram — the 100 + the 181) | 97 | **0** | **0.00%** | [0.00%, 3.81%] |

> **The answer to the question asked: it was far more than one or two. 5.79% — one WANT in
> seventeen — so it was a structural fault, not a page-level miss. Under both current briefs
> it is zero, and the intervals do not overlap** (pre-v8 lower bound 4.11% against v8 upper

| mean low-band tiles on pages he WANTS | 0.579 |
| mean low-band tiles on pages he does not | 0.840 |
| **AUC, low-band count predicting WANT** | **0.498** |
| AUC predicting REJECT | 0.502 |

**0.498 is a coin flip.** On a clean arm, the low-band dialogue count carries no aggregate
signal — which independently confirms the 0.500 finding and vindicates v8's withdrawal of the
dialogue veto.

**But the tail is real and should not be thrown away with the average:**

| cut rule | pages cut | he WANTED | he did not | precision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| low-band >= 1 | 38 | 8 | 30 | 0.789 — **below the 0.81 base rate** |
| low-band >= 2 | 21 | 3 | 18 | 0.857 |
| **low-band >= 3** | **10** | **0** | **10** | **1.000** |

A cut at 1 is worse than doing nothing. A cut at **3 or more is perfect on this sample** and
kills 10 pages. That is a narrow, sound rule hiding inside a signal that is useless in
aggregate — and it is the shape C24's third count should be used for, if it is used at all.

### Two things that must be said before this number is used

**1. It is not comparable to the 89, and not because of noise.** These 192 were *selected* as
the pages the changed rules can reach — deliberate enrichment for disagreement. The 100 is an
unselected sample. This answers "how does the judge do where the new rules bite", which is a
different and narrower question. Quoting 68.5% against 97 would be a denominator error.

**2. There is a confound I cannot remove.** The 100 were judged from **native profile-grid

provenance, plausibly different legibility. I cannot separate "the rules bite harder on this
population" from "reconstructed grids are harder to read", and nothing in this round measures
that split. Treat 68.5% as *directional corroboration* that the fall is real and larger where
the rules concentrate — not as a second estimate of the same quantity.

What it does establish without ambiguity: **`creator` is now the dominant chip**, firing on
**46 of 178** pages, ahead of `no_qualifying_tiles` (21) and `not_english` (11), with
`still_pictures` firing only twice. C21 is doing most of the rejecting on this population.
