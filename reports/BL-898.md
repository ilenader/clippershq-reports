## 2. The dashboard number: the brief's premise had already changed

**The brief says the panel overstates by 13× — 242 shown against 18 real. That is no longer
true, and it is worth being precise about why rather than quietly doing something else.**

MEMEBOT-042 landed the `dict_of` fix and ended between the two rounds. `dict_of` now carries
every field in `MATCHER_FIELDS`, `tests/test_matcher_boundary.py` is green, and I measured:

```
renderable on the RAW library record        : 247
renderable THROUGH clip_pipeline.dict_of()  : 247      GAP: 0
```

So a dual display would have been permanent complexity added for a bug that no longer exists.

**Agreeing today is not the fix. Not being able to disagree is.** The panel now counts
through `clip_pipeline.dict_of()` — the same boundary the renderer crosses — so the number on
the page is the number the pipeline can produce, by construction. If someone later adds a
field the matcher reads and `dict_of` does not forward, the page shows a **falling count**
instead of a silent overstatement.

Verified after the change: panel `253`, independent count through `dict_of` `253`, agreement
exact. (247 → 253 because BL-895/BL-896 are labelling clips live.)

Two tests pin it:

* **"counts through the same boundary the renderer uses"** — recomputes independently and
  fails on divergence. Tolerance is `max(5, 2%)`, which absorbs the live corpus moving under
  a poll but cannot hide a 13× gap.
* **"says which boundary it counted through"** — the response carries
  `render_boundary: "clip_pipeline.dict_of"`. A number whose provenance is unstated cannot be
  checked by the person reading it, and if `clip_pipeline` ever fails to import, the field
  reads `raw record (... MAY OVERSTATE)` rather than the panel silently reverting to the
  overstating path.

(Both live in `tests/test_dashboard.py`. Their full names are spelled out there rather than
here — the publish-time secret scanner flags long opaque identifiers, and it is a gate worth
respecting rather than arguing with.)

## 3. Region-aware detection is the default, and pinned there

It already was — `find_cuts(..., auto_region=True)` — and `clippershq/clip_cuts.py` is the
**only** cut detector in production code. What was missing was anything stopping that from
being changed back. Re-measured on the same 60 real clips, through the default path:

| detection | clips with a usable cut |
|---|---|
| full frame | **20 / 60 — 33%** |
| **default (region-aware)** | **46 / 60 — 77%** |

Three tests now hold it: `auto_region` must default to `True`, `region` must default to
`None` so detection actually runs, and **`DEFAULT_THRESHOLD` must stay ≥ 0.15** — so nobody
can buy the 92% figure by dropping to 0.05 and accepting noise as cuts. A third asserts
`content_region` samples at a computed `fps=` rather than taking a `-frames:v` prefix, which
is the §5 defect pinned at its source.

The only other scene-detection site in the tree is `scratch/bl823_sampling.py`, a completed
one-off measurement of OCR frame-sampling cadence over 10 clips. It uses **full-frame**
detection, so its cut counts are undercounts by exactly the argument above. It is a finished
scratch artefact and I did not modify it, but **whether that undercount changed BL-823's
conclusion is unaudited** and someone should check before its cadence numbers are reused.

## 5. The first-N-frames sweep

**The first pass was useless and that is worth saying.** Broad patterns matched **1,468 lines
across 514 files** — `img.shape[:2]`, `str(exc)[:120]`, `caption[:56]`. A sweep that cries
wolf gets switched off, which is the same failure mode as the bug it hunts. Narrowed to the
actual shape — *a prefix of a temporal or sequential stream used to derive a property of the
whole* — it reports **130 lines across 64 files**, and the real ones are few:

| site | verdict |
|---|---|
| `clippershq/clip_cuts.content_region` | **was the bug; fixed.** `-frames:v 48` = 1.9 s at 25 fps. Now samples at a computed fps across the clip, and a test asserts it. |
| **`memebot/scraper/edit.py::_content_crop`** | **REAL, UNFIXED EXPOSURE.** Probes `-t 90` and takes `cropdetect`'s answer for the whole clip. **98 of 1,999 library clips (4.90%) are longer than 90 s** — max 179.9 s, p99 150.2 s — so on those the letterbox crop is decided from the first half and applied to the rest. MEMEBOT-046 holds this file; **reported, not touched.** |
| `clippershq/song_loudness.py` | **not the bug — it is the canonical fix for it.** Its `-t` calls are explicit windows, and its docstring records the measured hazard: song01 is −9.5 LUFS whole-file but −17.2 LUFS over its first 20 s, a **7.7 LU** gap. |
| everything else | false positives, tests, or `scratch/mb0*/tree/` snapshot copies of `edit.py`. |

**I was wrong about the exposure at first.** I assumed the corpus topped out around 61 s —
true of the 60 local downloads, false of the library — and only measuring it showed 4.90% of
clips exceed the probe. The prefix assumption that produced the original bug is the same one
that nearly made me dismiss it: *the sample I had was not the population.*

**A bonus find of the same family.** `song_loudness._density` carries this comment:

> *NOT `_run()`: that captures with `text=True` and raw PCM is not UTF-8, so the reader thread
> dies decoding it and the measurement comes back empty with a traceback on stderr that looks
> like an ffmpeg failure.*

That is **exactly** the bug MEMEBOT-038 hit in `clip_cuts` and fixed with a separate
`_run_binary`. Two modules, two rounds, same trap, discovered independently — the second time
after the first had already written down the answer. A shared binary-capture helper would stop
the third.
