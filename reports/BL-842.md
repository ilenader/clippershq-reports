## 3. `speech_frac` and `ocr`: unwired, and in two different ways

Neither is "correctly gated out". Resolved by AST rather than by reading the log:

```
STAGE_ORDER                     : views, content_genre, valence, layout, speech_frac, ocr
counter.enter() sites in runner : content_genre, layout, speech_frac, valence, views
NEVER entered in the walk path  : ocr
speech_fn is an injectable param: True      <- socket exists
any ocr_* param (plug point)    : NONE      <- no socket at all
```

- **`speech_frac`** has a counter site and an injectable `speech_fn`, threaded through `run()` → `clip_record()`, defaulting to `None` at every level. **Nothing in the repo ever passes it** — the only three matches are the definitions themselves. The source says so plainly: *"nothing in this repo has ever computed speech_frac."* Status: **unbuilt, with a socket.**
- **`ocr`** has no counter site and no parameter. `ocr_features()` exists but lives in `repost_layout.py`, serving the layout scorer, never the walk. Status: **absent entirely.**

**BL-804's warning is confirmed.** `run_stages` skips an absent stage and leaves its counter at 0, so `speech_frac: 0, ocr: 0` is indistinguishable from "no survivors reached this stage" — and it reads as ladder evidence in every walk report including mine.

In fairness to the code: it is documented as deliberate (`clip_runner.py:196`), and `test_clip_walk` proves the mechanism gates correctly with synthetic stages, asserting entered counts of 2, 5 and 0 in the right scenarios. **The mechanism is sound; the run output is the misleading part.** Two of six counters cannot be non-zero, and nothing in the line says so.
