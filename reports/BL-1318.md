## 1. Your pattern, written down before anything was fitted

`scratch/bl1318_pattern.md` was written and committed **before** the rule was built, from your
eight quoted lines and nothing else. In short:

- **P1 — first-person or POV framing.** `me and my bro`, `13 year old me`, `my brain`,
  `how it feels when you`, `her: … / me: …`
- **P2 — a setup, not a description.** `when life gives you a reality check at 3AM`,
  `0.03 seconds after finding out…`. **Not** "a man falls off a chair" — that distinction has
  cost this project several rounds and is coded as a negative marker.
- **P3 — the picture underneath is borrowed, recognisable footage.**
- **Hard exclusions:** no animals anywhere, no photos.

## 2. The defect I caught in my own measurement, before reporting it

My first run said your pattern does not separate at all — it rejected `noirrud`,
`backlegendarymemes`, `thesongdetective` and two more pages you **want**.

It was wrong, and the reason matters. **The OCR returns drawn captions with the spaces
stripped:**

```
"whenwe'rewatchingTobeygetjumped by"     <- noirrud, a page you WANT
"Youfinallyfoundthepagethat"             <- thesongdetective, WANT
"Wewillmeetagainasanormalperson"         <- vishkook__7, WANT
```

Measured across your 200 pages: **44.7% of caption lines contain no space at all**, and 15.8%
of tokens run longer than 14 characters. Every word-boundary marker (`\bwe\b`, `\bme\b`) is
blind to that, so a line literally opening *"when we're watching"* scored **zero** first-person
hits — and the rule then blamed your specification.

Fixed by matching twice: the word-boundary patterns against the raw line, and a set of
de-spaced phrases (≥5 characters, so `me` cannot fire inside `some`, `game` or `time`) against
a normalised copy. `noirrud` went from `p1=0` to `p1=3`.

**Both measurements are below. The first one is not reported as a finding, because it was a
finding about my reader.**

## 6. Why the ceiling is where it is

**The drawn text carries P1 and P2 but not P3.** Your specification has three parts, and the
third — *the picture underneath is a recognisable borrowed clip* — is not in the text at all.
No amount of caption reading can tell a film clip from a phone video.

**Your two hard exclusions are invisible to text.** Animal words appear in the drawn text of
**1 page in 200**, and you want none of them. The exclusion is about the **footage**, and text
cannot see footage. The same is true of "photos, not videos".

**The OCR is the binding constraint on what remains.** 44.7% of lines lose their spaces, and
**10 of the 200 pages read empty** — meaning the strongest reject signal is partly measuring
OCR failure rather than page emptiness. That is the first thing to fix, and it is free: read
the nine tiles individually instead of one downscaled grid.

---

## 7. What would have to change to raise it

1. **Read tiles, not the grid.** The current capture is one downscaled 3×3 image; per-tile
   reads at full resolution would recover most of the 44.7%.
2. **A cheap signal for P3.** Anything that says "this frame is borrowed footage" — even a
   coarse classifier — would unlock the keep-side bucket that text alone cannot support.
3. **A frame-level animal check**, which is a picture question, not a text one.

Without at least (1) and (2), 14–30% is the honest ceiling for a code-only decision at zero
loss of wanted pages.

## In plain language

You asked whether code could do 90% of the judging. **It can do about 14–30%, and only the
rejecting half.**

The good news is that the part that works, works cleanly: if a page shows almost no written
caption, it is never one you want — that held on both your sets, across 200 pages, without
losing a single page you wanted.

The part that does not work is deciding a page is **good** without looking at it. Your rule has
three parts, and the third is *what the video underneath actually is*. That is not in the text,
so code reading captions can never get there. Every version I tried that kept pages unjudged
was wrong about more than half of them.

One honest caveat: my first attempt said your description did not work at all. That was my
mistake — the text reader was dropping spaces on nearly half the captions, so lines that
plainly say *"when we're watching…"* looked blank to it. Fixed and re-measured before anything
was reported.
