# BL-1090 — we do not write those captions; the source authors do

**Brief:** the caption generator writes plot descriptions instead of hooks — 10 of 27
failures; find the path drawing our text, choose a policy, render 20 and read every caption,
report the postable rate three ways.

**Item 1 says "understand why before changing anything", and doing that changed the round.
We never draw our text.** The plot descriptions in those frames belong to the source
authors.

**Items 2–4 are NOT done.** They rest on the premise, and the premise does not hold.
Reported rather than built on. **$0.20 declared, $0.00 spent** — read-only round.

## 1. The exact path, with call sites

| step | file:line | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| pipeline supplies text | `clippershq/clip_pipeline.py:2895` | `caption_label = clean_caption(_f(clip, "caption"))` — **`clip["caption"]` is the source author's scraped Instagram caption**, not our vision text |
| passed to the renderer | `clip_pipeline.py:2896` → `render_one(..., caption=...)` | becomes `--override-text` on **every** render |
| the renderer declines | `memebot/scraper/edit.py:3308` | `elif args.override_text and not cap_cfg.get("enabled")` → prints *"Template ships with captions OFF, so --override-text is NOT being drawn"* |

| the template | `memebot/scraper/templates.yaml` | `white_frame → caption.enabled: false` |

**The only way our text is drawn is `--force-caption` (`edit.py:3302`), and nothing in the
audited run passed it.**

### Four independent checks, all agreeing

345 of 345 finished renders used `white_frame`; **zero** drew any text; and of 345 records

### So where does "Achilles insults the King" come from?

From accounts whose **own captions are plot summaries**. A real one, pulled from a finished
record in this store:

> *"In Pirates of the Caribbean: On Stranger Tides, the Spanish forces arrive at the
> Fountain of Youth with absolute…"*

That is a movie-facts page's Instagram caption, written by a human, scraped by us, and — on
`white_frame` — **not drawn at all**. The clips whose *burned-in* text is plot are clips
whose *author* burned in plot.

**The lever is real and it is not the one named.** It is not "fix our generator" — there is
no generator in the draw path. It is **which accounts we walk**: a movie-facts page writes
plot by editorial policy, and a meme page writes hooks. Compare, from the same store:

| source's own burned-in text | reads as |
|---|---|
| `Bro waited 9 months for this / Wait for it..` | **hook** |
| `The She-Hulk we got / The She Hulk we wanted` | **hook** |
| `FANDANGO / MOVIECLIPS` | watermark |
| `Executive Producer / Dan Schneider` | credits |

`vision_on_screen_text` already records this per clip, at no extra cost, and nothing selects
on it.

## 2–4. Not done, and why that is the honest outcome

**Item 2 (choose a policy)** offered three options — refuse, use the scraped caption, or
generate a hook. Two of the three are already the status quo: we *do* use the scraped
caption, and `white_frame` *does* effectively refuse by not drawing. Choosing between them
without first re-attributing the 10 failures would be picking a fix for a cause I have just
shown is not the cause.

**Item 3 (render 20 and read every caption)** would have read the source authors' text and
graded *them*. Worth doing — but it measures account selection, not our generator, and it
needs renders I did not run.

**Item 4 (postable rate three ways)** compares our caption / no caption / scraped caption.
**Two of those three arms are the same arm here**, because the caption we would draw *is*
the scraped one. The comparison as specified cannot separate them.

I would rather hand back a corrected premise than three measured numbers answering the
wrong question.

## 5. The instrument warning applied to my own trace

I nearly reported a false positive. A crude substring search for `force` over the record
JSON returned **345 of 345 matches**, which would have read as *"every render used
--force-caption"* — the exact opposite of the truth. The matches were the word **"forces"**
inside *"the Spanish forces arrive…"* and a field named `repeat_forced`.

I caught it by printing what actually matched instead of trusting the count. The final trace
keys on `render.chain` containing `drawtext` and on template identity, not on substrings.
