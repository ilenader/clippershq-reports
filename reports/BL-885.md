### The measurement can no longer be built from a partial log set

`clippershq/tag_yield.py` replaces the scratch script:

* **`discover_logs()` takes a directory and globs it.** There is no parameter that accepts a
  hand-picked subset — *that parameter was the bug*. A test asserts the parameter does not exist.
* **`IncompleteExposure` is raised** when any tag has confirmed pages but no clips recorded in
  any scanned log. A numerator without its denominator now stops the ranking instead of
  dividing by a number from somewhere else.
* **Every row carries `logs_contributing`**, so a figure travels with its denominator's provenance.

12 checks in `tests/test_tag_yield.py`, including one that five logs of 60 clips sum to 300.

Also corrected: the four dead tags surfaced **573** clips for zero pages, not the 231 in
circulation — the same partial-log undercount, in the direction that *understated* the case.

## 3. The provenance signal does not separate — measured, not asserted

You asked for "did someone else film this", cheapest first. I built the two free ones and
**measured them against BL-858's six known failures plus twelve hand-read good pages** (18 paid
calls, $0.0108):

| | n | provenance mean | credited somebody |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **WRONG** (should score low) | 6 | 0.383 | 2 |
| **GOOD** (should score high) | 12 | 0.404 | 5 |
| | | **gap +0.021** | |

**That is not a signal.** A third of the wrong pages credit somebody and more than half the
good pages do not. `@screenrant` and `@theoff.recordfiles` — a publisher posting its own footage
and a conspiracy text-card page — both score *higher* than `@moviezar`, the best page in the pool.

**Cross-account footage fired zero times: 0 of 144 distinct cover frames appeared under more
than one account.** The `viral_shared` fingerprint that worked at 100% precision on captions
does not transfer, because two pages reposting the same scene upload different files with
different cover frames. It needs perceptual hashing over a persistent corpus, not exact frame
hashes within one run.

**So the gate stays off** (`provenance_min: 0.0`). The score is recorded on every row —
including rejected ones — so a later round can set a threshold from measurement rather than
taste. Shipping it as a live cut would have deleted good pages on a +0.021 gap.

### The four candidates, costed against the six known failures

| signal | cost | would it catch the six? |
|---|---|---|
| caption credit markers | **$0** (captions ride free) | **measured: no.** 2/6 wrong pages credit somebody; 5/12 good pages do not |
| same footage on multiple accounts | **$0** per run, but needs a perceptual-hash corpus | **measured: fires 0/144.** Exact frame hashes cannot see re-uploads |
| own face in most frames | $0 API, ~0.3–1.5 s/frame local model over 12 frames/account | would catch `@zackrawrrshorts` (webcam) and **nothing else** — `@sniper_yatinn` has no face on screen, `@primecuttv` posts posters |
| account appears as SOURCE in another page's clips | most expensive: OCR over every stored frame to index watermarks | the strongest in principle; `@jack.potpulse` carries a `netflixnmovies` watermark. Not attempted here |

**Honest verdict: the cheap provenance signals do not work, and the one that probably would
(watermark indexing) costs an OCR pass over the corpus.** That is the finding, and I would
rather report it than ship a gate that scores 0.383 against 0.404.

## 4. Followers captured — and BL-858 was wrong that it is free

BL-858 said follower count "rides free on a call already paid for". **Measured on live
responses for the same account:**

```
  the GQL clips surface   user.follower_count PRESENT   (what the clip library reads)
  the v2 clips surface    user.follower_count ABSENT    (what the account gate calls)
```

`ig_api.medias_endpoint` points at the **v2** surface, so on the shipped configuration it is
**not there**. My first verification run returned `followers=?` for all 18 accounts, which is
how the error surfaced.

So it is captured on the free path when the surface provides it, and otherwise costs **one
`user_by_username` call per CONFIRMED page** — a winner, not a candidate, so roughly half of
gated accounts. Verified live: `user_by_username('moviezar')` → 4,685,171, agreeing with the
clip library.

**Live proof — 25 confirmed pages now carry a follower count** where all 189 previously carried
none:

```

  @nanycoliver          16,447        @smolscorpion         13,643
  @ft.filmedits            313        @movie_mania.07           24
```

And it immediately measures the thing BL-858 could only infer:

| newly confirmed pages | share |
|---|---:|
| under 1,000 followers | **56%** |
| ≥ 10,000 followers | 32% |

**56% of what the gate confirms is in the bucket BL-858 measured at ~91k median views**, against

## 5. The English gate already read the OCR text — the blind spot is diacritics

BL-858 said the gate "reads IG caption text, not the burned-in text". **That is wrong.** `run()`
appends `v.get("ocr_text")` and `account_gate` hands it to `account_language`. It has always
read the burned-in text.

The real blind spot, measured on identical words:

```
  "SE VOCÊ É FÃ DO FÉLIX JÁ DEVERIA ESTAR SEGUINDO ESSA PÁGINA" x4
    with accents      192 letters -> ('rejected', 'lang:portuguese')
    accents stripped  192 letters -> ('unknown',  'language unknown')   <- KEPT
```

`unknown` always keeps, by design. And OCR of a lossy thumbnail drops diacritics — confirmed on
a **committed golden frame**, not a hypothetical: `025.jpg` reads

> `Yo recordando de cada momento que vivimos disfrutamos juntos`

— unambiguous Spanish, 60 characters, **zero accented characters recovered.**

The fix is an accent-blind function-word test that runs **only** where the shipped detector
found no language at all. It cannot overturn an `allowed` verdict, it needs 4 marker tokens and
3 distinct ones, and it never fires when English markers are as common. A standing test asserts
no marker is an English word — `film` and `per` were both in my first draft and both are English.

Verified against the real OCR output of the committed golden frames: every English bar survives.

## Honest limits

- **The provenance signal is a negative result, not a working feature.** It is recorded and off. If you want provenance to actually gate, the next round has to build the watermark index, and that is an OCR pass over the stored corpus — materially more expensive than anything here.
- **The provenance measurement is n=18** (6 wrong, 12 good), and its credit signal ran on **captions only**, not burned-in OCR, because OCR costs minutes per account on this machine. That makes the credit number a **lower bound** — a page crediting only inside the frame counts as crediting nobody. A gap of +0.021 is unlikely to be rescued by that, but I have not proven it.
- **The accent-blind fallback has no live validation set.** It is tested against constructed strings and the real OCR of 25 committed golden frames. I did not re-fetch the five non-English pages BL-858 delivered to confirm it would now reject them — that needs OCR over live thumbnails, which is the slow path. **It is plausible but unproven that it fixes the specific leaks BL-858 named.**
- **The live proof run never exercised the paid-gate rejection reasons.** Every account that reached the gate passed; only `deduped` rejections were captured live. `thin`, `below_consistency`, `non_english` and `provenance` are covered by tests, not by production traffic.
- **I purged 124 rows from the production audit trail.** They were fixtures I caused; the backup is committed alongside. If any of those handles were real, they are in the backup and not in the live file.
- **Spend attribution is approximate.** $0.0120 is unambiguously mine (`BL873_VERIFY`). The other $0.0498 is `REPOST_FINDER` rows timestamped after my run began — but BL-855 and BL-866 also run that funnel, so the true figure is **at most** $0.0618 and possibly less.
- **My proof run added 27 pages to the live master** under the corrected top-5 tags. That is real production data written by a round whose purpose was to fix the gate — the pages are genuine confirms, but they were admitted by the *unfixed* quality bar, and 56% of them are under 1,000 followers.
- **`follower_count` is read at account level only.** BL-850's within-account artefact does not apply, and `clip_walk.py` says so explicitly, but the median-per-account choice is mine and rests on BL-858's 1.3% spread measurement.
