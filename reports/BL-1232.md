## The one-paragraph version

The brief said follower size is the whole game and told me to filter by size before spending
anything else. **Size-filtering is not free on this vendor** — `follower_count` appears exactly
once in its entire OpenAPI spec, in the profile schema, so learning a candidate's size costs
the same call as resolving it. What made the round work was not the size gate but the
**seed choice**: snowballing `suggested` from 65 large meme pages returned candidates that are
87.8% ≥10k and **47.2% carry an email**, against 26.4% and 6.4% for the wide crawl mined last
round — **7.4x the email yield from an identical $0.0006 request.** And the band curve on this
population says size was never the mechanism: sub-1k pages found this way carry an address
**13.2%** of the time against **1.3%** for sub-1k pages found by the old crawl. Same size, ten
times the yield. Follower count was a proxy for *being a professional account*; `suggested`
selects for that directly. Result: **848 passing meme-page emails, 779 new master rows, send
file 158 → 874**, for $3.24.

## 1. The instruction that was wrong, and the measurement that says so

> "A follower count is cheap; OCR is not. Skip or defer anything under ~10k."

`follower_count` occurs **exactly once** in the vendor's 154-path OpenAPI document, inside
`components.schemas.User` — the response of the **profile** endpoint. `UserShort`, the declared
item type of `/v1/user/following`, `/v1/user/followers`, `/v1/media/likers` and
`/gql/user/following`, has nine properties: `pk, id, username, full_name, profile_pic_url,
profile_pic_url_hd, is_private, is_verified, account_badges`. **No count of any kind.**

Confirmed live, not just from the spec: across **4,435** users returned by discovery this
round, the number carrying a follower count was **0**.

So the size gate costs one profile call per candidate — it *is* the spend, 26x discovery. The
gate was still applied (`--min-followers 10000`) and it **cost 120 pages of the 1,724 emailed**,
all in the 1k–10k and <1k bands. At the measured 52.9% pass rate that is ~63 send rows
forgone, against ~$0.07 of judging saved. **On this population the gate is close to a
no-op** — 87.8% of candidates clear it — because the seed choice already did the filtering.

**What rescues the economics is the double-buy fix**, below: the same call now returns the
size, the address and the `user_id`, so sizing, resolving and preparing-to-judge are one
request instead of three.

## 2. Shipped: `profile_and_contact` — one call where there were two

`profile_facts` and `resolve_contact` hit the **same endpoint with the same handle** and
`ig_client` has no response cache, so judging a page bought the identical payload twice.
BL-1230 measured the waste at **$1.37 and 56 minutes per 5,700 pages** and then paid it 281
times in its own harvest. The two functions could not simply be widened — they return disjoint
halves of one response (`resolve_contact` gives the email and drops `user_id`; `profile_facts`
gives `user_id` and drops the email) and each has callers.

`meme_finder.profile_and_contact()` composes them over a single response using **three existing
pieces and no fourth copy**:
- `_facts_from_payload` — the `profile_facts` mapping, extracted;
- `ig_discovery.enrich_profile(..., resp=…)` — a new optional parameter that skips the request;
- `_contact_from_enriched` — the business-field-vs-bio-text split, **extracted so both callers
  share one copy**. That split is what BL-1179 spent two rounds recovering; a second
  implementation would have drifted from it the first time either caller changed.

**Verified 35/35 offline**: one call instead of two, `facts` and all nine contact fields
byte-identical to the old pair, across both wrapper shapes (`bare` and `{"user": …}`) and five
contact cases, plus the failure paths — a raising client returns an error rather than crashing,
and `on_call` fires exactly once, on the **attempt**, before the request.

Effect in production: judging became a **1-call stage**. BL-1230 paid 2.

## 3. Discovery is nearly free; sizing is the entire bill

| stage | calls | cost | output | unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| discovery (65 seeds, `suggested`) | 130 | **$0.078** | 4,435 raw → **4,097 unique fresh** | 34.1 users/call |
| sizing + resolving + `user_id` | 3,650 | $2.19 | 1,724 emails, 3,204 ≥10k | $0.0006/page |
| judging (1 call/page) | 1,604 | $0.9624 | 848 passed | 1.07 s/page, 8 workers |

Discovery costs **$0.000019 per candidate**. Sizing costs 31x that. There is no way around it
on this vendor, which is why the seed choice — not the gate — is the lever.

`suggested` has **no pagination** (one call is the whole result) and needs a numeric `user_id`;
in a snowball that is almost free, because every discovered user arrives carrying its own `pk`,
so only the first hand-typed seeds cost a resolution call.

## 4. Yield per band, re-measured on this population — and the thesis correction

| band | n | emails | rate | Wilson 95% | same band, crawl population |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1M+ | 782 | 378 | 48.3% | [44.9, 51.8] | 39.1% |
| 250k–1M | 730 | 408 | **55.9%** | [52.3, 59.5] | 56.9% |
| 100k–250k | 528 | 285 | 54.0% | [49.7, 58.2] | 41.3% |
| 10k–100k | 1,164 | 533 | 45.8% | [42.9, 48.7] | 29.5% |
| 1k–10k | 355 | 108 | **30.4%** | [25.9, 35.4] | **6.8%** |
| <1k | 91 | 12 | **13.2%** | [7.7, 21.6] | **1.3%** |

**Every band yields more here than the same band did on the crawl population, and the gap
widens as the pages get smaller** — 10x at sub-1k, 4.5x at 1k–10k, 1.0x at 250k–1M. A
follower count cannot explain a tenfold difference between two groups that have the *same*
follower count. What differs is how they were found: Instagram's `suggested` graph clusters
**commercially active** accounts, and a business account is what has a contact button.

**So follower size was a proxy for professionalisation, and the discovery path measures it
directly.** That is a correction to this round's own premise, and it is the most useful thing
here: the next round should optimise the path, not the size threshold.

**Seed size, split honestly:**

| seeds | children | children ≥100k | children with an email |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≥1M followers | 2,178 | **71.9%** | 45.5% |
| <1M followers | 1,472 | 32.2% | **49.9%** |

Snowballing from size **works for size** — 71.9% vs 32.2% ≥100k, confirming the cached-data
prediction of 41.6% vs 13.0% in direction and exceeding it in level. But it **does not buy
email yield**: the smaller seeds' children were slightly *more* likely to carry an address.
Seed for reach; do not expect seed size to raise the address rate.

## 6. A production defect found on the way: the hashtag list is built on a mislabelled metric

`meme_finder.py:73` carries `HASHTAG_YIELD = {"dailymemes": 0.50, "gymmemes": 0.38,
"relatablememes": 0.38}` and `:71 YIELDING_HASHTAGS` is the list every production run walks;
`MEASURED_ZERO_HASHTAGS` excludes `#dogmemes` and `#schoolmemes` at 0.0.

Those numbers are **not page-finding yields**. `reports/BL-1133.md:130` defines them:
*"Yield = the share of WALKED **clips** clearing both the meme-format test and the no-speech
gate"* — denominator **8 clips** per tag. `reports/BL-1139.md:321` relabelled them
*"For finding pages the tags do work, at measured yields"*, and the constant inherited the
relabelling.

In BL-1133's own table, the **pages found** column reads:

| tag | pages found | "yield" (clips) |
|---|---|---|
| #relatablememes | 27 | 38% |
| **#schoolmemes** | **26** | **0%** |
| **#dogmemes** | **25** | **0%** |
| #dailymemes | 24 | 50% |
| #gymmemes | 24 | 38% |

**The two tags production excludes as "measured zero" found more pages than either tag it
keeps.** And 4/8 vs 0/8 is [0.215, 0.785] vs [0.0, 0.324] — touching intervals at n=8, so the
clip metric could not rank them either.

**Not fixed this round.** The snowball path is 34 users/call at 87.8% ≥10k, so hashtags are not
where this budget belonged, and re-deriving the tag list needs its own measurement rather than
a swap of one unmeasured list for another. Recorded with the file:line so the next round can.
