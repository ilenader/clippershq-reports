# BL-1145 — nine vetted design skills, then a left rail and one server instead of two

**One sentence:** ten tabs across the top became a rail down the left edge ordered by what
the operator actually does, the two servers on two ports became one, the standalone harvest
page folded in as a sidebar entry — and every one of the eight figures the new rail shows was
hand-counted against its source first, which caught two rows-versus-records traps before they
shipped.

**Spend: $0.0000 of a $0.10 hard cap.** No paid leg was attempted. No credential was entered
anywhere. Everything installed sits under one directory, reported and sized in §1.4.

### 1.2 The vetting, and it found two things that change how these get used

A skill is **instructions Claude will follow**, steering an agent that already has Read,
Write, Bash and network tools inside a repo holding `master_leads.csv`. So the threat model is
not "this has a bug", it is data egress, credential handling, scope escape and supply chain.
`scratch/bl1145_skills.py` scanned all 106 files for those patterns and printed every hit with
its line for reading in context — 48 flags in hallmark, 6 in industrial-brutalist-ui, 1 in
design-taste-frontend, 0 in the other five.

**30 of the 32 un-negated flags are false positives** — "token" meaning *design token*,
"telemetry" as the name of an aesthetic, "Send the request in the background" describing
optimistic UI. Two are real:

1. **`hallmark/references/study.md` instructs `WebFetch`.** Its `study` verb extracts a design
   direction from a URL. It is **not** an egress path — it fetches *to* the public web and
   sends nothing from the repo — and its SSRF guard is genuinely careful: it refuses non-http
   schemes, raw IP literals, `localhost`, `.local`, `.internal`, and every private, loopback,
   link-local and **cloud-metadata** range including `169.254.169.254`, and re-checks
   redirects. **Verdict: safe as written, and not invoked here** — there is no external URL to
   study.
2. **`hallmark/references/assets.md` instructs CDN embeds** —
   `<img src="https://cdn.simpleicons.org/github">` and a `jsdelivr` `<use href>`.
   **This directly violates the page's zero-external-requests property** and would fail
   `tests/test_dashboard.py`. **Verdict: must not be followed in this repo.** It was not.

One line each, for the record: *design-taste-frontend* sets variance/motion/density dials and
component rules; *emil-design-eng* encodes animation timing and easing (and opens by
recommending a paid course — not an attack, still an instruction to say something unasked);
*hallmark* picks a macrostructure, dresses it in a theme and runs 57 slop gates; the four
*taste-skill* aesthetics lock palette/type/texture tokens; *redesign-existing-projects* scans
an existing codebase and upgrades it without breaking it; *stitch-design-taste* emits a
`DESIGN.md`; *frontend-design* holds one of eight aesthetic anchors and forbids fabricated
demo data.

### 1.3 The one installed this round, vetted line by line

## PHASE 2 — THE REBUILD

### 2.1 The design decision I made against the skill's advice, and why

The `frontend-design` skill says to pick one of eight anchors and commit, leaning
*unexpected* — and warns specifically against letting "it's a data tool" route every brief to
Industrial. Its Swiss anchor (white ground, one grotesque, hairline rules, left-aligned) is
close to a perfect description of a calm control panel, and that is what I started building.

**Then I read this file's own header.** `app.css` records that **INFRA-003 shipped exactly
that** — "a minimal, whitespace-heavy document: a centred column, one thing per screen,
nothing visible until asked for" — and **the operator rejected the aesthetic outright**,
asking instead for full width, density, charts and colour.

So applying a fresh anchor would have re-shipped a design he has already refused. His
complaint this round is not *too dense*; it is **"I wouldn't understand it"** — a
navigation and hierarchy problem wearing an aesthetics costume. The density stays; the
structure changes. That is `redesign-existing-projects`' brief, not `frontend-design`'s, and
I took it deliberately rather than by drift.

### 2.2 The left rail, and the one move that deleted a panel

Ten tabs across the top became a rail down the left, **grouped by what he does**:

```
DO      Paste links  16     Harvest pages  5     Teach a song  4     Watch & post  12
CHECK   Money  $12.09       Clips  2,749         Video  35
SYSTEM  Runs  1             History              All numbers          Settings
```

**The rail carries the standing numbers.** Each entry shows its own live figure on the right
edge in tabular mono, so navigation and status are one object — and that is what let the
nine-card Overview grid stop being the landing screen **without losing a number**: the panel
is still there, last in the rail, renamed "All numbers". Nothing was deleted.

**The rail fetches nothing.** Every value is read out of the payloads `refresh()` has already
retrieved. A rail issuing its own requests would be eight more chances for two panels to
disagree about one payload, and eight more requests on a 5-second poll.

**The tab contract did not change**, which is why every renderer survived untouched: the
sidebar buttons keep the same `role="tab"` / `aria-controls` / `id` triple the horizontal
strip had, on a tablist marked `aria-orientation="vertical"`. The only JavaScript change is
the arrow axis — Down/Up now move, and Right/Left stay bound because `runlog.html` still
renders the horizontal strip and this is the project's only tab handler.

**And one duplication cut.** The Paste view printed every refusal reason in the batch table's
"what happened" column *and again* in a "Skipped, and why" panel below it — 12 refusals
rendered twice, most of why the landing screen was **5,146 px tall**. It is now behind a
`<details>`, one keystroke away and still in the accessibility tree, because the reasons are
his only feedback on which of his picks fail.

### 2.3 The typeface

`Inter-Bold.ttf` — the exact file the renderer already burns into every video — served from
`/font/Inter-Bold.ttf`, **an endpoint on this same server**, never a CDN.

An endpoint rather than a copy into `static/` for two repo-specific reasons: `memebot/` is a
**nested git repo with its own remote**, so copying its 938 KB binary into the parent would
commit memebot's asset to `clipper-finder` and create a second Inter that can drift from the
one on the clips.

**Only weight 700 exists and that is declared honestly.** There is no Inter Regular in this
repo or installed on this machine — both checked. So `@font-face` declares 700 only:
headings, the wordmark and the numerals get real Inter, body copy falls through to the system
grotesque. Declaring one file across `font-weight:100 900` would have made the browser
*synthesise* the other weights by smearing bold outlines, which looks like a font and is not.

**I could not find the message where he names the font.** I searched the round messages and
found nothing; Inter is what this project ships and renders with, so that is what I used. The
inference is flagged rather than dressed up as a quote.

### 2.4 One server, one port

The merge was **already done** in `server.py` by a later round — it installs both
`paste_routes` (5 routes) and `teach_routes` (7) above the static mount. What survived was
`dashboard/paste_app.py`, still telling the operator to run a second server on `:8788`. **That
file was the two-servers problem, not the code.** It is deleted, and `paste_routes.py`'s
docstring no longer describes a handover that has happened.

Measured before: `netstat` showed **8788 and 8791 both listening** — two stale servers. Both
stopped. One server on `:8787` now answers everything:

```
/                        200   /api/paste/songs      200   /api/harvest/result   200
/app.css                 200   /api/teach/songs      200   /api/postable         200
/font/Inter-Bold.ttf     200   (960,832 B, same origin)
dashboard: paste_routes mounted (5 route(s))
dashboard: teach_routes mounted (7 route(s))
```

`/harvest.html` (BL-1139's standalone page) is folded in as the **Harvest pages** sidebar
entry — same endpoints, same server, one entry per thing. The old URL still resolves; nothing
links to it.

connected at all. The brief's instruction for exactly this case is to verify through Playwright
and **say so rather than claim a look I did not take**. This is that: Playwright 1.60.0 driving
bundled Chromium, a real engine executing real CSS, fonts and JavaScript — but **not the
operator's own Chrome profile**, so anything depending on his extensions is out of scope.

**The server was restarted before the pass**, because a prior round's screenshot pass measured
pre-edit code and drew the old chart.

## 4. EVERY NUMBER HAND-COUNTED — the seventh rows-versus-records check, and it caught two

Each figure recomputed **from the files**, not from a second endpoint, because two endpoints
agreeing proves only that they share a bug.

| rail figure | endpoint | hand-count | raw rows fold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clips | 2,749 | **2,749** | 9,729 lines → 2,749 records |
| Video | 35 | **35** | 55 files → 35 videos |
| Watch & post | 12 | **12** | 24 files → 12 pairs |
| Paste links | 16 | **16** | counts.total = items[] |
| Teach a song | 4 | **4** | songs.json |
| Money | $12.089486 | **$12.088286** | drift $0.0012, tolerance $0.005 |
| Harvest survivors | 5 | **5** | harvest_result.json |

**Two traps caught before shipping**, and both would have looked entirely reasonable:

```
/api/videos     videos: 55   distinct: 35    _v01/_v02 are VARIANTS of one clip -> 1.57x high
/api/postable   videos: 24   renders:  12    every pair is an OURS and an ORIGINAL -> 2.00x high
```

My first draft wired `videos` in both places. The rail now shows `distinct` and `renders`, and
the reason is a comment at each call site.

**And I got the hand-count itself wrong twice, which is the more useful finding.** First it
walked every `out/` and `final/` directory in the repo and returned 58 against the endpoint's
55 — because `scratch/*_work/` and every `outputs_*/_work/<clip>/…/final/` holds per-clip
render scratch, not finished video. Then, scoped correctly, it returned 55 against 35 because
it folded on full filename and counted `_v01`/`_v02` as two videos. **Both times the endpoint
was right and the check was wrong.** A hand-count that widens the definition will "catch" a bug
that is its own — which is worth more than the two real traps, because a wrong check is how a
correct number gets edited.

The money drift is **$0.0012 across 975 run rows** — two calls' worth, landing between the two
reads because `spend.json` is shared and other rounds flush to it concurrently. The check now
reads the file first and the endpoint immediately after, and reports the residual as a
tolerance rather than asserting it to zero.

## 7. WHAT I GOT WRONG, AND WHAT IS STILL OPEN

* **I started building the wrong thing.** I picked the Swiss anchor and had begun re-tokening
  the palette before reading that INFRA-003 had shipped and been rejected for being exactly
  that. Caught by the file's own header, which is the argument for those headers.
* **The hand-count was wrong twice** before the endpoint was wrong once. See §4.
* **A repo guard blocked one of my own commands** — a grep pattern containing the literal
  string `p`+`ip install` tripped `pipguard`. Correct behaviour by the guard; my pattern was
  rewritten.
* **Still open, and mine:** the operator's four primary tasks are now obvious, but the Paste
  landing view is still the tallest screen in the product even after folding the duplicate.
  The batch table's "what happened" column carries full paragraphs per row; it wants
  truncation with a per-row expander, which is a further round.
* **Still open, not mine:** `test_doc_citations` (BL-1146's live edit, above), and the three
  large skill collections at 118 MB in `~/.claude/skills` that nobody has claimed ownership of.
