## The answer

**Five tiles is as accurate as twelve. On both platforms, at every count, the accuracy
differences are inside the noise — and twelve is not the best setting on either.**

**But the saving is not large, and the reason is arithmetic he has already been given:** a
judged page is 12,393 tokens and the picture is 5.1% of it. Cutting TikTok from 12 tiles to 5
removes **153 image tokens — 1.23% of a page.** The real saving is elsewhere: **7 of 12
plain-HTTP cover fetches, 58% of the capture stage's network work.**

**And one thing he should know before deciding anything: "12 tiles" is a canvas, not a count.**
The TikTok sheet is a fixed 3×4 slot grid, and a page with six videos fills six slots and
leaves six painted dark. **The median TikTok page in his own marked set shows 9 tiles, not 12.
Only 35 of 100 ever reach 12. Fourteen already show exactly 3.** He has been running a 3–12
tile mixture all along.

## 2. What it actually buys

### Tokens — a small single-digit percentage, and he should not plan around it

A judged page is **12,393 tokens**; the screenshot is **5.1%** of it, output is 80%, and 49.3%
of that output is invisible reasoning that does not shrink when the picture does.

| tiles | TikTok image tokens | Instagram image tokens |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 154 | 462 |
| 5 | 308 | 923 |
| 8 | 461 | 1,385 |
| 12 | 461 | 1,846 |

* **TikTok 12 → 5 saves 153 tokens: 1.23% of a page.** 12 → 3 saves 307: 2.5%.
* **TikTok 8 and 12 cost the same**, because the median page only has 9 tiles — the 12 crop is
  already capped for most pages. That row is the canvas-not-a-count finding showing up in the
  cost table.
* Instagram tiles are ~4× the pixels, so there the saving is real: **12 → 5 saves 923 tokens,
  7.4% of a page.**

**This round's own consumption:** 85 batches, **6,893,999 subscription tokens**, **10,213 per
stimulus** — consistent with BL-1327's 10,483 per page judged. No paid vision call was made.

### Wall clock — this is where the saving is

The contact sheet is built from **one plain-HTTP cover fetch per tile**. At 5 tiles that is 5
fetches instead of 12 — **7 fewer, 58% of the capture stage's network work removed.** BL-1327
measured 2,455 cover fetches for 256 pages and found that once the lane pool landed, **the
cover fetches became the binding stage**, not the vendor. So the tile count is now aimed at the
right target — it is simply worth less than "large".

**Report both honestly, as asked: tokens and wall clock do not move together.** Tokens fall
1.2%; fetches fall 58%.

## 3. Legibility — and the thing that must be said first

**Dropping tiles buys NO legibility.** The sheet is 3 columns in a 465px canvas, so every tile
is 155px wide whether the sheet holds 3 tiles or 12. Removing rows changes the canvas height
and nothing else. Tile width — and therefore text height — is a function of **columns alone**:

| columns | tile width | vs shipped |
|---:|---:|---|
| 3 (shipped) | 155 px | 1.00× |
| 2 | 232 px | 1.50× |
| 1 | 465 px | 3.00× |

**Measured, and it confirms exactly that.** OCR over all 200 grids, one pass each, boxes
attributed to the tile they fall in:

| platform | tile width | median text height | illegible tiles at 3 / 5 / 8 / 12 |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| TikTok | 155 px | **14 px** | **32.0% / 31.1% / 31.4% / 31.0%** |
| Instagram | 294–311 px | **20–21 px** | 18.0% / 21.9% / 23.2% / 24.1% |

**The TikTok row is flat to within a percentage point across a 4× change in tile count.** That
is the arithmetic above, measured rather than argued.

**The natural experiment is on disk and it points the other way.** Instagram's tiles are ~1.9×
TikTok's width, carry **1.5× the text height**, and are **~10 points less often illegible.**
**If he wants legibility, the lever is columns, not rows** — and a 2-column TikTok sheet at
232px is available without fetching anything new.

### The OCR space defect is real, still present, and both platforms fail differently

| platform | lines with a space | verdict |
|---|---:|---|
| TikTok | 781 of 1,681 (**46.5%**) | unsafe |
| Instagram | 941 of 2,308 (**40.8%**) | unsafe |

I did not trust the percentage on its own — a short caption legitimately has no space — so I
read the raw output. It is stripping, verbatim:

* Instagram, 311px tiles: `'WhenItoldherthatIcamefroma'` · `'toxicrelationshipandshestarts'` ·
  `'tastesgoodandshesays'` — every space removed from a full sentence.
* TikTok, 155px tiles: `'plyagththe'` · `'Tinaswhoru'` · `'FOv:Your ite cousinfinaly'` — not
  stripping but **garbling**, which is the 14px text height failing.

**Any word-boundary rule run over this OCR is unsafe on both platforms**, and this is the same
defect recorded at 44.7% before. It is not fixed. It does not affect the judge — the judge reads
the picture, not the OCR — but it invalidates any code rule that greps this text.

## 4. The recommendation

**Set TikTok to 5 tiles. Leave Instagram wherever it is, or set it to 5 too.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **what it costs in wanted pages** | **nothing measurable.** TikTok 5 wanted lost at 12 → 4 at 5. Instagram 1 at both |
| what it saves | 7 of 12 cover fetches (58% of capture network work); **1.23% of tokens on TikTok**, 7.4% on Instagram |
| what to refuse | **3 tiles on TikTok** — it loses 6 wanted of 37 against 4 at five tiles. Two extra pages is small, but he said quality must not drop, and this is a drop |
| do it for | wall clock, not tokens |

**And the measured refusal he asked me to be willing to give: the speed gain is not large.** He
offered to accept a 1–2 point accuracy drop for it. He does not have to — there is no drop to
accept — but he should know he is buying 58% of one stage's fetches and roughly one percent of
the tokens, not a step change. **If he wants a step change on this pipeline, the tile count is
the wrong dial.** BL-1327 measured the two that are not: the vendor lane pool (5.8× on page
throughput) and OCR, which ships on at 32.9 s/page and provably changes zero verdicts.

**The one change here that would visibly improve his experience is not fewer tiles — it is
fewer columns.** Two columns at 232px would roughly halve TikTok's 31% illegible-tile rate,
using the same 12 covers he already pays for. That is worth its own round.

### Two measurement defects I caught in my own instruments

Recording these because both would have produced a confident wrong answer:

1. **The blank-tile test called dark padding "content".** My first version accepted a tile if
   `std > floor` **OR** `mean > floor`. TikTok's padding is a flat dark grey at **mean 24.0**,
   which passed — so it reported **12 filled tiles on all 100 pages** and the whole
   canvas-not-a-count finding would have been invisible. Caught by opening one grid and counting
   by eye. Filled tiles measure std 55–85; padding is **exactly 0.00**, so the mean term was
   deleted rather than retuned.
2. **The Instagram marks join silently scored nothing.** That file nests its payload under
   `"marks"` beside three metadata strings, so iterating the top-level dict yielded **four**
   entries and the arm printed *"his 4 marks, want rate 0.0"* — a broken join that does not
   raise. Every Instagram number above is from after the fix, on all 100.
