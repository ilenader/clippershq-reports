# BL-968 — a day of this system: what it makes, what it costs, and what actually stops it

**Brief:** measure the sustainable daily rate per output type and what binds each; the true
cost per output from receipts only; the sunk cost sitting idle; supply exhaustion and
refresh; whether $100 would buy anything; and a one-page week.

**READ-ONLY.** No code changed, no runs started, no paid calls. Everything below is read
out of `spend.json`, `clip_library/`, `master_leads.csv`, `memebot/runs.jsonl`,
`output/ALL_BOT_READY.csv` and `scratch/songs.json`.

---

## THE ANSWER FIRST

| output | sustainable rate, one 8-hour attended day | binding constraint |
|---|---|---|
| **finished videos** | **226/day** now — **433/day** with one config bug fixed | **WALL CLOCK**, then a **render bug**. Money is irrelevant: $0.000457/render. |
| **labelled clips** | **~0/day at current settings** — the library is already walked out | **SUPPLY.** 166 accounts × 12.1 clips. New clips need new *pages*. |
| **leads** | **~300/day** (median 333 in the metered era) | **SUPPLY** per source, and then a **HUMAN STEP that has never run**. |

**The one number: 0.** Zero of 59,006 leads have ever been sent, and zero videos have been
posted. Every rate above is production into a bucket with no outlet. **The binding
constraint on the business is not any of the three constraints in that table — it is the
human step at the end**, and no amount of money or wall clock moves it.

**Lifetime spend is $9.34 over 23 days.** The system is not cost-constrained and never has
been. It is roughly **$0.47 per active day**, and the single most expensive day in its
history was **$5.05**, of which **$3.12 was a reconstructed estimate**, not a receipt.

Before any cost figure: **35.5% of lifetime spend is not metered.** 13 of 239 ledger rows

| `link_usd` | $0.0000 | $0.0000 | — |
| **`vision_usd`** | **$0.0029** | **$2.1409** | **99.9%** |

> **The entire vision cost line is one reconstructed row.** Campaign `VISION_BACKFILL`,
> 2026-08-01, $2.1409, 1,580 calls, `estimated: true`. Metered vision spend across the

The ledger otherwise reconciles **exactly**: row sums equal the header totals to

## 1. The sustainable daily rate, per output

### Finished videos — WALL CLOCK, and a bug that is costing half the throughput

`memebot/runs.jsonl`, 235 records at the moment of measurement (it grew from 220 to 235
*while this round ran* — two other rounds are rendering concurrently, so treat these as a
snapshot, not a final count):

| status | records |
|---|---:|
| `ok` | 45 |
| `failed:render` | 48 |
| `pending` | 83 |
| *(no status field — legacy rows)* | 59 |

**Success rate of records that reached a terminal state: 48.4%.** And **60% of all records
never reached a terminal state at all**, which is its own problem — a `pending` row is
indistinguishable from a lost one.

MEASURED duration of an `ok` render, `retrieval.retrieved_at` → `render.finished_at`:

```
n=45   median 36 s   mean 61 s   p90 127 s   max 266 s
```

DERIVED from those two MEASURED numbers:

| | seconds per FINISHED video | per 8 h attended day | per 24 h |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| as it stands | 126.9 s | **226** | 680 |
| with the bug below fixed | 66.4 s | **433** | 1,301 |

**41 of the 48 failures are one config bug**, not a ceiling:

```
ambient_bed  (skipped: C:\Users\Game Centar\OneDrive\Desktop\clipper finder not found)
ERROR instagram/moviezar/...: ambient_bed.file='C:\\Users\\Game Centar\\OneDrive\\D...
```

A path that stops at the space in `clipper finder`. Fixing it takes the success rate from
**48.4% to 92.5%** and roughly doubles daily video output for zero additional spend. *(Five
more failures carry `returncode: 0` — recorded as failed while the process exited clean, so
the true failure count may be lower still.)*

**Money is not remotely the constraint here.** 226 videos/day costs **$0.10/day** in render
metering.

### Labelled clips — SUPPLY, and the well is dry

2,003 distinct clips from 6,503 JSONL lines (**ratio 3.25** — line count is not clip count;
the store is append-only with `rev` and reads last-wins, BL-851). **1,916 are vision-labelled
(95.7%)**, across **166 accounts** at 12.1 clips/account.

I cannot give a clips-per-day rate, and that is a finding rather than a gap: **a clip row
carries no acquisition timestamp.** `posted_at` is when the *creator* posted it. There is no
`first_seen`. So the rate at which this library was built is not recoverable from the
library — only from the ledger, where `CLIP_LIBRARY` shows 43 rows and $0.6354 over 4 days.

The binding constraint is supply, and it is nearly exhausted — see §4.

### Leads — SUPPLY, then a human step

59,006 rows; 4,730 with an email (**8.0% fill**); `clipper` 56,047 / `client` 2,899.

The corpus has two eras and they must not be averaged:

| era | days | leads | leads/day | ledger? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2026-06-30 → 07-07 | 6 | **53,388 (90.5%)** | up to 41,658 in one day | **none — predates the ledger** |
| 2026-07-11 → 08-01 | 19 | 5,618 | **median 333**, p25 58, p75 691 | yes |

**90.5% of the lead corpus has no cost record at all.** Any "cost per lead" over the whole
59,006 is therefore uncomputable, and any figure that quotes one is dividing a 19-day cost
by a 25-day output.

The honest, receipts-only figure is the metered era alone: lead-side metered spend **$4.1534**
over **5,618** leads = **$0.00074/lead, or $0.74 per 1,000.**

## 3. What is already paid for and sitting idle

| asset | quantity | idle | measured sunk cost |
|---|---:|---|---|
| labelled clips | 1,916 | **1,881 never rendered (98.2%)** | $1.87 metered clip-side |
| sendable leads | **2,971** in `output/ALL_BOT_READY.csv` | **0 ever sent** | $4.15 metered lead-side |
| hook windows | 21 marked by hand across 4 songs | **16 never used** (only h1–h5 appear in `ok` renders) | operator time, unpriced |

**Total measured sunk cost: $6.03. Roughly all of it is idle.**

**What conversion would cost — and it is almost nothing in money:**

- **1,881 idle clips → videos:** **$0.86** in render metering and **66 hours** of wall clock
  at the current rate (**37 hours** with the `ambient_bed` bug fixed). The clips are already
  bought and already labelled; rendering them is essentially free and entirely time.
- **2,971 sendable leads → sent:** **$0** in vendor cost. `docs/SENDING.md` is written, the
  merge renders all 2,971 rows, and nothing has gone out. This is a human decision, not a
  capability gap.
- **16 idle hook windows:** operator time in `hookmark/`, no vendor cost.

The single largest asset in the system is **2,971 contactable people who have never been
contacted**, and its conversion cost is zero dollars.

## 4. Supply math — how long until each source is exhausted

| source | ceiling | consumed | remaining | refresh |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| repost pages | ~344 | **166 walked** | ~178 | new meme/repost accounts appear — slow, weeks |
| clips (derived) | **~4,162** if every known page is walked at 12.1 clips/page | 2,003 | **~2,153** | pages posting new content — days |
| Twitch in-band channels | ~655 | — | — | new channels crossing the size band — weeks |
| Spotify seed pool | ~777 | — | — | new artists entering the seeds — weeks |

**The whole known clip universe is ~4,162 clips.** At 226 videos/day that is **18 attended
days of video supply** if every remaining page were walked, and **8.4 days** from the
1,916 already labelled. With the render bug fixed, **4.7 days**.

**This is the most important structural fact in the report: the system can render its entire
supply faster than the supply refreshes.** Clip *acquisition* is a page-walking problem
bounded at ~344 pages; video *production* is a 66-second-per-item problem. They are not
balanced, and the imbalance is roughly an order of magnitude.

Lead refresh is the healthiest of the four — creators appear continuously — but the metered
era's ~300/day against 59,006 already banked means **new leads are not the constraint for
years**, at a send rate of zero.

## 5. Would $100 do anything? No. Be blunt.

**$100 buys 178,253 retrieval calls.** At the measured 4.8 calls per library clip that is
~37,000 clips.

**The supply contains ~2,153 more clips.** Money over-buys the available supply by roughly
**17×**. The next $100 would purchase capacity for a universe that does not exist.

Spelled out per lever:

| spend it on | what you would get | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| more clips | supply caps at ~2,153 more; costs **~$2** | **money is not the constraint** |
| more leads | 59,006 banked, 0 sent | **would make the unsent pile bigger** |
| better labels | 95.7% already labelled; re-labelling costs ~$2.60 at the estimated rate | **marginal** |
| more renders | 226/day costs $0.10/day | **wall clock, not money** |

**What the constraint actually is, in order:**

1. **A human step that has never run** — 0 sends, 0 posts. Unblocking it costs $0.
2. **One config bug** — `ambient_bed` path; doubles video throughput for $0.
3. **Wall clock** — 66–127 s per finished video.
4. **Page supply** — ~178 unwalked pages, and they refresh in weeks.
5. **Money** — last, by a wide margin, at $9.34 lifetime.

If the operator wants to spend money usefully, the only lever the measurements support is
**buying more songs**: 4 songs and 21 windows currently gate which clips are matchable at
all, prior rounds put ~1,700 clips in the parked state for want of a matching route, and a
song is a fixed cost that unlocks a recurring supply. That is a *supply-of-routes* purchase,
not a supply-of-clips purchase — and it is the one place where dollars convert into output.

## 6. The one-page week

**At steady state, five attended 8-hour days:**

| | now | with the `ambient_bed` fix |
|---|---:|---:|
| finished videos | **~1,135** | **~2,030** |
| render + retrieval cost | **$0.52** | **$0.93** |
| new clips | ~0 — supply exhausted inside the week | same |
| new leads | ~1,500 (at the metered-era median) | same |
| **vendor spend, all in** | **under $3** | **under $3** |

**Human time required per week:**

| task | time | can it be automated? |
|---|---|---|
| marking hook windows for a new song | ~30 min/song | partially — `hookmark/` exists |
| choosing and buying songs | operator judgement | no |
| reviewing/approving videos before posting | the real unknown — **never once done at scale** | unknown |
| sending to leads | **never once done** | the tooling exists; the decision does not |

**The week's output is gated on the last two rows, and neither has ever been performed.**
Producing 1,135 videos in a week is achievable at a vendor cost under $3. Whether 1,135
videos can be *reviewed and posted* in a week by one person is not something this repository
contains any evidence about, and I will not invent a number for it.

## What I got wrong / could not measure

- **The brief says 172 clip-library accounts; I measure 166.** Both from `clip_library/`,
  last-wins by `clip_id`. I did not chase the 6 — it may be a different counting rule
  (`clip_seen.json` holds 1,453 and 600 entries in two copies) and it does not move any
  conclusion.
- **I could not reproduce $0.0504 for a cold-library video** from receipts; my components
  sum to ~$0.0045. Flagged, not restated.
- **Clips-per-day is unmeasurable** from the clip store — there is no acquisition timestamp
  on a clip row. That is a real instrumentation gap, not an oversight in this round.
- **The render ledger moved during measurement** (220 → 235 records) because other rounds
  are rendering concurrently. All render figures are a snapshot at 2026-08-02 ~14:00.
- **Every DERIVED daily rate assumes failures cost the same wall clock as successes.** I did
  not measure failed-render duration; failures are usually faster, so the derived rates are
  mildly conservative.

## What is still broken, and whose file

- **`ambient_bed` path bug — 41 of 48 render failures.** In `memebot/`, which is its own git
  repo. **Not mine and not touched** — MEMEBOT-071 and MEMEBOT-074 hold render paths right
  now. This is the single highest-value fix in the report.
- **60% of render records never reach a terminal status.** `pending` is written and never
  resolved, so a killed run and a running one look identical. Same owner as above.
- **No acquisition timestamp on a clip row.** Whoever owns `clip_library` next.
- **99.9% of vision spend is one reconstructed row.** The metering gap it papers over
  (BL-886: the IG retrieval leg was never metered) is closed going forward, but the
  historical figure cannot be recovered.
