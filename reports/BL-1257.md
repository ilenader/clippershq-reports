## 0. Two things did not go to plan, and both changed the method

**The Chrome extension was not connected.** `list_connected_browsers` returned `[]`,
`tabs_context_mcp` reported the extension absent, and `switch_browser` found nothing to
switch to — three probes, all negative, with 13 Chrome processes running. Neither deviceId

So the capture moved to **Playwright**, which is this project's own established fallback —
`scratch/bl1198_browser.py` says so in its docstring, for this exact reason. It works logged
out: 12 post tiles in the DOM, HTTP 200, on the first probe.

**It is also strictly better for this job, and the brief's lane ceiling dissolves with it.**
The extension can only screenshot the ACTIVE tab, which is why background tabs failed 0 of 3
with CDP timeouts and why the ceiling was two *windows*. Playwright contexts have no such
constraint: the lane count is a parameter, not a property of Windows. It also gives exact
pixel control, which the capture-size floor experiment *requires*. And logged out there are
no cookies to burn, so "two accounts, one IP" collapses to a plain per-IP rate limit, which
`--gap` respects.

## 1. THE COST LEVER — the screenshot is 13.7% of the bill, not the bill

### 1.1 The formula, validated against his own number

`image_tokens = ceil(w·h / 750)`. His stated 1,844-token 1080p capture is reproduced
exactly by **1080×1280 → 1844**, which pins both the formula and the geometry.

### 1.2 The floor, measured — 14 controls with stored verdicts, re-judged at four sizes

Controls are 7 stored `WANT` + 7 stored `REJECT`, drawn with a fixed seed from the eight
production verdict files. Reading those files reproduces **BL-1247 exactly: 573 lines, 556
distinct handles, 17 judged twice.** Each (page, size) went to its own agent with a fresh
context, so nothing carried between judgements.

| capture | image tok | agreement | lines actually read | tiles ILLEGIBLE |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 934×934 | 1164 | **12/14 = 85.7%** | 86 | 2 |
| 560×560 | 419 | **12/14 = 85.7%** | 88 | 7 |
| 467×467 | 291 | **12/14 = 85.7%** | 86 | 8 |
| 374×374 | 187 | **11/14 = 78.6%** | 84 | **10** |

**The floor is 467×467 (291 tokens).** Agreement is flat from 1164 tokens down to 291 and
breaks at 187.

**The break has a mechanism, not just a percentage.** `mcu_universe__` is a stored REJECT.
At 934, 560 and 467 the judge resolved three low-band burned-in speech tiles and fired the
`dialogue` disqualifier. At 374 it could only resolve one or two, so `dialogue` never fired
and the page passed. The verdict flipped *because the burned-in text stopped being readable*
— exactly the failure the experiment was built to catch.

**Agreement is the lagging indicator; ILLEGIBLE is the leading one.** The illegible-tile
count rises monotonically 2 → 7 → 8 → 10 while agreement sits flat, then the verdict goes.
That counter exists only because the judging prompt forbids guessing an unreadable line and
requires it be counted instead. Without that rule the degradation is invisible until it has
already changed an answer.

**Operating point: 560×560, one rung of margin above the floor.** Same accuracy as full
size, 419 tokens against 1,844 — a **77% cut** on the image — and it is not sitting on the
cliff edge. The 100 pages were judged there.

### 1.3 The finding that matters more — what the procedure actually sends

Measured from disk, not assumed:

| per-page item | tokens |
|---|---:|
| judge brief `scratch/bl1242_brief_v4.md` (21,140 chars) | **5,420 – 5,872** |
| reference card `bl1240_reference_card.png` (1202×1074) | **1,722** |
| grid capture @560 | 419 |
| everything else read and written | ~151 |

The brief and the card are **7,142–7,594 tokens of fixed overhead**, and *"reset context per
page"* — which is correct, and which I followed — means they are re-sent on every single
page. The reference card alone is a bigger image than the grid shot it is there to explain.

| route | tok/page | pages per 1M |
|---|---:|---:|
| as the procedure runs today (@934) | 8,909 | 112 |
| + capture at 560×560 | 8,164 | 122 |
| + reference card 1202×1074 → 600×536 | 6,871 | 146 |
| + brief and card cached as a shared prefix (0.1×) | 1,199 | 834 |
| + brief trimmed 21,140 → ~7,000 chars, then cached | **802** | **1,247** |

**So: the target of 1,000 pages inside 1M tokens is reachable, but not by shrinking
screenshots.** Even a *zero-token image* leaves 7,745 tok/page. The screenshot lever moves
8,909 → 8,164, which is 9.4% of the distance to the ceiling. The two levers that actually
get there are **caching the brief and card as a shared prefix** and **trimming the brief** —
it is 21KB of history and rationale, most of which a judge does not need to apply the rules.

### 1.4 A second lever the brief does not mention: stop paying for the header

Followers, following, post count, bio and the address are **DOM text** — free. Only the grid
has to be pixels. In a full 1080×1280 profile frame the header and highlight bubbles eat 540
of 1280 rows, **42% of the picture and 42% of the bill**, to re-show what the text already
gave away. Clipping to the grid gives **9 tiles instead of 6 at 1,164 tokens instead of
1,844**: more evidence, 37% cheaper, before any downscaling.

### 1.5 Do not split the model — confirmed, and say it plainly

The model that sees the image is the model that judges it. There is no way to have something
cheap take the picture and something strong think about it, because the picture *is* the
input to the thinking. BL-1244 already measured the cheaper model at **70.0% against 74.0%**
while returning "high confidence" on **50 of 50 and being wrong on 15** — a confidence signal
that carries no information cannot be used to route, so nothing can be routed. Nothing in
this round changes that.

## 3. THE RUN — 2 lanes, 100 pages, a written reason on every one

**Capture:** 100 pages, 100 ok, **0 errors**, 99 with a grid, **526.3 s wall clock across 2
Playwright lanes = 11.40 pages/min**, 50 pages per lane. One round in flight (this one).

Three capture defects were found and fixed by measurement, not by looking:

1. **The dismissal ate the grid.** Removing every `role="dialog"` *and* `role="presentation"`
   took tiles from 12 → 0 on all four probe pages, because Instagram wraps real content in
   `presentation`. The fix is a rule: never remove a node that contains grid links. The
   capture now records `tiles_before`/`tiles_after` and treats a dismissal that destroyed the
   grid as a **failed capture**, because the first version shipped four all-zero pages

2. **A viewport clip is clamped and does not say so.** A 934×934 clip at y=544 came back

   twelve posts would have been judged as having six. Fixed with a 2200px-tall viewport, which

## 4.5 This round leaked an address into `scratch/`, and the guard caught it

Recorded because it is the same failure the project has now hit three times, and because it
was caught by a check rather than by luck.

`scratch/bl1257_probe.py` stored `body[:400]` — the first 400 characters of the profile's own
rendered text — into `scratch/bl1257_probe.json`. `scratch/` is **tracked**. On the very
first probe page that slice contained the account's bio address in the clear.

`tools/write_point_guard.py` found it: **1 real address across 22 tracked files.** The other
90 email-shaped hits are my own screenshot filenames: the capture joins the handle to the
pixel size with an at-sign and ends in `.png`, which is exactly the shape of an address with
a domain, so the regex matches every one. That is worth knowing, because a scanner that cries
wolf 90 times is a scanner someone stops reading. **It was never committed**;
`git log --all -- scratch/bl1257_probe.json` is empty, and it was untracked at the moment the
guard ran.

The publish scanner then made the point for me, twice: it refused this very report, and the
only thing it flagged was the example filename I had written out in full one paragraph above
— then refused again when I replaced it with a placeholder of the same shape. Zero live lead
addresses both times; one false positive, caused entirely by *describing* the false positive.
The naming convention is the real defect — a future capture step should join the size with a
dash rather than an at-sign, and 90 phantom hits go away.

Both halves are fixed: the file was repaired through `write_point_guard.redact`, and the
script now redacts **at the write point** with the incident written into its header so the
next person to add a field to that record knows why the import is there. Re-run after the
fix: **0 real addresses in 22 tracked files.**

The general lesson, again: **a page's own rendered text is lead data.** It is not a caption
or a field you chose to store — it is whatever the page happened to say, and pages say their
email addresses out loud.

**And a screenshot is lead data the guard cannot read.** The same probe left five PNGs in
`scratch/bl1257_shots/`, and the full-size one has that bio address rendered legibly on
screen. No text scanner will ever flag it. They were deleted rather than committed — every
capture this round lives in `output/bl1257_fresh100/`, which is gitignored. Worth a rule:
**no page screenshot belongs in a tracked directory**, because the redaction discipline that
protects `.json` and `.md` does not reach a picture.

## 6. What I would do next, in order

1. **Cache the brief and the reference card as a shared prefix, and trim the brief.** That is
   the whole 1,000-page problem. Everything else is rounding.
2. **Shrink the reference card.** 1202×1074 → 600×536 saves 1,293 tokens per page, more than
   the entire capture costs at 560. Nobody has ever downscaled it.
3. **Ship the grid crop.** 9 tiles at 1,164 tokens beats 6 tiles at 1,844, for free.
4. **Decide what "already judged" means, in writing.** 43 vs 557 fresh pages turns entirely on
   whether a BL-1232 verdict counts, and BL-1237 says it should not. Whichever way it goes, it
   should be a recorded decision rather than a filter each round re-invents.
5. **`creator` at 37.5% of rejects is a discovery problem.** The judge is doing its job; the
   supply is full of people posting themselves. Cheaper to filter at harvest than to judge.
