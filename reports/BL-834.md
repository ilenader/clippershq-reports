## The headline: my own BL-832 number was wrong by 18×

BL-832 said 1,550 crawl rows name an Instagram handle in a bio, and I estimated "realistically 800–1,100 genuine". Building a proper extractor and reading the output:

| level | rule | handles | precision (hand-checked) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1 strict** | `instagram.com/<h>` URL, or the whole word *instagram* + separator + token | **42** | **~33%** (13–15 of 42) |
| **L2 medium** | L1 + `ig`/`insta` as a whole word + token | **250** | **~56%** on the L2-only slice (14 of 25) |
| **L3 loose** | L2 + a lone bare `@mention` | **5,752** | **~11%** (2 of 18) |
| **L4 refined** | L2 **and** the token is handle-*shaped* | **88** | **~90%** (≈9 wrong of 88) |

**None of these is 1,550.** BL-832's figure came from a pattern that matched the word following any occurrence of "ig", which in practice matches ordinary prose. The defensible number is **88**, and even that needs a caveat.

### Why the strict level fails — it captures the next English word

All 42 L1 hits, read individually. The failure is uniform: bios say *"Instagram too"*, *"instagram after effects"*, *"Instagram Follow"*, *"find me on Instagram"* — and the extractor takes the next token as a handle.

Genuine: `x_animevideos_x`, `stverc`, `neelstuffs`, `lu.k4ya`, `magical_girl_kate`, `de.spina750`, `bigsnakob`, `viralanimee`, `dominite16.scp`, `swales_scp`, `blurrydamelios`, `matching_attitude`, `tmn.fan`.
Not handles: `too`, `follow`, `quality`, `inactive`, `after`, `everyone`, `primarily`, `plz`, `jaja`, `powerlifting`, `kindergarten`, `facebook`, `beats`, `style`, `some`, `under`, `has`. One (`juzottcontact`) is the local part of the email in the same bio.

### The refined rule, and the defect it exposes

**L4 = an `ig`/`insta`/`instagram` label AND the captured token looks like a handle** — it contains a digit, a dot or an underscore, or it equals the TikTok handle — and is never an email local part present in the same bio. That gives **88 candidates** (43 matching the TikTok handle, 27 with a digit, 18 with a dot/underscore), and reading all 88 puts precision near **90%**.

But the residual errors are the wrong kind. Three are plainly wrong tokens (`payhip.com`, `2nd`, `60k`). **Four are one character off**, because my handle grammar refuses a trailing underscore:

| bio says | extracted | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `ig john.zatara_` | `john.zatara` | different account |
| `Ig beaupre96_` | `beaupre96` | different account |
| `Ig hemsparker_` | `hemsparker` | different account |
| `Insta:- xero.amv_(150k+)` | `xero.amv` | different account |

A miss costs nothing. **A one-character-off handle sends the DM to a real stranger**, which is the exact harm you named.

## Why I did not write them

Three numbers decide it.

**Size.** 88 handles against an IG-reachable pool of 3,319 is **+2.7%**, not the +47% BL-832 projected. It does not change the shape of the DM channel.

**Expected yield.** This cohort — TikTok accounts declaring an IG handle — was measured in an earlier round at **6% / 1.2% response against 22.7% for cold IG**. And these are worse than that cohort: they are people who put the handle in *prose* rather than in the profile field. 88 × ~90% correct × 1.2–6% ≈ **1 to 5 replies**.

**Cost of being wrong.** ~9 of the 88 are wrong, ~4 of them one-character-off to a real account. So the trade is **1–5 replies against ~4 DMs to strangers**, in a channel whose entire advantage is that it carries no reputation risk.

Your own rule settles it: *a wrong handle is worse than none*. **Recommendation: do not write, and drop the idea unless verification is added first.**

## What verification would take (item 2)

**Free:** fetch `instagram.com/<handle>` unauthenticated and read 200 vs 404. Real, but the repo already documents IP-block risk for unauthenticated Instagram scraping (`ig_follower_scrape` is default-off for exactly this reason), and 404 only proves existence — not that the account belongs to this creator.

**Paid and decisive:** one profile lookup per handle, **88 × $0.0006 = $0.053**. It returns the profile, so you can check the follower count and bio against the TikTok account and confirm identity rather than mere existence.

**This is the gate to put in front of any write.** Five cents converts an 90%-precision guess into a verified list, and it is the only version of this work I would recommend running.

| measurement | value |
|---|---|
| ledger runs with a parseable timestamp | 113 of 113 |
| ledger spans | 2026-07-11 → 2026-08-01 |
| master spans | 2026-06-30 → 2026-07-31 |
| **master rows added before the first ledger run** | **53,735 of 58,354 = 92.1%** |
| sampled rows with exactly ONE run within ±2h | **3.8%** |
| sampled rows with 2+ runs within ±2h (ambiguous) | 4.7% |
| sampled rows with NO run in window | 91.4% |

The only join available is time, and it does not work: **92% of the master predates the ledger entirely**, and of the remainder only about one row in twenty-six falls inside exactly one run's window. Anything else would be attribution by guess.

**Backfill is not possible. Attribution only works going forward**, and only if a run stamps its rows at write time — which is a one-line addition to each funnel's row builder (`run_id`), not an analysis problem.

## Limits

Precision figures are my own adjudication by reading bios, not an independent label set: all 42 L1 rows, 25 sampled L2-only rows, 18 sampled L3-only rows, and all 88 L4 rows — 173 judgements, one reader. The L4 precision of ~90% is a point estimate on 88 items; the true value could reasonably sit between 85% and 95%. Handle grammar rejects trailing underscores and truncates at 30 characters, and both defects are demonstrated above rather than fixed.
