**Headline:** today it is **24 clippers and $390.50** platform-wide (BL-638 measured the somesome subset: 20 clippers, $366.82, on 2026-07-23). **$383.61 of it is one event** — BL-584's 2026-07-18 bulk retirement — and only **$6.89** has been added by the daily cron in the six days since. It is a **display bug, not lost money**: the earnings are real and untouched; one screen shows them as available while the withdrawal path, following the platform's own consistent rule, refuses them.

---

## PART 0 — the plain-English explanation

A clipper's earnings page shows one number: "available to withdraw." When they press withdraw, a second piece of code decides how much they may actually take. **Those two pieces of code do not count the same clips, so the first number can be bigger than the second.**

The difference is clips whose **video is gone** (deleted, taken down, or set private on Instagram). When a video disappears, the platform marks its clip "video unavailable." From that moment, almost every money calculation on the site stops counting that clip — including the withdrawal check. But **the earnings page forgot to add that same exclusion**, so it keeps counting the gone clips. The clipper sees money that the withdrawal button will not hand over.

Nobody lost a cent. The earnings were written when the videos were live and are still stored exactly as they were. This is purely about which screen counts which clips.

### A real clipper, redacted, with real figures

Clipper `3159ac` earned **$147.61** on somesome, every dollar of it on clips whose Instagram videos were taken down in the 2026-07-18 sweep. Their earnings page shows **$147.61 available**. Their withdrawal check counts only clips whose video is still up, and they have **none** left, so it offers **$0.00**. They have withdrawn nothing and been penalised nothing; the stored $147.61 is intact. But the two screens disagree by the full $147.61, and that clipper reasonably believes they have $147.61 waiting that they cannot get. That is the whole bug, one person at a time, 24 times over.

## PART 2 — why does this money exist at all?

### Was it legitimately earned? Yes, without qualification.

Each of these dollars was written by `writeClipEarnings` on a normal tracking tick while the video was live and returning views. Earnings are `views x cpm` (`earnings-calc.ts`); the views were real, the clipper did the work, and the amount was computed and stored the same way as any paid clip. Nothing about the money is provisional or erroneous.

### Was it payable before the clip was stamped? Yes.

The withdrawal gate excludes only `videoUnavailable = true`. Before 2026-07-18 these clips had `videoUnavailable = false`, so the gate counted them and the clipper could have withdrawn them. **The stamping is precisely what removed the withdrawability of money that was withdrawable the day before.** No earned amount changed; its payability did.

### Is refusing to pay a gone clip deliberate policy, or an accident? It is BOTH, on two different layers, and the distinction is the whole decision.

**Layer 1, the exclusion rule itself: DELIBERATE and consistent.** Every money sum on the platform except the earnings page excludes `videoUnavailable`: the withdrawal gate (`payouts/route.ts:411`), the campaign clip-side spend (`balance.ts` `getCampaignBudgetStatus`, `videoUnavailable:false`), the owner-earnings display guard (`docs/runbooks/money-and-earnings.md:604`: owner shows $0 when `clip.videoUnavailable === true`), and CLAUDE.md's standing rule ("add `videoUnavailable: false` to ALL queries summing APPROVED clip earnings"). By the codebase's own consistent design, a gone clip's earnings are frozen and do not count as withdrawable. **The withdrawal gate is behaving as the platform's rule intends; the earnings page is the lone violator of that rule.** So on this layer, the DISPLAY is the accident.

**Layer 2, who is allowed to declare a clip gone: here is the real tension.** The flag was designed for a human decision. `docs/runbooks/tracking-and-cron.md:99` and `:154` are explicit: "Manual `videoUnavailable=true` flag is reserved for owner action on truly-dead clips" and "**DO NOT** auto-flip `clip.videoUnavailable` — that flag is reserved for OWNER action per spec." The same doc notes earnings **already** freeze on their own when a video stops returning views (the formula has no accumulator), so nothing about cost-saving *requires* touching this payment-affecting flag.

**Yet BL-584 (one-off) and BL-590 (a daily cron) now set that flag automatically, for a tracking-cost reason** — to stop paying Apify/HikerAPI to probe dead Instagram URLs. That is the side effect the owner rightly worried about: a flag whose payment consequences were designed around a deliberate human "this video is truly dead" call is now being flipped by an automated cost optimisation, and each flip silently makes earned, previously-payable money unpayable. The retirements were at least *evidence-based* (BL-584 retired only clips returning a fresh live HTTP 404 at the moment of retirement, and re-probing had already revived 24 of 734 candidates), so the clips are genuinely 404. But "the URL 404s" and "the clipper should lose money they earned for real past views" are two different questions, and the automation quietly answered the second by answering the first.

### Was the campaign, or the owner's share, charged for money the clipper cannot get?

Asymmetric, and worth stating precisely (this is BL-642's finding, re-confirmed here):

* **Clipper side: the campaign was NOT charged.** `getCampaignBudgetStatus`'s clip aggregate filters `videoUnavailable:false`, so the moment these clips were stamped their earnings dropped out of the budget's clip-side spend. The budget shows *more room*, not a charge.
* **Owner side: still counted.** The legacy `spent` figure adds the owner's agency accrual **unfiltered** (`balance.ts` `ownerAgg`, no `videoUnavailable` filter), so the owner's share on these same gone clips still sits in `spent`. The BL-164 cap math uses the *filtered* `ownerAggApproved`, so only the display-side `spent` carries the phantom owner amount — a known, safe-direction over-count (it makes a campaign auto-pause slightly early), not a charge against the clipper.

So no one was "charged" for the clipper's frozen money; it simply stopped counting on the clipper side while the owner's share on the same clips kept counting on the display path.

## PART 3 — what are the 20 (24) owed?

**Direct answer: by the platform's own rule as written in code, they are owed the DISPLAY, not the dollars — i.e. the honest fix is to stop showing the $390.50, not to pay it. But that rests on a policy the owner has never actually decided out loud, and he should decide it before the display is "fixed" to hide the debt.**

**The case for paying them.** They earned it for real views on real work, and it was withdrawable until an automated cost-saving job took it away. If a clipper posts a clip, it gets a million legitimate views, they earn their CPM, and then the poster later deletes the video, the clipper still did exactly what the deal asked. Refusing the money in that case is not a display bug, it is taking earned wages from clippers, which is far worse than a cosmetic glitch. The fact that the flag was "reserved for owner action" and is now flipped by a cron makes this worse, because no human ever decided these particular clippers should not be paid.

**The case against paying them.** A CPM deal implicitly buys *lasting* placement; a deleted video delivered a spike and then vanished, and a platform may reasonably decline to pay for content the brand can no longer point to, and may worry that "post, farm views, delete" becomes an exploit if deleted-video views always pay. The entire codebase already encodes this stance: the withdrawal gate, the campaign spend, and the owner display all refuse gone-clip earnings. That is not one forgotten filter; it is a consistent rule with the earnings page as the single exception.

**What the owner's own rule appears to be, from the code:** *do not pay gone clips.* Four independent surfaces enforce it; only one violates it. So the code's revealed intent is that the display is wrong. **Recommendation: fix the display (PART 4a), because it makes the site tell the truth immediately and takes nothing from anyone that the platform's rule ever intended them to have** — with one loud caveat: if the owner's actual intent is that genuinely-earned past views should be paid even when the video later dies, then the rule itself is wrong, not just the display, and he wants PART 4b instead. That is a policy call only he can make; the code cannot make it for him, and $390.50 is small enough to pay if he decides the clippers earned it.

## PART 4 — the fix, both directions

Neither option recalculates a single clipper's earned amount. Both touch only visibility or payability.

### (a) Make the display match the gate — RECOMMENDED

**Change:** add `videoUnavailable: false` to `clipWhere` at `src/app/api/earnings/route.ts:64`, so it reads `{ userId, isDeleted:false, videoUnavailable:false, campaign:{ isTestCampaign:false } }`, matching the gate at `payouts/route.ts:411`.

**Exactly which numbers move:** the 24 clippers' *displayed available* drops by their phantom column (`3159ac` $147.61 to $0.00, `20d221` $70.19 displayed-available down by $60.06, and so on); the total $390.50 disappears from earnings-page balances. **No stored earning changes. No withdrawable amount changes** (the gate already ignored these). Every other clipper is byte-identical.

**What must be proven:** that the per-clip earnings array and the headline balance are the only consumers of `clipWhere`, and that adding the filter does not hide a clip's *history* row (the earnings page also lists clips; the owner may want gone clips still listed as "video unavailable, frozen" rather than vanishing, so the filter belongs on the balance SUM, not necessarily on the clip LIST). Confirm the marketplace sub-queries (`:81`, `:98`) already filter `videoUnavailable:false` so they are unaffected. Re-run the 24-clipper query and confirm all phantoms read $0.

**Rollback:** remove the one filter clause. Pure display; nothing in the database to undo.

**Risk if it is the wrong call:** this makes a real debt invisible. If the clippers were owed the money, option (a) hides it rather than resolving it.

### (b) Make the gate match the display — pay them what they earned

**Change:** remove `videoUnavailable: false` from the withdrawal gate at `payouts/route.ts:411` (and consistently at `:119`, `:162`, `:441`, `:491`) so retired-clip earnings become withdrawable again. This aligns the gate with the earnings page instead of the reverse.

**Exactly which numbers move:** the same 24 clippers' *withdrawable* rises to match their displayed available, releasing **$390.50** in total for withdrawal (`3159ac` gains $147.61 of withdrawable, etc.). **No stored earning changes** — only the ceiling the gate enforces.

**What must be proven, and why this needs the owner:** that releasing these does not breach any campaign budget cap (somesome is at/over its frozen split; paying its clippers their frozen earnings must be checked against the L1 budget lock in `clip-earnings-writer.ts`), that it does not re-open the "post, farm, delete" exploit, and that it is applied consistently across all five gate sites plus the campaign spend so the owner side and clipper side agree. This is a **policy reversal**, not a bug fix, and it spends $390.50 (growing) of real money, so it is the owner's decision, not an engineer's.

**Rollback:** restore the filter clauses; already-approved withdrawals cannot be un-paid, so this direction is only partly reversible once a payout settles — which is exactly why it needs sign-off first.

**Recommendation:** **(a)**, unless the owner decides the platform's rule itself is wrong. (a) tells the truth today at zero cost and takes nothing the rule ever granted; (b) is a deliberate, owner-authorised policy change that pays a real (if small) debt and must be proven safe against the budget caps first. The safest sequence is: the owner rules on the policy question in PART 3; if "do not pay gone clips," ship (a); if "pay earned views," ship (b) as its own guarded, budget-checked round.

It clears only newly-dead Instagram clips (a few per day: 6, 1, 1, 4, 9, 1 over the last six days), and most carry cents, so the payable gap grows far slower than the clip count. **Projection: roughly $390.50 + ~$35 = about $425 in a month**, decelerating, because the large dead-clip backlog was already drained by BL-584 and the cron now catches only the trickle of freshly-dead videos. It will not compound toward the $3,531 of total frozen earnings unless a second mass-death event (another campaign's clips being taken down en masse) occurs; a single such event, like 07-18, would move it far more than a month of the cron.

So the urgency is not the cron's drip; it is that the underlying rule question is unresolved while a daily job keeps quietly converting small amounts of earned money into unwithdrawable money, and the next bulk retirement would add hundreds at once.

**Watch query the owner can run any time** (read-only; the live figure, redacted):

```sql
WITH clip_agg AS (
  SELECT cl."userId",
    SUM(cl.earnings) FILTER (WHERE cl.status='APPROVED' AND cl."isDeleted"=false AND cl."videoUnavailable"=false) AS a_live,
    SUM(cl.earnings) FILTER (WHERE cl.status='APPROVED' AND cl."isDeleted"=false) AS a_disp
  FROM clips cl JOIN campaigns c ON c.id=cl."campaignId"
  WHERE c."isTestCampaign"=false GROUP BY cl."userId"
),
pay AS (
  SELECT pr."userId",
    COALESCE(SUM(CASE WHEN pr.status='PAID' OR (pr.status='VOIDED' AND pr."paidAt" IS NOT NULL)
      THEN COALESCE(pr."actualPaidAmount",pr.amount) ELSE 0 END),0)
    + COALESCE(SUM(CASE WHEN pr.status IN ('REQUESTED','UNDER_REVIEW','APPROVED')
      THEN COALESCE(pr."actualPaidAmount",pr.amount) ELSE 0 END),0) AS money_out
  FROM payout_requests pr JOIN campaigns c ON c.id=pr."campaignId"
  WHERE c."isTestCampaign"=false GROUP BY pr."userId"
)
SELECT COUNT(*) AS clippers, ROUND(SUM(gap)::numeric,2) AS phantom_total
FROM (
  SELECT GREATEST(ROUND(COALESCE(ca.a_disp,0)::numeric,2) - ROUND(COALESCE(p.money_out,0)::numeric,2), 0)
       - GREATEST(ROUND(COALESCE(ca.a_live,0)::numeric,2) - ROUND(COALESCE(p.money_out,0)::numeric,2), 0) AS gap
  FROM clip_agg ca LEFT JOIN pay p ON p."userId"=ca."userId"
) t WHERE gap > 0;
```

Today this returns `24, 390.50`.

* **Whether the 07-18 videos are all still dead today** was not re-probed; that is a live-HTTP question outside a read-only DB audit. BL-584 confirmed a fresh 404 per clip at retirement time, and BL-638 noted the auto-restore path is not currently firing for the somesome set, so they are effectively permanent until a poll revives them.
* **Exact per-clipper attribution of phantom to source** (BL-584 vs cron) was computed in aggregate ($383.61 vs $6.89) rather than per row; the per-row split would require joining each clipper's gap to the stamp date of each of their gone clips, which does not change any total here.
