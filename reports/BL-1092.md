## The seventh way to miscount, found by doing it

This is the sixth rows-versus-records check in the project. It found a new one, and it found
it by **being wrong twice first**.

**Wrong the first time — the key.** I grouped ledger lines on `record_id or run_id`. 22 of 944
lines carry either. The check reported **22 records and ZERO finished videos against 391 `ok`
lines** — a 42.9x error in the *opposite* direction to the five it was written to catch. A
count is only as good as its key.

**Wrong the second time — and this is the finding.** With the key fixed to `render_id`, my
hand-count said 448 and `clip_pipeline.read_records` said 447. One record apart, on a file
being appended to live. Freezing the bytes and counting the same snapshot both ways isolated
it to a single phantom record keyed `?@?`:

> **`memebot/runs.jsonl` holds TWO KINDS OF RECORD.** Renders, and `kind: "outcome"` lines
> recording how a posted video performed. They share the file *by design* — that is how
> `outcome_loop` joins a video to its numbers. An outcome line carries `record_id` but **no
> `render_id`, no `output`, no `clip_id` and no `ts`**, so every one of them collapses into
> one bucket unless excluded.

`clip_pipeline.iter_records` already excludes them and was right all along. 48 of 959 lines
are outcomes. The production reader is correct; **the trap is live for anything new that
counts this file**, and this round's own checker fell in it.

A third self-inflicted one worth recording: my verifier probed three plausible key names for
the finished-video count, got `None` from all three, and printed **MISMATCH against a number
that was correct**. The checker can be the broken half.

## 1. The 12 postable videos, on the page

`/api/postable` parses `START_HERE.md` — **the markdown is the source, not a second index**.
Re-deriving the ranking or attribution here would create a second answer that drifts the
moment the next batch lands.

Each of the 12 carries its **source permalink**, **ledger `record_id`**, **song @ applied
window**, and the human's note on why it works. Watch it inline, **Copy attribution**, or
**Record an outcome** (which fills the form below with that video's `record_id`).

**Proven, not asserted:** the player was driven and read back —
`currentTime 2.05s, readyState 4, duration 20s` off `/api/postable/video/23da2c13867b2f9f`.
A screenshot of a `<video>` element proves markup, not playback.

Counts are reported **separately and compared**: `rows` from the markdown, `videos` from the
folder, with a `disagreement` field. They agree at 12/12 today. A batch where they do not is a
batch where somebody posts a video with another clip's permalink under it — a takedown with
the wrong creator's name on it.

**Streaming is allowlisted by the parsed rows**, addressed by a 16-hex hash so there is no
path to traverse. `../../../etc/passwd` → 404, malformed → 400, range requests → 206.

## 2. Recording an outcome, from the browser

`POST /api/outcome` → `outcome_loop.append_outcome`. **One implementation** — the same
function `python -m clippershq.outcome_loop import` calls. No second store, no re-derived rate.

**A BLANK FIELD RECORDS NOTHING, NEVER ZERO.** Proven end to end from the browser onto a
**copy** of the ledger, with four boxes deliberately left empty:

```json
{"record_id": "3739691966683559087_74742599861__20260803T172451Z", "rev": 2,
 "kind": "outcome", "views": 2048, "post_url": "https://www.instagram.com/reel/BROWSERTEST/"}
```

`views` is there. **`likes`, `comments`, `saves` and `shares` are not keys on that line at
all** — not `0`, not `null`, absent. The page said so too: *"Left blank (recorded as nothing,
not zero): likes, comments, saves, shares."*

The mirror case is pinned by a test: **a deliberately typed `0` IS recorded.** And a typo is
refused by name rather than coerced — `12k` → *"'12k' is not a whole number — type digits
only, or leave it blank to record nothing for it"*. `1,204` parses to 1204, because the
operator is copying off a phone screen.

The real `memebot/runs.jsonl` was **never written to** by this round.

## 3. The decision log, prominently — and all three kinds of nothing

On the **Overview** tab, where the operator lands, as *"Why the last run made what it made"*.
**Not a second renderer**: `stageTableHTML` is mounted twice, once here and once in History's
detail card. `index.html` already carried the warning that two renderers of one decision log
is two things to keep in step.

The three states were all being served and none was rendered. Now, as **words, never colour
alone**:

| | means | shown as |
|---|---|---|
| **SILENT** | it ran, took input, emitted nothing — the logic is wrong | tag + `took 8 and emitted NOTHING` |
| **DEGRADED** | it ran and produced output, but warned — the number is real and too small | tag + the warning text |
| **NEVER RAN** | never reached; nothing is wrong with it, look upstream | a run-level block, **not** a `0 in, 0 out` row |

And the measured value beside the reason, exactly as briefed:

```
duration Ns outside [N N]     3.1s–7.41s < 7.626s        385
```

The reason keeps its digits normalised out because **it is the grouping key** — a float in the
reason gives every clip its own bucket and the ranked summary stops ranking. The values travel
beside it as a range.

**This needed a fixture and that is said out loud.** The live log has 25 runs, 7 with a SILENT
stage, and **zero** with `never_ran` or `warnings` — so NEVER RAN and DEGRADED could not be
photographed from production data. They are rendered from a constructed run passed to
`renderWhy`, and the screenshot is named `…-FIXTURE-…` so nobody mistakes it later.

**0 of 335 drops on the live log carry a measured value.** The mechanism is live and correct —
proven by calling it — but the 25 runs on disk predate it. Rather than render a blank, the
panel says so: *"This run predates the measured range, so its reasons carry no values — `N` is
a normalised digit, not a missing number."*
