## PART 1 — the blocker: the account gate was reading nothing

This had to be found before anything could be proven, because a caption layer reading zero posts proves nothing.

HEAD (`927bd17`, 2026-07-29) flipped `ig_api.medias_endpoint` from `/v2/user/medias` to `/v2/user/clips` — correctly, and for good measured reasons (play_count coverage 39% → 100%). Its commit message says:

> "The code needs no change at all -- the path has been config-driven since ig_client.py:64-65."

That is true of `ig_discovery.extract_ig_media`, which unwraps. **It is not true of `repost_finder.iter_medias`, which is a separate extractor in a separate module.** `/v2/user/clips` returns `response.items[]` where every item is a `{"media": {…}}` wrapper. The sections branch of `iter_medias` has always unwrapped that (`x.get("media")`); the `items`/`medias` fallback returned the wrappers verbatim.

A wrapper has one key. So `normalize_media` read `username=""`, `best_thumb` read `""`, and the caption read `""`. **Measured on three real saved `/v2/user/clips` payloads:**

| account payload | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| clipcapture.tv | 0 video / 0 thumbs / 0 captions | **12 / 12 / 12** |
| culturemeetsonline | 0 / 0 / 0 | **12 / 12 / 12** |
| decibeldrops | 0 / 0 / 0 | **12 / 12 / 12** |

Downstream that is not a crash, which is what makes it nasty: zero scored posts falls under `min_account_posts_scored`, so every candidate was filed **THIN — "not enough to judge"** — after `client.user_medias()` had already been billed for it. A run would report candidates found, spend one call each, and deliver nothing, with no error anywhere.

The fix is three lines and mirrors what the sections branch already does. `iter_medias` and `ig_discovery.extract_ig_media` now agree on all four saved payload shapes, which is asserted in the suite.

**Not established:** whether a live run has actually been executed since the flip. The last repost output in the tree predates it, so this may never have cost a real dollar. It would have.

## PART 2 — what runs, where, and what it costs

**Zero calls.** Captions are already in the discovery response and in the account-gate response. The parser is offline — no model, no network, no key. The suite asserts `ig_calls == page_calls + account_calls` and that the count is unchanged.

Two call sites, and both matter:

1. **At discovery, on EVERY candidate** — from the one caption that came free with the hashtag page. This is what makes the brief's "every discovered account" literal rather than "every delivered account": candidates that are deduped or gated out are profiled too. That is not thoroughness for its own sake — **a rejected account's fingerprint is what names the farm its delivered sibling belongs to.** Asserted both ways in the suite (a gated-out account and a deduped account each still produce a profile; the deduped one costs 0 account calls).
2. **At the account gate**, the fuller reading replaces it, using up to 12 captions. Captions are collected for **every** post including ones whose thumbnail GET failed — caption analysis does not need the frame, and tying it to an image fetch would discard free signal for an unrelated reason.

**One post is enough, and nothing fetches a back catalogue to decide.** BL-692 measured a single post as a ~95.5% sensitive / ~96% specific test for templating because templating is all-or-nothing per account. We hold exactly one caption for two of the six farm handles, and both classify correctly from it — asserted.

## PART 3 — template detection, against the known accounts

BL-694's three, reproduced through the account-level roll-up:

| account | `templated` | fraction | BL-694 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **clipcapture.tv** | **yes** | **1.00** | templated 24/24 ✅ |
| **culturemeetsonline** | **no** | **0.00** | 0/24 ✅ |
| **decibeldrops** | **no** | **0.00** | 0/24 ✅ |

Total separation on the twelve posts the gate actually fetches, which is what BL-692's 95.5%-vs-4.0% predicts.

**A fixture-fidelity trap, found by the suite, and worth recording because it changed two answers.** The first cut of the committed fixture merged each account's `_clips.json` and `_medias.json` payloads and re-sorted by pk. That is a sample production never sees, and it silently moved the results: clipcapture.tv's recurring vocabulary collapsed from 13 labels to 8, its `templated_fraction` read 0.70 instead of 1.00 (its earliest posts predate its template), a 5-post sample flipped to `False`, and cluster B stopped grouping. The fixture is now read **through `repost_finder.iter_medias` out of each account's own `/v2/user/clips` capture** — byte for byte what the account gate receives. The general lesson: a fixture assembled differently from the live call is a different population, and it will not announce itself.

**`decibeldrops` is `templated=no` and still gets a signature. That is the design, not a contradiction.** Two different words for two different things:

| | definition | drives |
|---|---|---|
| `templated` | BL-694: ≥3 **content-metadata** fields | the sheet flag, genre, title |
| `template_signature` | BL-692: ≥4 **recurring labels of any vocabulary** | farm detection only |

decibeldrops emits a recurring `Credits & Fair Use` / `Keywords` legal block and no content metadata at all. One question asks *does this page declare what the clip IS*; the other asks *who generated this caption*. (On the 12-post production sample it has only 2 recurring labels, below the signature bar, so it reports neither.)

## PART 4 — the farm detector, and why it is not a byte-equality check

**All six of BL-692's cluster-A handles land in one group**, from the committed 12-post fixture, two of them from a single caption:

```
tpl:b4f6d75b914a   jakeswims69, richardthedis6067, shinbizarre312, thomasabg, weareassassin
tpl:99cb17b26237   loste1980
                -> ONE farm group of 6
```

Byte-identical vocabulary hashing alone gets **5 of 6**. `loste1980` emits `Format` where the other five emit `Seasons` — a movie-vs-series *content* difference, not a different generator; 9 of its 10 labels are identical. So the signature (an exact hash, the literal BL-692 statement) is emitted per account, and *grouping* is done on vocabulary overlap.

**Threshold swept 0.3–1.0 over all 340 captioned accounts in the saved payloads** (19 clear the ≥4-recurring-label bar):

| Jaccard | cluster A | cluster B | groups | largest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≥0.80 | splits 5+1 | split | 1 | 5 |
| 0.60–0.75 | **whole (6)** | split | 1 | 6 |
| **0.30–0.55** | **whole (6)** | **whole (2)** | 2 | 6 |

**0.55 is the top of that plateau** — the most conservative value that reproduces both of BL-692's clusters, and far from the 0.80 cliff. Across 0.30–0.55 the answer is *identical*: two groups, largest 6, nothing else merges. Over-grouping — accusing independent operators of being one farm — is the failure that would actually cost something, and it does not occur anywhere in that range.

### The honest limit: cluster B groups, by 0.012

BL-692's second cluster — clipcapture.tv + hollywood_jan07 — also groups. It should not be trusted the way cluster A is, and the suite pins exactly how narrowly it passes.

These two share **semantics but not label strings**: hollywood_jan07 writes `Director`, `Plot Summary`, `Theme` where clipcapture.tv writes `Created by`, `Storyline`, `Themes`.

| sample | Jaccard | vs 0.55 threshold | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| each account's `/v2/user/clips` page (what the gate fetches) | 0.562 | **+0.012** | groups |
| a different 12-post slice of the same two accounts | 0.429 | −0.121 | **does not group** |
| cluster A, any pair | 0.80+ | +0.25 or better | groups, from **one post** |

So: **cluster A is robust and cluster B is sample-sensitive.** A run that fails to flag cluster B is not a bug, and no threshold was lowered to make it more reliable — doing so would start merging unrelated pages, which is the failure that would actually cost something. The margin is asserted as a test (`< 0.05`) rather than left as a footnote, so if a future change quietly widens it, that gets noticed.

### It is not a quality gate, and cannot become one by accident

BL-692 tested templating as a lead-quality signal and **refuted it**: engagement is significantly *worse* on templated pages (1.0% vs 2.7% likes-per-view, **p=0.021**), and the 4.29× view headline (p=0.064, not significant) dissolves into a 3.7× split *inside* the templated group once shared generators are separated (bespoke 29,996 vs farm 8,186 median views). Nothing here filters. A test feeds two accounts with identical frames and opposite caption shapes and asserts both get the same verdict — the templated one gets no free pass.

## PART 5 — provenance, and what an empty cell means

Six new sheet columns: **Caption mood · Genre (declared) · Title (declared) · Templated captions · Template signature · Shared-template farm.**

Every value is read through `caption_parser.field()` at the sheet boundary, so a field marked absent — or carrying no provenance entry at all — renders as `""`. The suite asserts that forging a value onto a record without a provenance entry leaves it invisible in the cell.

Real provenance from the run below:

| account | valence | arousal | conflict | genre | title | templated | signature | farm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| thomasabg | derived | **absent** | **absent** | declared | absent | derived | derived | derived |
| clipcapture.tv | derived | **absent** | **absent** | declared | **declared** | derived | derived | derived |
| culturemeetsonline | derived | **absent** | **absent** | absent | absent | derived | absent | absent |

**`arousal_audio` and `affect_conflict` are None on every row**, provenance `absent` — asserted across all ten real accounts. `False` there would read downstream as "checked, no conflict": a fabricated negative. The suite also proves the axis is *wired, not stubbed* — supply `arousal_audio=-1` against a positive caption and `affect_conflict` becomes `True` with provenance `derived`, no signature change needed on either side.

**Unknown never renders as "no" or 0.** The distinction the sheet has to preserve:

- `templated = "no"` — we read this account's captions and it is not templated.
- `templated = ""` — there was no readable caption. **A different statement.**

An empty-caption run is asserted to produce `""`, not `"no"`. Likewise `farm`: `""` when there is no signature (unknowable), `"no (none seen in this run)"` when there *is* a signature and no co-occurrence — which is a real, run-scoped measurement, and the column says so.

## PART 7 — the measured weakness, stated rather than tuned away

**On the cluster-A farm shape, only 5.2% of the caption reaches the valence scorer.**

| shape | posts | median raw chars | median scored | kept | valence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **cluster A (emoji-per-field farm)** | 50 | 1,490 | **78** | **5.2%** | pos 4 · neg 13 · **neutral 33** |
| cluster B (bare-label) | 48 | 736 | 260 | 35.3% | pos 16 · neg 23 · neutral 9 |
| untemplated | 72 | 960 | 707 | 73.6% | pos 59 · neg 7 · neutral 6 |

The mechanism, exactly: `_drop_factual_blocks` deletes each factual field from its marker to the **next known marker**. Cluster A's affect-bearing prose sits under `📝 Summary:` and `🔥 Why Watch?` — labels that are *not* in caption_parser's closed content vocabulary — so `🎭 Starring:` runs all the way down to `🏆 Rating:` and takes the entire Summary with it. On the one caption held for jakeswims69, 1,529 raw characters become 79:

```
kept:    'Breaking Bad (2008) - The bell that saved Tuco from a lethal dinner\n📺 TV Series'
dropped: 'Summary:\nWalter and Jesse attempt a desperate move to neutralize Tuco by poisoning
          his meal, but they underestimate the watchful presence of Tuco's uncle, Hector…'
```

This is BL-694's own open item #1 ("section-header and editor-credit shapes are not parsed at all") landing on real data, and **it was not fixed here, deliberately**: the repair is adding `Summary` / `Why Watch` to `TEMPLATE_FIELDS` so they terminate the preceding block — a one-line vocabulary change to a module the brief said not to retune, and one that would invalidate BL-694's published 78.6% / macro-F1 0.643 until re-measured on new labels.

**The failure mode is the safe one.** The parser abstains to neutral rather than getting the sign wrong, and three of the six farm accounts report *no* account valence rather than a guess. BL-694's own framing: an abstention is recoverable, a confident wrong sign is not.

Two consequences to carry forward: valence on farm pages is **not usable today**, and BL-694's 78.6% is measured on the cluster-B and untemplated shapes only — it does **not** describe cluster A.

## PART 9 — open

1. **Cluster-A valence is unusable** until `Summary` / `Why Watch` terminate blocks (PART 7). One line, plus re-measurement of BL-694's 42 labels.
2. **Cluster B clears the threshold by 0.012 and flips on a different 12-post slice.** Semantically identical generators with different label strings sit right at the boundary. A synonym map (`Director`≡`Created by`, `Plot Summary`≡`Storyline`) would make it robust — and would need its own false-positive measurement before shipping, since synonyms are exactly how unrelated pages would start merging.
3. **Farm scope is one run.** `shared_with` counts co-occurrence *within the run*, which the column states. Persisting signatures to master would make it cross-run, and needs a schema column — not taken here.
4. **Nothing is written to master.** The caption fields live on the output sheet only. `master_leads.csv` has no caption column (BL-692: `median_caption_len` is populated on 0 of 56,173 rows) and adding one is a migration, not a wiring round.
5. **Non-English templates unmeasured.** BL-692 found Portuguese, Spanish and Indonesian label vocabularies. The permissive matcher would fingerprint them; no measurement here says how well.
6. **Whether a live run has occurred since the endpoint flip** (PART 1) is not established from the tree.
