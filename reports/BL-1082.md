## 1. The per-account ranking — built, then refuted

241 accounts, 2,728 clips. Two signals are measurable at scale: the gate's own pass rate, and
the fraction of an account's *text-bearing* clips that carry a mark in the **pixels** (not the
caption — a caption saying "follow @me" is not a mark burned into the video).

`scratch/bl1082_rank.json` has all 241. But the score saturates — **102 accounts tie at 1.000,
holding 685 clips** — and when tested against the 91 hand verdicts it separates nothing:

| audit | accounts scoring 1.000 | every other account |
|---|---|---|
| MEMEBOT-074 | **0 of 5** postable | 3 of 25 |
| MEMEBOT-086 | 2 of 3 | 3 of 4 |
| MEMEBOT-119 | 3 of 8 | 7 of 22 |

**Two of three audits are worse in the "good" group.** Pooling would have hidden this, and
pooling is not available here anyway — the bar moved between audits (BL-950 found 0 of 25
postable, MEMEBOT-086 found 5 of 7).

### The reason it cannot be answered, which is worth more than the answer

**The label set cannot support a per-account question at all:**

```
accounts carrying >=1 hand label : 58 of 241
labels per account              : 41 accounts have 1, nine have 2, only 8 have >=3
```

The one account with real within-account replication and a high rate is
`@randomly_curious.offic` — 3 postable of 4. The one with a low rate is `@thomasabg`, 0 of 5.
Two accounts. And three accounts (`smoovereactss`, `solidshampooz`, `zackrawrrshorts`) carry
labels that **disagree across judges** — `no` from MEMEBOT-074, `post` from MEMEBOT-086/119.
That is the moving bar, visible per account.

**A ranking that cannot be validated must not spend the walk budget.** That is the finding.

## 2. What DID ship at the walk layer, and why it is a different kind of rule

`clip_runner.order_unwalked_first` now ends with `branded_last(...)`: **pages that brand a
majority of their clips go to the back of the walk order.**

**This is not a correlation, it is a mechanism.** A reposter cannot post a clip carrying
another page's brand, whatever else is good about it. That needs no audit to support it —
which is exactly why it is the only account-level rule this round ships.

**It orders. It does not filter.** `tests/test_account_yield.py` asserts the returned set is
unchanged, an account with no measured rate is treated as clean (*unmeasured is not branded*),
and an unreadable library yields the input order — the rule `order_unwalked_first` already
follows.

**The bottom quartile costs 22% of the library for a change that is not consistent in sign** —
+3.0, **−11.4**, +6.7 points. It dropped two of MEMEBOT-086's five postable clips. **Refused.**

The branded rule never removed a hand-postable clip (0 of 4, 0 of 0, 0 of 1) and moves the rate

## 3. The watermark widening — measured against 40 frames, one shipped, one refused

**The labels are mine and they come from pixels.** Labelling from `ocr_gate_text` would be
scoring a widening of a pattern against the text that pattern reads. 40 clips, 10 contact
sheets, full frames, read one cell at a time: **34 carry a burned-in mark, 6 do not.**

**The sample is enriched** — every clip is one where some candidate fires — so **precision is
measurable and recall and the base rate are not.** Nothing below claims them.

| candidate | fires | real | precision | 95% Wilson |
|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| `W_now` — foreign `@handle` / `domain.tld` (shipped before) | 9 | 9 | 1.000 | [0.701, 1.000] |
| **`W1` — count the source's OWN handle too** | 22 | 22 | **1.000** | **[0.851, 1.000]** |
| **`W2` — the account's own NAME in the pixels, no `@` needed** | 21 | 20 | **0.952** | **[0.773, 0.992]** |
| `W3` — a bare token ending in a page suffix (…TV/…HUB/…CLIPS) | 8 | 4 | **0.500** | [0.215, 0.785] |
| `W4` — a token recurring across the account's clips | 16 | 14 | 0.875 | [0.640, 0.965] |

**SHIPPED: `W_now + W1 + W2` — 32 fires, 31 real, precision 0.969, 95% CI [0.843, 0.994].**
Both bounds clear the 80% bar.

**REFUSED WITH ITS NUMBER: `W3` at 0.500 [0.215, 0.785].** Its four false positives were three
FIFA infographics and a clip with no name in frame at all. The suffix heuristic fires on
ordinary words ending in `-world`, `-zone`, `-daily`. That refusal is recorded in
`DECISION_LOG` beside the term, because a refusal without its number is an opinion.

**NOT SHIPPED: `W4` at 0.875 [0.640, 0.965].** The point estimate clears the bar and the
interval does not, and it needs library-wide context that `classify(clip)` does not have. `W2`
delivers most of the same catch per-clip, so the complexity buys 3 clips in this sample.

### `PRIMECUTTV` — the brief's own example, and what actually catches it

```
ocr_gate_text: 'PRIMECUTTV MOVIE SFAIES'   ->  _HANDLE finds nothing, _DOMAIN finds nothing
```

`W1` cannot see it either — there is no `@` anywhere. **`W2` catches all three PRIMECUTTV clips**
by asking whether the page's own name, which we already know, appears in the pixels.

### What it does to the library, and what it deliberately does not

```
identity FOUND      232 -> 332   (+100 clips whose mark was previously invisible)
third-party          126 -> 126   (unchanged -- no regression)
postable by DEFAULT 2249 -> 2249  (+0 -- THE DEFAULT GATE DOES NOT MOVE)
```

**Detection and cutting are decided separately, and only detection changed.** `foreign()` still
clears the source's own mark, so a page that signs every clip is flagged, not cut — a
deliberate call by an earlier round on cost grounds, which this round does not overturn. What
it adds is the number that call was missing: the marks are real at precision 1.000 [0.851,
1.000], and cutting them costs **178 clips, 6.5%**. A new `exclude_self_watermark=False`
exposes the choice, measured, defaulting off.

## 4. What makes a clip good — nothing in the stored fields, and here is the shape of it

91 rows, four judges. BL-950 is one-sided (0 postable of 24) so an AUC is undefined inside it;
three audits can score.

**17 of the 20 numeric fields flip direction between audits.** Only three keep a consistent
sign, and the strongest is measured on two audits:

| field | pooled | MEMEBOT-074 | MEMEBOT-086 | MEMEBOT-119 |
|---|--:|---|---|---|
| `speech_frac` | 0.651 | — | 0.90 (5/2) | 0.67 (7/7) |
| `speech_dbfs` | 0.600 | — | 0.60 (5/1) | 0.63 (6/5) |
| `engagement_per_follower` | 0.400 | 0.24 (3/26) | 0.00 (5/2) | 0.46 (9/20) |
| **`play_count`** | **0.237** | 0.30 (3/27) | 0.50 (5/2) | 0.47 (10/20) |
| `text_bands` | 0.601 | **0.95** (2/20) | **0.20** (5/2) | 0.72 (5/10) |

**`play_count` reproduces the brief's warning exactly**: it looks like a strong pooled signal in
the wrong direction (0.237) and sits at 0.30 / 0.50 / 0.47 inside the audits.

**`text_bands` is the clearest illustration of why sign matters**: 0.95 in one audit and 0.20 in
another is not a weak signal, it is no signal pointing opposite ways.

**The honest read: nothing here is actionable.** `speech_frac` — postable clips have more speech
— is the only candidate with a story and a consistent sign, and it exists on two audits with
5/2 and 7/7. It is a hypothesis for the next audit to test, not a ranking key.

## What I got wrong inside this round

- **My first draft of the widening shipped a regression and the library caught it.** An early
  `return True, False` on the self-mark meant a clip carrying **both** its own sign-off and a
  competitor's handle answered "self" and stopped being refused: third-party fell **126 → 114**.
  Found by re-measuring rather than by a test, and there is now a test for it.
- **I put a measured-sounding number in a code comment before measuring it** — "431 of 2,728
  clips (15.8%)". The measured figure is **178, 6.5%**. Corrected in place, and the comment
  says it was corrected.
- **I appended five tests after `if __name__ == "__main__"`,** so the file reported 14 passing
  tests while silently not running them. Now 19.

## Honest limits

- **The enriched sample cannot estimate recall.** Every one of the 40 is a clip where some
  candidate fires, so I know how often the terms are right and **not** how many marks they miss.
  Fixing that needs 40 more labels drawn at random, which is another round.
- **`W2` was tested on the same 40 clips that motivated it.** The three `PRIMECUTTV` clips are
  in the sample. The precision figure is honest for the sample and optimistic for the library.
- **The branded-account rule's quality evidence is three clips** (0 of 4, 0 of 0, 0 of 1). It
  ships on its mechanism; the audit evidence is a direction, not a result.
- **`speech_frac` is measured on two audits** with 5/2 and 7/7. It is the best candidate in §4
  and it is nowhere near actionable.
- **The ceiling assumes the head rate holds for the pool.** Stated in §5 with the reason it is
  probably not badly wrong.
- **I did not re-measure BL-1004's dead-canvas refusal or BL-980's precision figures**; they are
  taken as given and only their consequences are counted.
- **`clip_walk.py` is in my claim and I did not change it.** The rule belonged one layer up in
  `clip_runner.py`, which I claimed mid-round.
