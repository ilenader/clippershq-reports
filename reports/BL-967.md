## 1. Every declared field

**2,003 clips · 172 distinct accounts · 81 declared fields.**

**On concentration first, because the brief is right to warn about it and the answer here is not the one the warning expects.** Every field above 30% fill draws from 100–172 accounts with a top-account share of **1.1%–6.5%**. This library is broad, and no high-fill number in this report is a property of two accounts. Eight fields are flagged CONCENTRATED and **every one is below 1.3% fill** — `themes` (7 accounts), `title` (4, top 60%), `language` (5, top 62%), `hashtags` (3, top 59%), the two `vision_reopened_*` (4 each), `vision_last_error_kind` and `vision_transport_failures` (2 each). Those eight are the ones where a percentage is a statement about a handful of pages, and none of them carries an argument in this report.

**How "who reads it" was resolved, because a wrong reader-count is worse than none.** `field(rec, "k")` calls, resolved through the alias `clip_pipeline._f` — **without that alias the scan reported `audio_class` and `media_renditions` as read by nothing**, while `audio_treatment()` reads the first to choose an audio treatment and `renderability()` reads the second to decide a clip is fetchable at all. Plus named field-lists that get iterated (`MATCHER_FIELDS` and the export column tuples), **excluding `CLIP_FIELDS` itself** — counting the declaration would have marked all 81 as read. Plus raw `rec["k"]` / `rec.get("k")`, but **only inside the nine modules that actually hold a clip record**: unrestricted, it credited `google_play_finder` with reading `language` (an app's), `listenbrainz` with `release_date` (a track's) and `control.py` with `hashtags` (a campaign's).

### The three verdicts that matter

**DEAD — 5 fields, nothing writes them and nothing reads them.** `arousal_audio`, `affect_conflict`, `genre_derived`, `medium_derived`, `derivation`. The first two are deliberate: `CLIP_FIELDS` says the audio axis stays UNKNOWABLE rather than a fabricated False, and that is a decision, not a gap. `genre_derived`/`medium_derived` are declared columns the analyzer never fills. **`derivation` is different and it is mine**: BL-959 declared and attributed it last round, so every row written from now on is readable — but 1,950 of 2,003 stored rows carry the token with no provenance entry, so `field()` returns None and the column reads as 0.0% today. It is a backfill I named and did not run, and this table is what that decision looks like from outside.

**MISSED SIGNALS — 33 populated fields nothing reads.** The sharpest, by fill:

- **`vision_model` at 100%** — every clip records which model labelled it, and no line anywhere reads it. It was added (BL-849) precisely so a model change would be visible; nothing has ever asked. **Missed signal, and cheap: it is the only field that could tell a `gemini-2.5-flash-lite` label from its successor's.**
- **`vision_control_declined` at 99.1%** — the per-row honesty signal. `CLIP_FIELDS` calls it "the only per-row honesty signal available": False means the model answered an unanswerable question on that row, so every other vision field on it is suspect. **Nothing reads it, so no consumer of `vision_scene` knows whether that row fabricated.** This is the highest-value unread field in the library.
- **Six analyzer outputs at 69.2%** — `caption_chars`, `is_templated`, `valence_score`, `valence_evidence`, `genre_conflict`, `medium_conflict`. `valence_score` is the numeric behind `valence_text`, which the matcher **does** read; the matcher gets the label and not the magnitude. `genre_conflict`/`medium_conflict` were built (BL-703) because declared-vs-shown disagreement is itself information — and the flags are computed, stored on 1,386 clips, and read by nobody.
- **`audio_decodable` at 4.3%** — declared by BL-947 and still read by no production line, which is the second half of §2's finding.

**RAW-ONLY — 2 fields a production line reads off the dict, never through `field()`.** `bed_dbfs` at `clip_runner.py:452` and `subtitle_text` at `clip_export.py:261`. Both bypass the consumer rule, so an `absent`-attributed value reaches the consumer. `clip_export` is the defensible one — it emits a `_src` tier column beside every value, so it delegates the rule to whoever reads the CSV rather than dropping it. `clip_runner`'s is a plain bypass.

## 2. Computed and not stored, priced

Three cost classes, because they are not the same gap. **Prices are BL-952's measured constants: retrieval $0.000807/item, one billed page $0.0006.**

**PAID-TO-RECOMPUTE — produced by local work inside a paid call; it dies with the process.** Five values off `clip_media._mux_audio`, all with **zero production readers** across 19 call sites of `retrieve()`: `audio` (the mux verdict), `audio_decodable`, `audio_rendition`, `audio_bytes_len`, `audio_error`. **1,898 of 1,985 vision-labelled clips discarded them.** Recovering the whole group costs **$1.5317** — *one* re-retrieval returns all five.

> **The naive sum is $7.66 and it is wrong by 5×.** Every one of these rides out on the same call. A per-row total that bills the same retrieval five times is exactly the kind of number that gets acted on and shouldn't be.

**Would storing them help? Yes, and one of them decides a route.** `audio_decodable` is only ever computed on the `AUDIO_OK` path, so a stored `None` means *either* "no audio track" *or* "nobody probed" — and **only `audio` separates them.** BL-940's split is 50.6% keep-original / 49.4% mute-and-replace and keep-original **must** decode. `audio_bytes_len` is the weakest of the five and storing it would help nothing.

**FREE-TO-KEEP — on a response already bought.** The seven B1 drops. Recovering all of them: **$0.1020** (one page per media, shared). Cost to have kept them: **$0.0000**.

**LOCAL-CPU — on disk, one pass away, $0.** Re-running `caption_parser` over the 2,000 stored captions recovers **969 field-values** across 13 fields that predate the analyzer being wired: `cast` 152, `genre` 144, `where_to_watch` 137, `ratings` 137, `summary` 132, `why_watch` 121, and seven more. **CORRECTION:** `CLIP_FIELDS`' own comment quotes BL-855's "cast 23.5% available / 0.6% stored". Measured today: **15% available / 7% stored**. Both halves have moved — the backfill partly happened and the availability estimate was high. The comment is stale.

**Grand total to recover everything discarded, shared calls counted once: $1.6337. To have kept it at the time: $0.0000.**

## 5. The ledger and the feedback loop

**"Which song, hook window and clip produced the best result" is two joins and one measurement.** A ledger can be perfect at the first and answer nothing.

**JOIN 1 — render → song + window. Partial.** On `status: ok` rows: `clip_id` **100%**, `song` (a path) **100%**, `song_id` **78%**, `hook_id` **51%**, `applied_start_sec` **100%**, `applied_end_sec` **89%**, `treatment` **82%**, `rule_tier` **0%**, `mood` **0%**. **28 of 55 ok rows carry all three of clip_id + song_id + hook_id.**

**JOIN 2 — render → the post we published. ABSENT.** Keys that could name a published post: `permalink`, `posted_at` — **both describe the SOURCE clip we borrowed**. Keys that could carry that post's performance: **`needs_review`**, which is a QC flag. `play_count` is on 81.5% of rows and is **the source clip's** view count.

> **There is no field on any of 260 ledger rows that records what happened to a video after we made it.** Not a URL, not a post id, not a view count of ours. The question in the brief is not partly answerable — **it is not answerable at all**, and every other ledger gap is downstream of one missing string.
>
> The contrast makes it sharper: **the LEAD half of this repo already has this loop.** `outcomes.py` plus seven master columns track which leads pay. The RENDER half has nothing equivalent.

### CORRECTION — the hook-window premise is half true and out of date

The brief states only 4 of 21 windows are reachable because `pick_song` passes `count=False`, so `uses` never persists.

**True:** `clip_pipeline` does call `pick()` with `count=False` — correctly; a planner that mutates the operator's store cannot be called twice — and **all 21 windows sit at `uses: 0` in the store file.** Measured.

**Out of date:** `apply_ledger_uses` (`clip_pipeline.py:1159`) now **hydrates an in-memory copy** from `hook_uses_from_ledger()` before picking, counting DISTINCT `status: ok` records. **Rotation works. It just does not persist.** Measured on the ledger:

```
distinct hook_id VALUES        5   h1..h5   <- counting these is the trap: hook ids are PER-SONG
distinct (song_id, hook_id)   11 of 21 marked windows have carried a real render
    sng_0001   1 of 5      sng_0002   0 of 6      sng_0003   5 of 5      sng_0004   5 of 5
```

**11 of 21, not 4.** And the real gap is a different one: **`sng_0002`'s six windows have never carried a single render**, and `sng_0001` has only `h1` of five. That is mood routing, not a counter — MEMEBOT-067 measured 86.1% park with the first servable song at rank 55.

## Honest limits

- **Suites were not run and I am not claiming they are green.** Nothing outside `scratch/` was touched.
- **"Nothing reads it" is an AST claim, not a runtime one.** A read through `getattr`, a dict comprehension over a name I did not resolve, or a template string would be invisible. I resolved `field()`, the `_f` alias and named field-lists, and I hand-checked six fields against the source; the other 75 rest on the scan.
- **The raw-read category is bounded to nine modules and that bound is a judgement.** Outside it a matching key name is a different dict — I removed `control.py` after finding it credited a campaign's `hashtags` to the library. If a tenth module handles clip records, its reads are missing here.
- **B4's "12 keys never read" is measured on the variable named `plan`.** A key read after being copied into another dict under a different name would not be caught.
- **The ledger moved under me** — 210 → 260 rows during the audit, because another round is rendering. Every ledger percentage is a snapshot.
- **`_probe_samples/` is 170 media in 8 envelopes from a handful of accounts.** The payload fill rates (42.9% `post_lang`, 100% `media_type`) are properties of that sample, not of Instagram. This is the concentration hazard applied to the thing I used to measure concentration, and it is the weakest denominator in the report.
- **I did not measure whether the repost funnel's own response carries `original_lang_for_translations`** — the 42.9% is on `/gql/user/clips` and hashtag envelopes. Same gap I left open in BL-959.
- **Item 6's ranking is my judgement, not a measurement.** The costs are measured; the ordering of "what it unlocks" is argued. A reader who thinks the caption backfill outranks the honesty signal has a case.
