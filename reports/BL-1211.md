## 2. Why there was no card, and why the text was wrong — one cause

The clip is a **two-part meme**. Its burned-in line changes about **5.1 s** in:

| t | the source's own line |
|---|---|
| 0.5s, 3.0s | "Me having a very specific problem no one else has:" |
| 5.0s, 7.0s, 9.0s | "(Deleted user) on reddit 13 years ago:" |

That single fact produced both remaining symptoms:

**It broke the card detector, correctly.** `measure_label_card` finds the card by the
property that it does not move while the picture does — card rows measure 0.0–0.4 against
4.7–31.2 for the first picture row. When the glyphs change, the card's own rows move.
Measured on this clip: rows 0–280 read **13.8, 71.5, 27.6, 47.8, 30.9, 50.1, 27.7, 36.9,
72.2, 76.9, 67.4** against a 4.15 threshold. Only 34 of 281 rows are static. The detector
returned None and was right to.

**It broke the vote, silently.** `read_source_line` samples five frames and needs three to
agree. On a 10.24 s clip that is t = 1.23 / 3.07 / 5.12 / 7.17 / 9.01 — **2 votes for the
first card, 3 for the second.** Three beat two, `ok=True`, and the renderer drew the second
card's sentence over the first card. The function's own refusal message states the
assumption it was resting on:

> *"the burned-in line does not change, so disagreement is this engine failing on this clip"*

**Fixed in the shared reader, so both paths get it.** `clip_label_text.changes_mid_clip`
runs **before** the vote — by the time a majority exists the evidence has been thrown away.
It requires two things together, because either alone has a false positive: the reads must
split into **one contiguous change point** (a card cannot change back; scattered
disagreement is the engine), and the two strings must be **substantially different**
(`SequenceMatcher` below 0.60).

The threshold sits in a measured gap with nothing in it:

| case | ratio | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| @lncept, one sentence, a space lost mid-word | **0.99** | engine noise — still reported as `frames_disagree` |
| @nishsturr, two different cards | **0.35** | `line_changes` — refused |

@nishsturr now refuses with a reason he can check against his own screen:

> *the burned-in line CHANGES about 5.1s into the clip — it reads "Me having a very specific
> problem no one else" at the start and "(Deleted user) on reddit 13 years ago:" later.
> There is no single line of yours to re-type.*

This is a **physical** refusal, so it keeps §6 intact: there is no single line of his to
re-type, which is the same family as "the burned-in line cannot be read confidently".

## 3. A fourth defect, found by reading the frames

Reading every output frame turned up something his screenshot did not show and no check
would have caught: **@lmaoys' drawn line has its first two lines swapped.**

| | |
|---|---|
| his source | "The type of inner peace you / have to find to avoid getting / ragebaited by your mom" |
| we drew | "have to find to avoid getting **The type of inner peace you** ragebaited by your mom" |

His words, his line count, lines one and two exchanged. **`his_line == our_line` was `True`
the whole time**, because both fields hold the same scrambled string — the check that was
supposed to catch a rewritten label cannot see a reordered one.

The cause, measured off the card:

```
y 245-296  "The type of inner peace you"      x0=78
y 291-344  "have to find to avoid getting"    x0=73
y 346-391  "ragebaited by your mom"           x0=115
```

Rows one and two **overlap by five pixels** of a ~52 px line — one row's descenders against
the next row's ascenders. `assemble_reading_order` treated *any* vertical overlap as "the
same line", fused them into one logical line, sorted that line left to right, and **73
sorted before 78**.

Fixed by requiring real overlap — a fraction of the shorter box's height, `LINE_OVERLAP_MIN
= 0.40`. Side-by-side words overlap almost totally; stacked rows overlap by ~10%. Verified
on the live clip: the read is now character-identical to his card, and two words on one row
still join left to right.

I also sorted `lines` by `y0` before joining. The docstring had claimed "lines are ordered
top to bottom" since it was written and nothing did it — the list was in creation order.
That was a latent bug, not this one; it is fixed and pinned regardless.

## 4. "Errors: 1" — the diagnosis in the last round was wrong

BL-1209 attributed link 12's silent failure to a **115-character label** against a strip
that "renders 104 and fails at 108". Two runs of the same twelve links disprove it:

| link | label chars | BL-1209 | this round |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 sarcasticbutreal | **115** | failed | **rendered** |
| 11 clipscommunity87 | **91** | rendered | **failed** |

The real reason was in the log the whole time:

```
edit:fail  [fc#0] Error sending frames to consumers: No space left on device
```

**The defect is that the reason never reaches the caller.** `apply_template` has seven
`return False` paths — missing font, filter-graph build failure, ffmpeg's stderr, a failed
integrity probe, the duration floor, the duration ceiling — and **every one already logs its
reason**. Only the `except` branch printed to stderr; the `ok is False` branch just
incremented a counter. So a nameable failure reached the pipeline as `Errors: 1`, and the
pipeline guessed.

Fixed in `memebot/scraper/edit.py`: `log()` remembers the last message per
`(scope, video_id)` and the `ok is False` branch prints it to stderr. One edit at the
logging function rather than seven at the exits, so any future `return False` that logs is
reported for free. The operator's results line will now read *"No space left on device"*
instead of *"Errors: 1"*.

Re-running link 11 by hand rendered it cleanly (`Errors: 0`) — the exhaustion was transient,
and the disk currently reads 277 G free of 931 G.
