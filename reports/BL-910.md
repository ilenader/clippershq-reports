## 6. The censored-limit sweep

**The lesson, stated so it survives this report:**

> **A ceiling measured from data collected AT that ceiling is censored exactly where the question is.** The test: *could an observation have exceeded the limit and still been recorded as its true value?* If not, the figure is a lower bound on the population and an exact value only for the survivors.

`max_tokens` was raised 700 → 2000 by BL-844 and found truncating again at 2000 by BL-892. **Both numbers came from samples whose entire right tail was a spike at the limit.** BL-904 lifted it to 8000, nothing hit it, and the real p95 was 3795.

### Two instances checked concretely rather than listed

**`max_pages` — the signature in its purest form.** BL-864 and this round both report retrieval depth measured at `max_pages=2`:

```
scratch/bl864_run.json   pages=24  labelled=12  ->  2.00 pages/clip
```

**Exactly 2.00 against a cap of 2.** A clip needing three pages is recorded as a *failure*, never as depth 3, so *"clips are ~1–2 pages deep"* describes **only the reachable ones**. Every retrieval-depth figure in circulation — including MEMEBOT-004's 55/40/5 planning split — inherits this.

**`view_floor` — the floor speaking, not the population.**

```
clip_walk.DEFAULT_VIEW_FLOOR = 20000
library min play_count       = 20029      <- 29 views above the floor
p05 / p50                    = 25121 / 268670
```

*"The least-viewed clip we have has 20,029 views"* is a restatement of the configured floor. The left tail is **unmeasurable from this data**, and any claim about low-view clips is unsupported by it.

**The sweep is deliberately noisy** — it flags call sites, not conclusions, and cannot know which numbers anyone later quoted. `timeout` at 313 sites is mostly ordinary HTTP hygiene. It is a list to read, not a list of bugs.
