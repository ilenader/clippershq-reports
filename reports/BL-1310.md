## THE SIX LINES

1. **`share_per_play` — the one rule this project believes in, AUC 0.818, "zero wanted
   killed" — does not reproduce on his 200 independent marks.** It reads **0.548 / 0.608**

   features, so the brief's "nobody has used it" is wrong. **Sound as a JOIN also refuses**:
   only **14 of 916** sounds appear on more than one of the 100 accounts.
4. **Sound as a DISCOVERY ENGINE is the finding. 30 calls, $0.03 → 343 unique accounts not
   in master. 11.47 new accounts per call, $0.000087 each.** Quality is UNMEASURED.
5. **Three vendors, not two, and 1,149 uncalled endpoints** (139/154 HikerAPI, 17/23
   LamaTok, 993/994 TikHub). Two of them return *reposted content* — literally the product —
   and nothing calls either. `fetch_user_repost_videos` probed **LIVE, 20 rows**.
6. **The brief's own premise is half false.** `/v1/user/following` has depth but **no free
   bio**: the declared schema is `UserShort`, and a live probe returned `biography` on
   **0 of 25** users.

**The single most useful line:** every filter this round tested failed, and the one thing

### The brief's own premise, corrected

`/v1/user/following/*` was handed down as "ALIVE WITH DEPTH AND FREE BIOS".

* **Alive — yes.** 200, 25 users.
* **Depth — yes.** `/chunk` returns a real, non-empty `max_id` cursor. The plain path has
  **no pagination at all** and ignored `amount=5`, returning 25.
* **Free bios — NO.** The declared response is `array[UserShort]`, which has no `biography`.
  A live probe returned exactly eight fields on all 25 users — `account_badges, full_name,

  `public_email`, `external_url`, `follower_count` **0 of 25**. This agrees with BL-1280's
  "biography is 0/237 in the discovery payload".

So the 10.8% clipper-token rate cannot have come from a bio on this endpoint; only from
`username` + `full_name`. **Any signal set built on "free bios from the following list" has
no foundation.**

## 3. THE THREE MEASUREMENTS — AND THE CONTROL THAT FAILED

**Scored against his 200 independent marks and nothing else.**
`ground_truth/review_marks_BL-1298_{instagram,tiktok}.jsonl` carry
`mark_kind: OPERATOR_OWN_VERDICT`. The other 669 in `ground_truth/review_marks.jsonl` carry
`mark` + `filter_verdict` — a GOOD/BAD button on the judge's own verdict.

**The split is BL-1307's, not a new one.** `scratch/bl1307_labels.json` already assigns the
same 200 to A/B on the same label set; inventing a split here would have let me choose the
one that flattered the result. TikTok: **A = 46 (18 WANT), B = 54 (19 WANT)**.

Payloads: 100/100 handles resolved, **978 posts**, 0 errors, 200 calls, $0.20.

### 3.1 The control failed, and that is the finding

`share_count / play_count` is the one rule this project currently believes in: BL-1291
measured **AUC 0.818**, mean 0.0256 WANTED vs 0.0117 REJECT, **zero wanted killed** on a
held-out half. My harness declared in advance that if it could not reproduce that number,
nothing else in the file would mean anything.

**It did not reproduce.** Re-measured on his marks: **0.548 on A, 0.608 on B.**

Three candidate causes, all separable, all separated (`scratch/bl1310_control.py`):

| aggregation | AUC all (100) | AUC A | AUC B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| median of post ratios | 0.581 | 0.548 | 0.608 |
| **mean of post ratios** (BL-1291's statistic) | 0.537 | 0.510 | 0.562 |
| ratio of totals | 0.540 | 0.526 | 0.544 |

**Aggregation is not the cause** — all three are flat. And scoring the same feature on the
same pages against the *judge's* label instead of his gives **0.546 / 0.489 / 0.494** —
**label source is not the cause either.**

**The cause is population.** BL-1291's corpus carried **14 WANT of 99 = 14.1%**; his TikTok
marks carry **37 of 100 = 37.0%**. The rule was tuned and held out *within one corpus*, and
did not survive the move to another. That is precisely what a held-out split cannot catch:
it protects against overfitting a threshold, not against overfitting a corpus.

> **A measured by-product worth keeping.** On these 100 pages his label and the judge's
> agree **84 of 100 = 0.840 [0.756, 0.899]**, and the judge is systematically more
> permissive: **judge WANT 49% against his 37%**.

### 3.2 Engagement ratios — six tested, none ships

Threshold rule fixed in advance and taken from BL-1291 so the comparison is like for like:
largest zero-loss cut on A, then **one grid step back**; direction fixed on A; scored **once**
on B. **Ship rule: on B, the precision lower bound must clear the base rate upper bound of
0.485.**

| signal | AUC A → B | kept on B | precision on B [95%] | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `digg_per_play` | 0.677 → **0.713** | 52 / 2 dropped | 0.365 [**0.248**, 0.501] | **REFUSE** |
| `collect_per_play` | 0.691 → 0.649 | 42 / 12 | 0.381 [0.250, 0.532] | **REFUSE — killed 3 WANT** |
| `comment_per_play` | **0.748** → 0.514 | 50 / 4 | 0.380 [0.259, 0.518] | **REFUSE** (collapsed) |
| `share_per_play` (incumbent) | 0.548 → 0.608 | 52 / 2 | 0.365 [0.248, 0.501] | **REFUSE** |
| `shared_sound_frac` | 0.474 → 0.618 | 54 / 0 | 0.352 [0.238, 0.485] | **REFUSE** (no cut) |
| `download_per_play` | 0.364 → 0.433 | — | — | **REFUSE** (no zero-loss cut on A) |

`comment_per_play` is the instructive one: **0.748 on the tune half, 0.514 on the held-out
half.** That is the shape of a signal chosen on the data it was measured on, and it is the
same shape the incumbent has across corpora.

### 3.3 Sound — refuted twice, in two different ways

**As per-account features, BL-1291 already refuted it** and the brief's "nobody has used it"
is wrong. Their numbers, on 99 pages for $0.063: `share_original_sound` **0.555**,
`share_matched_song` **0.495**, `has_human_voice` **0.596**, `commerce_music` **0.610**,
`music_owned_by_page` **0.419**, duration **0.602** — with the mechanism named:
`music.title` reads *"original sound"* on **69.9% of WANT and 73.1% of REJECT**, because
TikTok stamps that on any audio not from the commercial library, **including a reposted
clip's baked-in audio**. The field records how the audio entered the app, not who shot the
picture. My own `original_sound_share` reproduces it at **AUC 0.500 exactly**.

**As a JOIN it refuses on sparsity.** Of **916 distinct sounds** across the 100 accounts,
only **14 appear on more than one account**. There is not enough shared sound inside a
100-page sample for the relationship to carry information, and `shared_sound_frac` scores
0.474 → 0.618 — a sign flip between halves, which is noise.

## 4. THE FINDING: A SOUND IS ELEVEN ACCOUNTS

**Two rounds disagreed about whether this endpoint exists, and the disagreement is the
interesting part.** BL-1291 wrote: *"there is no 'videos using this sound' endpoint on this
vendor, confirmed against the full spec"* — and that was **true of LamaTok**, whose spec has
23 paths. The project also holds a **TikHub** key, and TikHub's spec documents
`app/v3/fetch_music_video_list` at **$0.001, the price we already pay**. A capability was
written off because one vendor's spec was checked and the other was not.

Measured (`scratch/bl1310_discover.json`), seeding the 30 most-used sounds from payloads
already bought:

```
30 calls  $0.0300
  ->  350 distinct-account slots
  ->  343 UNIQUE accounts not already in master_leads.csv
      11.67 accounts per call | 11.47 NEW per call | $0.000087 per new account
```

**98.3% were new.** Only 6 of 350 were already in master.

**What this is NOT.** It is not a filter, it does not judge, and **the quality of those 343
accounts is entirely unmeasured**. A discovery endpoint can only be responsible for the top
of the funnel. What it changes is the shape of the problem: every filter in this round
failed, and the supply side turns out to be a tenth of a cent for eleven accounts.

**The honest next step**, costed but not taken here: judge a random 100 of the 343 at the
known judged-page cost to get a base rate with an interval. Until that exists, no yield
claim may be made, and this report makes none.
