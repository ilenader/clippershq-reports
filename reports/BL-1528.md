## 2. Where bonuses meet the marketplace, and the decision that is the owner's

**Worked by hand first, then run.** A $100 gross clip, both parties at the cap:

```
creator 60.00 + 15.00 bonus = 75.00
poster  30.00 +  7.50 bonus = 37.50
owner   10.00 +  0.00       = 10.00
                    disbursed 122.50, owner realises 8.1633%
```

**THE ANSWER TO THE PRECISE QUESTION.** A bonus applies to **that person's SHARE**, not to the clip's
gross before splitting. `earnings-calc.ts:667` is `round2(creatorBase * (creatorBonusPct / 100))`, and
:668 is the same for the poster. The platform leg at **:663** is
`Math.max(0, round2(round2(gross) - creatorBase - posterBase))`: a residual of the **pre-bonus** gross,
computed **before** any bonus and **never recomputed**. So the two bonuses are paid **on top of the pool**,
and every bonus dollar enlarges the denominator the owner's fixed ten dollars is divided into.

**Which does the code do, and which should it do?** The code applies each bonus to that party's own share,
and that is right: the 60 leg and the 30 leg are two different people who may each have their own level,
streak and app bonus on the same clip, and a bonus on the gross would pay one person for the other's
standing. What is wrong is not the base the bonus is computed on. **It is that the owner's leg is called
10 percent when it is 10 percent of the GROSS and not of what is DISBURSED.**

**MEASURED EXHAUSTIVELY.** 11,546,080 grid points, 427 view counts from 1,000 to 5,000,000 by 40 CPMs from
$0.10 to $10.00 by creator bonus 0..25 by poster bonus 0..25, through the real production function:

- the owner realises **below 10 percent in 11,466,687 points, 99.312%**
- **median realised owner percentage 8.98876%**
- restricted to grosses of $10 or more, where rounding cannot be the explanation: **99.911%**, worst
  **8.0871%**

**BL-849's $127 and 7.87 percent REPRODUCES EXACTLY, but only through a door the claim did not name.**
Under the ordinary cap the worst case on a $100 clip is **$122.50 and 8.16%**. **$127.00 and 7.8740%
requires an owner-set `manualBonusOverride` at 30 on BOTH parties**, which no clipper can reach by
levelling up and which **zero users hold today**.

### The three options, their consequences, and the 30-day real cost

**The 30-day real cost of all three is $0.00, and that is measured rather than assumed: there are zero
marketplace clips, zero `marketplace_creator_earnings` rows and zero `marketplace_platform_earnings` rows.
The defect has cost nothing to date. It is a live code path with no live data.** The per-$100 figures are
what the decision actually turns on.

| option | what happens on a $100 clip, both parties capped | consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. bonuses on top** (today) | disburses $122.50, owner keeps $10.00 | owner's dollars are safe and fixed; his SHARE falls to 8.16%, and the headline "10 percent" is not true of what leaves the clip |
| **B. bonuses out of the platform's share** | disburses $100.00, owner keeps $0.00 at the cap | the split stays exactly $100; **the owner's leg is wiped out entirely at a 22.5 point combined bonus**, and goes to zero long before that on any clip where both parties are well levelled |
| **C. bonuses out of the clip's pool** | disburses $100.00, each party's bonus comes out of the other two | the pool is honest and the owner keeps a share, but **a clipper's own bonus is then paid by the poster and the owner**, so one person's streak reduces another person's pay for the same clip |

**RECOMMENDATION: keep A, and change the words rather than the money.** A is the only one of the three
where a person's bonus is paid by the platform that promised it rather than taken from someone else, and
it is the only one where the owner's leg cannot be wiped out by other people's achievements. What should
change is the label: the owner's cut is **10 percent of the clip's gross**, and the marketplace screens
should say that instead of implying 10 percent of what is paid out. **THIS IS THE OWNER'S DECISION AND
NOT MINE.** Nothing in this round changes which option is in force; A is what shipped and A is what is
still running.
