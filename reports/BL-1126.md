## STEP 4 — the framing, and a diagnosis that was not what the brief assumed

**The white is not square-source padding.** `white_frame` sets `scale_width: 864` on a
**1080×1920** canvas, so *every* video is shrunk and ringed with white — including 9:16
sources that already fill the frame exactly. **56 of the 60 most recent renders had 9:16
sources**, which is why the previous batch looked white-heavy despite no square footage.

Measured on magenta, on the top clip (1.42M plays, source **1080×1920**):

| option | dead canvas | cropped? |
|---|---:|---|
| (a) as-is, `scale_width 864` | **36.0%** | no — uniform scale |
| (b) fill the width, `scale_width 1080` | **0.0%** | no — uniform scale |
| (c) blurred backing | **0.0%** | no |

36.0% is exactly the arithmetic — 1 − (864·1536)/(1080·1920) — and matches the brief's 35.4%.
Because these sources are black-background cards, it renders as a **white border around a
black video**: literally "a white overlay across the whole video".

**The operator chose (b).** At 9:16 (c) is identical to (b) — there are no bands to fill.

## THE FINDING THIS ROUND EXISTS FOR — the white was hiding a crop

Option (b) fixed the white and immediately exposed a defect that had been invisible:

> ours: "**ɔmple** explains her visual thinking"  ·  original: "**Temple** explains her visual thinking"

The anti-fingerprint transform runs **`zoom: 1.05–1.2`** and **`rotation_deg: ±0.8`**. Zoom
scales up and crops back to frame, losing **5–20% of every edge**. At `scale_width 864` that
crop happened inside a 216px white margin, so it destroyed nothing. **At full bleed it eats
the source's own headline** — and the frame is visibly tilted.

**Measured 10 of 10 sliced.** Capping **zoom → 1.0 and rotation → 0.0** restores the headline
complete and level, verified by direct three-way comparison (zoom-on / zoom-off / original).
Every other anti-fingerprint knob stays on: brightness, saturation, gamma, hue, speed, trim,
position shift, sharpening, vignette, colour balance, pitch, frame noise, audio EQ, encoder
params. **The shipped batch is the corrected render.**

This is the constraint the pillarbox revert was about, arrived at from the opposite
direction: there the trim sliced the headline, here the zoom does. **The margin was load-bearing
and nobody knew.**

## What I got wrong

1. **`output_dir` is the clips root.** I pointed it at a sibling `out/`, so `--only` matched
   zero videos on all 10 clips **while returning rc=0**. Ten silent no-ops behind a success code.
2. **The dead-canvas measurement read 100% on 4 of 10 good renders.** `-ss` before `-i` seeks
   without rebasing timestamps, so the video reached the filter graph at t≈3s while the
   1-second colour source ran 0–1 and `overlay` emitted bare magenta. **A measurement that
   fails toward "defect" is worse than one that errors.** Fixed with `setpts=PTS-STARTPTS`.
3. **I leaked 2 billed requests.** The first `retrieve_video` client was collected before
   `flush_spend`, exactly the BL-876 shape. Recovered as a correction row; the ledger now
   matches the 4 requests actually made.
4. **My edge-ink slicing metric was contaminated** — it sampled our 20s excerpt and the 58s
   original at 35% of each file's own duration, i.e. different moments. The headline is
   static, so the direct visual comparison is the valid evidence; I report that instead.
