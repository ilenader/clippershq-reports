| `clips_metadata.original_sound_info.progressive_download_url` | 9/14 — original sounds |

This independently reproduces BL-679's 180-clip result on a different sample. **The 36% figure in `scratch/media_endpoint_findings.md` is not in conflict** — that measurement is about *title/artist metadata*, which genuinely is licensed-only, and it remains correct as written. Two different questions, two different answers, one shared path prefix.

### Every saved URL is expired, and the cause is proven rather than inferred

All 14 return **HTTP 403**. These URLs carry an `oe` query parameter which is a **hex Unix expiry**; decoding it across the corpus gives **2026-07-17 22:35 UTC through 2026-07-20 22:44 UTC** — 9 to 12 days before today. This was checked against the alternative explanation: it is not a method or User-Agent problem, because a signed URL past its expiry cannot be recovered by changing how it is requested.

**Cross-reference, added at publication.** [BL-681](BL-681.md), published while this round was running, measures `progressive_download_url` presence at **83.8% (192/229)** across a wider corpus — but the entire shortfall is 36 clips carrying **no `clips_metadata` at all**, and among clips that do carry it presence is **192/193 = 99.5%**. That is consistent with the table above rather than contradicting it: all 14 clips here carry `clips_metadata`. **The correct statement is '~100% of clips that carry `clips_metadata`', not '100% of clips'** — the field is only reliable on surfaces that emit `clips_metadata`, which is not every surface.

There is no other audio on the machine — no `.mp3`, `.m4a`, `.mp4`, `.wav` or `.aac` anywhere in the repo tree.

**This is what blocks §3 of the brief.** Fresh URLs require a fresh `clips_metadata` call, which is paid, which this round was told not to make.

## PART 2 — the detector

`scratch/energy_step.py`. RMS envelope in dB (librosa `feature.rms`, frame 2048 / hop 512 at 22.05 kHz — 23.2 ms per frame, far finer than the ±0.5 s target), plus spectral flux (`onset.onset_strength`) as a corroborating second view.

**The step is scored distributionally, not by peak-picking.** At every candidate boundary *t*, over a window *W*:

| quantity | definition | what it rejects |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `step` | median(dB in [t, t+W]) − median(dB in [t−W, t)) | — the jump itself, in dB |
| `sep` | p25(after) − p75(before) | **transients.** A 150 ms spike cannot move the 25th percentile of a 2 s window |
| `climb` | median([t+W, t+2W]) − median([t, t+W]) | **crescendos.** A step plateaus; a build keeps rising |

`sep` and `climb` together are what make "sustained" a measurement rather than a word. A peak-picker has no defence against either failure mode.

**Output contract** — `energy_step_ts`, `step_db`, `confidence` (0–1), `sep_db`, `climb_db`, `transition_s`, `flux_agree_s`, and **`shape`**. `shape` is what makes NULL usable: `flat` (nothing cleared the bar), `spike_only` (got loud, did not hold), `ramp` (rose gradually, no edge), `too_short`. **A downstream bot can always tell which kind of nothing it received.**

### Three fixes during the run, each a measured delta

1. **Vectorised the scan.** The obvious implementation — a Python loop calling `np.median`/`np.percentile` per frame — cost **1,155 ms** on a 52 s clip and made librosa look expensive when the loop was the entire problem. `sliding_window_view` plus axis reductions: **78 ms**, a **14.8× cut with identical output**.
2. **Smoothed the transition-width measure.** Walking the *raw* envelope terminated at the first percussion frame that crossed the threshold, so an 8-second crescendo was reported as a 2-frame edge.
3. **Added the plateau test** (`climb`). Fixes 2 and 3 together eliminated the run's only fabricated timestamp.

## PART 3 — accuracy against constructed ground truth

40 clips, durations 12–55 s, music-like beds (harmonic stack + filtered noise + periodic kick transients, so the envelope is as jagged as real music), fixed RNG seed, ground truth exact by construction.

| class | n | result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **step** (4, 6, 9, 12, 15 dB) | 12 | **12/12 within ±0.5 s**, median abs error **0.065 s** |
| **double_step** (must pick the larger) | 6 | **6/6 within ±0.5 s**, median abs error 0.090 s |
| **flat** (uniformly loud) | 6 | **6/6 correctly NULL** |
| **spike** (150 ms transient) | 6 | **6/6 correctly NULL** |
| **ramp** (long crescendo) | 6 | **6/6 correctly NULL**, `shape='ramp'` |
| **step_down** (fade-out) | 4 | **4/4 correctly NULL** |

**Positives within ±0.5 s: 18/18 = 100%. Fabricated timestamps: 0/22 = 0.0%.**

Detection holds all the way down to a **4 dB** step, which is at the edge of what is visible in a rendered waveform.

### The operating point is not knife-edge

20 combinations, `min_step_db` ∈ {2,3,4,5,6} × `win_s` ∈ {1.0,1.5,2.0,3.0}:

* **Fabrication is 0.0% in every single cell.** The null path does not depend on threshold luck.
* Hit rate is **100% at `min_step_db` ≤ 3 dB**, degrading gracefully to 88.9% (4–5 dB) and 83.3% (6 dB) — purely because genuine 4 dB steps fall below a raised bar, which is correct behaviour, not failure.

### What this number is NOT — stated plainly

**The corpus is synthetic, so its steps are pure gain changes on a stationary bed.** Real reels bring compression and limiting, speech, sound effects, and section changes where instrumentation shifts without the level shifting. A constructed step is an easier target than a real one, and the generator and the detector share an assumption about what a step looks like.

**So: this establishes that the arithmetic is correct and the null path is trustworthy. It does not establish agreement with a human eye on real music.** Those are different claims and this report does not merge them. **The brief asked for the second one; only the first was obtainable.**

## PART 4 — the creator's own cut point is not a usable free label, and the reason predates the audio blocker

`highlight_start_times_in_ms` is **not** a creator cut point. BL-679 verified it is a *suggested start point* that lives on the music-catalog `Track` object, is identical across every post sharing an audio cluster, and is fed by `instagrapi` into `audio_asset_start_time_in_ms` — the app trimmer's slot.

Confirmed on this sample: present on **5/14 clips** (licensed only), and `music_info.start_time_in_ms` present on **0/14**.

**The per-post offset needed to test "the creator's cut predicts the energy step" is absent from the payload we hold.** That is a second, independent blocker: even with live audio, this specific label could not be built from this payload shape. **Worth revisiting only if a payload carrying a genuine per-post audio offset is found** — the idea is sound, the field is missing.

## PART 5 — CPU cost

Single core, decode excluded (audio already in memory), median of 5 runs.

| clip length | RMS + flux | RMS only | per 1,000 clips (full) | per 1,000 (RMS only) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 s | 17.9 ms | 4.2 ms | 0.30 min | 0.07 min |
| 20 s | 33.4 ms | 9.0 ms | 0.56 min | 0.15 min |
| 30 s | 52.8 ms | 12.4 ms | 0.88 min | 0.21 min |
| **52 s** | **77.9 ms** | **19.3 ms** | **1.30 min** | **0.32 min** |
| 60 s | 94.6 ms | 22.9 ms | 1.58 min | 0.38 min |
| 90 s | 151.5 ms | 35.2 ms | 2.53 min | 0.59 min |
| 120 s | 199.4 ms | 51.5 ms | 3.32 min | 0.86 min |

**Reported as a curve, deliberately, because the median reel length is contested.** BL-680 carries **52 s** (n=180). The local sample gives **median 22.4 s** (n=14: 9.6 / 15.0 / 15.0 / 16.7 / 17.6 / 20.8 / 22.1 / 22.7 / 24.6 / 25.4 / 26.2 / 36.2 / 47.0 / 63.3). n=14 cannot overturn n=180, so 52 s is used for the headline — but the curve stays valid under either figure. **`median_duration` in the master CSV cannot arbitrate: the column exists and is populated on 0 of 56,173 rows.**

**Cost is a non-issue at any plausible scale.** Even at 52 s and 1,000 clips/day the DSP is 1.3 CPU-minutes. Flux costs ~4× RMS alone and is corroboration only, so `with_flux=False` is the obvious lever if it ever matters. **The real bottleneck at scale is decode and network, not this arithmetic.**
