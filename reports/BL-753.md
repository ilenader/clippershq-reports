## 2. What each filter discards, and whether it was right

Clipper-side verdict distribution (n=55,428): **EDITOR 63.1%, LIKELY 16.9%, UNSURE 14.1%, CREATOR? 5.8%, MEME_PAGE 0.1%.**

`UNSURE` + `CREATOR?` = **11,020 rows (19.9%)** retained but demoted. Sampling their bios, the demotions read as correct: they are talking-head creators and promo accounts, not fan editors.

The one drop I would question is structural rather than sampled: **`deep_median_views` is populated on only 2,088 of 55,428 clipper rows (3.8%)**, while `avg_views_sampled` is populated on 55,307 (99.8%). So the 4,000 floor at gate 6 has an opinion about 3.8% of the corpus and is silent on the rest — consistent with the standing "UNKNOWN never cuts" rule, but it means the floor is not doing the work its config value implies.

## 4. Is any clipper funnel discarding leads the way Spotify does? **No — and one of them was fixed before you asked.**

The Spotify condition is real and still there: `spotify_finder.py:1111` — `if not r.get("email") or r.get("already_in_db") == "yes": continue`.

Checked every clipper save path:

- **`repost_finder.append_found_to_master` (`:858-898`)** — writes `email: ""` deliberately and documents why: *"These are DM targets… the handle IS the deliverable."* **No email gate.** ✅
- **`crawl_suggested.append_leads_to_master` (`:729-756`)** — **had exactly the Spotify bug and it was fixed on 2026-07-17.** The docstring is explicit: it used to write only leads with an email, *"For a DM-only pipeline that threw away ~94% of what was paid for — the crawl's '6% yield' was an EMAIL rate, not a LEAD rate."* It now keeps every `EDITOR`/`LIKELY`; the old behaviour survives only behind `crawl_require_email`, which is **absent from config → False**. ✅
- **`writer._append_master` (`writer.py:1194-1214`)** — the main IG/TikTok path. Person-key dedup only; **no email condition.** ✅
- **`crossdedup.append_leads` (`:320-367`)** — shared path, **no email condition.** ✅

**Measured confirmation:** email fill by side — TikTok 5.1%, IG hashtag 13.6%, repost finder **0.0%**, IG crawl 91.1%. A repost-finder population that is 0% email is being saved in full. If an email gate existed anywhere on that path, those 42 rows would be zero.

## 5. New finding: the IG crawl writes the master with no lock and no cross-funnel dedup

`crawl_suggested.py` **imports neither `filelock` nor `crossdedup`** (verified by grep — zero matches for either). Its master write at `:808-818` is `shutil.copyfile` → `DictWriter` full rewrite → `os.replace`, outside any lock.

Every other master-writing path takes the cross-process lock and routes through cross-funnel dedup:

| path | lock | crossdedup |
|---|---|---|
| `writer._append_master` | ✅ `filelock.file_lock` | ✅ person index |
| `crossdedup.append_leads` (twitch/spotify/yt/GP/repost) | ✅ | ✅ |
| **`crawl_suggested.append_leads_to_master`** | ❌ **none** | ❌ **none** |

Two consequences, both latent while `ig_crawl_enabled=false`:
1. **Lost writes.** A full rewrite from a stale snapshot silently drops rows another funnel appended in the interim — the exact failure `crossdedup.append_leads:332-337` documents and guards against. You run multiple agents concurrently; this is the one path that can clobber them.
2. **Duplicate humans.** A person already in the master under another funnel is re-appended, because the person index is never consulted.

I did not fix either (read-only audit). Severity is *latent, high* — harmless today, data-losing the moment the crawl is switched on.

## 6. The persona question, answered with a number: **~0.2–0.3%, not the bulk**

Classified all 55,428 clipper-side rows on bio + display_name + handle + niche + post_hashtags + theme_match.

**Keyword-matched "adjacent professional" (VFX / 3D-motion / wedding / photographer / commercial / tutorial / freelance): 873 rows = 1.58%.**

Then I read a deterministic 30-row sample of those hits, and **most are false positives of my own classifier**:

- `.vfx` is a **handle suffix convention among fan editors** — `teenluv.vfx` is *"Learning AE Day 1/100"*, `aep.gone.vfx` is *"Ae"*, `flame._.vfx` is *"17y AM & Blurrr"*. Not compositors.
- **"presets"** matched my photographer pattern — but preset-trading is core fan-editor behaviour (`velv3nt`, `flickzfilms`, `moh_ae25`).
- **"studio"** matched **Studio Ghibli** — `stupiddumbnorton` (*"studio ghibli & ranma 1/2 ! edits most of the time"*), `stawb3rry_tea` (*"Studio Ghibli lover"*). A fandom, not a production house.
- **"maya"** matched a girl's name and a character name, not Autodesk Maya.
- **"bride"** matched *"2026 Bride 💍"* (someone's own wedding) and *"freezer bride scenepack"*.

Genuine adjacent professionals in the 30: **`weddingprocass`** (*"Wedding Pro & Expert… Now booking 2027 Couples"*), **`chloe_production`** (*"Professional equestrian photographer #sonyalpha"*), **`smzstudioz`** (*"News Video Editor, Filmmaker, Podcaster"*), **`dgraphicssport`** (*"Sport Graphic Designer"*), arguably `fangs.animation`. That is **4–5 of 30 = 13–17% of the keyword hits.**

> **1.58% × ~15% ≈ 0.2–0.3% of clipper-side output.** Roughly **1 lead in 400**.

**The hunch is wrong.** VFX compositors, 3D motion designers and wedding cinematographers are not the bulk of the error above 85% — they are a rounding error. Whatever sits between 85% and 100% is something else, and the 225 rows that matched *both* an adjacent term and a clipper term suggest the real overlap population is **fan editors who also sell presets or take commissions** — the same people, monetising, not a different profession.

## Limits of this audit

Static code reading plus one read-only pass over the 57,386-row master. **No live runs**, so per-run drop reasons were not sampled at the moment of firing — §2 infers from persisted verdicts, which is survivorship-biased by construction. The 76% crawl precision and 96.4% repost precision **could not be verified either way**. The persona adjudication is 30 rows read by me, not a labelled set. The unread-field sweep covers `discovery.normalize_item`; the IG equivalent did not match my extraction pattern and was not swept.
