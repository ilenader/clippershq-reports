## The critique was right, the specific prediction was not

**Right:** BL-700's 8.5% and BL-682's 0/108 were properties of three movie/TV clip pages, not
of the population. On a corpus drawn from the actual discovery hashtags the music share is
roughly **5× higher**, and track metadata goes from **absent** to **present on nearly a
quarter of clips**.

**Wrong, and worth saying plainly:** the expectation was *"anime/fan edits near 100% music,
movie-clip pages near 0%."* The ordering came out close to **reversed** on the anime side:

- **franchise-clip scored 67%**, not ~0% — #theboys was **6/6 music/non-speech**, median
  `speech_frac` **0.03**, with empty transcripts. Those are edits cut to music.
- **anime-edit scored 30%**, the second lowest — not ~100%.

The reason is that **the tag is not the format.** #anime, #onepiece and #naruto surface a
great deal of *anime clip and commentary* content — dubbed and subbed scenes with real
dialogue — rather than music-driven edits. Spectrograms of anime clips classed
speech-dominant confirm genuine speech formants with true dark gaps between phrases, not
misread singing. The narrower #animeedit tag returned 0/6 music, but n=6 is too small to
lean on.

So the useful axis is **edit vs clip**, not **anime vs movie**. A page that cuts footage to a
song is music-first whatever fandom it belongs to; a page that reposts scenes is
dialogue-first for the same reason.

licensed track exists, Instagram already hands you the title and artist for free — you do not
need to fingerprint it. Fingerprinting would only be for the `original_sounds` clips that
carry music without metadata, and that is a smaller, harder subset.

## A structural finding worth recording

**`licensed_music` clips carry no `original_sound_info.progressive_download_url` — 0 of 82.**
The 264 audio URLs all come from the 265 `original_sounds` clips. That is coherent: a clip
using a licensed catalogue track has no "original sound" asset to expose.

This has a direct consequence for the measurement below, and for any future pipeline: the
audio you can download for free is precisely the audio *without* track metadata, and the
clips with track metadata are precisely the ones you cannot download. The two paths are
complementary, never overlapping.

## Method

Discovery used the repost finder's own call, `hashtag_medias_clips()`, across 12 tags —
the ones named in the brief plus #animeedit, #animememe, #capcut. 12 paid calls, 351 reels,
**317 unique accounts**.

Classification used **only `speech_frac` from whisper VAD**, the one feature BL-700 validated
against spectrograms. BL-700's chroma, HPSS and spectral-peak-persistence features were
**not used**, per its own finding that a pure-speech clip outscored a known music clip on the
Shazam primitive. Thresholds unchanged from BL-700: `<0.30` music/non-speech, `0.30–0.60`
mixed, `≥0.60` speech-dominant.

Audio: 72 clips downloaded free from the CDN (URLs valid to 2026-07-31 02:15 UTC), balanced
6 per tag, **66 unique accounts**, 30.5 MB.

## Limits — read before quoting the 47%

- **The audio measurement runs on the subset least likely to be music.** Only
  `original_sounds` clips have a downloadable asset, so `speech_frac` could never be computed
  for the 82 `licensed_music` clips. Those are counted as music by declaration, which is
  sound, but it means the two halves of the total come from different kinds of evidence.
- **The singing confound is real and unquantified.** BL-682 documented that VADs classify
  singing as speech. Transcripts here show it happening — `"I love you baby, you're my God"`
  (#onepiece, `speech_frac` 0.99) is lyrics, `"Belelele, belele, bele jahva jahva"`
  (#animememe, 0.98) is vocables, and several clips returned emoji-soup transcripts that are
  Whisper failing on non-speech audio. **Every music rate in the table is therefore a lower
  bound.** Spectrogram checks on eight anime-edit speech-dominant clips found mostly genuine
  dialogue, so the confound does not appear to dominate — but it is not zero and it was not
  measured.
- **Family assignment is by discovery tag, not by inspecting each account's catalogue.** With
  317 accounts and roughly one clip each, per-account format classification would have cost
  another ~300 calls. The tag is a proxy, and the #anime result shows the proxy is imperfect
  — that tag mixes edits with clips.
- **Small per-tag n.** Each tag contributed 6 clips to the audio measurement. Family-level
  n (18–30) is more trustworthy than the per-tag rows.
- `#animeedit` n=6 returning 0% music is the single most counter-intuitive cell and is the
  first thing worth re-measuring at larger n.

## What this changes

1. **Music presence must be recorded per account, not assumed corpus-wide.** It ranges from
   11% to 68% across formats measured the same way. Any funnel field that assumes a single
   rate will be wrong for most pages.
2. **The cheap discriminator already exists and is free.** `clips_metadata.audio_type` is
   `licensed_music` or `original_sounds` on every reel, and `speech_frac` costs ~1.6s of CPU
   per 30s clip. No paid call is needed to label a page's format.
3. **BL-700's conclusion stands for movie/TV clip pages and should be scoped to them**, not
   withdrawn. Its 8.5% remains the right number for that format; it was never the right
   number for the population.
4. **Song identification via `music_info` is live again** on ~23% of clips, with title and
   artist, and needs no audio processing at all.
