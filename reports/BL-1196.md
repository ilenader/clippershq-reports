# BL-1196 — the marking tool: ten rows at a time, two clicks a page, and a save that cannot be lost

The sheet that froze his browser put **every** row and **every** thumbnail into one document.
Run over the 425 pages this round judged, that renderer emits **3.59 MB, 4,035 `<img>` and
65,342 elements**. The new one holds **ten rows** in the page at a time, and never has more
images loaded than the viewport is showing — measured in a real browser at **4 of 20**. The
other 415 rows are not in the document at all.

Every figure came from **`.venv\Scripts\python.exe`, Python 3.11.9**. Bare `python` is 3.13
and lacks the packages; it was used only for the stdlib-only claim readers.

**I lost two hours and $0.55 of billed work in the middle of this round to the exact defect
the round exists to eliminate.** Section 7 is that, in full.

---

## 1. It does not lag, and here is the measurement

Both renderers were run over **the same judged pages**, so this is two artifacts on disk
rather than an extrapolation.

| | old renderer | new windowed sheet |
|---|---:|---:|
| bytes before he can work | 3,589,223 (**3.59 MB**) | 52,232 (**52 KB**) |
| `<img>` in the DOM at once | 4,035 | **92** (one batch) |
| rows in the DOM at once | 425 | **10** |
| elements in the served document | 65,342 | **39** |

**68.7x less to download and parse before the first row is usable, and 43.9x fewer images to
decode.** All 43 chunks together are 1.36 MB, fetched ten rows at a time, and the next batch
is prefetched while he works on the current one.

The 39 elements is the shell alone; the ten rows are then built by script, so the honest
in-page figure is 39 plus one batch. The old renderer's 65,342 is the whole document, which is
the number that has to be laid out before anything is usable.

**Measured in Chrome, not asserted.** `domContentLoaded` **552 ms**, 3 requests, 39.8 KB
transferred, 13 MB JS heap, on a batch whose rows were rendered and scrolled with real input
events. That measurement was taken on a smaller batch earlier in the round; the browser
extension disconnected before the final 425-page build, so the page-weight and image figures
above are from the artifacts on disk and the attach/release behaviour below is from the live
run. Nothing here is extrapolated from the 100-row sheet.

| where he is | images with a `src` attached |
|---|---:|
| top of the batch | 4 (row 1 only) |
| scrolled to row 4 | 4 (row 4 only) — rows 1–3 **released**, row 5 not yet attached |
| scrolled past everything | 0 |

That is the requirement working: **the number of decoded images tracks the viewport, not the
sheet.** `loading="lazy"` alone does not do this — once seen, the bitmap stays resident — so
an `IntersectionObserver` with a 400 px margin attaches on entry and **removes the `src` on
exit**.

**The thumbnails got bigger, not smaller.** They were hard-coded at 200 px wide, which is not
enough to read a drawn meme line on a 1080-wide frame. `parallel_judge` had no way to pass a
width at all, so one was plumbed through `run()` → `judge_one()` → `_save_thumb()`;
`DEFAULT_THUMB_WIDTH` is now **420**. Bigger images, ten rows at a time, is the trade the
brief asked for and it is the right way round.

## 2. Two clicks per page — and the third answer he keeps giving

| he presses | what happens |
|---|---|
| **G** or one click | right call — saves, jumps to the next row. No typing, no reason. |
| **B** then **1–5** | wrong call, with his own most common complaint on key 1 |
| **R** then **1–7** | **right call, wrong reason** — then which rule it should have been |
| J / K · [ ] · D | move row · move batch · download every mark |

**The five presets were counted, not invented.** From the 70 free-text reasons inside his 149
marks:

| preset | times he wrote it | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| A person / content creator — not a meme page | 17 | 24% |
| Too much dialogue | 7 | 10% |
| Text on the video isn't English | 6 | 9% |
| Quotes or personal brand | 4 | 6% |
| No real video — stills or screenshots | 4 | 6% |

Each button stores the sentence he actually typed, so a later round can check the button still
means what he meant.

**Right-verdict-wrong-reason is a different list on purpose.** `low_views` is deliberately
**not** one of the five BAD presets but **is** in the wrong-reason list — his example is a
real meme page the filter rejected *for low views*, a verdict he agrees with for a reason he
does not. If both lists were the same, the case he named could not be recorded. A test asserts
the BAD presets are a strict subset.

The record keeps the axis intact: right-verdict-wrong-reason stores `mark: GOOD` (so the
derived truth is unchanged) **plus** `verdict_right_reason_wrong: true` and
`should_have_been: <rule>`. It adds information without moving the thing every previous
measurement rests on.

**All three paths were exercised end to end** against a server whose marks file was redirected
to scratch, and all three landed on disk with the right shape:

```
codingvision          GOOD  REJECTED
hala_madrid_indians   BAD   PASSED    A person / content creator — not a meme page
magnifico.06          GOOD  REJECTED  Views too low   SHOULD-HAVE-BEEN=low_views
```

## 3. The save cannot be lost — demonstrated by killing the server

He lost ~50 marks once and the failure was printed in the success colour. So the server was
killed mid-marking and the page was watched:

| | |
|---|---|
| banner | **white on red** — `rgb(255,255,255)` on `rgb(212,15,15)`, `role="alert"` |
| wording | "1 mark(s) are held in this browser and are **NOT** on disk. Marking is **STOPPED**." |
| further marking | **impossible** — `pointer-events:none`, and the key handler refuses |
| counter | `3 saved · 1 waiting`, always on screen |
| Download | present in the markup from the start, not conditionally created on failure |
| after restart + Retry | `4 saved · 0 waiting` — **the held mark drained. Nothing lost.** |

The mark is written to `localStorage` **before** the network is touched, and a separate mirror
of every mark this browser has ever taken backs the Download button, so Download always yields
everything rather than only what is stuck.

**His 149 existing marks were backed up first** to
`ground_truth/backups/review_marks_pre_BL1196.jsonl`, byte-identical (`sha256 503033d4…`), and
the live file is still 154 rows / 149 handles. The test server never had the real path in its
module global, so the probe could not reach it even if the save path changed underneath — a
previous round put three fake marks into his real ground truth exactly that way.

**Marks are keyed by HANDLE now.** `latest_by_handle()` is what every analysis should use.
Row numbers restart at 1 on every sheet, so the row-keyed reader merges row 47 of the
100-sheet with row 47 of the 50-sheet into one record — a test builds that collision and shows
the row-keyed reader returning 1 record where the handle-keyed one returns 2.

## 4. He is never shown a page with no videos

Two gates, and they are **not** the same gate. The difference is the account's own claim about
itself: gate 1 is "there is nothing there", gate 2 is "there is something there and we failed
to get it".

| | excluded | what it is |
|---|---:|---|
| **no videos** | 112 | `media_count == 0` **AND** under 1,000 followers |
| **unjudgeable** | 85 | profile declares posts, the fetcher returned zero |
| profile error | 2 | private, deleted, or a non-dict response |
| **total kept off his screen** | **199** | of 1,000 across two passes attempted |

**Both fields must agree before refusing, because he named the exception himself** — row 28,
*"he legit just started"*. A page with no declared posts but a real audience still gets its
posts call, and is judged normally if posts come back. One field alone would delete exactly
the page he asked to keep.

**Unjudgeable is not a rejection.** Pages reporting 648, 508, 230 and 123 posts returned zero
to the fetcher while the live account had hundreds; the old sheet drew a verdict from nothing
and showed it to him. These are excluded from the sheet **and from the base rate** — counting
a fetch failure as a rejection would corrupt the very number this round exists to measure.

The no-videos refusal happens **before the second billed call**. The profile call cannot be
avoided (`media_count` only exists on that payload), but the posts call can be, and that is
the one skipped.

## 5. Dialogue, with UNKNOWN kept separate from none

He asked for this directly, and it is measured locally and free — Silero VAD over clips
pulled from the CDN. **2,069 clips across all 425 pages**, five per page:

| | clips | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| TALKING | 935 | 45.2% |
| clean | 1,100 | 53.2% |
| **UNKNOWN** — audio would not decode | **34** | **1.6%** |

Pages where **every** measured clip talks: **43 of 425**.

**UNKNOWN is a third outcome, not a zero.** A clip whose audio cannot be decoded renders as
`3 UNKNOWN (audio would not decode — not "no speech")` in its own colour, never folded into
the clean count. That distinction is the whole reason the read is trustworthy: a page shown as
"0 of 5 talking" when four clips were unreadable is the benign-zero defect this repo has
recorded eighteen times.

**A prediction in the brief did not survive contact.** It expected ~35% of clips to carry
xHE-AAC that ffmpeg 8.0 cannot read. **Measured: 1.6%** — 34 clips of 2,069. BL-1195 saw the
same thing from the other direction (0 of 72 undecodable). The UNKNOWN path is built, tested
and displayed regardless, because the next batch may differ and a silent zero would be worse
than a wasted branch — but the 35% figure should not be carried into another brief.

**And the sheet does not pretend a threshold sits under it.** He asked for the rule *"if all
ten talk, scrap it; if even one is clean, keep it"*, and the count is shown — but moviezar,
which he criticised for dialogue, and a page he called BEST are **both 6 of 10 talking**, and
BL-1195 measured speech-to-bed contrast failing at every threshold. So the number is displayed
and no verdict is derived from it. `all_talk` is recorded in the data so a later round can
score his rule against his marks instead of assuming it.

## 6. The 500, and what they are worth

**500 attempted → 425 judged → 215 passed, a base rate of 50.6%.** 166.4 minutes,
23.5 s/page, 4 workers.

| rule | pages it fired on |
|---|---:|
| format_share | 96 |
| creator_page | 95 |
| low_views | 63 |
| promotional | 32 |
| too_few_posts | 23 |
| thin_sample | 18 |
| not_english | 5 |
| news_media | 2 |
| no_text | 2 |

**He sees passes and rejections in the proportion the filter actually produces them.** The
sampling rule is printed in the sheet itself: every 2nd handle of 1,173 fresh candidates
sorted by handle, with the 149 he has already marked and the 124 already-excluded removed
first. No stratification, and no filtering on the filter's own opinion — this is not a
highlight reel, and at 50.6% pass he will spend roughly half his clicks on each verdict.

**What the 425 are worth against the ceiling.** BL-1195 measured why the filter is stuck: at
149 labels a 97% result reads [93.3, 99.0] and a 90% result reads [84.1, 93.8], and those
overlap, so no filter change can be *shown* to hit his target. Roughly 450–500 labels separate
them.

**425 new + 149 already marked = 574 labels** once he works through the sheet — past the range
that makes 97% and 90% distinguishable. That is the point of the round, and it is the number
to hold this work to: not 425 pages judged, but whether the marking actually gets done.

**75 pages he will never see** were refused in this pass alone (41 no-videos, 33 unjudgeable,
1 profile error), on top of the 124 from the first. Under the old sheet every one of them
would have been a row he had to look at and reject.

## 7. What I got wrong, and what it cost

**I lost about 240 judged pages and $0.55 of billed work to a defect I was in the middle of
fixing for him.** The runner accumulated every judged page in a list and wrote the file **once,
at exit**. Two hours in, the process was killed — and the entire result set went with it,
while the thumbnails it had already written sat on disk as evidence of work that no longer had
a record.

The diagnosis needed no guessing, because the same run contained the control: **the excluded
file was written line by line and all 124 of its records survived the identical kill.** One
file streamed and lived; the other buffered and died.

This is the same shape as the ~50 lost marks — *the only copy was in memory* — which is what
makes it worth writing down rather than quietly re-running. The fix is the one I had just
built into the sheet: `on_result` now writes each page as it lands, with `flush()` and
`fsync()`, and the final step re-reads that file instead of dumping memory into it. The rerun
was launched **detached** through the repo's own `proc_detach`, and it survived a later kill
that took every other background task with it.

Two things that softened the cost: the 124 surviving exclusions were skipped on the rerun, so
they were not paid for twice; and the streamed file means an interruption now costs whatever
is in flight rather than everything.

**One real limit found while measuring, not a defect:** `parallel_judge.run()` completes its
entire billed fetch loop before entering the `as_completed` loop, so `on_result` cannot fire
during the API leg — the first 82 pages appeared in one burst the moment fetching
finished. Streaming therefore protects the long OCR tail, which is where the kill happened,
but not the fetch phase.

## 8. Accessibility — a project hook demanded agents; I did the audit instead

A `UserPromptSubmit` hook fired repeatedly insisting this is a web project and that I must
delegate to an accessibility agent before touching UI code. The standing instruction for this
session is not to spawn agents unless asked, so I did the audit directly and am naming the
conflict rather than silently resolving it either way.

It found three genuine WCAG AA failures in what I had already written, and the worst one was
the one that matters most:

| | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| **the blocking failure banner** (white on `--warn`) | **3.71:1** | **5.42:1** |
| `.lbl` / `.none` small grey text on a post | 4.04:1 | 4.96:1 |

**The single most important sentence on the page — "SAVE FAILED … marking is STOPPED" — was
the least readable thing on it.** That is a quieter version of the same defect as printing it
in the success colour, and it was mine. `--warn` moved from `#ff2d2d` to `#d40f0f`, which
reads as more urgent, not less.

Also added: `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` on the two disclosure buttons (kept honest on
the keyboard path, which is the one he will actually use), `role="alert"` on the failure
region, `aria-live="polite"` on the row list, and per-post alt text — ten identical alt strings
per row told a screen reader nothing about which post it was looking at.

Six checks now pin this, computing contrast from the shipped CSS variables rather than
asserting a hex string, so a later palette change is measured rather than assumed.

A third suite went red on the first run and it was worth the detour.
`tests/test_server_freshness.py` asserts that a server started "an hour ago" reports itself
stale — which is only true **when somebody edited code within the last hour**. My round made
`review_sheet.py` the newest file on disk; ninety minutes after I stopped editing it, the test
failed with nothing about the server or its freshness logic having changed. It now anchors to
the newest file's own mtime instead of the wall clock. *A test whose result depends on how
recently a human typed is not testing the code.*

My first attempt at that fix read a key (`code_newest_mtime`) that does not exist and fell
back to `time.time()`, so it kept passing the wrong thing while looking fixed. The key is
`code_newest_epoch`; the fallback is gone, so a future rename fails loudly instead of quietly
reverting to the bug.

**64 tests** across `tests/test_review_sheet_window.py` (35, new) and `tests/test_review_sheet.py`
(24, unchanged and still green, plus BL-1192's save-failure contract) and the four
`parallel_judge` dependents. `test_governance_rules` green — **campaigns unchanged**
(`7a029ee5447cddd8` and `8e02f8d6f6307ae8` are the same object under different separators).
`test_config_contract` green.

### The commit is two commits, and no hook was bypassed

`review_sheet.py` and `OPEN_REVIEW_SHEET.bat` were held by BL-1192, which had **already
landed** (`17234d7`, with BL-1193 and BL-1194 built on top) and simply leaked its claim.
`claim.py takeable` sanctioned the takeover — over 480 minutes old, paths clean — and recorded
it in this round's claim.

The pre-commit hook still refused, because the *registry* attributes those two paths to
BL-1192 and the hook's invariant is that **no single commit may span two rounds** (bundling
destroys the intermediate state each round's measurements were taken against). It offers
`--no-verify` as the escape. I did not take it: the invariant is a real one and it is
satisfiable. The work went in as **two commits** — the two contested paths alone with a
`Claim-Override` trailer, then everything else — so neither commit spans a round and the hook
passed both times.

BL-1192's claim itself is **left alone rather than force-released**: `claim.py end` refused
because it still has uncommitted scratch artifacts, and orphaning another round's work in
progress is that author's call, not mine.
