## THE PARAGRAPH AT THE TOP

You asked for 5x. **I measured 3.54x and I did not get to 5x.** The walk now runs eight
pages at once instead of one at a time, and a page that took 22.9 seconds takes 6.5. Nothing
was cut to get there: every page still buys its own videos, builds its own contact sheet from
its own posts, and gets its own model call with the same eleven-field facts pack. I proved
that rather than asserting it — with the input frozen and only the lane count changed, the
request that leaves this machine is **byte-identical 8 of 8** by sha256 of the real
serialised body, and the one-byte control fires so the comparison can actually fail. The
MAYBE rate, which is the canary for the model being fed less, came in at **32.0%** of judged
pages (16 of 50), or **35.7%** (20 of 56) once the six recovered pages are folded back in,
against last round's 38.5%. Both readings sit below it.

**Whether more lanes would buy the rest of the way to 5x, I could not establish, and I am
not going to pretend I did.** See section 2: the bench that was supposed to answer it timed
the *same* work at 85 s and 167 s on two consecutive attempts, so it cannot support a claim
about where the curve turns.

**And the round found something worse than slowness.** 48 of the 61 pages the first lane run
walked — **78.7%** — had already been walked by BL-1539 last round. (An earlier draft said 39
and 63.9%; that counted only `bl1539_run/rows.jsonl` and missed the three other directories
BL-1539 walked. Against its full union of 85 handles the real figure is 48. The bug was worse
than first published, not better.) Your rule is no
duplicates ever. The guard that was supposed to catch this checked the seen store, which
these runs deliberately never write to so that no page gets latched, so it answered "never
seen" for every page the previous round had just walked, and it answered confidently. My own
verification printed "0 duplicates" and that number was correct about the wrong question.
That is fixed, and the fixed run's first line reports the guard's own denominator so it can
never be silently empty again.

The delivered set is **56 pages** on a target of 50, none of them a duplicate of anything any
previous round walked.

## 1. WHERE THE TIME ACTUALLY WENT

Instrumented on a real run of 12 pages. **Median and tail separately, never a mean** — the
p90 is 2.9x the median here and a mean would hide exactly the thing worth attacking.

Median and p90 are NEAREST-RANK -- `sorted(v)[n//2]` and `sorted(v)[int(0.9*n)]`, so every
figure below is an actual measurement and not an interpolation between two of them.

| stage | n | median | p90 | max | total | share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the model call | 12 | 13.56 s | 52.92 s | 61.74 s | 254.1 s | **77.2%** |
| the account's own videos | 12 | 5.83 s | 7.22 s | 7.37 s | 67.2 s | **20.4%** |
| building the sheet | 12 | 0.62 s | 0.79 s | 1.13 s | 6.1 s | 1.9% |
| the cover downloads | 12 | 0.00 s | 0.00 s | 0.00 s | 0.0 s | 0.0% |
| encoding it (per tile) | 108 | 0.01 s | 0.02 s | 0.03 s | 1.5 s | 0.5% |
| writing the row | 12 | 0.00 s | 0.00 s | 0.00 s | 0.0 s | 0.0% |
| **the whole page** | 12 | **20.38 s** | **59.01 s** | 67.29 s | **328.9 s** | 100% |

The one-off tag call is 6.09 s and is not per page, so it is not in the table.

**The tail is the model, and it is the model on a slow day, not a different kind of work.**
The three slowest pages spent 52–62 seconds inside the judge call and were unremarkable
everywhere else.

**The cover downloads are already parallel** — `COVER_WORKERS = 12`, a `ThreadPoolExecutor`
that has been there all along, on a pooled `requests.Session`. My first instrument patched
`requests.get` and read **zero seconds of cover time**, which would have been a lovely
headline. It was measuring nothing, because the code does not call `requests.get`. I threw
the zero away rather than publishing it.

**Dead waiting: none on this path.** There is no `time.sleep` anywhere in the TikTok walk. I
planted one and the detector fired, so that zero has a control behind it.

**Subprocess timeouts: one real gap, and it is not on this path.** `video_strip.py:138` and
`:146` invoke ffmpeg with no timeout and no tree kill — and ffmpeg is a grandchild, so a
plain kill would leave it running. That code is reachable only through `hero_sheet_for`,
which is gated on `hero_picture`, which is **off**. It is latent, it is named here, and I did
not touch it, because a subprocess-killing change does not belong in the same round that
proves the walk unchanged.

## 2. THE LANE CURVE — NOT ESTABLISHED, AND HERE IS WHY

The brief asked for the lane count where throughput stops improving, and the curve. **I do
not have it.** This is the part of the round that failed.

I had a five-point curve — 1.00x / 1.59x / 2.29x / 4.01x / 3.46x at 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 12
lanes, turning at 12 — and it was written into the shipped code as the justification for
`LANES = 8`. When I went to cite it I checked the evidence file. **It contains two arms, not
five, and it is stamped `identical: false`** — it is the output of the confounded experiment
that rebuilt its sheets inside each arm, the one whose identity result I had already thrown
out. The curve had been carried forward in a code comment and in my own notes with nothing
behind it.

So I re-measured, on frozen input, each arm run twice:

| lanes | attempt 1 | attempt 2 | spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 85.1 s | 166.5 s | **1.96x** |
| 2 | 43.6 s | 49.2 s | 1.13x |
| 4 | 51.9 s | 33.1 s | **1.57x** |
| 8 | 49.3 s | 51.8 s | 1.05x |
| 12 | 57.7 s | 61.4 s | 1.06x |

**The same work, in the same condition, timed nearly twice apart.** The gap between lane
counts is smaller than the gap between two runs of one lane count, so this measurement
cannot order them. Taking the best of each pair would say throughput peaks at *4* lanes —
the opposite of what I nearly published — and that reading is noise, not a finding. Two
things contaminated it: the full test suite was running on the same machine, and the model
endpoint's own latency varies by the minute (the profile in section 1 already shows a p90
of 52.9 s against a 13.6 s median for a single call).

**What this does not change:** the end-to-end 3.54x in section 5 is measured on real runs
with nothing else on the machine, and the identity proof in section 3 is unaffected — it
compares *content*, not clocks.

**What it does change:** `LANES = 8` is no longer justified by a measured curve. It is a
setting that demonstrably works and is not known to be optimal. Establishing the curve needs
a quiet machine and enough repeats to beat that variance, and it is the first item in
section 9.

## 3. NOTHING CHANGED BUT THE CLOCK — PROVED, WITH A CONTROL THAT FIRES

**My first attempt at this proof was worthless and it told me so.** Each arm re-fetched the
accounts and rebuilt the sheets, so between arms the vendor's own content moved and the
picture changed with it. The prompt hashes differed for reasons that had nothing to do with
parallelism, and the run reported "SOMETHING OTHER THAN THE CLOCK MOVED" about a difference
it had manufactured itself.

The real proof freezes the input. Sheets built once, facts built once, then the same frozen
pair judged at 1 lane and at 8.

| what | result |
|---|---|
| request body byte-identical, by sha256 of the real serialised messages | **8 of 8** |
| delivered SET identical (the set, not the count) | **yes** |
| facts pack fields sent | **11**, both arms |
| the sheet file judged | literally the same file, hash unchanged |
| **control:** one byte added to one facts pack | **sha changed — the comparison can see one byte** |

The hash is of **every text part and every image byte of the real request body**, not of a
filename. A name is not a file, and hashing one would pass even if the picture changed
completely.

**One thing did vary, and it is not parallelism:** the model's own answer matched on 7 of 8.
An LLM is not required to be deterministic on an identical prompt. "The prompt is identical"
and "the answer is identical" are different claims and only the first is about lanes, so they
are reported as two numbers rather than one.

## 4. THE STOP — AND WHAT A STOP CANNOT DO

Two rounds ago a "stop after 5 pages" run walked 16, because the stop was raised from a
callback that `_tick()` swallowed in an `except Exception`. Then it was fixed and worked
serially and still did nothing at lanes > 1, because `ex.map` hands every task to the pool
before the first one finishes — a stop tested only by the dispatching loop is a stop that
never runs.

**Driven at lanes > 1, asking for 3:**

| lanes | asked | delivered | bound is target + (lanes − 1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3 | **4** | 4 |
| 8 | 3 | **10** | 10 |

**Both hit the bound exactly.** That is not a stop failing — it is a stop that cannot un-buy
work already in flight. When the third delivery landed at 22:58:57, seven other lanes were
already *inside* their model calls; they returned between 22:59:20 and 22:59:49. Asking for
fewer pages than there are lanes guarantees this.

What the stop *does* do, from the real run's own log:

```
23:09:02  L25    stop is set (50 of 50 delivered) -- page b829b897e2d8 NOT walked, no call bought for it
23:09:06  L26    the target was met -- abandoning this page BEFORE the model call, which is 77.2% of a page.
```

It is now checked at **three** points: the top of the worker, before the own-videos call, and
before the model call. The bound is stated in the code and in the committed test, not smoothed
over.

### Why the fed queue is not faster, though it should be

I removed the barrier between tag pages, expecting a gain. **It did not produce one** —
0.1184 min/page against 0.1077 for the simpler shape. The measurement says why: **113 seconds
of a 447-second run, 25.2%, is one gap** — the tag boundary, where `#movieedit`'s pool must
finish every page still in it before `#animeedit` can open. I did not remove the barrier. I
**moved** it, from between tag pages to between tags: fewer barriers, but each waits on a
longer tail.

The two runs are also not a clean A/B, and I am not going to pretend otherwise: the duplicate
fix landed in the same run and changed the work mix, forcing twice as many tag pages and a
second hashtag to find the same 50 pages. **This measurement cannot attribute the difference
to either change.**

## 7. WHAT YOU GOT WRONG

**I did not hit 5x. I hit 3.54x.** That is the headline and it is a miss.

**My duplicate guard was fail-open and I published a clean number from it.** The verify pass
printed "a handle already in the seen store: 0" while 78.7% of the run was pages BL-1539 had
walked days earlier. The check was reading `PageSeen`, which these runs never write to. A
guard that reads a store nothing updates cannot fail, and I did not notice until I compared
the two runs' handles directly, by hand, for a different reason.

**I broke the row file with my own optimisation and it cost six paid pages.** The fed-queue
loop exits the moment the target is met, while up to 8 futures are still running. The pool
waits for them, so they finish and they are billed — and then their results were dropped on
the floor. The log recorded 62 verdicts; `rows.jsonl` held 56. **Six delivered pages were
bought, judged, and never written down.** Your 50 were all present so the deliverable
survived, but if the target had been met a few seconds earlier the loss would have landed
inside the 50. Fixed by draining the queue before exit, and the six are recovered from the
play-by-play into `recovered_rows.jsonl`, marked as reconstructions and kept separate — a row
rebuilt from a log is weaker evidence than a row written by the run, and merging them would
launder that difference away.

**My cover-fetch instrument was blind and would have published a beautiful zero.** It patched
`requests.get`; the code uses a pooled `Session` and is already 12-way parallel.

**My first identity experiment measured its own bug twice.** Once by re-fetching inside each
arm so the vendor's drift looked like a parallelism defect. Once by storing the prompt hash in
a single shared attribute that other lanes overwrote — it reported "1 of 8 identical" about
nothing but its own race. The delivered set being identical the whole time is what gave it
away.

**I reported 0 of 55,628 edit-tag rows in master.** That reads exactly like a real finding —
"the edits funnel has never reached master". It was my own bug: the field is stored as
`tt:hashtag:<tag>` and I stripped only a leading `#`. The true figure is 141.

**I hardcoded a baseline from a comment instead of reading it.** My verify script printed
"BL-1539 serial was a median of 12 tiles". It is 4. The 12 came from a sentence about a
different, discarded arm of that round. It now reads the baseline from the baseline run's own
rows.

**I nearly published a five-point lane curve that had no measurement behind it.** It was in
my notes, it was in a code comment justifying `LANES = 8`, and it was written into the draft
of this report as a table with two decimal places. The evidence file has two arms and is
stamped as the run I had already discarded for being confounded. I found it because I went to
cite the file rather than the memory — and the re-measurement not only failed to reproduce it,
it pointed the other way before the noise swamped it. Section 2 is what that looks like when
it is admitted instead of rounded.

**I ran a timing benchmark while the full test suite was running on the same machine.** Then
spent a minute confused by 2x variance.

**The log said `L11` in an eight-lane run.** A fresh pool per tag page mints new threads, and
my lane counter was keyed on thread id, so it climbed forever. The walk was correct and the
label was not — and a log line that says L11 when you set 8 lanes is one you are right to
distrust.

## 8. SAFETY

- **Backed up before anything ran:** `config.json`, `spend.json`, `master_leads.csv` and all
  five seen stores, sha256-verified, into `backups_bl1540_20260909/` with a manifest. Both
  tamper controls fired — a one-byte change and a deleted duplicate row were each caught by
  the index-qualified fingerprint, and the naive natural-key check missed the second, which is
  why both are run.
- **Spend counted at the wrapper by this run's own counter, never a ledger delta.**
  `spend.json` is shared, so a before/after difference cannot attribute a round. Cap $2.00,
  spent **$0.2364**.
- **The cap is serialised.** Test-and-take is one atomic step under a lock. Two lanes each
  reading "one call left" and each deciding to spend is how a hard cap silently becomes an
  advisory one.
- **No page was latched.** The seen store held 1,855 handles before and 1,855 after. These
  runs write no verdict.
- **No handle, address or key is printed in any committed artefact.** Handles in logs are
  sha-prefixed; the address instrument reports counts only.
- **No Python processes were killed. No `--no-verify`. No `/T` on any taskkill.**

**Committed in this round's single commit** (it was NOT committed when this report was first
written, and the report said it was): `tests/test_bl1540_parallel_identical.py` — 7 tests, every check raising rather
than asserting (`python -O` strips `assert`, which would make the whole file a silent pass;
the last test parses this file for `ast.Assert` with a planted control). Every negative claim
has a positive control that is required to fire: the dispatcher-only stop must actually walk
past its target, the unlocked counter must actually overspend, and a shared dict must actually
be clobbered — otherwise the corresponding fixed-version test is declared vacuous and fails.

**WHERE THE FASTER CODE ACTUALLY LIVES, and why that was nearly a disaster.** No production
file changed this round: `clippershq/tiktok_finder.py` is untouched, because page-level lanes
were already shipped by BL-1333 (`DEFAULT_LANES = 8`). What is new is the **fed queue with a
bounded drain**, and until this commit it existed only as a throwaway file in a session
temp directory. The 3.54x, the queue-drain fix and the duplicate guard would all have gone
with the next temp clear. The whole harness is now in the repo at
`scratch/bl1540/harness/` — 21 files, `bl1540_run.py` being the runner — so every number
above can be re-driven rather than taken on trust.

## 9. WHAT THE NEXT ROUND SHOULD DO

1. **Establish the lane curve properly** — quiet machine, nothing else running, enough
   repeats to beat a 2x within-arm spread. Until that exists, `LANES = 8` is a setting that
   works, not a setting that is known to be best, and it may well be wrong.
2. **One queue across tags, not per tag.** The measured 113-second drain is 25.2% of the run.
   Removing it arithmetically gives 4.31x — *arithmetic, not a measurement*, which is why it
   is a suggestion here and not a result above.
3. **Record an address field during the walk.** It is free — the bio is already in hand — and
   it would replace the survivor-set projection in section 6 with a measurement.
4. **Ask why 7 of the 8 edit hashtags have never reached `master_leads.csv`.**
5. **Give ffmpeg a timeout and a tree kill** before `hero_picture` is ever turned on.
