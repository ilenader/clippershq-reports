## The short version

**You could not read the grid because half of every tile was blank.** An Instagram reel
thumbnail is 750×1333, but the caption and the picture sit in a middle band — on the three pages
I measured, only **47%, 61% and 74%** of the tile is content. The old grid montaged the raw
tile *and* squashed it into a square, so a 9:16 frame lost 44% of its height and took the text
with it.

Trimming the padding, keeping the native shape and dropping to nine tiles raises a caption line

### Gating on legibility, not on agreement — as instructed

The floor of record is **467×467 for the whole 12-tile capture** (BL-1257), about **155 px per
tile**, justified because *agreement* is flat from 934 down to 467. That same measurement noted
illegible tiles rise **before** agreement falls, which is the tell: a judge can agree for the
wrong reason while reading none of the text.

I built a grid at that floor. **A caption line at the 467² floor is 11–12 px.** That is the
number the old gate was protecting, and it is not readable by you or by anything else.

**The gate this round proposes is geometric, free, and checkable at build time:** nine trimmed
tiles at a 560 px cell. That configuration produced 29–36 px captions on every page tested. It
needs no OCR in the capture loop — which matters, because OCR costs 32.9 s/page.

**One honest limit.** Legibility is measured on **three pages**, chosen as WANT pages from his
marked set. The direction and the mechanism are not in doubt — the padding is arithmetic and the
squash was geometric — but "29–36 px" is three pages, not a distribution.

## 5. `play_count`: a real signal, and a floor that does not earn its place

Scored against **200 Instagram pages he marked himself** — 100 enriched (28% want) and 100
new-discovery (9% want), 37 wanted in total — using **his** verdicts only. `judge_verdict` was
never read; using it would have made the result circular.

| floor (median views) | wanted KILLED | rejects killed | of measured rejects |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 | **0** | 10 | 6.4% |
| 1,870 | **0** | 25 | 15.9% |
| 1,871 | **1** | 25 | — |
| 5,000 | 6 | 40 | — |
| 25,000 | 22 | 94 | — |

**The lowest median among his wanted pages is 1,870.** That single page is the entire
constraint — and its "median" is the play count of **one post**.

### Two findings that kill the floor

**`play_count` is not universal.** It is present on `product_type=clips` posts and **absent as a
key** on `igtv` posts, where the same quantity is called `view_count`. Verified by dumping the
real key lists, not by testing a guessed name. **7 of 200 pages carry no `play_count` at all —
and one of them, `goet`, is a page he WANTS**, with 12 igtv posts averaging ~645,000 views.

**Read both names, and the floor collapses.** The highest floor that kills zero wanted pages
becomes **443**, removing **4 of 158 rejects — 2.5%**.

> **Verdict: the view FLOOR does not ship.** At zero wanted loss with the fields read correctly
> it is worth 2.5% of rejects, and the version that looks worthwhile (1,870, 15.9%) has **zero
> margin** and rests on one page measured on one post.

**What ships instead is the score, not the cut.** `paid_grid.page_view_score()` reads both count
names, requires **five** measured posts before it will call anything a median, and returns
`None` — never `0` — for a page it cannot measure. Ordering on it is free and cannot lose a
page; dropping needs a decision taken on the record, exactly as `judge_batches` already argues
for the SigLIP probe. `tests/test_bl1339_viewfloor.py` fails if a `FLOOR`/`CUT` constant ever
appears in the module.

**No follower floor was recommended or tested.** For the record only, his wanted pages run from
**957 followers** to 10.9M, median 33,790.

## In plain language

The reason you could not read that grid is stupid and completely fixable: Instagram's thumbnails
have big white margins, and the code was pasting the margins in and then squeezing everything
square. Cutting the blank space, leaving the pictures the shape they actually are, and showing
nine instead of twelve makes the writing about twice as tall. It reads now.

The view counts are real and they are new — Instagram has never given us a view number before.
But when I tested whether a "too few views" rule would throw away pages you want, it did, unless
I set the bar so low it barely removes anything. So I have shipped the number and refused the
rule. You can sort by it safely; cutting on it would cost you pages.

On the wall: I could not find the safe run size, because Instagram never let this connection
back in. Four hours and eleven minutes of checking, eighty-four requests, not one of them let
through. So the honest position is unchanged from yesterday — **135 pages is known safe, 1,200 is
known to fail, and the number in between is still unknown.** I would rather hand you that than a
figure I made up, and the probe is set up to find it the moment the connection recovers.
