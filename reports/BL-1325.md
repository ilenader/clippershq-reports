# BL-1325 — He is right: the vendor is 39× faster than we use it, and the slow part is ours

## The short answer

**5.05 profiles/min is a property of our code, exactly as the brief suspected.** The same
vendor, same account, same endpoint, measured live this round, serves **197.52 profiles/min at
8 concurrent workers** — **39.1×** the standing figure. His 40–100× is not only plausible, the
bottom of his range is what I measured on the first try.

**Nothing had to be bought, negotiated or re-architected to get it.** The vendor publishes no
per-request rate limit. Its own words: *"concurrency is handled by our infrastructure."* We
have been making one request at a time into a service that never asked us to.

**The single most useful sentence in this report:** `tiktok_finder.py` is **the only finder in
the repo without a lane pool.** youtube, twitch, spotify and google\_play all have one, so does
the judge, so does the MX probe. TikTok's per-author loop at `tiktok_finder.py:1214` is a plain
`for`. The client it calls is already thread-safe. This is a missing feature, not a hard problem.

## 1. The concurrency curve — measured twice, in opposite orders

I ran it ascending, then ran it again **reversed**, because the first run's fast arms were also
its *late* arms and I could not tell concurrency apart from the vendor simply settling down.

### Run 2 — reversed order, 0 errors, 0 retries (**the clean run, use this one**)

| workers | p50 | p90 | p99 | **profiles/min** | vs 1 worker |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1.954 s | 4.464 s | 7.250 s | **25.16** | 1.00× |
| 2 | 1.913 s | 4.038 s | 6.115 s | **58.72** | 2.33× |
| 4 | 1.535 s | 3.455 s | 4.647 s | **124.46** | 4.95× |
| **8** | **1.805 s** | **3.578 s** | **4.167 s** | **197.52** | **7.85×** |
| 16 | 2.594 s | 5.843 s | 7.103 s | **153.64** | 6.11× |

**The plateau is at 8, and 16 is worse than 8** — throughput falls 22%. That is a real ceiling,
not a measurement artefact: it reproduced in both runs, and 16 ran *first* in this one.

**Why it plateaus — the per-call p50 answers it.** From 1 to 8 workers latency is **flat**
(1.954 → 1.805 s). A vendor that was rate-limiting us would push latency *up* as workers rose;
it does not. Only at 16 does p50 climb to 2.594 s and p90 to 5.843 s while throughput drops —
the classic shape of a queue forming. **So the cause is contention somewhere above 8 lanes
(vendor-side or network), and below 8 there is no limiter at all.** Efficiency confirms it: at
8 workers we achieve 74% of the ideal 8 ÷ p50; at 16 only 41%.

### Run 1 — ascending order, and what it accidentally proved

Run 1 hit a burst of vendor HTTP 500s in its first three minutes. Its 1-worker arm read
**3.28/min**; run 2's clean 1-worker arm read **25.16/min**. **Same code, same level, 7.7×
apart** — the difference is entirely transient vendor 500s plus our own 13–16 s retry backoff.

That is worth keeping as a finding in its own right: **our backoff turns a brief vendor wobble
into a 7.7× throughput collapse.** Run 1's p99 of 36–40 s across three arms is not latency, it
is our retry sleeping. Any future measurement that does not report its error count is
unreadable for this reason.

### The self-imposed limiter — tested, and it is not the culprit

`api_client.py:441` sleeps `polite_delay_ms` (config: **150 ms**) before *every* call. Measured
head to head on the same pool, serial:

| arm | profiles/min |
|---|---:|
| polite delay **removed** | 25.29 |
| polite delay **as shipped (150 ms)** | 23.46 |

**It costs 7.2%.** Real, worth removing, and nowhere near the answer. I tested it rather than
assume it, and it is the one candidate the brief named that turned out innocent.

## 2. Where a page-second actually goes

Two independent methods agree. (a) An on-disk decomposition of two real runs via contact-sheet
mtimes — no network — solving `total = v·calls + s·sheets` across two runs whose calls-per-sheet
mix differs **3.0×** (8.83 vs 2.87); they agree within **2%**. (b) The live billed measurement
above.

| stage | s/page | share | note |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **handle search (billed)** | **7.02** | **59.0%** | of which **3.19 s is pure waste** — see below |
| profile call (billed) | 3.81 | 32.0% | one billed call |
| discovery, amortised (billed) | 0.90 | 7.6% | re-resolves the tag id on **every** depth page: 32 calls where 20 would do |
| polite delay (self-imposed) | 0.43 | 3.6% | measured at 7.2% of serial throughput |
| contact sheet (local CPU) | 0.36 | 3.0% | not vendor |
| **OCR** | **0.00** | **0.0%** | did not fire in these runs — **but see the trap in §3** |
| ffmpeg | 0.00 | 0.0% | did not fire |

**The vendor round-trip is 97.0% of the wall clock — and that is the point, not a defence of
the vendor.** It is 97% *because we buy them one at a time*.

**The tell that settles it:** even **serial**, the bare profile endpoint runs at **25.16
profiles/min**. The shipped funnel manages **5.05**. *The funnel is five times slower than its
own slowest single call*, because it spends ~2.87 billed calls per page and sleeps between them.

### 3.19 s/page is bought and then thrown away for free

`tiktok_finder.py:1223` spends a billed handle search. The **free** `judge_author` cut sits
**13 lines later at :1236**. In the measured run, **249 handle searches produced 135 profiles**
— **114 authors were paid for at 3.78 s each purely to be deleted by a rule that costs
nothing.** Moving the free cut above the billed buy is the cheapest fix in this report and it
costs no accuracy, because the cut is already being applied — just afterwards.

Row 2 reproduces the standing 5.05 at **5.35** — a 6% self-check on the model.

| Instagram configuration | capture | judge | pages/min | pages/8 h | **binding stage** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| paid judging (4 lanes) | 123.22 | 10.18 | **10.18** | 4,886 | **judging** (capture 12.1× faster) |
| free sub-agent judging | 123.22 | 6.46 | **6.46** | 3,101 | **judging** (capture 19.1× faster) |

**The Instagram finding is CONFIRMED, not corrected.** Capture is free and 12–19× faster than
judging; judging binds. Nothing this round touched it.

**The TikTok finding is CORRECTED, and twice over.**

* **"TikTok is vendor-bound" is wrong.** Fix the lane pool and the vendor stops binding at any
  volume this project runs.
* **But 5.05 was also flattering.** OCR was *off* in the run that produced it, while
  `config.json` ships `read_on_screen = true` and `run_funnel` honours it (`:1411`). The
  shipped path OCRs 12 frames/page at 2.74 s = **32.9 s of OCR alone**. **A real
  `--funnel tiktok` run today is ~44 s/page — 1.36 pages/min, not 5.05.** Anyone quoting 5.05
  as "what the pipeline does" is quoting a configuration nobody runs.

**So the honest sequence is: fix concurrency and the bottleneck moves to OCR, not to judging.**
Row 4 is the trap — pool the vendor, leave OCR on, and you land at 14.60/min with OCR binding.
Only rows 3 and 5 clear judging's 10.18, and both need OCR off or a decision about it.

## 4. What the vendor actually permits — quoted from its own specs

Three vendors, all three specs public and unauthenticated. I read them.

| vendor | published rate limit | concurrency |
|---|---|---|
| **LamaTok** (TikTok, ours) | **none published** | *"concurrency is handled by our infrastructure"*; contact them above 100 RPS |
| **HikerAPI** (Instagram) | *"All paid plans share the same rate limit of 15 requests per second"* | no numeric cap; *"Use semaphores"* |
| **TikHub** (TikTok + IG + ~20) | `⚡ Rate Limit: QPS: 10/Second` — and **per endpoint, not per account** | ~10·N RPS across N distinct paths |

**Our best measured point is 3.29 requests/second.** Against the two vendors that publish a
number, we are using **22–33% of the documented allowance**, and against ours, which publishes
none and mentions 100 RPS only as the threshold for *calling them*, we are three orders of
magnitude below the point where they want a conversation.

**There is no bulk profile endpoint on LamaTok** — no arrays, no comma-separated params, no
POST. N handles cost N calls. That is not the gap: his "2,000 videos in five minutes" is
~0.13 req/s at `count=50`, a *different unit* (videos in bulk) fetched in parallel. **The 40–100×
is parallelism plus the max-50 `v2` paths, not an endpoint we are missing.**

**Worth a follow-up round, not this one:** TikHub `fetch_user_search_result` at $0.00005/profile
carries **free server-side follower-band and verified filters** — the only pre-payment follower
floor in any of the three specs. `fetch_user_follower_list` is the same price with a documented
ceiling (*"count: Number, do not exceed 20"*). Both beat the $0.000087 baseline on paper. The
catch: every cheap many-profile endpoint returns a *short* user object with no follower count,
bio or external URL, so qualifying still costs a second call.

## 5. The 9% — the record needs correcting, but not in the direction expected

**I checked all three mark files myself, and all three are his own verdicts.**

| rate | k/n | population | file |
|---|---|---|---|
| **9.0%** [4.8, 16.2] | 9/100 | Instagram **new discovery** — zero overlap with anything he'd seen | `ground_truth/bl1296_his_marks.tsv` |
| **28.0%** [20.1, 37.5] | 28/100 | Instagram **enriched stock** — pages he had already marked | `ground_truth/bl1298_instagram_marks (1).json` |
| **37.0%** [28.2, 46.8] | 37/100 | TikTok, first human ground truth | `ground_truth/bl1298_tiktok_marks.json` |

The 9% file's column is literally named **`his_verdict`** and its values are literally **`WANT`
/ `REJECT`** — 9 WANT, 91 REJECT. **9% is a want rate.** It is not an address rate.

Corroboration: of those exact 100 pages, **1** has a known address. If the 9 were addresses it
would read ~9. And `reports/BL-1309.md:195` says that source carries *"no email, no follower
count"* in words.

**Where the address-rate theory came from — it is itself the error.** `reports/BL-1320.md:203`
says *"The 9% looks like the rate at which pages publish an address"*. That was written without
opening the mark file, and it cites 10.6%, not 9%. There *is* a real 9/100 address figure that
probably seeded the confusion: of the 100 marked **TikTok** pages, 9 have an address — **0 of
the 37 he wants, 9 of the 63 he rejects**. Different platform, different set, coincidental
numerator.

**The genuine arithmetic misuse is a platform mismatch, and it is 4.11×.**
`reports/BL-1320.md:183` divides 1,000 wanted by the **Instagram** new-discovery 9%, gets
11,111 pages, then spends that on **TikTok** vendor fetch hours (36.7 h at `:191`). Against his
own TikTok rate of 37% it is 2,703 pages and **8.9 h — overstated 4.11×.** Its source
`scratch/bl1320_ceiling.py:22` hardcodes `"the brief's 9%": 0.09`, and **neither brief contains
a 9% want rate** (grepped `bl1265_brief_v8.md` and `bl1270_brief_tt2.md`: they carry a 29.2%
precision and a 30.9% base rate). The false provenance is probably why the number looked invented.

### So what should he be told

**All three are his.** 28% and 37% are the rates for pages that reach him through the current
enriched supply. **9% is the rate for genuinely new discovery**, and it is the one to plan new
supply against — using 28% to buy new supply overstates the yield by roughly **three times**.

Address rates, each with its own denominator and **not averaged**: master rows 11.7%
(7,345/62,675); `lead_kind == clipper` 7.0%; `platform == tiktok` 5.4%; TikTok fresh 10.6%
(n=170); Instagram fresh 16.9% (n=361). (`platform == instagram` reads 56.9% but rows only
enter master once an address resolves — a selected population, not quotable as a discovery rate.)

**One documentation defect found, not fixed, because I do not hold the file.** `docs/FACTS.md`
prose at `:185` declares the 19.0% void and says *"Use 28.0%"* — but its machine-readable claim
block at **`:490` still publishes `instagram_pass_rate_operator_marks = 19.0`**. Its §4 table
has **no 9.0% row at all**, so the new-discovery rate is missing from the one place that is
supposed to hold it. Both need a round that holds `docs/FACTS.md`.

   is a complete reusable pattern *including a reserve-counter spend gate*. `LamaTokClient` is
   already thread-safe (`_req_lock`). Set the pool to **8** — measured optimum; 16 is worse.
   **Expected 5.35 → 43.48 pages/min.**
   ⚠️ **`clippershq/tiktok_finder.py` is held by BL-1326 as of this writing.** The fix belongs
   there. This round changed no code by design.
2. **Move the free `judge_author` cut above the billed handle search** (`:1236` before `:1223`).
   Deletes 114 of 249 paid calls — **3.19 s and ~0.84 billed calls per page, for free.**
3. **Decide what `read_on_screen = true` is for.** It is 32.9 s/page and it is on in shipped
   config. Either turn it off or pool it; leaving it serial caps TikTok at **1.36 pages/min**
   whatever else is fixed.
4. **Cache the hashtag id** — `videos_from_hashtag` re-resolves it on every depth page (32 calls
   where 20 would do).
5. **Drop `polite_delay_ms` to 0 for TikTok.** Worth 7.2%. Do it last; it is the smallest.
6. Correct `docs/FACTS.md` `:490` and add the 9.0% new-discovery row.
