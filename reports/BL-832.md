# BL-832: The crawl costs **$0.88 per 1,000 contactable leads** — $20.63 was a category error, but so was "4.3%", and the DM pool is not the goldmine BL-757 implied

**Date:** 2026-08-01 · **Class:** Audit · **Spend:** **$0.00** — no paid calls, no changes.
**Provenance:** every figure below from one frozen snapshot — `measured on master_leads.csv, 24,422,509 bytes, mtime 2026-07-31 23:46:36`, read via `read_snapshot.measure_master()` so numerator and denominator agree.

---

## 1. The headline, counting handles

**100% of crawl rows are contactable.** All 55,383 carry a handle; that is what the crawl produces. So:

| basis | figure |
|---|---|
| **cost per 1,000 CONTACTABLE leads** | **$0.88** |
| cost per 1,000 rows | $0.88 (identical — every row is contactable) |
| cost per 1,000 emailed | $14.74 (recomputed; BL-826 said $20.63) |

**$20.63 was a category error and you were right to suspect it.** It divides the full cost of the crawl by the 5% of its output that happens to carry an email, as though the other 95% were waste. They are not waste — they are the product. The crawl is a handle machine, it is priced as a handle machine, and at **$0.88 per 1,000 contactable leads** it is the cheapest large-scale contact source in the system.

The honest sentence is: *the crawl buys handles at $0.88/1,000 and throws in an email on about one row in twenty.*

---

## 2. Why email fill is ~5% — not drift, not a thin slice, a **mix effect**

BL-826 flagged its own window as thin. I measured the whole population instead, and the answer is neither of the two hypotheses:

| slice | rows | with email | fill |
|---|---|---|---|
| **all crawl, lifetime** | 55,383 | 2,915 | **5.26%** |
| BL-826 window (≥2026-07-11) | 1,995 | 119 | **5.96%** |
| everything older | 53,388 | 2,796 | 5.24% |
| 2026-06 | 1,060 | 30 | 2.83% |
| 2026-07 | 54,323 | 2,885 | 5.31% |

**The recent window is not worse than the lifetime rate — it is slightly better** (5.96% vs 5.24%). So the figure does not rest on an unrepresentative slice, and there has been no drift.

The reason it reads as a drop from earlier rounds is that "the crawl" is three funnels blended, and their email rates are an order of magnitude apart:

| sub-funnel | rows | with email | fill | earlier measurement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok hashtag | 54,521 | 2,757 | **5.06%** | 5.1% — **unchanged** |
| IG hashtag | 806 | 107 | **13.3%** | ~15% — **unchanged** |
| IG editor crawl | 56 | 51 | **91.1%** | (15% figure was not this) |

**Nothing drifted.** TikTok still sits at 5.1% and IG hashtag still sits in the low teens, exactly where they were measured before. The blended number is ~5% because **TikTok is 98.4% of the crawl** — the blend simply reports TikTok's rate. The "15% IG crawl" figure earlier rounds quoted describes a funnel that has produced 56 rows in its entire life and is currently switched off.

I could not reproduce BL-826's exact 85/1,961; on this snapshot the same window definition gives 119/1,995. The master gained ~3,000 rows between the two reads, which is enough to explain it, and is the reason this round used a frozen snapshot.

## 3. Cost per handle across every funnel

| funnel | ledger spend | rows attributable | **per 1,000 handles** | lifetime rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Twitch | $0.1512 | 877 | **$0.17** | 877 |
| Spotify | $0.6696 | 1,721 | **$0.39** | 1,721 |
| email-finder | $0.0324 | 60 | $0.54 | 60 |
| **crawl (ZHUS + DAYLIGHT)** | $1.7536 | 1,995 | **$0.88** | **55,383** |
| repost | $0.1170 | 71 | $1.65 | 71 |
| Google Play | $0 | 212 | **$0.00** | 212 |
| YouTube | $0 recorded | 27 | $0.00 | 27 |

**Twitch is 5× cheaper per handle than the crawl. It is also capped.** Twitch has produced 877 handles in its lifetime against the crawl's 55,383 — sixty-three times fewer. Google Play is free but has produced 212. The answer to "is the crawl the best handle source or just the biggest" is: **it is the only one that operates at scale.** Nothing else in the system produces handles in five figures, and the cheap funnels are cheap partly because they stop early.

**Caveat, stated plainly:** the ledger holds 113 runs totalling $4.26 while the master holds 58,354 rows, so most rows predate or are not attributable to a recorded run. Per-funnel costs above divide recorded spend by rows in the attributable window; they are order-of-magnitude comparisons, not audited unit costs. The crawl's $0.88 is BL-826's own window basis, kept for comparability.

## 4. Is the crawl discarding free contact surfaces? **Almost none — and that is the finding**

The five-times pattern (Twitch `socialMedias`, YouTube `snippet.description`, the clips engagement panel, Spotify `followers`, Helix `title`) is: payload carried it, we stored it, nothing read it. For the crawl the stored surface is `bio`, present on **89.8%** of rows. So I searched every crawl row whose `email` column is empty for an address hiding in its bio:

- strict `a@b.c` in bio with the email column empty: **22 rows**
- obfuscated only (`(at)`, `[at]`, ` at `): **154 rows**
- **total recoverable: 176 rows = 0.32% of the crawl**

And reading those 22, several are my regex misfiring on handle mentions — `Duo-@cris.vfxs`, `Z***@makoto.gif` — so the genuine count is lower still. Real hits look like `upminaa@1up.management` and `promo@hypeorbit.music`.

**This is a negative result worth having: the crawl is not failing to extract emails. There are no emails to extract.** The bio scanner is working — the traced lead in BL-819 got its address from exactly this path — and after it runs, the remaining 52,468 bios contain essentially nothing. **TikTok editors do not publish email addresses.** That closes item 4 for email: no wasted surface, no fix available, 5% is the ceiling of what this population offers.

**One surface is genuinely under-read, and it is handles, not emails.** 1,550 crawl rows name an Instagram handle in the bio while the `instagram` column is empty — 2.9% of rows lacking one. Extracting those would take the IG-reachable pool from **3,319 to 4,869 (1.5×)** at $0.

Two things temper that. My regex over-matches (`ART ACC IS : @•Zhyzhy•`, `Etsy - AMLD64` are not IG handles), so realistically 800–1,100 are genuine. And an earlier round already measured this exact population: **TikTok-declared IG handles responded at 6% / 1.2% against 22.7% for cold IG** — they are reachable but they are the worst-performing IG cohort measured. A 1.5× bigger pool of the weakest cohort is worth having for free and is not worth building a project around.

## 5. The DM pool — **BL-757's framing does not survive the full distribution**

| | count |
|---|---|
| DM pool (handle, no email) | 53,662 |
| **IG-reachable** | **3,319 (6.2%)** |
| TikTok-only | 50,343 (93.8%) |

BL-757 said the best email lead had 1.76M median views while the DM list opened at 6.9M and reached 31.7M, and concluded the biggest accounts do not publish an email. **That was a top-25 comparison. On the full distribution it does not hold:**

| | n | median reach | p90 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **IG-reachable DM pool** | 2,185 | **21,400** | 1,022,550 | 31,700,000 |
| **rows with an email** | 2,844 | **25,210** | 966,800 | **36,800,000** |

The email population has a **higher** median (25,210 vs 21,400), a comparable p90, and the single largest account in the system (36.8M, above the DM pool's 31.7M). The two are the same kind of population. BL-757's observation was real at the extreme tail and does not generalise — the biggest accounts are not systematically email-less.

**What a realistic DM strategy costs:** 3,319 IG-reachable handles, free to message, no reputation risk, at roughly the same value per contact as email. That is a genuine parallel channel — it is simply not the *better* one, and it is 6% the size of the raw DM pool. The other 50,343 are TikTok handles where DMs are not free at scale, so the honest DM inventory is **3,319 today, 4,400ish if the bio handles are extracted**, not 53,662.

---

## 6. Recommendation: **keep it, reframe it, change nothing about how it runs**

**Keep.** At $0.88 per 1,000 contactable leads it is the cheapest scale contact source you have, and it is the only funnel producing handles in five figures. Judged as a handle machine it is working exactly as designed.

**Stop quoting the per-emailed figure.** $20.63 (or $14.74 on current data) is the price of a by-product. Publishing it invites the conclusion that the crawl is broken, which the contactable figure refutes by a factor of 17.

**Do not retune for email.** §4 shows the surface is already fully harvested and the population does not carry addresses. Raising email fill from 5% would require a different population, not a better crawl.

**The one free improvement worth doing** is extracting bio-declared Instagram handles — 1.5× the IG-DMable pool at $0 — with expectations set by the 6%/1.2% response measurement on that cohort.

**And fix the denominator problem underneath all of this.** The ledger cannot attribute most of the master's rows to a run, which is why every cost-per-lead figure in this report needs a caveat. Until a run stamps its rows, unit economics stay approximate.
