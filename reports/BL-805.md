## 4. The 20,000 floor is correctly placed. It is not the cause

View distribution across 492 observed clips:

| percentile | views |
|---|---|
| p10 | 2,840 |
| p25 | **18,402** |
| p50 | **137,089** |
| p75 | 666,712 |
| p90 | 1,910,020 |

| floor | clips kept | % | pages with ≥1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10,000 | 390 | 79% | 41 of 41 |
| **20,000** | **365** | **74%** | **41 of 41** |
| 50,000 | 326 | 66% | 40 of 41 |
| 100,000 | 274 | 56% | 37 of 41 |

The floor sits almost exactly at **p25** and keeps **74%** of what these pages post. Dropping
it to 10,000 buys **25 clips (+6.8%)** and costs the bottom quartile's quality; dropping to
5,000 buys 46 (+12.6%).

**No page is excluded by the floor** — all 41 have at least one clip above it. A floor that
excludes nobody cannot be the cause of a six-account library. **Leave it at 20,000.**

## 5. Recommendation: a per-account cap — and it needs no code

`clip_runner.run()` has no clips-per-account limit. It walks accounts in master order and
stops only when the global target is met, so the single bound is
`clip_max_pages_per_account` (default **10** = up to 120 clips from one page).

Modelled against the measured yields, target 200:

| per-account cap | clips | accounts used | top-2 share | calls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **observed run (no cap)** | 200 | **6** | **95.5%** | 27 |
| 5 | 177 | 41 | **6%** | 82 |
| **10** | **200** | **20** | **10%** | 40 |
| **12 (= 1 page)** | **200** | **17** | **12%** | **34** |

**Do this first: set `clip_max_pages_per_account: 1`.** It is an existing config knob, already
read by `clip_runner.load_knobs()`, requiring no code change. One page per account is a
de-facto 12-clip cap, and it takes the library from 6 accounts at 95.5% top-2 concentration to
**17 accounts at 12%** — for **34 calls ($0.0204), fewer than the 27-call run that produced the
concentrated version at comparable cost**.

**Then more discovery, for a different reason.** A cap of 5 gives the flattest library (6%
top-2) but yields only **177 of 200** from all 41 pages — the pool is too small to support the
most diverse setting. BL-732's path (~140 pages, $0.13, 1.7 h at 52.2% marginal confirm) is
what makes a cap of 5 reachable, and it is cheap.

**Do not lower the floor.** It buys 25 clips and no additional pages.

**One caveat on the cap table.** The censored accounts are counted as exactly 12 because 12 is
all page 1 reveals, so the `cap ≥ 12` rows are indistinguishable in this model and the no-cap
case is *understated* — in reality an uncapped walk concentrates worse than the table implies,
as the observed run's 95.5% shows.

**A cap alone does not choose *which* accounts.** With target 200 and a 12-cap, `run()` takes
the first 17 pages in master order and never reaches the other 24. Genuine coverage of all 41
needs either a target ≥492 or round-robin ordering — and round-robin is a code change, out of
scope here.

## Honest limits

- **Yields are censored for 23 of 41 pages.** Page 1 shows the top 12 only, so "≥12" is all
  that can be said. The 365 lower bound would need a second page per account (+41 calls,
  $0.0246) to tighten, and the true figure is materially higher.
- **The distinct-operator count rests on handle spelling.** With no bio, email, follower or
  hashtag data on these rows, two unrelated-looking handles run by one operator are
  undetectable by this sweep. Caption comparison — the method that caught the one known pair —
  has been run on 6 of 41 pages.
- **Page 1 is view-sorted, so these are best-case catalogues.** Median-clip performance for a
  whole account is not measurable from the top 12 and is not claimed here.
- **The cap figures are a model over measured yields, not a run.** No capped walk was executed;
  doing so would write to the library, which this brief excluded.
- `rainbowmovies_9` has failed to resolve twice (BL-804 and here). It is dead supply still
  counted in the 42 and should be marked, not silently re-attempted every run.
