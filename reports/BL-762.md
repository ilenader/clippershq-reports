### Why 2M and not 1M or 5M

1. **1M is too low.** Median views/year of the rescued group is 352,572 — barely distinguishable from routine. Stepping to 2M triples it to 1,107,117. That step is the boundary between "has an audience" and "posted a lot once".
2. **The control separates cleanly at 2M.** Sub-50k channels below 1M views (n=108) run a median of **25,358 views/year**. The N=2M group runs 1.1M — a 44× gap. There is no need to climb higher to avoid junk.
3. **2M is calibrated against what already gets in.** Channels passing on subscribers have p10 = 2,718,523 views. N=2M sits just below that, so the clause is not a side door — it admits channels at least as watched as the quietest decile already flowing through. Only 3 of 64 passers (4.7%) are below 2M.
4. **3M and 5M buy nothing.** 3M rescues 13 instead of 14 for an identical median profile. 5M drops to 9, losing five live channels (e.g. 3.9M views / 1.5M per year) with the control already separated.

**The brief predicted N between 2M and 5M. The measurement puts it at the bottom of that band.**

### A guard gap found and closed

BL-750 measured the yield *through* `valid_email` + the junk blocklist + `PLATFORM_HOP_DOMAINS`. The helpers this funnel inherits (`_pick_email`/`_clean_email`, borrowed from the Google-Play finder) check regex shape and the asset-TLD trap but **deliberately never call `bio_parser.valid_email`** — on their own they pass `info@youtube.com`. A `_guarded_pick()` wrapper now applies the full chain at this call site. **`_clean_email` itself was left untouched**: it is shared with Google Play, and widening it would silently move that funnel's numbers.

A funnel-scoped `_OWN_PLATFORM_DOMAINS` set was added for the same reason — an address *at* the platform hosting the channel is boilerplate, never the creator's inbox — following the precedent `bio_parser` already sets for funnel-scoped hop domains, rather than widening the shared lists every funnel reads.

**A pleasing accident worth recording:** the one address rejected post-gate was **a role address at the `meri.et` domain (channel name withheld)** — the very channel BL-748 flagged as the self-declared-country false pass (declares `country=US`, Ethiopian domain). It was caught by `valid_email`'s TLD allowlist, **not** by any geo check. Do not mistake this for the country gate working.

## PART 3 — hiddenSubscriberCount

When YouTube flags the subscriber count as not public, that number is not evidence of smallness and can no longer be the reason for a cut. Such channels **skip the subscriber test entirely** and are judged on views; if views are unknown too they are **kept and flagged** (`size_flag = "subscriber count hidden"`) rather than dropped on a figure the API disowns.

**Honest limit: `hiddenSubscriberCount` was true on 0 of 200 channels in this sample.** The guard is correct by construction and is unit-verified, but this sample cannot demonstrate its real-world impact.

## PART 6 — customUrl and publishedAt, kept

Both arrive free in the same `channels.list` response and were being parsed and discarded.

- **`customUrl` → `tiktok_url`** (the generic profile-URL column) as `https://www.youtube.com/@handle`, falling back to the `/channel/<UC…>` form. The `@handle` link survives a channel rename; the id form is stable but opaque.
- **`publishedAt` → `quality_note`**, as `channel created YYYY-MM-DD`, alongside lifetime views and any size flag. **Deliberately not written to `last_post_date`**: that column means "last activity" and channel creation is the opposite end of the channel's life. Filing it there would have quietly corrupted every dormancy calculation downstream.
- `viewCount` also now surfaces in the xlsx (`Lifetime views`, `Channel created`) so the number driving the new gate is visible to whoever reads the sheet.

Fill on the sample: `viewCount` 200/200, `publishedAt` 200/200, `customUrl` 199/200.

## PART 7 — the country gate's self-declared field, recorded not fixed

`snippet.country` is **set by the channel owner and not verified by YouTube**. The measured case stands: a 236k-subscriber podcast declares `country=US` while its contact domain is `.et`. The gate therefore filters *stated* geography, not actual geography.

Recorded as a documented precision ceiling in the code, with the reason no fix is offered: there is no free corrective signal (a contact-domain TLD is weak on its own — plenty of US creators use `.co`/`.io`), and a paid geo lookup is precisely the cost this free gate exists to avoid.
