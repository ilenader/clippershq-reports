# BL-1271 — what a wanted page actually costs, and whether more browsers would help

**Reconciliation round. $0.00 spent** — declared before starting, in `scratch/bl1271_budget.json`,
which deliberately does **not** claim `spend.json`, so any spend would have required re-filing.
Every number below is a ledger row, a recorded usage block, or a k/n I counted. Anything
computed is labelled a projection and the measured inputs it multiplies are named.

**Interpreter: `.venv\Scripts\python.exe` (3.11.9) by full path**, for every figure in this
report. `python` on PATH is 3.13, lacks the project's packages, and produced nothing here.

**The five answers, up front:**

1. **A wanted TikTok page costs $0.0038. A wanted Instagram page costs $0.0076.** For 1,000 of
   them: **$3.82** and **$7.58**, on the free judging path.
2. **Judging paid instead of free multiplies that by 24× on TikTok and 29× on Instagram** —
   $91.95 and $223.01 per 1,000 wanted pages.
3. **The $1.721753 that "produced 160 judged pages" did nothing of the sort.** 15.7% of it
   fetched 135 pages; 84.3% of it judged **thirty-four**; 124 more were judged for nothing.
4. **Emails are the trap, and not for the reason anyone assumed.** P(email | WANT) on TikTok is
   **2.6%** — not the 10.6% discovery rate. Addresses concentrate in the pages the judge
   **throws away**.
5. **More Chrome windows would not help. Do not open them.** I measured the stage that is
   actually the bottleneck: **8× the agents buys 1.94× the throughput**, and per-page latency
   *rises* as agents are added.

### 1.2 Calls per page, counted from the ledger and not from the code

**TikTok — 3.0815 calls per profile bought.** 17 ledger rows under `TIKTOK_FINDER`
(21:07:04 → 21:32:35) sum to **416 billed calls / $0.249600**; the run's own
`run.json` records **135 profiles bought, 134 sheets built** from them. Those 416 calls also
paid for the discovery that found the authors in the first place — 317 videos → 265 authors →
249 handle searches → 207 reachable — and that overhead belongs in the unit cost, because you
cannot buy a profile you have not discovered.

```
3.0815 calls x $0.000600 = $0.001849 per TikTok page fetched      (n=135)
```

*Floor, stated because the ledger errs high:* `reserve()` counts **attempts before the HTTP
call**, and the vendor counted **377 of the 416 reserved** — 9.4% over. The receipt-true unit
is **$0.001676**. I use the ledger figure throughout, so every TikTok number here is
conservative by ~9%.

**Instagram — 2 calls per page, and they are structurally necessary.** `user_medias` answers
`400 user_id must be a number` for a username, so `user_by_username` must run first for the id.
Measured at **$0.0012/page** on n=4 unloaded and n=25 under concurrent load (`reports/BL-1239.md:115`).

### 1.3 The profile double-buy, costed both ways

`resolve_contact` returns the email but drops `user_id`; `profile_facts` returns `user_id` but
drops the email. Same endpoint, same handle, no cache — so a **third** profile call is bought
for every page that passes the meme filter. Measured over 281 real pages
(`reports/BL-1230.md:305-310`): **$1.37 and 56 minutes per 5,700 pages**.

That reconciles exactly, and the reconciliation is worth showing because the prose overstates
its own scope. 5,700 × $0.0006 = $3.42, not $1.37 — because $1.37 / $0.0006 = **2,283 calls =
5,700 × 40.06%**, the pass rate. The dollar is correctly scoped to the *passing* subset that
reaches `resolve_contact`; the sentence "every judged page" is wrong in the same paragraph as
the right number.

| | per page fetched | per 1,000 fetched | per 1,000 **wanted** | extra time per 1,000 wanted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| double-buy **live** (today) | $0.001440 | $1.44 | **$7.58** | +0.86 h |
| double-buy **fixed** | $0.001200 | $1.20 | **$6.32** | — |
| **the defect costs** | $0.000240 | $0.24 | **$1.26** | **0.86 h** |

**Status: unfixed at HEAD.** The fix exists as dead code — `profile_and_contact`
(`meme_finder.py:2666`) has **zero production callers**, which BL-1251 already flagged. Both
live call sites are unchanged (`meme_finder.py:2877` and `:2987`), and `ig_client.py` has no
cache of any kind.

### 1.4 The pass rate, and which number I use

| population | k / n | rate | 95% CI | label |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| **TikTok, all judged** | **77 / 159** | **48.4%** | [40.8, 56.1] | **PASS RATE — used** |
| TikTok, paid arm | 13 / 35 | 37.1% | [23.2, 53.7] | metered, independence in code |
| TikTok, free arm | 71 / 134 | 53.0% | [44.6, 61.2] | independence only instructed |
| **Instagram, his marks** | **19 / 100** | **19.0%** | [12.5, 27.8] | **PASS RATE — used** |
| Instagram, "18 of 100" | 18 / 100 | 18.0% | [11.7, 26.7] | **understated** |
| Instagram, "26.4%" | 19 / 72 | 26.4% | [17.6, 37.6] | ⛔ **FILTERED denominator** |

**Three corrections, each of which changes a number he has been given.**

**"77 of 160" divides a judged numerator by an offered denominator.** 160 is the *sheet row
count*; one row is `NOT JUDGED - no sheet`. **159 rows carry a verdict.** (The verdicts file's
own `tally` key is also stale — it still holds the 34-page paid tally. The verdicts recompute
from the rows.)

**"18 of 100" is the wrong Instagram figure; 19 is right.** `mark` in this project means *"the
judge's verdict was correct"*, not *"this page is good"*, so the label must be derived:
`he_wants = (judge == WANT) XOR (mark == BAD)`. That gives WANT∧GOOD 18, plus **REJECT∧BAD 1** —
one page he wants that the judge lost. 18 is the WANT∧GOOD cell alone and silently drops it.

**26.4% is not comparable to anything else here.** It is 19 WANTs over **72 survivors of a
free pre-sort that killed 28 pages before judging**. Same 19 WANTs, smaller denominator. It is
a cost-per-WANT improvement, not a supply improvement — zero extra wanted pages exist — and
quoting it beside 19.0% or 48.4% compares a post-filter yield to a pre-filter one.

## 2. The BL-1260 reconciliation — where the figures went wrong

$1.721753 was spent and 160 rows were shipped. Dividing gives **$0.010761 per page**, and that
number is meaningless. Here is what the money actually did:

| what the money did | $ | share | what it bought |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **FETCHING** | $0.270600 | **15.7%** | 451 calls → **135 profiles, 134 sheets** |
| **JUDGING, paid** | $1.451153 | **84.3%** | **34 pages** at $0.042681 |
| **JUDGING, free** | $0.000000 | 0% | **124 pages** |
| reused from the first leg | $0.000000 | — | 1 page |

**Pages FETCHED: 135. Pages JUDGED: 159. They are not the same number and they are not the
same money.** The naive average divides *judging* money by a page count that is mostly pages
judged for free, and blends three unit costs that differ by two orders of magnitude. That is
the whole error.

## 3. Emails — his real unit, and the number that changes the plan

### 3.1 Fresh base rates, each with its own denominator

| | k / n | rate | 95% CI |
|---|---|---:|---|
| TikTok — publishes an email at all | 18 / 170 | **10.6%** | [6.8, 16.1] |
| TikTok — has a structured bioLink | 28 / 170 | 16.5% | [11.6, 22.8] |
| TikTok — Instagram cross-link | 3 / 170 | 1.8% | [0.6, 5.1] |
| TikTok — cross-link, larger sample | 55 / 1,047 | 5.3% | [4.1, 6.8] |
| **Instagram — any address route** | **61 / 361** | **16.9%** | [13.4, 21.1] |

**⛔ Never quote these as base rates:** 59.1% bioLink and 27.8% cross-link were measured on
**115 pages selected for already having an email**. They overstate the fresh rates by **3.6×
and 5.2×**. An account that publishes one contact route publishes others; the figures are
conditioned on the outcome being measured.

**Instagram's email supply is a ceiling, not a gap.** Of 300 addressless pages, **230 publish
no link at all** — 76.7% [71.6, 81.1]. There is nothing to follow. Every additional automated
path measured was worth **+1 page, +0.28pp**.

### 3.2 The join that matters, and it is brutal

The operator's unit is emails, so the number that governs is P(email | **WANT**) — not
P(email | any page). They are different, and they run the wrong way:

| TikTok | with email | n | P(email \| verdict) | 95% CI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **WANT** | **2** | **77** | **2.6%** | **[0.7, 9.0]** |
| REJECT | 15 | 67 | **22.4%** | [14.1, 33.7] |
| any judged page | 17 | 159 | 10.7% | [6.8, 16.5] |

**A REJECT is 8.6× more likely to carry an address than a WANT.** The mechanism is the same one
that inflates the 115-file: publishing an address correlates with being a promo, agency or
creator page — exactly what the rubric kills. **Sizing an email pipeline off the 10.6%
discovery rate overstates deliverable leads by about 4×**, because most of that 10.6% does not
survive the judge.

Corroboration from a round in flight as I write: BL-1270 ran this join deliberately on its own
surviving WANTs and got **4 / 63 = 6.3% [2.5, 15.2]**. Another round's number, same direction,
same order of magnitude.

## 4. The browser question, settled by measurement

He has asked three times. The answer is **no**, and here is the measurement rather than the
argument.

### 4.1 What the 10.18 pages/min is actually made of

First, what it *is*: `scratch/bl1263_run_sized.json`, **12 pages, 4 lanes, paid**, and it
contains **the model HTTP call and essentially nothing else**. Capture was read off disk; the
brief and reference card were loaded before the clock started; the cache-warm call was made
before the clock started. Harness overhead measured by subtraction is **0.1 s of 70.7 s**.

It is also **a harness artefact**. It uses a static split `handles[i::lanes]`, so lane load
ranged 47.9–70.6 s against a 70.6 s critical path — 16% of the wall is idle lanes. The same
vendor and model with a **shared queue** at 6 lanes did **15.93 pages/min** on 34 pages
(`output/bl1260_tiktok_run/verdicts.json`), with near-identical per-call latency (median 17.23 s
vs 17.56 s). **Nobody has been quoting that figure.**

So I measured the free path's stages directly, with each agent stamping its own clock
(`scratch/bl1271_curve.py --stamp`, one directory per agent):

| stage, 1 agent × 8 pages | seconds | share of wall |
|---|---:|---:|
| **agent startup** (launch → agent's first stamp) | **23.13** | 10.0% |
| reading the 28,282-char brief | 7.48 | 3.2% |
| **8 × model turn** (9.94 … 39.36, median 19.8) | **165.55** | **71.4%** |
| writing verdicts + final stamp | 35.56 | 15.3% |
| **outer wall** | **231.72** | **2.07 pages/min** |

**Model latency is 71% of it.** Fixed per-agent overhead — startup plus brief — is 30.6 s,
which at one page per agent would be larger than the work itself.

### 4.2 The parallel JUDGING curve — measured, not inherited

Every curve on record is **capture**. The famous 5.91 / 11.91 / 23.42 / 33.96 / 33.48 at
1/2/4/6/8 lanes ran Playwright screenshots with **no model call in the loop**, and its
six-lane "plateau" was arithmetic: 12 pages over 8 lanes leaves the deepest lane holding 2,
identical to the 6-lane arm, so the wall could not fall. Per-page service time was flat at
10.07–10.75 s across every arm — the signature of nothing saturating.

This is the judging curve. Same 8 pages in every configuration, so page difficulty cannot
confound it. Free path, **$0.00**:

| agents | pages | outer wall | **pages/min** | speed-up | efficiency | per-page median | per-page **max** |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 8 | 231.72 s | **2.07** | 1.00× | 100% | 19.8 s | 39.4 s |
| 2 | 8 | 146.01 s | **3.29** | 1.59× | 79% | 17.8 s | 38.5 s |
| 4 | 8 | 139.03 s | **3.45** | 1.67× | 42% | 21.6 s | 51.7 s |
| 8 | 8 | 119.79 s | **4.01** | **1.94×** | **24%** | 21.2 s | **62.0 s** |

**Eight times the agents buys 1.94× the throughput.** And unlike the fake capture plateau,
this one is real saturation: **per-page latency rises** with concurrency — the maximum goes
39.4 → 62.0 s — which is precisely the diagnostic the capture curve lacked.

Two more things fell out for free:

- **Repeatability is 87.5%.** 7 of the 8 pages were judged identically in all four
  configurations. One page (`footyfr3`) came back WANT, WANT, WANT, REJECT. On the free path,
  roughly **one page in eight is a coin-flip**, which matters more than throughput does.
- **The paid path is ~4× faster than the free path** (15.93 vs 4.01 pages/min). Free is free;
  it is not fast.

### 4.3 So: should he open 5–10 Chrome windows?

**No. Four independent reasons, each measured.**

1. **Browsers serve capture, and capture is not the bottleneck.** Instagram capture already
   runs at **123.22 pages/min** against judging's 4–16. It is 8–31× faster than the stage
   behind it. Making it faster changes nothing.
2. **On TikTok, browsers are irrelevant entirely.** Playwright cannot reach a TikTok profile —
   headless gets "Something went wrong", headed gets a slider CAPTCHA. TikTok pages are
   rebuilt from cover images over plain HTTP, and that pipeline runs at **5.05 profiles/min**,
   which is a **vendor-API** rate. No browser is involved and no number of windows moves it.
3. **The 123.22 figure is Instagram-only, and using it for TikTok is a 24× error.** These two
   rates have been quoted side by side as if they were the same pipeline.
4. **The stage that IS the bottleneck barely parallelises** — §4.2, 1.94× for 8×. And the
   extension can only screenshot the **active tab** (background tabs failed 0 of 3 with 30 s
   CDP timeouts), so N windows were never N lanes anyway. That is exactly why Playwright
   replaced the extension and lanes became a parameter — **lanes are already a parameter**, so
   there is nothing windows would add.

**Each agent got its own output directory** (`scratch/bl1271_curve/cNN_agentNN/`), because one
agent in fourteen once read another's crop off a shared scratchpad. That requirement had been
recorded and never implemented; this round implements it.

### The verdict on 1,000 emails a day

| configuration | emails/day | $/day | short by |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| TikTok, free judging | 73 | $10.68 | 13.8× |
| TikTok, paid judging | 92 | $323.96 | 10.9× |
| Instagram, free judging | 185 | $8.32 | 5.4× |
| **Instagram, paid judging** | **737** | **$972.22** | **1.4×** |

**No. Not at any measured throughput.** The best configuration on record reaches **737 emails
a day** and costs **$972 a day** to do it — $355,000 a year, to miss the target by 1.4×. The
cheap configurations miss it by 5–14×.

**What would have to change, in order of leverage:**

1. **Stop planning on TikTok for email.** P(email | WANT) is 2.6% there and 16.9% on
   Instagram — **6.5×**. Every TikTok row in that table is dominated by an Instagram row.
2. **Judge throughput is the binding constraint in five of eight rows.** The paid path was
   measured at 15.93 p/min at 6 lanes with a shared queue and **has never been tried above
   6 lanes**. That is the one untested lever with real headroom, and it is a scheduler change,
   not a browser.
3. **`effort=low` halves model latency** — median 17.56 s → 8.83 s — which would roughly double
   judge throughput. It is **unproven on accuracy** and would need scoring against his marks
   before it could be trusted.
4. **Accept that Instagram's email supply has a ceiling.** 230 of 300 addressless pages publish
   no link at all. More crawling does not reach them; it is not a gap to close.
5. **Fix the double-buy.** Small on this scale — $1.26 and 0.86 h per 1,000 wanted pages — but
   the fix is already written and merely has no caller.

An honest floor: at **$44.86 per 1,000 emails and 185/day**, Instagram on the free path is the
cheapest sustainable configuration on record. Reaching 1,000/day means finding roughly 5× more
judge throughput without losing accuracy — and nothing measured so far suggests more browsers
is where it comes from.
