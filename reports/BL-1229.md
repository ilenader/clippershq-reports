## THE HEADLINE

**The seam was not a bug in the rotation. It IS the rotation, and it was put there
deliberately eleven hours earlier by BL-1226 — which fixed a real defect and created this
one in the same line of code.**

His report:

> "the song is going and it literally cuts, and after about a second and a half it starts
> from a different part. It's like h2 is playing and then it switches to h1. That should
> NEVER happen. One part only, from start to finish."

`clip_pipeline.hook_chain` builds the bed as an infinite loop of a marked window of length
`L`, opened at phase `X = (L − D mod L) mod L` so the window's first sample lands at clip
second `D`. A loop opened at a phase **wraps**, at

```
t_seam = L − X = D mod L
```

and for every `D < L` — which is every clip in his batch — that wrap is **inside the clip,
at D**. Putting the drop on the beat and jumping back to the window's start were the same
event. There is no value of `D` that avoids it except `D = 0`.

BL-1226's own docstring describes the defect without seeing it: *"What changes is only
what plays BEFORE `D`: the loop's own tail instead of silence."* The loop's own tail is a
different part of the song.

**Measured on his batch `outputs_paste_20260812_141520`, 18 rendered clips:**

| | |
|---|---|
| clips carrying a mid-clip seam | **15 of 18** |
| every measured jump | **exactly −15.600 s** — the length of `sng_0004 h1` |
| predicted seam second vs measured seam second | **18 of 18 agree** |
| clips with no seam | **3** — and they are exactly the 3 with `D = 0.0` |

Those last two rows are the whole case. The three clean clips are the control: the defect
scales one-for-one with the placement and vanishes when the placement is zero, which is
proof by construction rather than by symptom.

## HOW IT WAS MEASURED, AND WHY THE OBVIOUS INSTRUMENT IS THE WRONG ONE

**A per-second RMS envelope cannot see this defect.** A loop seam inside a continuous
passage of music does not change the level at all — both sides are music at the same
volume — so the envelope is flat across the very thing being reported. BL-1224's envelope
was the right instrument for a *different* bug (music-at-t=0 versus silence-at-t=0) and
reaching for it again would have produced a confident clean bill of health.

Asserted directly in the selftest rather than written down:
`THE ENVELOPE IS BLIND TO IT: level flat across the seam (<3 dB spread)`.

**The evidence is the SONG-POSITION TRACK.** For every 23 ms frame of the rendered clip,
ask the song "which second of you is this?" by cosine similarity over a 40-band log-spaced
spectral vector, L2-normalised so level and fades cannot look like movement. Under one
continuous stretch the residual `song_t − clip_t` is a constant; a seam is a **step** in
it, and the step's size is how far the song jumped.

**Two instruments, no shared code, no shared input:**

| | reads | says |
|---|---|---|
| `scratch/bl1229_envelope.py` | the rendered PCM | where the song jumped, and to where |
| `scratch/bl1229_arith.py` | each render's own `config_*.yaml` | where the loop *must* wrap |

They agree on **18 of 18 clips**. A worked row:

```
  n  file                              clip  hook  windowL  delay  predicted        measured
 12  12_wasted__OURS.mp4              17.00    h1     15.6   0.77  [0.77, 16.37]    [0.67, 16.28]
```

Two seams, one loop period apart, from a filter string that says so and from audio that
says so.

### A null worth keeping: the sample-domain scan cannot find this seam

A waveform-discontinuity detector is the intuitive second opinion, and it **does not
work** here. On a rotated loop the join is the window's last sample against its first, and
those need not differ in value or in slope — on the selftest signal the seam scores z=2.7
while unrelated musical transients score 7.6. On the real clips it fires **76–204 times
per clip** on ordinary music and separates nothing.

He is not describing a click. He is describing the song starting from a different part.
That is a *structural* discontinuity and a sample-domain scan is the wrong instrument for
it. Asserted, so nobody re-promotes it:
`DETECTOR B IS BLIND TO A ROTATED-LOOP SEAM (asserted, not assumed)`.

`scratch/bl1229_envelope.py selftest` — **16/16**, including a hand-built splice whose
answer is known, a rotated loop whose seam second is known by construction, and a
faded-and-quietened control that must produce zero seams. The first draft of the step
detector published five seams on that control; requiring both sides of a candidate step to
be internally *stable* is what fixed it, and the stability test — not the step size — is
the discriminator.

## THE FIX, AND WHAT IT COSTS

**Not a better rotation. No loop inside the clip at all.**

`drop_placer.MODE_CONTINUOUS` (the new default) plays **one continuous span** of the song:

```
bed opens at SONG second   drop − action
span length                clip_len + 1.0 s
placement                  0.0   — nothing rotated, nothing delayed
```

The drop lands on the action because the span opens exactly `action` seconds before it.
The span still **loops** — the audio leg has to stay infinite or `-shortest` bounds the mux
by the shorter stream and truncates the *picture* — but its period outlasts the clip, so
the wrap falls past the end of the file and is never heard. The 1.0 s margin is not a round
number: `edit.py` bounds the bed at `atrim=end=<video> + 0.25`, so anything under 0.25 s
would put the wrap back inside.

### The two properties do not conflict. The seam and the window *boundary* do.

The brief asked for the arithmetic if they conflict. Over his **21 marked windows** against
**every (clip length, action second)** the renderer can produce
(`scratch/bl1229_arith.py cost`):

| scored per WINDOW, in isolation | | scored per SONG, placer free to choose | |
|---|---|---|---|
| seamless AND inside the marked window | 14.5% | | **36.9%** |
| seamless, spilling into unmarked song | 75.7% | | **63.1%** |
| no room at all | 9.8% | | **0.0%** |

**The right-hand column is what a render actually does** — the placer picks among the
song's five or six windows, and the per-window figure understates it badly by counting a
window that cannot as a failure even when a sibling can.

**No window of his four songs, at any clip length, at any action second, fails to place
seamlessly with the drop on the beat.** The cost is that 63% of the time the bed reaches
outside the marked window's edges — still one continuous stretch of the song, nothing
repeating and nothing jumping. Every render records `bed_spill_before_s` and
`bed_spill_after_s` so he can see how far, per clip.

This directly overturns BL-1163's rule that *"the operator's marked window is a floor, so
no unmarked song is ever pulled in."* That rule was right for a bed that loops one window.
It is incompatible with a bed that does not jump, and he has been clear about which he
wants. `tests/test_drop_trim.py` and `tests/test_drop_placer.py` now pin themselves to
`MODE_WINDOW_LOOP` rather than being weakened — BL-1163's arithmetic still has to be exact
when he chooses that mode.

### He gets the choice

`MODE_WINDOW_LOOP` is kept and reachable, reproduces BL-1226's chain **byte for byte**, and
now *names its own seam* in the record (`SEAM AT 0.77s: this mode loops the marked
window…`). `tests/test_bl1229_bed.py::TheTwoModesMustNotConverge` asserts the two modes
still disagree — a regression that quietly made the loop bed behave like the continuous one
would make every other test pass while destroying the evidence.

### One window is physically impossible, and it clamps rather than lying

`sng_0002 h2` marks its drop **0.360 s into the song**. A 20 s clip with the action at 6 s
wants six seconds of run-up and the file does not contain them. Refusing hands the clip to
`edit.py`'s own input-side windowing, which loops — so refusing does not even buy the
seamlessness it costs the clip for. The bed opens at song second 0, **the drop lands early
and the record says by how much** (`drop_lands_at_s`, `drop_shift_s`, and a note beginning
`THE DROP COULD NOT LAND ON THE ACTION`). `place_at_s` reports where it **landed**, not
where it was aimed.

It cannot fire when the placer is free to choose. It exists because he can now pin a window
from the paste page.

### The guard

`hook_chain(..., clip_len_s=...)` **raises** rather than return a chain that wraps inside
the clip, checked on the built string's own numbers rather than trusted from the planner —
the planner is what was wrong last time, and a guard reading the same variable as the

## 2. EDIT AND DELETE HOOK WINDOWS

`dropmark.html` could only ever change the offset. He named `sng_0004` h1, h2, h3 and h5 as
windows he may want gone, and a window he cannot remove is a window the placer keeps
choosing.

Added: **change a window's start, end, note and drop offset**, and **delete a window
entirely**.

**His file is treated as his.** Every write is preceded by a **timestamped backup** that is
never overwritten, replaced **atomically** via temp-file-then-`os.replace`, and appended to
the **append-only journal** `ground_truth/drop_offsets.jsonl` — a delete writes the *whole
removed window* there, not its id, because a journal entry you cannot replay is a log line.

**Nothing is written without a diff he has read.** Every change goes to the server twice:
once with `preview: true`, which validates and returns exactly what would change while
touching nothing, then again to apply. The refusal he reads in the preview is therefore
literally the refusal the write would have given — the browser never decides whether an
edit is legal. A delete additionally requires the hook id echoed back as `confirm`, and the
confirmation panel shows the window's bounds, its offset and the note he typed, plus what
the song would be left with.

Refused, with reasons rather than clamps:

| | |
|---|---|
| `end_s` ≤ `start_s` | `a window must end after it starts (13.769 -> 5.000)` |

| deleting a song's **last** window | refused — it would leave the song rendering from 0.0 s with no hook |
| renaming `hook_id` | not offered at all — it is the key the outcome ledger joins on |

**"Not marked" and "marked at 0.0" stay different facts** throughout: an empty offset box

## 3. THE HOOK PICKER IS BACK, AND THIS TIME IT REACHES THE RENDER

BL-1156 removed it, and removing it was **right**: the backend threw the answer away.
`resolve_song()` chose a window at plan time, before the clip was even fetched, and
`drop_placer.plan()` re-chose at render time over *all* the song's windows. Every batch
record on disk shows the override — `hook: 'h1'` beside `placement_hook_id: 'h4'`.

What makes it safe to re-add is `plan(only_hook_id=...)`, which **pins** the placer to that
window instead of competing with it, and three new fields on every record:
`hook_requested`, `hook_applied`, `hook_choice_honoured`. A silent override now shows up as
`false` on every row instead of as nothing at all.

The control is **optional** — left alone, the placer chooses per clip exactly as before.

The guard that used to assert the picker was gone has been **inverted, not deleted**: the
picker may exist only while the wire exists. Delete `only_hook_id` and
`tests/test_dropmark.py` goes red — the alarm BL-1156 did not have. The behavioural half
includes a control that fails if pinning ever becomes a no-op.

## 4. THE CAP IS PRE-FILLED AND STILL HIS

The estimate now returns `suggested_cap_usd` — **10% over the estimate, rounded UP to the
cent**, floored at $0.01. The page fills the box with it and the estimate stays on screen
beside it.

- **Rounding up is load-bearing.** `plan()` refuses when `estimate > cap`, so a cap rounded
  down can land under the estimate it came from and refuse the batch it was computed for.
- **It never overwrites a number he typed.** The moment he edits the field the auto-fill
  switches off for the session, and it never touches a field that has focus.
- **The pad is not cover for failures.** The estimate is already the worst case — **every
  link is charged on the ATTEMPT, not the success**, because retrieval runs first and fails
  often. The pad covers the price moving between the estimate and the run, which is the
  only thing that can put a correct estimate over its cap.
