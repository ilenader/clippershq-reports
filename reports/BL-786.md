## 3. Kick — a regression in BL-783, and it is mine

**Where the 35 rows came from:** every one was written between **20:37:09 and 20:38:19 on 2026-07-20** — a single 90-second window on the day the funnel was retired. They are the residue of its final fit-test. Master never deletes, so they outlived the funnel.

**Are they stale?** Eleven days old and frozen. The people are real (`jahrein`, `LordKebun`, `rameeirl`, `tjr`, `4head`), but 6 of 35 route to one org (`stormmedia.gg`) and 2 more to `flamenzi.com`.

**Why they are shipping — a defect I introduced.** Kick was deleted on strategic grounds: it runs a native clipping programme, so its creators get clips free and will not pay. **`kick_finder.py` does not exist anywhere in the codebase.** The *only* surviving reference to Kick in the entire repository is the line I added in BL-783:

```python
# writer.py:235
"kick_finder": LEAD_KIND_CLIENT,
```

I mapped `kick_finder` → `client` while building the migration backfill, without checking whether the funnel was still live. That one line reclassified 35 dead rows as sendable client leads and pushed them into the export. **BL-783 resurrected a deliberately-retired funnel.**

**Should they be suppressed? Yes** — and the correct fix is to delete that mapping so the rows fall back to `unroutable`, which is what they are. That is smaller and more honest than adding a suppression rule, because it restores the state the deletion decision intended. Not done here: this round is read-only.

## Limits
