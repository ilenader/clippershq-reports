**Brief:** take the `y_offset` lever conditional on `caption.enabled`; re-measure dead canvas
on magenta at HEAD; hand-label 40 clips into inset/slab/clean and test whether any signal
separates inset from clean; refuse with a number if nothing clears ~80% precision; do not
trim the pillarbox.

**Budget:** $0.20 hard cap, declared before spending. **Spent: $0.0114** (one paid leg —

## Summary

The lever in item 1 does not exist at HEAD, and I did not take it. `y_offset` is **inert**:
`edit.py` reads it only on the `vertical_align: top` branch and this template is `center`.
Simulated across all 2,712 library sources, `center`, `top+280` and `top+40` give the
**identical** median dead canvas of 36.0% with **zero** height-bound sources. The circulating
−23.1 pt figure is real but belongs to a configuration this repo does not run.

Item 2 re-measured dead canvas on magenta: **median 35.4%, n=130 fresh renders**, with 92.3%
of clips sitting exactly on that value. The white-canvas measurement of the same frames says
46.0% — it **overstates the template's contribution by 10.6 pt** because a light source blends
into a white pad. This is why the brief said magenta, and the 10.6 pt is the size of the error
it avoids.

Items 3 and 4: **REFUSED, and the refusal is about the sample, not the signal.** Two signals
separate inset from clean at a point estimate of 11/12 = **91.7%** — but the 95% lower bound is
**64.6%**, because in 40 hand-labelled clips only **3 are clean**.

## 1. The `y_offset` lever — inert, worth 0.0 pt

`memebot/scraper/edit.py:2607-2622` builds the pad two ways:

```python
v_align = str((vid.get("vertical_align") or "top")).lower()
if v_align == "center":
    shift_reserve = abs(int(tx_shift_y or 0))
    avail_h = max(2, int(canvas_h) - 2 * shift_reserve)     # y_offset ABSENT
else:
    shift_reserve = max(0, int(tx_shift_y or 0))
    avail_h = max(2, int(canvas_h) - int(vid_y) - shift_reserve)
```

`y_offset` appears only on the `top` branch. `templates.yaml` sets `vertical_align: center`
(MEMEBOT-082). I did not settle this by reading it — `scratch/bl1046_layout.py` pushes every
library source through the real filter arithmetic
(`scale=W:H:force_original_aspect_ratio=decrease` = `k = min(scale_w/src_w, avail_h/src_h)`):

| layout | avail_h | median dead | mean | p90 | **height-bound sources** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HEAD `center` (y_offset inert) | 1920 | 36.0% | 38.1% | 36.0% | **0** |
| `top` + y_offset 280 | 1640 | 36.0% | 38.1% | 36.0% | **0** |
| `top` + y_offset 40 | 1880 | 36.0% | 38.1% | 36.0% | **0** |

All three are identical because at `scale_width: 864` the **width** constraint always binds
first: `864/1080 = 0.800` is smaller than `1640/1920 = 0.854`. The height budget is never what
limits the picture, so changing the height budget changes nothing.

**The brief's figure is reproducible — at a `scale_width` this template does not use.** At
1080 the constraints swap and y_offset becomes live:

| scale_width | layout | median dead |
|---|---|---|
| 1080 | `top` + y_offset 280 | 27.1% |
| 1080 | `top` + y_offset 40 | **4.1%** (the −23.1 pt) |
| 1080 | `center` | **0.0%** |

So the lever is real, is worth −23.1 pt in its own configuration, and is **already beaten by
4.1 pt** by the centring that is in place. I did not make the change: at HEAD it is worth
0.0 pt, and the only key that moves the number is `scale_width`, which `templates.yaml`
explicitly reserves — *"a template redesign and a taste call, so it is left to you."*

I did land the measurement as a comment on the key so the next round does not re-derive it.

**Item 1 asked for 20 renders and for the frames to be read.** Both done: 20/20 rendered ok
(`scratch/bl1046_render.log`), and the frames are read in §2. There were **no height-bound
sources to render**, so the 20 are a representative sample, not the height-bound class.

## 2. Dead canvas on magenta at HEAD

Composed the 20 renders' **sources** (recovered free from the run's work dirs) onto a magenta
1080×1920 canvas with HEAD geometry — `scale=864:1920:force_original_aspect_ratio=decrease` +
`pad=1080:1920:(ow-iw)/2:(oh-ih)/2`. Magenta appears in no real frame, so every magenta pixel
is canvas the template added.

| | value |
|---|---|
| n | 130 composed frames |
| **median dead canvas** | **35.4%** |
| mean | 37.4% |
| min / p90 / max | 35.4% / 35.4% / 67.7% |
| on the 35.4% plateau | **120 of 130 = 92.3%** |
| above 50% | 10 |

**The white-canvas figure is wrong by 10.6 pt.** The same class of frames measured on the
shipped white canvas gives median 46.0%, because a source carrying its own white bar (a white
caption slab is the *norm* in a meme library) is indistinguishable from the pad. Measuring dead
canvas on white silently counts the source's own bars as the template's fault.

**Decomposing the plateau** — and this corrects my own first reading of the frames, which was
that the picture spanned the full height:

| band | median |
|---|---|
| pillarbox (fully-magenta columns) | 19.4% of width |
| letterbox (fully-magenta rows) | 19.8% of height |
| clips with **any** letterbox band | **130 of 130 = 100%** |

Both bands come from **one** parameter. Scaling is uniform, so `k = 0.8` shrinks width and
height together: coverage `0.8 × 0.8 = 0.64`, dead 36%. There is no separate vertical defect to
fix — which is the deeper reason `y_offset` cannot help. It is a vertical lever against a
problem that is not vertical.

**I did not trim the pillarbox.** Raising `scale_width` to 1080 sets `k = 1.0` and takes
35.4% → 0.0% on 92.3% of the corpus, removing both bands at once. That is the taste call and
it stays with the owner.

## 3. Hand labels — the clean class barely exists

40 renders sampled with a fixed seed from the 120 most recent, read as four contact sheets and
labelled by eye. Scored against **my own labels only**; no detector is used as ground truth
anywhere (the circularity that produced MEMEBOT-098's fake 4.15×).

| label | n | share |
|---|---|---|
| slab (a caption band, picture otherwise fills) | 24 | 60.0% |
| inset (picture sits inside a card or panel) | 13 | 32.5% |
| **clean** | **3** | **7.5%** |

Clean prevalence 7.5%, 95% CI **[2.6%, 19.9%]**. The finding is not that a signal is weak — it
is that **the thing the gate is supposed to protect is nearly absent from the library.** A
caption slab is the norm; a bare picture is the exception.

## 4. REFUSED — with its number

Each signal computed **inside the picture bounding box**, after stripping the template pad.
Without that step all 40 clips carry ~36% pad and every one reads as inset.

| signal | inset med | clean med | best precision | 95% CI | clears 80%? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `interior_flat_frac ≤ 0.7208` | 0.603 | 0.751 | **11/12 = 91.7%** | [64.6%, 98.5%] | **NO** |
| `picture_aspect ≤ 0.989` | 0.805 | 1.000 | **11/12 = 91.7%** | [64.6%, 98.5%] | **NO** |
| `source_bar_frac ≥ 0` | 0.000 | 0.000 | 13/16 = 81.2% | [57.0%, 93.4%] | **NO** |

**The third row is a trap worth naming.** `source_bar_frac ≥ 0` selects *every* clip. Its
"81.2%" is the base rate wearing a threshold's clothes — and it sits just above the 80% bar. A
sweep that reports best-precision without checking how many clips a threshold rejects will
ship it.

**Why nothing can clear 80% on this sample, independent of the signal.** To show precision
≥ 80% at 95% confidence you need ≥ 16 correct calls with zero errors (Wilson lower bound of
16/16 = 80.6%). With 3 negatives the sample cannot produce that many trials. **Even a perfect
signal caps at 13/16 = 81.2%, CI [57.0%, 93.4%].** One mislabelled clean clip moves measured
precision by 11.6 pt.

So: no gate term added. Recorded in `clip_postable.NOT_GATED["letterboxed_inset"]` with the
numbers and with the condition that would change the answer — **re-run with ≥ 60 clean clips
(~800 labelled at this base rate) and `interior_flat_frac` may well clear.** This refusal is
reversible by sampling; the `dead_canvas_unreadable` refusal beside it is not, because there
the classes genuinely overlap.
