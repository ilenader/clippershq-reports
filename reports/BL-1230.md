## The one-paragraph version

The brief asked me to make meme-page harvesting 5x faster and named OCR at 10.9 s/page as the
ceiling. **That number is not a measurement** — it is `0.73 × 15.0`, two constants typed into a
scratch script — **and it prices a code path this project does not ship.** The shipped funnel
never downloads a frame; its cost is audio measurement, and the fix is to stop measuring once
the rule it feeds has been decided. That change is exact (it flips no verdict), it cut posts
measured per page from **12 to 2.68 (4.47x)** on real pages, and in the harvest that followed
it judged **281 pages in 8.4 minutes** and put **99 new rows** in the meme send file, taking it
from 59 to **158**. The 300-email target was **not** reached and was not reachable from this
inventory inside the cap; the reason is measured and stated below.

## 1. The premise, and why it was wrong three times over

`scratch/bl1178_ceiling.py:21`:

```python
OCR_SECONDS = 15.0        # measured: 2 videos downloaded + 3 frames each + RapidOCR
CHEAP_GATE_KILL = 0.27    # measured BL-1177: 77 of 287 pages never needed a video
```

`0.73 × 15.0 = 10.95`. Three independent errors, compounding:

1. **It is not OCR.** Its own comment says the 15.0 covers download **+ decode + OCR**. The
   claim "OCR is the ceiling" was never what the constant said.
2. **It is at 2 posts per page.** A real page downloads, decodes and OCRs **9.46** posts /
   28.4 frames (n=50). `DEFAULT_POSTS_PER_PAGE = 2` is used by nothing that has ever run —
   every real script passes `--posts 10 --frames 3`.
3. **It is per *offered* page, not per judged page**, having been discounted by a gate rate.

The two scripts that would show where `15.0` came from were deleted in `c67a02d`; the
derivation is unrecoverable. The constant propagated through BL-1184, BL-1185 and BL-1213.

**And it prices the wrong path.** `run.py:101` maps the `meme_pages` funnel to
`meme_finder.run_funnel`. `parallel_judge` — the module that does OCR — is imported by
**nothing** in `clippershq/` except its own docstring. `run_funnel` never fetches a frame.

| | `run_funnel` (SHIPPED) | `parallel_judge` (NOT shipped) |
|---|---|---|
| OCR / burned-in text | **none** | yes, ~2.42 s wall/post at 4 workers |
| speech / RULE 8 | yes, `attach_speech` | **none anywhere in the file** |
| cost per page | ~60–75 s | ~23 s at 10 posts |

The brief's own throughput figure is the tell: at ~60 s/page and a 40% pass rate you get ~24
passing pages/hour, which is the "~20/hour" reported. 10.9 s/page predicts ~130/hour.

**The consequence is worse than a wrong number.** Every review sheet the operator has marked
was built by `parallel_judge` (`scratch/bl1196_judge.py:276`). So the ground truth — 623 marks,
the ~70% agreement figure — describes a filter configuration **production does not run**. His
marks judge captions + OCR; the harvest judges captions + speech.

## 2. What Phase 1 measured (7 sections in parallel, read-only, zero vendor spend)

### A — where the time goes
End-to-end `judge_one` on one real page: wall **182.60 s**, instrumented stages **182.55 s**,
residual **0.05 s (0.027%)**. Nothing sums to 10.9 because 10.9 was never a sum. **OCR is not
the largest stage** under either load condition: decode 53.4% on a loaded box, download 43.4%
on a quiet-box model, OCR 37.5% / 34.8%.

A clean free lever on that path: `frame_text.extract_frames` runs 1 ffprobe **+ 3 separate
`-ss` seek-and-decode ffmpeg invocations** per post. One invocation keeping 3 frames at the
same instants: **10.494 s → 2.497 s, 4.20x**, identical output (3/3 frames on 15/15 clips).
Extracting a *single* seeked frame costs 5.33 s — more than three in one pass — which proves
the cost is per-invocation overhead, not decode. **Not shipped this round:** it only helps the
unshipped path.

### B — how many posts (churn at 3 / 5 / 10)
Model C (blank the drawn line after the first N; all posts still judged) against his 623 marks:

| posts | agreement | recall | wanted lost of 222 | 5,700-page hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 69.7% [65.9, 73.1] | 79.3% | 0 | 36.6 |
| 5 | 67.3% [63.5, 70.8] | 72.5% | 19 | 19.2 |
| 3 | 63.2% [59.4, 66.9] | 61.1% | 51 | 11.5 |

The published "15.8% churn at 3 posts" reproduced **for its own day** (15.1% on the pinned
module) and is **now stale**: BL-1216 replaced RULE 3's share test with a flat
`MIN_HOOK_COUNT = 2`, and a count does not scale with read depth the way a share does.

### C — how many frames · **a clean negative**
**3 is already the floor and `MIN_FRAMES = 3` enforces it.** At N=2 the reader returns nothing
on **100% of 82 clips** while reporting the **highest agreement in the sweep** (page mean 0.811
vs 0.748 at three) — maximum apparent confidence, zero evidence, zero output. Final text is
identical to the 24-frame read at every N tested. Head-sampling at the same N inflates
agreement to 0.973 and the two extra "confident" reads it buys are **one invention and one
fail**. Rolling text is 13.5% of the corpus and is where every invention came from.
*Also corrected: "~24 frames per post" is wrong by 8x — it is 3 frames × ~10 posts ≈ 28 per page.*

### D — cheap pre-OCR kills · **nothing new beats base rate**
The filter already has OCR-free rules (`too_few_posts`, `low_views`, `creator_page`); they
reject **27.35% of pages with zero false rejects**. After they fire, only **18.9%** of survivors
are rejects — that is the bar any new signal must clear. The two candidates that clear it are
unaffordable: a caption-only pre-judge throws away **41% of the pages he wants**, and
`mean caption < 300` is RULE 10, already retired for that reason. Anti-signals (they mark GOOD
pages): `category == Digital creator` lift 0.34, view-statistic UNKNOWN 0.48, followers <10k 0.78.

### E — email density by discovery path · **path does not predict, size does**
All ten hashtags' intervals overlap. `#dailymemes`: 48 walked → 2 passed → **0 emails**. The
one apparent path effect is a size effect and dies when the follower band is held fixed. What
does predict is **follower band, monotonically** — confirmed independently in Phase 3 below.

### F — the parallel ceiling
AMD Ryzen 5 5500, **6 physical / 12 logical**. Full CPU leg plateaus at **4 workers**; OCR alone
peaks at 8. Mechanism named: onnxruntime's own intra-op pool already consumes the 6 physical
cores — a *single* Python thread burns **3.82 CPU-seconds per wall-second**, and `cpu/wall`
pins at **6.75**, the physical count, never the 12 logical. The GIL is exonerated twice:
that figure is impossible under a held GIL, and **processes lose 2.63x**. Pinning
`intra_op_num_threads=1` is **1.74x worse**.
**Prior refuted:** "OCR threads cap at 1.77x" — measured **3.66x at 8 workers**. The old harness
ran 6 page-units against 8 and 12 workers; a pool cannot use more workers than tasks, so its
flat top was measuring itself.
**Honest caveat that outranks the tuning:** the 1-worker baseline moved **2.05x between two
reps 20 minutes apart** on identical frames. No tuning decision finer than ~20% is measurable
while other rounds are live.

### G — resolve before filter
Current order confirmed: profile → shape gate → posts → judge → resolve, gated on the verdict.
Resolution is **1 billed call, $0.0006**. Re-measured on 459 cached pages: **pass rate 40.09%
[35.70, 44.63]**, **email rate 27.67% [23.77, 31.93]** — the 17.4% prior was measured on
*passed* pages only. The filter barely predicts an address: P(email|passed) 33.7% vs
P(email|rejected) 23.6%, and with stored verdicts the sign **flips**.

### H — can a rule change make 3 posts safe? · **no, and don't try**
Every depth-aware variant of `MIN_HOOK_COUNT` that helps at N=3 **also moves the N=10 verdict**
(1c: 9 pages, 1e: 19, 1g: 38) and trades recall for precision at full depth. Those are widened
nets, not depth fixes. `min_judged` is worth exactly zero. **Do not touch `MIN_HOOK_COUNT`.**

What *is* free: **the OCR budget was being spent on posts the judge throws away.**
`judge_page:1310` strips pinned posts *before* its slice and Instagram serves pins first.
242 of 684 pages (35.4%) have a pin in the first three slots; 158 (23.1%) have all three pinned.

### I — the speech path · **my hypothesis was wrong, and the sibling refuted it cleanly**
I hypothesised the ffmpeg-invocation overhead A found on the OCR path also afflicted speech.
**It does not.** Per clip the shipped route is **1 HTTP GET, 0 ffprobe, 1 ffmpeg, 0 seeks** —
already what A's fix proposes. Two further corrections:
- **"9.99 s/clip" is not real.** That measurement started its clock *before* building the
  detector, amortising a one-off **1.562 s** `faster_whisper.vad` import into the per-clip
  figure. Re-run in a fresh process: **0.50 s per measured clip**.
- **"~35% xHE-AAC ffmpeg cannot decode" is wrong.** 23.6% [17.1, 31.7] of the corpus it was
  measured on, only 33.3% of those actually fail — and that corpus is the `ORIGINAL.mp4`
  rendition **this path never fetches**. Of 17 live production pulls carrying audio,
  **0 were xHE-AAC**.
- The real split per post: **download 85.8% / ffmpeg 7.8% / Silero 6.3%.** The download is the
  cost, so the lever is measuring **fewer clips**, not decoding faster.

## 3. What shipped (commits `793eed7`, `+ crossdedup`)

**1. Early-exit speech measurement, in judge order — exact, not approximate.**
RULE 8 is decided by one question, `clean` non-empty (`meme_finder.py:1439-1441`), and he
dictated it that way: *"If there is AT LEAST ONE video that isn't talking, that's good."* So
stopping at the first clean post is **verdict-identical** — verified 0 flips of 425 offline,
and 0 regressions in the live harvest. A flat 3-post budget is **not** identical: it gives the
rule fewer chances and recall falls.

**The order is load-bearing and worth 56 pages in 425.** `judge_page` drops pinned posts before
RULE 8 and Instagram serves pins first, so an API-order walk stops on a post the judge discards
and the page reports `unmeasured`. New `judged_order()` is the single shared definition of what
the judge reads, imported by both call sites rather than duplicated.

**2. Pinned-aware OCR budget** (`parallel_judge._budget_posts`). Same downloads, same decodes,
same OCR reads, spent on posts the judge will read. At 3 posts recall **61.1% → 70.4%**, wanted
lost **51 → 25**; at 5, 72.5% → 75.4%. **Zero verdict changes at full depth** — the control
that separates a depth fix from a loosened filter.

**3. His view floor now reaches the filter, and is set to 500.** Both `judge_page` call sites
passed `min_share` and `**_prof` and nothing else, so the keyword defaulted to
`MIN_AVG_VIEWS = 1000` **on every page ever judged** and the configured value was shipped
nowhere. Read via an explicit `is None` chain, never `or` — a floor of 0 means *no floor*, and
`or` would silently reinstate 1000. In the harvest, `low_views` fired **40 times**, so the
newly-live floor is doing real work. (`config.json` is gitignored — it holds credentials — so
that setting is a live local change, not a commit.)

**4. The statistic is named honestly.** It is the **median** `play_count` of **non-pinned** posts
among the latest 10, posts under 48 h excluded. BL-1206/1207 fixed the review sheet; the export
he actually opens still said "Avg views" over the same median. The **value is unchanged** —
BL-1193 made it a median deliberately, because a mean is dragged by one viral hit and these
pages run 790 to 4,329,361 views.

**5. Provenance survives the write.** `email_source` was fixed at the resolver and then dropped
by `_append_to_master`'s dict literal and by `EXPORT_COLS`. `instagram:bio-text` appeared **0
times in 61,195 master rows**. It is now carried end to end — and the harvest immediately
measured the split the bug was hiding (below).

**6. `clip_ocr.reader()` built one engine per thread.** The module had no `threading` import
while being called from a thread pool; **8 threads entering it cold built 8 engines**, 77.2 MB
for the first and ~27.7 MB each after, on a box observed with 0.94 GB free. Double-checked lock
around construction only.

**7. `crossdedup.py:504` used a bare `os.replace` on `master_leads.csv`** — the documented cause
of five `PermissionError` failures with ~22 python processes live, failing at the *end* of the
write when every lead is already paid for. `atomic_io.replace` is the retry wrapper this tree
already uses everywhere else.

**A guard converted, not deleted.** `test_bl1220`'s check grepped `meme_finder.py` as **text**
for the literal `attach_speech(posts, speech_fn)`, so adding a keyword argument broke it while
making the wiring better; its `assertIn("make_speech_fn", src)` passed on a **docstring**. It is
an AST walk now and was **mutation-tested**: dropping `order=` and removing the call both turn
it red.

## 5. Measured and NOT done

- **The profile double-buy.** `resolve_contact` returns the email but drops `user_id`;
  `profile_facts` returns `user_id` but drops the email. Same endpoint, same handle, no cache —
  so every judged page pays **two** profile calls. Worth **$1.37 and 56 min per 5,700 pages**.
  Stage B paid it 281 times this round.
- **Single-invocation frame extraction** (4.20x on decode) — only helps the unshipped path.
- **Split ffmpeg/OCR pools.** ffmpeg still improves at 12 workers (3.50x) and costs the Python
  process nothing; OCR plateaus at 8. Under one shared pool ffmpeg degrades **3.05x** against
  OCR's 1.69x.
- **`recommended_workers()`'s `cores` parameter is dead** — `cores=6` and `cores=64` both return
  4; the docstring describes arithmetic the body never performs.
- **Head-sampled frame fallback should refuse rather than read** — it manufactures 1.00
  agreement and invents text. `FALLBACK_DURATION_S` triggers it whenever ffprobe fails, and
  nothing records how often that is.
