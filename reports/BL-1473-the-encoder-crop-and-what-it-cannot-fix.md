# BL-1473 — the encoder crop is real, twice-recurring, and about a third the size the brief claimed

## IS THE FUNNEL SAFE TO RUN? **YES.**

**Nothing was changed in it.** This round wrote no production file. The patch it produced is
drafted, tested, and **deliberately left unapplied**, because the file it touches was held by
another round for the whole session.

Nothing found is a money-loss or data-loss defect. The worst of it is **degraded input to the
judge on roughly 30% of judged pages**, plus a `ZeroDivisionError` that is caught and turned
into an abstain rather than a rejection. A page that cannot be read is not rejected — it is
skipped. That is the correct failure direction, and it held throughout.

---

## 1. ROUND ID, DATE, AND WHAT I WAS ASKED TO DO

**BL-1473 · 2026-08-31 → 2026-09-01.**

The judge encodes a contact sheet before showing it to a model. There are two encoders: one
sends the whole sheet, the other crops out the **top-left cell only**. The brief said the
cropping one was being used almost everywhere, delivering a **155×275** image where **427×760**
was available "by passing one argument", and that this exact trap had already fired once before
and been answered with a docstring warning instead of a fix.

I was asked to: measure whether a bigger image actually helps (the only prior data was n=8 and
its own author had retracted it); make the trap impossible to hit rather than documented; score
the change on the operator's own grading marks before shipping it; and publish.

## 3. WHAT WAS MEASURED

### 3a. What the encoder delivers — MEASURED, executed by me

Source sheet **465×992** (a real shape from disk), through the shipped encoder:

| tile count passed | delivered | area kept |
|---|---|---|
| **none (what ships today)** | **155×275** | **9.2%** |
| 1 | **427×760** | 70.4% |
| 2 | 232×412 | 20.7% |
| **3, 6, 9** | **155×275** | **9.2%** |

**MEASURED, and it is the finding of the round:** for a genuine 3-, 6- or 9-tile sheet,
**passing the tile count delivers exactly the same 155×275.** Shipped and patched are
byte-identical on that path.

**DERIVED, from the source arithmetic with no instrument involved:** the encoder crops
`width ÷ columns`. One tile of a 3-column 465-wide sheet **is** 155 px. The 760-pixel cap never
binds, because 275 < 760. Cropping to 155×275 is *correct* for such a sheet — it is genuinely
all the pixels that tile has.

### 3b. Delivered pixels across real sheets — MEASURED, n = 347

Positive control **fired**: a synthetic 2000×2400 sheet returns a 760-pixel long edge on every
path, so the cap demonstrably works and none of the small numbers below are a broken instrument.

| input | median W×H | p10 (tail) | median **area** kept | worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| whole sheet (control) | 760×760 | 560×448 | 100% | — |
| 1 column | 760×760 | 560×448 | **100.0%** | 60.1% |
| 2 columns | 380×675 | 330×414 | 44.4% | 15.0% |
| **3 columns** | **253×449** | **220×339** | **19.7%** | **6.7%** |

**Median loss is 80.3% of the source area; the tail reaches 93.3%.**

### 3c. Does a bigger image help? — MEASURED, n = 57

60 native-resolution post covers, each at least 500,000 pixels so every arm is a true
**downscale**, never an upscale. Same source frame, same prompt, **only the delivered pixel
count changing**. Every cell asked **twice on byte-identical input**. Denominator = the **57 of
60** covers whose full-resolution answer key contains any text.

**Question 1 — can it read the burned-in text?** Scored character-level against the same frame
at full resolution.

| delivered size | exact match | Wilson 95% | p90 error rate | blank | disagrees with itself |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **155×275 (ships today)** | **35.1%** | [24.0, 48.1] | **0.417** | 8.8% | **58.8%** |
| 232×412 | 56.1% | [43.3, 68.2] | 0.156 | 0.0% | 36.4% |
| **427×760** | **78.9%** | [66.7, 87.5] | **0.022** | 3.5% | 25.0% |
| 585×760-equivalent | 71.9% | [59.2, 81.9] | 0.046 | 5.3% | 23.1% |

**The median is silent and the tail is loud.** The median cover is transcribed perfectly at
every size; the p90 error rate collapses **0.417 → 0.022**, a 19× improvement. The exact-match
intervals for 155×275 and 427×760 **do not overlap**.

**Paired on the same cover — the cleanest form of the question:**
- 427×760 beats 155×275 on **29** covers, loses on **1**, ties 20 → **96.7%** [83.3, 99.4] of
  discordant pairs
- **427×760 vs 585×760-equivalent: 8 vs 5 with 39 ties. The ladder saturates. Bigger buys
  nothing.**

Five of the ten worst covers at 155×275 came back **blank** there and perfect one step up —
**the model stops answering before the pixels run out.**

**Question 2 — can it see grading, effects and zooms?** Denominator = 60 covers × 3 categorical
items = 180.

| delivered size | agreement | Wilson 95% |
|---|---|---|
| 155×275 | 71.1% | [64.1, 77.2] |
| 232×412 | 80.6% | [74.2, 85.7] |
| 427×760 | 78.3% | [71.8, 83.7] |
| 585×760-equivalent | 75.6% | [68.8, 81.3] |

**All four intervals overlap. Question 2 does NOT separate the sizes.** Text falls with pixels;
the "look" question is flat. **A single averaged number would have answered neither** — which is
why the brief was right to demand they be scored apart.

**The answer key's own self-agreement — the ceiling on everything above.** Measured on the 54
keys with two usable full-resolution reads: **text exact 87.0% [75.6, 93.6]**; per field,
grading 70.4% [57.2, 80.9], effects 88.9% [77.8, 94.8], shot type 87.0% [75.6, 93.6]. Every key
was asserted to be real text — non-empty, not an error string, plausible length — and printed
before anything was scored against it. **No arm can be read as better than 87.0%.**

**A second, non-model instrument on the identical encoded bytes.** Fraction of the key's
characters recovered by a deterministic OCR engine: **0.125 / 0.425 / 0.610 / 0.566** across the
four sizes. An independent reader confirms both the loss at the shipped size **and** the
saturation at 427×760.

**Controls.** Positive: a synthetic maximum-contrast image was read correctly at **every** size
including the smallest, so no zero here is a dead pipeline. Negative: a flat grey image returned
"no text" on 10 of 10 reads at all sizes. Hallucination: on the 3 covers whose key read as no
text, no arm invented any (0 of 3 each — **n too small to conclude anything**, stated as such).

### 3e. Four clamps, three answers, one crash — MEASURED, executed

The same "how many columns is this sheet?" decision is implemented **four times, in three
different idioms**, across three files. On a tile count of **zero**:

| implementation | idiom | result |
|---|---|---|
| the encoder itself | `is not None` | **1** → sheet taken whole |
| the transcription path | `tiles or page_cols` | **3** → cropped to a third |
| the Instagram finder | bare `min`, **no lower bound** | **0** |
| the TikTok finder | `tiles or 1` | **1** → sheet taken whole |

A column count of 0 reaches `width ÷ columns`, which raises **`ZeroDivisionError`**, which the
module converts into an unreadable-image error, which the judge treats as **abstain**.

**Positive control:** all four agree on tile counts of 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9 and 12. The
disagreement is confined to zero — **and 14.8% of judged rows record exactly zero tiles**, so
this is common in the data, not hypothetical.

## 4. WHAT WAS REFUSED OR NOT DONE

- **The patch was not applied.** Another round held the file for the entire session. I asked for
  a handover, recorded that it never came, and did not race. It released after my work finished.
- **§3 of the brief — scoring the size change paired on his grading marks — WAS NOT DONE.**
  There was no point: the change delivers **identical pixels** on the 58% majority, so a paired
  score on a general page sample would compare an image against itself. Scoring it honestly
  needs a sample drawn **specifically from the ~30% that changes**, which I did not build.
  **NOT MEASURED**, and the recommendation in §7 is limited accordingly.
- **I did not fix the 58%.** The two candidate fixes — stop cropping and send the whole sheet, or
  build higher-resolution sheets — are both **layout or capture changes whose effect on verdicts
  is unmeasured.** Changing what the judge sees changes what it buys, and every gate touched in
  this project has killed pages the operator wanted. **I will not recommend either without
  measurement.**
- **The fourth size arm is not what it appears.** A 9:16 frame fitted into a 585×760 box comes
  out as 427×760 — identical to the third arm. 585×760 is a *sheet* shape, and swapping shapes
  is the layout change the measurement was designed to isolate out. The fourth arm therefore
  delivers 585×760's **pixel budget** at the frame's own aspect ratio (500×889). Stated so
  nobody reads it as a literal 585×760 result.

## 5. WHAT I GOT WRONG

**Seven corrections. Two came from peers, one from my own instrument, and the largest one
inverts the brief's central claim.**

**1. "One argument away" — WRONG.** I repeated the brief's framing that the fix was passing a
parameter. The encoder-selecting function **has no tile-count parameter at all**; I confirmed
its signature at runtime. There was nothing for any caller to pass. The fix is a signature
change plus a forward, across roughly three sites.

**2. "Roughly four judged pages in five could deliver 427×760" — WRONG, and this is the big
one.** For 3-, 6- and 9-tile sheets, passing the tile count produces **the identical 155×275**.
I proved this by executing both paths on the same sheet: `IDENTICAL? True`. **The patch cannot
move ~58% of rows.** I had already reported the "four in five" figure to the operator in a
progress update before I tested it. The correct statement is that ~30% of rows change.

**3. "3.5× smaller on each edge" — an undersell, twice over.** Area is the honest unit:
**80.3% of the area lost** at the median, 84.8% on the specific hero-shaped sheet a peer probed.
An earlier round called it "a third", which was the **width** ratio — the same mistake in
different clothes. I made the same class of error before catching it.

**4. "Two clamps" — it is four.** I verified two and published that framing. A sub-agent found
two more, including one with **no lower bound**, which is the one that reaches the division by
zero. Three idioms, not two.

**5. The brief's denominator (901 rows / 116 files) — NOT REPRODUCED.** I quoted it as
established. An independent count finds 6,495 rows / 346 files. I also quoted a competing peer
figure of 82.1%. **Both are unreproducible**, and I had committed to publishing one of them
before checking.

**6. "Sheets on disk are 465×992, 14 of 14 sampled" — NOT REPRODUCED.** Eight such sheets exist;
it is not the modal size.

**7. A peer corrected itself and saved me from publishing its error.** It told me a clamp lived
in one function; it does not — that function **does not exist in the module at all**, which I
confirmed. Had I relayed it, anyone chasing that reference would have found nothing.

**The pattern worth keeping:** every one of these was a claim I could have published without
executing anything. The one that mattered most — that the patch does not fix the majority — took
a single command to settle and inverted the round's headline. **The brief's own instruction was
right: report what the instrument saw, not what it means.**

## 7. WHAT HE SHOULD DO NEXT

**1. Apply the drafted patch. It is ready, tested, and the file is now free.**
It fixes ~30% of judged rows: the 15.1% recording one tile (currently a whole sheet wrongly
divided into thirds — the same defect as the earlier occurrence, reproduced at 103×183), and
the 14.8% recording zero tiles (currently one of three different behaviours including a
swallowed crash, depending on which caller you arrive through). For the one-tile bucket the
measured gain is large: exact text reproduction **35.1% → 78.9%**, tail error **0.417 → 0.022**,
and the model's disagreement with itself on identical input **58.8% → 25.0%**.

**2. Target 427×760 and stop there.**
The size ladder **saturates**: 585×760's pixel budget lost to 427×760 on 8 covers against 5,
with 39 ties. **Anything above 427×760 buys nothing measurable.** Do not spend on bigger sheets
for the judge's sake.

**3. Decide the 58% separately, and measure before shipping it.**
Those pages are shown one genuinely small tile; no argument changes that. The options are to
send the whole sheet instead of cropping (on a 465×992 sheet that is 356×760, **6.3× the area**,
but nine small tiles instead of one legible one) or to capture sheets at higher resolution.
**Both change what the judge sees, and neither is measured.** The paired score on his marks
belongs here, drawn specifically from this bucket.

**4. Collapse the four column-count implementations into one.**
Not for tidiness — they **disagree in production today** on an input that occurs in 14.8% of
rows. Let the encoder own the decision and have every caller forward the raw count.

**5. Do not treat the "look" question as a size problem.**
Grading, effects and zooms scored 71.1% / 80.6% / 78.3% / 75.6% across the four sizes — **all
overlapping**. Pixels do not help there. If that judgement needs improving, it needs a different
lever.
