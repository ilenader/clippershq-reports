# BL-1329 — 100 Instagram pages, and what the probe's ordering was actually worth

**The short version.** The run found **100 pages the judge says you want**, drawn from **new
discovery at a 9% want rate** — the hard pool, not the enriched stock. The SigLIP probe was used
as **ordering, never as a gate**, and it put **all 100 wanted pages in the top 320 of 787**: a
uniform random sample of the remaining 467 returned **0 of 80 WANT**. Judging cost **320 calls
instead of 787** — 2.46×, **3.79 M subscription tokens saved**, and nothing was removed, so
nothing could be lost. Judge-first bought a profile for the 100 winners only: **$0.13 for the
whole run**, against **$1.39** for the same 100 pages the way the funnel ships.

## 1. The pool, named

**NEW DISCOVERY, want rate 9.0% [4.8, 16.2].** Not enriched stock at 28.0%.

Three reasons, and the first would stand alone: `output/bl1246_supply/SCHEMA.md` says in writing
that no row has been judged — it is the only pool whose "never seen" is documented rather than
inferred. Enriched stock is also **exhausted as a source**: every handle in it is already inside
the exclusion universe. And 9% is the harder number; a result that pays for itself there pays
anywhere.

So every cost below should be read against BL-1317's **$13.92 per 1,000 wanted**, not $4.48. The
whole gap between those two is the want-rate ratio and nothing else.

| | |
|---|---:|
| candidate union (public only) | 3,152 |
| **exclusion universe, rebuilt not inherited** | **5,355** |
| fresh after exclusion | 2,867 |
| drawn for capture, `sha256(handle)` order | 1,724 |

**The exclusion universe was rebuilt from eight source classes rather than inherited.** BL-1296's
stored list is three rounds old. The rebuild caught **2 already-judged handles among the 1,724
picks** (`intoxication`, `moviemoments`) — both excluded by name rather than by re-rolling the
pre-registered ordering, which would have made the draw unreproducible. It includes
`quarantine/**`, because BL-1315 measured the trap: quarantining a round makes its 1,885 judged
handles read as *fresh* to any sweep that discovers by listing.

## 2. The free capture has a session ceiling, and that is new

| | pages | with a grid | rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| block A (picks 1–1,200) | 1,200 | **788** | **65.7%** |
| block B (picks 1,201–1,724) | 524 | **0** | **0.0%** |

**Block B returned nothing. 524 of 524 were Instagram's logged-out login wall.** Block A's losses
are the same wall — **387 of 406 (95.3%)**, HTTP 200 redirecting to `/accounts/login/`.

Two things follow, and they matter more than the capture rate itself:

- **The 34.3% lost in block A is not dead accounts.** Those pages are alive and simply
  unreachable for free. Judge-first rests entirely on free capture, so it is only available on
  the two-thirds of new discovery you can see. What fraction of *wanted* pages sits behind the
  wall is unmeasured and unmeasurable without paying for them.
- **Within block A the loss is flat with position** — 62–71% grid rate across every 25-page
  slice, so it is not progressive throttling inside a run. The cut-off arrived between runs. The
  free path has a **session/IP budget**, and 1,200 pages is inside it while 1,724 is not.

Capture ran 6 lanes at **26.1 pages/min**, 46 minutes, $0.00.

## 3. The probe as ordering — before and after

`allow_drop=False`. The threshold is recorded and **not applied**, because at its zero-loss
operating point it killed 0 of 9 held-out wanted pages and **Wilson on 0 of 9 is [0, 29.9%]**;
the reverse split direction has no zero-loss threshold at all. Ordering cannot lose a page,
because nothing is removed.

**The geometry matches on purpose.** This round captured through the same
`bl1257_capture.py --only-full` path BL-1296 used, so the grids are **934×934 → exactly 9 cells**
— the geometry the operating threshold was derived on. A probe can separate the two prior
capture sets at AUC 0.8455 from these vectors, so that was worth arranging. It is still not a
licence to drop.

**Where the wanted pages landed:**

| judged down to rank | wanted found | share of all wanted |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 | 28 | 28% |
| 100 | 53 | 53% |
| 160 | 75 | 75% |
| 240 | 91 | 91% |
| **320** | **100** | **100%** |
| 400 | 100 | 100% |

**And the tail is empty.** A uniform random sample of the 467 unjudged pages (seed 1329, 80
pages, judged blind on the same brief) returned **0 WANT — 0.0% [0.0, 4.6]**. That sample is
what makes the "before" a measurement instead of an assumption: without it the counterfactual
would be a guess, and it would flatter the probe, because the tail is exactly where the probe
says the wanted pages are not.

| to find 100 wanted pages | judge calls | judge sees | tokens (measured 8,114/page) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **AFTER** — probe ordering | **320** | **40.7%** | **2.60 M** |
| BEFORE — capture order | 787 | 100.0% | 6.39 M |
| **saved** | **467** | **59.3 pp** | **3.79 M** |

**2.46×.** At BL-1317's v8 figure of 12,393 tokens/page the saving is 5.79 M.

The knob that would have dropped pages: **174 of 787 (22.1%) scored above the recommended
threshold** — that is what a gate would have kept, and it is close to BL-1324's 19% judge share.
It was not used.

## 4. Judge-first: the profile bought last

The shipped funnel buys a profile for **every walked page** (`meme_finder.py:3120`, before any
rule runs). The switch that inverts it — `meme_finder.page_verdicts_path` — is implemented,
tested and **absent from `config.json`**, so every run today takes the ordinary order. This run
used the same verdict file that key consumes.

| | profile calls | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| shipped order (a profile per judged page) | 320 | $0.2039 |
| **judge-first (only what the judge wanted)** | **100** | **$0.0637** |
| **saved** | **220** | **$0.1402** |

Total vendor spend for the run — 100 profiles plus 100 `posts_for` calls for the `last post`
column — is **$0.1274**, with HikerAPI's **×1.062 billing multiplier applied**. `run_funnel`
does not apply that multiplier, so its own dollar print under-reports by 6.2%.

**Judge-first saves money. It does not save time or tokens** — the same pages are still captured
and still judged, and that is where the wall clock and the subscription tokens go. Anyone
selling it as a speed-up has the sign wrong.

**And the per-1,000 projection has two denominators.** BL-1300 measured 45 → 18 billed requests
live; BL-1309 corrected its projection to **$4.38 → $1.30 at a 28% want rate**. That is enriched
stock. This run is new discovery, where the same model prices a wanted page at **$13.33–$13.92
per 1,000**. Quoting $4.38 for a new-discovery run overstates the yield threefold.

**No pre-judge gate was added.** Every one ever tested here kills wanted pages.

## 5. The email half

**27 of the 100 wanted pages publish an address — 27.0% [19.4, 36.3].** That is higher than the
17.0% [10.9, 25.5] baseline, and the intervals overlap, so read it as *consistent with and on
the high side of* the prior figure rather than as an improvement. The populations differ: 17.0%
was 100 curated repost accounts that were neither judged nor wanted; this is 100 judge-WANT

**The sources are kept distinct, and they are distinct in the data, not just in the label:**

| source | n | what it is |
|---|---:|---|
| `instagram:business-field` | **14** | the contact **BUTTON** — a structured profile field |
| `instagram:bio-text` | **12** | an address **typed into the bio caption**, found by regex |
| `instagram:bio-link` | **1** | a link in bio, **followed for free**, address found on the page |

The button leads, at 52% of the yield — consistent with the 51% that was measured when the two

working, which is the whole reason to keep them apart. The link-follow is **$0.00** and
contributed one address; **230 of 300 addressless pages publish no link at all**, which is a
ceiling, not a gap.

## 8. Three defects in this round's own harness

### 8.1 The judge could read the pre-judge score — and the leak is in the SHIPPED module

The prompt told each agent its batch entry carried "a handle and an image path and nothing
else". **`clippershq/judge_batches.py` writes `probe_score`, `probe_bucket` and `"ordering":
"descending probe score"` into `batches.json` — which is also the file every judge must open to
find its pages.** All twenty agents in wave 1 reported it unprompted, quoting their own scores.

The fix landed in the module, not in this round: **`judge_batches.write_blind_views()`** writes a
score-free per-agent file, `<outdir>/blind/<agent>/batch.json`. The audit manifest keeps
everything — a manifest that does not record what it did cannot be checked. It is a separate
function so an *existing* manifest can be re-viewed blind without rebuilding it, which is
exactly what this round needed with 20 verdicts already on disk. Six tests in
`tests/test_siglip_probe.py`.

**Wave 1 was kept as a seed-visible control and the leak was sized:**

| | |
|---|---:|
| pages judged both ways | 160 |
| **agreement** | **140 — 87.5% [81.5, 91.8]** |
| WANT, seed-visible | 85 |
| **WANT, blind** | **75** |

**The visible score pushed the judge toward WANT by 13.3% relative.** Every number in §3–§7 is
from the blind pass. All 51 blind agents confirmed their file carried no score.

### 8.2 Two already-judged pages slipped into the draw

The first exclusion sweep missed `brief_leakcheck.discover()`, the judge output directories and
`quarantine/**`. Adding them caught **2 of 1,724**. Both are named in the pool file and excluded
from judging. Capture is free, so the cost was two pictures nobody looked at.

### 8.3 A redaction claim that was false, for the third round running

`scratch/bl1329_cross.py` dropped every address-**bearing field** from each record before
reading it and claimed on that basis to be *"structurally unable to emit an address"*. **It
emitted sixteen.** The `evidence` snippet is 120 characters of **bio text**, and a bio is where
people type their address; dropping the field called `email` does nothing about an address
written in prose two words from the word "tiktok".

This is the same shape three rounds in a row — BL-1326 stored `author_bio` "for context",
BL-1304's own redaction fingerprint *was* the whole address when the local part was short. **The
generalisation is worth more than the fix: a claim about which FIELDS are read is not a
redaction guarantee. Only a scrub of the text actually being written is.**

Fixed at the write point, and the file now **checks its own guarantee**: the serialised document
is scanned before it reaches disk and a surviving address raises rather than shipping. A full
regex sweep of every file this round wrote returns clean.

### 8.4 Smaller, and stated

- **Embedding is slower per page than judging.** 794 pages at **1.96 s/page** — 7.43× faster than
  BL-1324's 14.54 after dropping the unused `whole` representation, batching cells across images
  and running 4 worker processes. It is still **26 minutes** of CPU against **~7 minutes** of
  wall clock to judge the same pages at 19 agents wide. **The probe buys tokens, not time.**
- **The bill is modelled, not receipted.** Unlike BL-1326 this round did not read the client's
  own `http_requests` counter at the end of the buy, so it cannot quote a retry-inclusive count.
  A 5xx storm would make the real figure higher than $0.1274.
- **`config.json:meme_hashtags` — 12 tags — is read by nothing.** `run_funnel` reads
  `config["meme_finder"]["hashtags"]`, which is absent, so the funnel runs on three hardcoded
  constants. Not fixed here: `config.json` is not in this round's declaration.

## 11. What I would do next

**Fix the login wall or accept the ceiling.** One in three new-discovery pages cannot be
captured for free, and after ~1,200 pages in a session none of them can. Judge-first is worth
10× on the pages you can see; the question nobody has answered is what fraction of *wanted*
pages sits behind that wall. Measuring it costs one paid fetch each on a sample of the 406.

**Ship `extract_tiktok`.** 114 owner-declared TikTok handles are sitting in profiles already
paid for, at $0.00, in the direction BL-1321 identified and did not search — and the file
holding most of them is staged for deletion.

**Refused:** any pre-judge gate; re-tuning the probe threshold on this capture; writing
`config.json` while three rounds are live; and re-stamping a `FACTS.md` figure that is moving
under a live run.
