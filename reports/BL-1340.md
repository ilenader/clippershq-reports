# BL-1340 — Instagram under $2.00 per 1,000 wanted pages

**Instagram is now $1.27 per 1,000 wanted pages, and the switch that does it is on.**
It is not a discount and not a new endpoint. It is the order of two steps: judge the picture
first, buy the profile only for pages the judge kept. One config key, already written and
tested since BL-1300, absent from `config.json` for forty rounds.

The figure is **flat across the want rate** — $1.27 at 9% new discovery and $1.27 at 28%
enriched stock — because when you only pay for pages you keep, the want rate leaves the
arithmetic entirely. That is the whole reason the target is reachable.

**And the combination everybody assumed was the cheap one is the expensive one.** Probe-ordered
plus judge-first, in the mode that ships, costs **$7.23 per 1,000 wanted** — worse than
judge-first alone, worse than the target by 3.6×. Ordering works by *not judging* the tail, and
the shipped mode buys every unjudged page. This round found that, fixed it, and proved the fix
by counting purchases.

Every number below is from `.venv\Scripts\python.exe` (3.11.9) unless stated.
Round spend: **$0.0132** of a $2.00 cap, 22 billed requests, both rows on the ledger.

## 1. The two levers, costed honestly

There are exactly two ways to move dollars per wanted page, and the identity says so:

```
cost per wanted page  =  cost per page you PAY FOR  ×  pages you pay for per wanted page
```

**Lever (a) — buy fewer pages per wanted page.** Under the ordinary order you pay for every
page you walk, so the second factor is `1 / want rate`. That is why BL-1317's "$4.38 versus
$13.33 conflict" resolved to a rate ratio identical to ten decimal places: it was never two
measurements disagreeing, it was one measurement at two want rates.

Under judge-first the second factor becomes **exactly 1**. Not smaller — one. You buy one page
per wanted page no matter how bad the supply is.

**Lever (b) — pay less per page.** The bundle is `profile_and_contact` + `posts_for` at
$0.000600 each × HikerAPI's 1.062 billing multiplier = **$0.001274 per bought page**. There is
no cheaper profile endpoint; dropping `posts_for` halves it to $0.000637 and costs the last-post
date. That is the entire negotiating room on this lever: **2×, once, and then it is spent.**

Lever (a) is worth 11× at 9% and 3.4× at 28%. Lever (b) is worth 2×. **(a) reaches the target;
(b) does not, at any want rate, on its own.**

### The three configurations, priced

`scratch\bl1340_levers.py` → `scratch\bl1340_levers.json`.
Inputs: $0.000600/request; ×1.062 (BL-1179, 401 logical → 426 billed); 1.923 billed calls per
walked page under the ordinary order (BL-1309: 0.043 discovery amortised + 1.000 profile +
0.880 posts); 1.000 billed request for a paid grid page (BL-1334).

| pool | want rate | **A** ordinary, per 1,000 **fetched** | **A** per 1,000 **wanted** | **B** judge-first, free grid, per 1,000 **wanted** | **C** judge-first, paid grid, per 1,000 **wanted** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Instagram — new discovery | **9.0%** [4.8, 16.2] | $1.23 | $13.61 | **$1.27** | $8.35 |
| Instagram — enriched stock | **28.0%** [20.1, 37.5] | $1.23 | $4.38 | **$1.27** | $3.55 |

A = pay for every walked page. B = pay only for winners, picture free. C = pay a little for
every picture, plus winners.

**B clears $2.00 at both rates. C clears it at neither.** And the reason C fails is worth
stating as a number rather than a verdict, because it is the round's refusal:

> To reach $2.00 with a **paid** grid at a 9% want rate you have $0.726 left after paying for
> the winners, spread over 11,111 fetched pages. That allows **$0.000065 per page**. The grid
> costs **$0.000637**. It is **9.8× too expensive**, and the want rate would have to exceed
> **87.8%** for the sums to close. There is no negotiation available at that gap.

So the target depends on one external fact this round did not measure: whether the free capture
is available at the volume a run needs. See §3.

## 2. Judge-first is ON

`config.json`, `meme_finder` block, now carries:

```json
"page_verdicts_path": "output/bl1340_run/page_verdicts.jsonl",
"page_verdicts_mode": "reject_only"
```

**The verdict file is the union of every verdict any judge has ever written** —
`scratch\bl1340_judgefirst.py` reads seven source globs and produces **700 verdicts: 167 WANT,
532 REJECT, 1 UNSURE**. Not one round's file: a REJECT is a durable fact about a page, and
discovery is a crawl over a graph that keeps returning the same accounts. 36 handles were judged
twice and disagreed; **WANT wins**, recorded rather than silently overwritten — a disagreement
between judges is not licence to stop buying a page.

On the pages already on record the switch is worth **532 profiles never bought again = 1,000.2
billed calls = $0.6373**, one-off. The recurring value is that every future run skips them too.

### What it costs per 1,000 wanted, at both rates

| pool | want rate | per 1,000 **fetched** | per 1,000 **wanted** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Instagram — new discovery | 9.0% | $1.23 → **$0.00 for walked pages** | **$1.27** |
| Instagram — enriched stock | 28.0% | $1.23 → **$0.00 for walked pages** | **$1.27** |

**It has been mis-sold twice and this is the third telling, so plainly: it saves MONEY.** Not
time, not tokens. The same pages are still captured and still judged — identical wall clock,
identical subscription tokens. The only thing that changes is that a rejected page stops buying
a profile.

⚠️ **The real, permanent cost.** A page skipped this way gets **no rule verdict at all** — no
`judge_page`, no RULE 8 speech measurement, no drawn-line read, no follower or category or bio.
That is not recoverable afterwards without buying the page. The trade is deliberate and it is
the reason `reject_only` is the default: **UNSURE and ABSENT both still buy**, because absence
of evidence must never read as a kill.

## 4. Probe-ordered × judge-first — the combination nobody had costed

This is the round's finding, and it inverts the expectation.

`scratch\bl1340_combo.py` prices four cells from BL-1329's own counts: **787 pages captured and
judgeable, 320 judged by the blind arm, 467 left unjudged by the ordering, 100 judge-WANT.**
Every cell delivers the same 100 wanted pages — ordering does not change how many wanted pages
exist, only how many pages must be judged to find them, and judging is free.

| ordering | judge-first mode | profiles bought | per 1,000 wanted | $2.00? |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| capture order (sha256) | OFF | 787 | $10.03 | MISSES |
| capture order (sha256) | `reject_only` | 100 | **$1.27** | CLEARS |
| **probe-ordered** | **`reject_only`** *(shipped)* | **567** | **$7.23** | **MISSES** |
| **probe-ordered** | **`want_only`** *(new)* | **100** | **$1.27** | **CLEARS** |

**Read the third row twice.** Probe-ordering plus judge-first, in the mode that shipped, is
**worse than judge-first alone** — $7.23 against $1.27. The two levers meet on the unjudged
tail: ordering works precisely by not judging it, and `reject_only` buys a profile for every
page with no verdict. Adding the ordering would have *cost* $5.96 per 1,000 wanted, which is the
exact opposite of the reason to order.

(On the profile leg alone, excluding `posts_for` from both sides as BL-1329 did, the same cell
is $3.61 against $0.64. The ratio is identical; only the bundle differs.)

### The fix, and how it is proved

`clippershq/meme_finder.py` gained **`page_verdicts_mode`**, validated on read:

```python
_mode = str(cfg.get("page_verdicts_mode") or "reject_only").strip().lower()
if _mode not in ("reject_only", "want_only"):
    raise ValueError(...)          # a typo is a money decision; it refuses rather than guess
_v = page_verdicts.get(h)
_skip = (_v == "REJECT") or (_mode == "want_only" and _v != "WANT")
```

and `stats["judge_first_unjudged"]`, counted apart from `judge_first_skipped`, because *"we
looked and said no"* and *"nobody looked"* are different facts and a round that collapses them
cannot say how much supply it declined to inspect. The checkpoint records the difference too:
`judge_first: "UNJUDGED"` with a reason that says the page **was not judged and is not a
rejection**.

`tests/test_bl1340_judge_first.py` — **6 tests, green** — proves this by **running the funnel
and counting purchases**, with every paid collaborator replaced by a recorder. Four candidate
pages spanning WANT / REJECT / UNSURE / absent:

- `reject_only` buys **3** (`pg_want`, `pg_unsure`, `pg_absent`), skips 1, unjudged 0
- `want_only` buys **1** (`pg_want`), skips 3, unjudged 2
- a skipped page buys neither its profile **nor its posts**
- a misspelled mode (`"reject-only"`) raises **before a single call is made** — asserted on the
  call counter, not on the exception alone

That is deliberately not an AST test. `tests/test_bl1300_judge_first.py` (22 tests, green) asks
the structural questions — is the skip above the only unconditional purchase, is the reader safe
in five file shapes — and those are right for a change whose risk is *where it sits*. **An AST
can confirm a line exists and cannot count what it buys**, and the number of purchases is the
whole claim here.

`page_verdicts_mode` is documented in `clippershq/config_defaults.py`, which
`test_config_contract.py` RULE B enforces. **`want_only` is a per-run opt-in and never the
default**: under it an absent verdict silently means *do not buy*, which is safe only when the
verdict file is authoritative for every page the run walks.

## 5. Supply — the format lever does **not** transfer, and the free handles are real

### Format-targeted searches: measured, and no lift

TikTok's 8.4× came from changing *what is searched for* — seeds naming the craft and the output
("amvedit", "fan edit") returned 84.9% editing pages against the shipped seeds' 9.7%. The
Instagram equivalent is `/v2/fbsearch/accounts`, one billed request per query.
`scratch\bl1340_searches.py`, 11 billed requests, $0.0066:

| arm | queries | distinct handles | fresh (never judged, never shown) | **fresh per billed call** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **FORMAT** — "movie memes", "cartoon memes", "tv show memes", "gaming memes", "pov memes", "relatable clips", "film memes", "sitcom memes" | 8 | 154 | 151 | **18.9** |
| **CONTROL** — the three hashtags the funnel ships, searched as words | 3 | 60 | 57 | **19.0** |

**Lift: 0.99×.** No supply advantage whatsoever.

⚠️ **And read that carefully, because the design can only produce this answer.** Every query
returned 18–20 handles. That is the endpoint's **page size**, not a property of the query — so
handles-per-call cannot separate a good query from a bad one, and 1.0× is the null the
instrument is built to return. What it *does* establish is that changing the words buys no extra
**volume** per call, which is the half of TikTok's 8.4× that was about supply. **The quality
half is untested**, and testing it needs a want rate, which needs a judge, which needs a grid —
see §3. Cost to settle it: ~200 grids plus free judging, $0.13 and about 25 agents, once the
wall is characterised.

Also worth knowing: the endpoint is **not deterministic**. Two runs eleven minutes apart
returned overlapping but different sets, so repeat calls buy some genuinely new supply.
**207 fresh handles are on disk** at `output\bl1340_run\search_handles.tsv`, at $0.000032 each.
For comparison the shipped hashtag walk amortises to 0.043 calls per page — about 23 pages per
call. **Search is not better than what ships. It is the same, in different words.**

### The free handles — 495 of them, and BL-1312's attribution was wrong

`scratch\bl1340_handles.py` walks all 235 stored payloads (2,146 media objects) across
`_probe_samples\`, `scratch\bl712\` and `scratch\bl1334_payloads\`. Note `_probe_samples\` is
**gitignored**, so a ripgrep sweep silently misses the largest family — the trap that hid this
corpus from two earlier rounds.

| path | distinct | free of the author set | **new** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `caption.user` | 682 | **0** | **0** |
| `clips_metadata.*.ig_artist` | 495 | 171 | 169 |
| `usertags.in[].user` | 233 | 226 | 209 |
| `coauthor_producers[]` | 184 | 175 | 173 |
| **union** | **1,233** | **513** | **495** |

**495 new handles, free, at $0.00 — 2.11 per stored payload.** On disk at
`output\bl1340_run\free_handles.tsv`.

Three corrections come out of measuring rather than inheriting:

1. **BL-1312's "185 distinct handles at `usertags.in[].user` and `clips_metadata.*.ig_artist`"
   is a real count with the wrong label.** BL-1284's own artefact reaches 185 as *386
   user-shaped objects across **nineteen** paths minus 201 post authors*; those two named paths
   supply 260 of 896 objects — **29%**. Replaying the same 8-payload bundle through the two
   named paths alone gives **115, not 185**. The figure is sound; the attribution is 1.6× too
   generous.
2. **`caption.user` yields ZERO free handles** — it is *always* the post author, which
   `ig_discovery._lead_from` already reads. It looks like the biggest surface (682 distinct,
   84.7% fill) and contributes nothing. Subtracting the author set is what makes the difference
   between 1,233 and 513, and any figure that skips that subtraction is double-counting.
3. **`coauthor_producers[]` is the genuinely unread surface** — 173 new handles, and
   `ig_discovery.py:369` reads it as a **boolean** and throws the handles away. Nothing in
   `clippershq/` mentions `ig_artist` at all.

⚠️ **A field with two shapes will vanish silently.** My first extractor read
`coauthor_producers[].user` — the shape `sponsor_tags` uses — and reported **zero coauthors**,
which reads as *"the field is empty"* rather than *"I looked in the wrong place"*. It cost 173
handles until the count was compared against a figure someone else had measured.

**No contact-bearing field exists in this corpus at all** — no `public_email`,
`public_phone_number`, `external_url` or `biography` key anywhere in these media payloads. Those
live behind the profile call. So the "free contact data" idea does not survive contact with the
payloads, and there is nothing address-shaped in the handle files (checked by regex before each
write, not asserted).

**What this is not: a want rate.** These are handles, not judged pages.

### And seeds were NOT ranked

Deliberately. Ranking seeds by past yield is refuted three times over here — most recently 40
fresh pages from the "three best seeds" returned 17.5% against a 19% baseline, one of them
serving techno labels and astrology cards. Changing *what is searched for* is a different lever
from *which seed is searched*, and only the first one ever moved a platform.

## 7. What would break the $1.27

Stated as conditions, not caveats, because each one is a measurement somebody can take:

1. **The login wall.** $1.27 assumes the picture is free. 52.8% of BL-1329's draw was walled. If
   the free grid cannot be had at volume, the number is $8.35 at 9%. **BL-1339 owns this.**
2. **These are judge-WANTs, not his wants.** BL-1329 measured judge-WANT precision against his
   own marks at **69.2% [42.4, 87.3]**, recall 100%. 100 of *his* wanted pages needs ~145
   judge-WANTs, so the honest figure is about **$1.85 per 1,000 of his wanted**. It still
   clears $2.00 — and that interval is wide enough that it need not.
3. **The judge is free only because it is a subscription sub-agent.** 26 M subscription tokens
   per 1,000 wanted. `meme_finder.py` already prices a *paid* vision judge on the equivalent
   walk at **$215.43 against a $5.67 vendor saving** — 38×. **Judge-first's economics invert
   the moment the judge is paid.**
4. **`want_only` is only safe when the verdict file covers every page the run walks.** Point it
   at a partial file and it declines to buy pages nobody ruled on — silently, because that is
   what the mode means. This is why it is not the default and why the mode raises on a typo.
