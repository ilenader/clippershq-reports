## 1. The option he pressed and the module written for it were never connected

`run.FUNNELS["tiktok"]` resolved to `control._find_editors_tiktok` → `main._execute_run`:
the **legacy campaign/editor path**. Different funnel, different unit of work, and it could
not start headless at all without `config["headless_campaign"]`, a key this config does not
have.

Meanwhile `clippershq/tiktok_finder.py` — thirteen tested helpers, written for exactly this
option — was imported by **no shipping code**. Every importer was a test or a throwaway under
`scratch/`. Its own docstring referred to a `discover()` in the present tense; there was no
`discover()`. The 115-row file on disk was built entirely by `scratch/bl1191_run.py`, and no
production path could regenerate it.

**Fixed.** `tiktok` now resolves to `tiktok_finder.run_funnel`, shaped exactly like
`meme_finder.run_funnel` — the only other funnel hosted in its own module — so the dispatch
needs no special case. `discover()` exists and is the chain.

The legacy path was **kept, not deleted**, under the name of what it actually does
(`tiktok_editors`); it is the campaign-driven editor crawl `OPERATING.md` documents at `[6]`.
The `headless_campaign` blocker travelled with it, because a blocker belongs to the refusal
site and not to a label. `tiktok` now blocks on nothing and asks for hashtags and search
terms — six fields, every one of them a key `run_funnel` actually reads.

## 2. The estimate, before anything is spent

`POST /api/start/estimate` (read-only — it never writes `config.json`; estimating is not
starting) and an "Estimate — costs nothing" button beside "Start run". A worked example, three
tags at depth 10 plus one search term, target 30 emails:

| stage | requests | $ |
|---|---:|---:|
| discovery (3 hashtags × depth 10, 1 search) | 34 | 0.0204 |
| one search per author, for its OWN videos | 295 | 0.1769 |
| one profile per reachable author | 269 | 0.1615 |
| **TOTAL** | **598** | **0.3589** |

404 videos → 295 authors → 269 judgeable → **29 emails, 95% CI [18, 48]**, wall clock
**85m 38s**. Suggested cap **$0.40** at a 10.0% pad, and it stays editable — the pad and the
floor are `paste_batch`'s own constants, reused rather than re-chosen, because two caps padding
by two different amounts is worse than either alone.

It also refuses to round a shortfall away: *"the tags entered support about 29 email(s); 30
were asked for. Add tags or raise depth — the run cannot invent supply."*

**Every figure carries its own denominator**, which is the BL-1254 rule applied at the source:
`RATES` is a table of dicts, each with `n`, `source`, and — where it is a proportion — its
interval. The email rate is stated as 13 of 119 [6.5, 17.8], never as a bare 10.9%. A figure
this project has not measured is `None` and the page says **"unmeasured"** rather than
inventing a default that would look identical to a measurement.

## 3. The endpoint nobody had typed a username into

BL-1189 established, twice, that LamaTok has **no user-videos endpoint** — 23 paths,
`/v1/user/videos` 404s, playlists 200 without the videos. That finding is about one family of
paths and it stands; `client.user_videos` still refuses, unbilled.

`/v2/search` is not in that family. It takes free text, and TikTok's own search surfaces an
account's posts when the keyword **is its username**. Nobody here had ever typed one.

Measured across all 115 pages, one billed call each:

| | |
|---|---|
| reachable by handle search | **105 of 115 = 91.3% [84.7, 95.2]** |
| videos returned per call | **12.78** (n=115, counting the 10 unreachable as zero) |
| pages returning ≥3 of their own videos | **100 of 115 = 87.0%** |

**This is the supply fix.** Discovery yields 1.15–1.37 videos per author, so his rules were
being asked of ONE video and the evidence floor was unreachable at any price. One call here
returns 12.78 — **9.3×** — which is what makes a 3-video floor affordable instead of
theoretical: judgeable pages go from 3.2% to 87.0%.

A page search cannot reach is **UNKNOWN, never a reject**, and it gets its own tab in the
sheet so it cannot be read as one.

## 4. A default is not a floor

`MIN_VIDEOS_OBSERVED` was already 3. The live run that built every TikTok row on disk ran at
**1** — passed in from an env var, not by editing anything — and the filter then passed **117
of 119**, which is not a filter. Nothing in the output said which level it had used.

The defect was the silence, not the number. So: `MIN_VIDEOS_FLOOR = 3`, `judge_author` raises
`ThinEvidence` below it unless the caller passes `allow_thin_evidence=True`, and **every
verdict now carries `min_videos_used`** next to the answer, because "98% passed" means
opposite things at 1 and at 3.

Proven to fail against the pre-fix literal: `judge_author(author_with_1_video, {},
min_videos=1)` raised where it previously returned a pass.

## 5. OCR has now run on a TikTok frame

`clippershq/frame_text.py` was imported by no code on the TikTok path, so "text on screen" —
his core format rule — was decoration on every TikTok verdict ever recorded. Same shape as the
three shipped drawn-text rules that fired zero times on Instagram.

`on_screen_text_for()` is wired into `discover()` and runs **last**, after the free cuts. The
mp4 url rides on a payload already bought, so the download is plain HTTP and costs no vendor
call. Measured on five pages, two videos each: **10 of 10 read, zero refusals**, ~24s a page.

The readings match what the judge read by eye off the contact sheets — independent
corroboration of both:

- `thesmart1n` → *"When people rub your pregnant girlfriend's stomach and say
  congratulations, but nobody tickles your balls and says well done"*
- `andrewzviolin` → *"seeing if i have talent so i don't have to work"*

A download failure and "read it, there was no text" are counted **separately**; collapsing them
is how a silent zero becomes a measurement.

## 6. The 115 he already has: 13.3% are real meme pages

The browser judge is not a module — it is an agent procedure reading a profile GRID against
`scratch/bl1242_brief_v4.md`, at 86.7% [73.8, 93.7] agreement on Instagram. Two things blocked
the straight port and both are worth recording:

- **Playwright cannot reach a TikTok profile.** Headless Chromium renders "Something went
  wrong" and no grid; headed Chromium with a real UA gets a **slider CAPTCHA**. Solving it is
  out of scope and off the table.
- **The Chrome extension was not connected** — `list_connected_browsers` returned `[]`, same
  as BL-1257.

So the grid was rebuilt from the vendor: one billed search per page → that page's own covers
(`video.cover`, a 500×800 JPEG on a signed CDN url, fetched over **plain HTTP for free**) →
a 12-tile contact sheet. Same evidence in the same shape, so the rubric transferred unedited.
Sizing is matched **per tile** at 155px — BL-1257's ladder varied pixels-per-tile, and holding
the sheet total instead would have shrunk each tile below the measured floor.

**105 judged. 14 WANT, 87 REJECT, 4 UNSURE.**

> **14 of 105 = 13.3%, 95% CI [8.1, 21.1]**

Instagram's code-built send list reads **19.7% [11.6, 31.3]** on the same rubric. **The
intervals overlap**, so these two lists are not distinguishable in quality — both are roughly
one page in seven. The predicted failure mode reproduced exactly, and it has a single dominant
cause:

| disqualifier | n | share of the 87 rejects |
|---|---:|---:|
| `creator` | 64 | 73.6% |
| `dialogue` | 14 | 16.1% |
| `topical_aggregator` | 3 | 3.4% |
| `no_video` | 2 | 2.3% |
| `not_english` | 2 | 2.3% |
| `edit_page` / `lyrics_ambient` | 1 each | 2.3% |

**Sixty-four of eighty-seven rejects are personal creators** — a violinist filming himself, a
film reviewer on a lavalier mic, a page whose own tile reads *"Imma use this account for
talking videos from now on"*. The filter optimised for "has an email", and a personal creator
is exactly the kind of account that publishes one. It is not a tuning problem; the funnel was
never asked what the page posts.

## 7. The email picture, honestly

One billed profile call per page, 115 pages. **Every rate below is measured on a population
selected for already having an email — they are not base rates for discovery**, and reading
them as such would be the BL-1254 error in a new place:

| route | | |
|---|---|---|
| address in the signature | 113/115 | 98.3% [93.9, 99.5] |
| a structured `bioLink` exists | 68/115 | 59.1% [50.0, 67.7] |
| **the link field IS an address** | **0/115** | **0.0% [0.0, 3.2]** |
| an Instagram handle is advertised | 32/115 | 27.8% [20.5, 36.6] |
| the bio sells paid placement | 45/115 | 39.1% [30.7, 48.3] |

The Instagram figure needs its caveat stated, not buried: BL-1239 measured **5.3% [4.1, 6.8]**
on a fresh n=1,047, and an earlier round over-counted it at 30% by treating **any** `@handle`
as Instagram. Both can be true — this population is conditioned on publishing an address, and
people who publish one also cross-link. The matcher here requires an `instagram.com` URL or an
explicit `ig`/`insta` cue beside the handle, never a bare `@name`, because a bare `@name` in a
TikTok bio is usually the poster's own handle.

**TikTok's 10.9% against Instagram's 16.9% is a PLATFORM gap, not a filter fault.** Instagram's
payload carries a business-email field and a contact button; between them they give Instagram
40 hits and 31 addresses — 51% of its yield. TikTok has neither. Nothing on this path closes
that, and no route proposed here would.

Not re-proposed, each closed with evidence: **Sherlock** (refused), **name search** (0 for 6,
six namesakes, one address belonging to a stranger at a transit authority — it does not merely
fail, it produces confident wrong answers about real people), **email-finder services**
(name + company domain → permute; there is no company domain for a creator on gmail).

**Pages with no email are kept, flagged.** `drknsstd` is a WANT whose address has since gone
from its bio — a good page without an address is still a good page. Its EMAIL cell reads
`NO EMAIL ON THE PROFILE` rather than sitting blank, because a blank cell reads as "not looked
up" and this one means "looked up, and there is none".
