## 1. The scoring set — and why the brief's instruction could not be followed as written

This round was asked to score every rule **against his 823 marks** and, four paragraphs later,
**never to score against the judge's own verdicts**. Those are the same set, so the second
instruction had to win.

| kind of row | n | what it actually records |
|---|---:|---|
| `mark` = GOOD / BAD | **823** | *was the judge right?* — **a button on the judge's own verdict** |
| `mark_kind = OPERATOR_OWN_VERDICT` | **200** | **his verdict, formed independently** |

Agreement measured against the 823 is `baseline − broke + fixed`: a brief that changes nothing
scores its baseline **by construction**. That is precisely how the dialogue veto came to be
recommended at +3 and +5.6 points and then measured at **−7**.

**So every number in this report rests on 200 labels, not 823.** The intervals are wide
because of it, and that is the honest cost of having only 200 real labels. The 823 are used
for the one thing they can answer without a truth label: **how many pages a rule fires on.**

**Leak check, on the exact bytes** — `bl1265_brief_v8.md` (32,108 B, sha `8c2874e78de4ff7d`)
and `bl1270_brief_tt2.md` (35,312 B, sha `52dd6bee069ba44f`), across 119 mark sets and 1,556
handles. Each names 15 pages; **none is inside the 200**, so **0 pages void**.

## 3. The low-quality rule — his four words, measured one at a time

He said low quality means **blurry, barely any posts, lazy edits, and random junk**. Each was
built as its own check and scored separately. **One of the four survived.**

### ✅ "Random junk" — as *no drawn text at all*

| TikTok | |
|---|---:|
| fires on | **10 of 100** |
| he rejected | **10 of 10** |
| precision | **100.0%** [72.2, 100.0] |
| base rate | 63.0% [53.2, 71.8] |
| **clears the bar** | **yes** — 72.2 > 71.8 |
| recovers / breaks | **5 / 0** |
| **held-out** | fires 7, precision 100%, **net +5** |

**The entire gain is out of sample.** In-sample it fires 3 times and recovers nothing; on the
held-out half it fires 7 times and recovers 5. That is the check the veto never had, and it is
the reason this one is trustworthy.

Instagram: the same rule fires 4 times, recovers 1, interval [51.0, 100.0] — real but inside
the noise. **Not shipped for Instagram**, and that restraint is measured, not assumed.

### ✗ "Lazy edits" — refused, and the finding is that it cannot work

| threshold | fires | precision | net |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| score ≥ 1 | 30 of 32 | 53.3% | **−4** |
| score ≥ 2 | 26 | 50.0% | −3 |
| score ≥ 3 | 19 | 57.9% | −1 |
| ≥ 3 lazy tiles | 28 | 50.0% | −4 |
| ≥ 5 lazy tiles | 22 | 45.5% | −5 |
| ≥ 7 lazy tiles | 16 | 50.0% | −2 |

**Net negative at every threshold**, with precision at or below the 56% base rate of that
sample. **Nearly every repost page looks lazily edited** — four agents rating grids blind put
30 of 32 at score ≥ 1. The phrase is real to him but it does not separate what he wants from
what he does not, so it cannot become a rule.

### ✗ "Blurry" — measured free, and unstable

Median variance-of-Laplacian per tile, computed on all 200 grids already on disk. It does
separate on TikTok — pages he wanted score **3140** against **1510** for pages he rejected —
but as a rule:

| | fires | net | in-sample | held-out |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Instagram < 400 | 15 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Instagram < 800 | 38 | −4 | | |
| **TikTok < 400** | 10 | +2 | **−1** | **+3** |

**Refused on the split.** A rule that is −1 on one half and +3 on the other at n=10 is a coin
landing heads; shipping it would repeat the veto exactly.

### ✗ "Barely any posts" — never fires where it was expected

**No Instagram grid in the set has 6 tiles or fewer**, so the rule cannot fire there at all. On
TikTok, ≤3 tiles is +2 but its interval [52.4, 92.4] straddles the base rate. Not shipped.

*(Established by measurement rather than by analogy to the follower floor, as asked: the
post-count equivalent simply does not exist on Instagram in this sample.)*

## 5. The three smaller ones

**Q1 and Q2 do not generalise, and that is worth knowing.** He rejected the two pages those
questions were about, but encoded as rules they are **net −1 and −3**: the game-footage rule
fires on 6 pages and takes 2 he wanted; the descriptive-label rule fires on 11 and takes 5.
**His answers were about two pages, not a policy.** The estimated +2 does not survive
generalisation and is not shipped.

**Thin sheets**: TikTok ≤3 tiles is +2 but its interval straddles the base rate; Instagram
never fires. Not shipped.

**Dead profiles**: one page's "grid" is an Instagram *"Profile isn't available"* screen. The
judge correctly said UNSURE. **This is a capture failure, not a judging rule** — it belongs in
the capture step, and putting it in the brief would teach the judge to guess about pages it
cannot see.

## 7. In plain words

He said low quality means four things. **Only one of them can be turned into a rule.** A
TikTok page whose grid has no writing on it anywhere is a page he throws away — 10 out of 10,
and it holds up on pages the rule had never seen. **Animal pages** are the other one: 8 out of
8, and he has said it twice himself.

**"Lazy edits" cannot be a rule** — almost every repost page looks lazily edited, so the test
would throw away as much as it saved. **"Blurry" and "barely any posts" are not steady enough
to trust** at this sample size.

Those two rules take TikTok from **83 to 90 out of 100**. Instagram stays at 92 — nothing
tested was good enough to ship for it, and it already never throws away a page he wants.

The thing I got wrong last round is now bolted shut: a test fails if the dialogue veto ever
comes back, and the numbers that refute it are written into the facts file.
