## 0. The short version

**The drawn-text reader was never missing.** `frame_text.read_video` is complete and tested,
and `parallel_judge.judge_one` already calls it and already writes `p["top_text"]`.
**`parallel_judge` has no production caller** — nothing under `clippershq/` imports it. The
shipped funnel is `meme_finder.run_funnel`, and its stages were `posts_for → attach_speech →
judge_page` with no OCR step at any point. Two judging paths; the complete one was orphaned.

That is now fixed: `build_top_text_fn` + `attach_top_text`, wired into `run_funnel` beside
`attach_speech`, delegating every pixel decision to `frame_text`.

**It works.** On 25 live pages it read **151 drawn lines** off real Instagram clips — *"Me
when someone doesn't open the car door fast enough"*, *"That was too smooth"*. The language
rule fired **for the first time in the project's history**, correctly, on romanised Hindi.

**And the honest result is uncomfortable, so it leads.** On those 25 pages agreement against
his hand marks went **DOWN, 60.0% → 56.0%** — one page. Both changes the drawn text caused
went **against pages he marked GOOD**. At n=25 that is one page of noise, but the direction is
not the one anybody wanted and I am not going to bury it in a paragraph about the fix working.

**The full 874-page re-judge was not run, and could not be.** Not money — **time**. Measured
**69.9 s per page** unloaded. 874 pages is **17.0 hours**.

## 1. The cause, named

`meme_finder.posts_for()` returns a dict with no `top_text` key — that much of the brief is
right. But the interesting question is *why nothing ever added one*, and the answer is not
"the reader was not built":

| | |
|---|---|
| `clippershq/frame_text.py` | a complete, tested reader with a shipping entry point (`read_video`) |
| `clippershq/parallel_judge.py` | **already** downloads, extracts frames, OCRs and sets `p["top_text"]` (`judge_one`, line 268) |
| callers of `parallel_judge` in `clippershq/` | **zero.** Two comments and four test modules, and nothing else in the repo |
| `meme_finder.run_funnel` | the path that actually runs: `posts_for → attach_speech → judge_page` |

So `_post_text` returned `source == "caption"` for every post ever judged, and the two rules
that gate on `source == "top_text"` could not fire. Confirmed against BL-1237's own histogram
over all 1,885 judged pages: `format_share` 622, `dialogue` 277, `creator_page` 90,
`low_views` 43, `too_few_posts` 42, `no_text` 28, `thin_sample` 18, `news_media` 6 — and the
two language/promotion rules **absent entirely**.

This is the third instance of one shape in this project: RULE 8 before BL-1220, the helper
with no call site in `docs/CORRECTIONS.md`, and now this. **A correct component with no
caller measures exactly like a component that was never written.**

### 1.1 The rules are not called what the brief calls them

The brief, BL-1237 and BL-1237's sheet all name these rules **`foreign`** and **`promo`**.
Neither string exists in the code. The labels `fired` actually carries are **`not_english`**
(RULE 7) and **`promotional`**. Anybody grepping a verdict column for `foreign` finds nothing
and concludes the rule was never built. It was built, it is correct, and it never ran.

### 1.2 The fix

```
build_top_text_fn(http_get=None, work_root=None, emit=None, n_frames=None)  -> fn(post) | None
attach_top_text(posts, top_text_fn, *, order=None, budget=None)             -> stats dict
```

Named callables rather than twenty inline lines, for the reason BL-1223 recorded: a test can
then **construct the detector production uses** instead of grepping this file for a substring.
Wired in `run_funnel` immediately after `attach_speech`, passing `judged_order(posts)` — the
same order, for the same measured reason (35.4% of pages have a pin in the first three slots;
BL-1230 found 136 pages where every line the budget bought was discarded).

**There is no verdict-identical early exit here, and the code says so rather than implying
one.** `attach_speech` may stop at the first clean post because RULE 8 is settled by one
question. Nothing of the kind exists for the drawn line: `not_english` needs every judged post
to prove a page is all-English, and `promotional`/`format_share` are **shares** over the judged
posts. A budget changes their denominator; it does not short-circuit them. So
`meme_finder.top_text_budget` exists, defaults to None (read every judged post), and is
documented in `config_defaults.DEFAULTS` as an accuracy trade.

### 1.3 The three-state mapping, which is the part that would have been got wrong

`frame_text.top_text` signals **four** different outcomes all as `text=None` plus a `reason`,
and its docstring's promise that `""` means "measured, no line" is never actually delivered —
it never returns `""`. Reading `got["text"]` directly would file every refusal as *no text*,
which is precisely how BL-1180 found six clips judged on a bar sign. `_drawn_line_of` splits
them:

| `reason` | verdict | why |
|---|---|---|
| `frames_supplied < 3` | **None** — refusal | ffmpeg gave us too little to judge |
| `frames disagree` | **None** — refusal | the drawn text changes; no single line describes it |
| `no text detected on any frame` | **`""`** — measured | we read the frames; there is no line |
| `the only text found is not a line` | **`""`** — measured | a logo or stray glyph, not a hook |

A refusal leaves the key **absent**, so `_post_text` falls back to the caption and the page is
judged exactly as it is today. An expired URL must never vote NO-TEXT.

## 2. The cost per page

Measured on 3.11.9, live, against production Instagram.

| | unloaded (4 pages) | with a concurrent 4-way suite (25 pages) |
|---|---:|---:|
| **wall clock per page** | **69.87 s** | **89.01 s** |
| of which the two billed API calls | 3.30 s | 4.09 s |
| of which **frame reading** | **66.53 s (95.2%)** | 84.88 s (95.4%) |
| posts read per page | 10.00 | 9.72 |
| billed per page | **$0.0012** | $0.0012 |

**Frame reading is 95% of the stage.** Money is not the constraint and never was:

| population | billed | **wall clock at 69.9 s/page** |
|---|---:|---:|
| the 874 send-file pages | $1.0488 — **over the $1.00 cap** | **17.0 hours** |
| the 623 hand-marked pages | $0.7476 | 12.1 hours |
| what was actually run | **$0.0300** | 37 min |

Two facts the brief did not have, both of which cost billed calls to learn:

- **A page costs TWO billed calls, not one.** `user_medias` answers `400 user_id must be a
  number` for a username, so `user_by_username` must run first for the id. Six calls were
  billed learning this, and they are in the $0.0300.
- **The full 874 sweep is $1.0488 — it does not fit the $1.00 cap** even before the 17 hours.

**The lever, if he wants the sweep:** `meme_finder.top_text_budget`. At 3 posts per page the
stage drops to roughly 20 s + 4 s ≈ 24 s/page → **5.8 hours** for 874. That is a real accuracy
trade (§1.2), not a free one, and it is his call.

### 2.1 The rendition question — `READ_FRAME_W = 1080` does NOT hold on this path, and must not

The brief asked me to confirm it does. **It does not, and importing it here would be a
measured regression.**

`READ_FRAME_W = 1080` lives in `clip_label_text.py`. That reader reads **our own rendered
label strip** — a known small region — where BL-1233 measured enlargement fixing a real
failure (15.9% unreadable at a 1280 delivery, 0.0% at 1920). `frame_text` reads **somebody
else's full frame** looking for a poster's line, and BL-1180 measured the opposite result on
exactly that task: **736 → 1440 took exact reads from 58.3% down to 51.4%**, because more
pixels find more background signage, not more hook. `frame_text.FFMPEG_SCALE` is
`scale=720:-2`, deliberately, with that measurement written beside it.

Two readers, two questions, two measured answers. `tests/test_bl1239_drawn_text.py` now pins
them apart so nobody harmonises them into one number.

## 3. The re-judge — 25 pages, and why only 25

### 3.1 There is no offline path, measured before anything was bought

| | |
|---|---:|
| send-file pages with a post list anywhere on disk | **36 of 874 (4.1%)** |
| their stored posts, all carrying a `video_url` | 431 |
| **those URLs still fetchable** | **0 of 20 sampled — every one HTTP 403** |

Instagram CDN URLs expire. So **every page re-judged has to be re-bought**; the nine stored
post corpora cannot contribute a single drawn line. (`scratch/bl1239_corpus_survey.py`,
`scratch/bl1239_cdn_probe.py` — both free, no billed call.)

### 3.2 And only 25 of the 874 can answer the agreement question at all

The 623 hand marks and the 874 send-file pages **overlap on 25 pages**. The marks are mostly
on pages the filter *rejected*, which by definition are not in a send file. So 25 is not a
sample size I chose — it is the whole intersection.

### 3.3 The design: one purchase, judged twice

Each page's posts are bought once, then judged **twice** — once with `top_text` stripped (what
production did before) and once with it attached (what production does now). Same posts, same
profile keywords, same judge, same second. Any difference is the drawn line and nothing else.

`profile_facts` supplies the id but its profile keywords are **not** passed to `judge_page`
here, identically in both arms, so RULE 2 (`too_few_posts`) abstains in both. Stated here
rather than discovered in the numbers.

### 3.4 What changed

**25 pages, 243 posts read, $0.0300.**

| | |
|---|---:|
| drawn lines read | **151** |
| clips measured to carry **no** line | 34 |
| clips the reader **refused** | **58 (23.9%)** |
| **verdict changes** | **1 of 25** |
| pass → reject | 1 |
| reject → pass | 0 |
| **`not_english` fires** | **1** (was 0 — ever) |
| **`promotional` fires** | **0** (was 0) |

**`promotional` rejected nothing, exactly as he predicted.** He said he had seen heavy
promotion once in 600 pages and *"a few words, I don't care"*. 0 of 25. The rule is right and
the data now reaches it.

**`not_english` fired for the first time in the project's history**, on a page whose drawn
lines are romanised Hindi — *"Neyoo backs Godlike and Orangutan after strong PMWc stage
performance"* → `hindi_romanised`, 1 of 9 posts. The romanised-Hindi word list the brief asks
for **already exists** (`_HI_ROMAN`, BL-1187) and works; it had simply never been reached. That
page was already rejected on `format_share` and stays rejected, so this fire changed no verdict.

### 3.5 Agreement went DOWN, and the one flip was not the language rule

| | agreement vs his marks, n=25 |
|---|---:|
| **before** (caption-judged) | **60.0%** — 15 of 25 |
| **after** (drawn text) | **56.0%** — 14 of 25 |

One page. The 95% interval on 25 samples is roughly ±19 points, so this is **not a
distinguishable move** — but it is not an improvement either, and the two pages the drawn text
touched both went against him:

- **the flip** — a page he marked **GOOD**, previously `2 of 10 posts are HOOKS, 5 synopsis`
  (passing), now `1 of 10 HOOKS, 9 synopsis` → **`format_share`, rejected.** Reading the drawn
  line made the hook classifier see *fewer* hooks, not more. Its drawn text is
  `"OBSESSION' REVERSED"` — a title card, which reads as synopsis rather than as a hook.
- **the `not_english` fire** — also a page he marked **GOOD** (already rejected on other
  grounds).

**I am not going to claim this validates the change.** What it establishes is that the data now
arrives and the rules now run; whether the *rules* are right on the drawn surface is a separate
question this sample is too small to answer, and the first evidence points the wrong way. The
specific suspicion worth testing next is that **`is_hook`/`narrates_action` were tuned on
captions and mis-classify title cards** — which would be exactly the brief's own instruction in
reverse: *if it starts rejecting pages he wants, the rule is wrong, not the data.*

### 3.6 The refusal rate is 23.9% and that is a real limit

58 of 243 posts were refused — mostly frames disagreeing, i.e. clips whose drawn text changes
shot to shot. Those posts fall back to their captions, so **the caption surface is still in
play on about a quarter of posts**, including the 12.63% [10.34, 15.33] of pages carrying
scraper-polluted captions. The fix narrows the exposure; it does not close it.
