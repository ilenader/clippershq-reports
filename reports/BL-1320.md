## The finding that changes the question

He has been asking *"what can look at a picture cheaply?"* The arithmetic says that is the
wrong question.

**A premium judged page costs $0.040933. The screenshot inside it is 5.1% of that — $0.0021.
Output is 80%, and half of the whole bill is reasoning nobody ever reads.**

| | share of the bill |
|---|---:|
| capture (Playwright, his machine) | **$0.00 — no vendor call at all** |
| the image, once in context | **5.1%** |
| all input | 20.0% |
| **all output** | **80.0%** |
| — of which invisible reasoning | **49.3%** |

So the cost is not the picture entering the model. **The cost is the model thinking and
writing.** That is why the right family is one that emits *no tokens at all* — an embedding
and a threshold — rather than a cheaper model that still reasons.

### And the budget is 58× larger than he thought

If a pre-filter removes 85% of pages before the premium judge sees them:

| | per 1,000 pages |
|---|---|
| subscription tokens before | 12,393,000 |
| after | **1,859,000** — 10.5M saved |
| paid path | $40.93 → **$6.14** |

**The break-even price for the filter is $0.0348 per image.** His target was $0.0006 — that is
1.5% of a premium page. **Even a filter ten times his target still pays for itself.** Price is
not the binding constraint. Accuracy is.

## The answer was already on his disk, and it is measured

`_vision_dataset/` already holds **2,272 SigLIP embeddings over 137 labelled accounts**.
`RESULTS.md` in that folder concluded *"Winner = PROMPT ENGINEERING"* and *"Alternative models
— NO"*. **It never tested a trained probe**, and the embeddings to do it were already there.

A 5-fold cross-validated logistic regression on those embeddings, at sklearn's default C=1.0:

| | AUC | separation | recall | **false positives** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| shipped zero-shot | 0.639 | +28.2% | 82% | **53%** |
| best prompt engineering | 0.766 | +41% | 78% | 36% |
| **SigLIP + linear probe** | **0.937** | **+78.2%** | **88%** | **10%** |

**False positives fall from 53% to 10% while recall rises.** Training the entire 5-fold CV took
**27 milliseconds**. No install, no download, no new dependency, no GPU.

That is the thing to test first, and it costs nothing to test.

## What the survey found — 118 candidates

### Paid APIs: the target is beaten by 12×, not narrowly met

**Cheapest: $0.0000516 per image** — Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite on the Batch API ($0.000103
real-time). Working: a 1024×1024 screenshot tiles to 4×768² at 258 tokens each = 1,032 image
tokens × $0.10/M. **Eleven candidates land under his $0.0006 target.**

| candidate | $/image | note |
|---|---:|---|
| Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite (batch) | **$0.0000516** | cheapest found |
| DeepInfra Qwen3-VL-30B | $0.000201 | open weights → self-host escape hatch |
| Moondream Cloud | $0.000219 | **resolution-independent** — fixed 729 tokens per image |

**The trap worth naming: GPT-4o-mini at `detail=high` bills 25,501 tokens for a 1024² image
against GPT-4.1-mini's 1,660 — 5.8× *more* expensive despite the cheaper per-token sticker.**
Cost-per-token and cost-per-image rank differently, which is exactly why the brief asked for
per-image.

**Per-1,000-image vendors are genuinely token-decoupled** — AWS Rekognition $1.00/1,000, Google
Cloud Vision $1.50/1,000, Azure from $1.00/1,000, no tokenizer anywhere in the pricing and no
resolution sensitivity. **But they buy features, never a verdict**: label detection returns
"Person, Indoors, Screen" and moderation returns "no violations". Neither has any concept of
provenance. At his volume they are also *10× dearer* than Flash-Lite.

*One exception worth a spike:* **Google Cloud Vision Web Detection ($0.0035/image)**
reverse-image-searches the web — it could **find** a borrowed clip's source rather than judge
it. That is a different and possibly better question to ask.

### Open weights: Windows is the discriminator, and three strong candidates fail on it

The proven-native path is **llama.cpp's `mtmd`** pre-quantised list plus its Windows x64
CUDA release binaries — covering Gemma 3, **Gemma 4**, SmolVLM, Moondream2, Qwen2/2.5-VL,
InternVL 2.5/3, Pixtral, Mistral Small 3.1 and Llama 4 Scout.

**Three documented Windows blockers** — found, not inferred:

| model | blocker |
|---|---|
| **Phi-4-multimodal** | pins `flash-attn==2.7.4.post1`; the reported workaround is hand-editing line 2137 inside the HF module cache |
| **Ovis2.5** | pins `flash-attn==2.7.0.post2`, no GGUF escape — 78.3 OpenCompass and it will not install |
| **Florence-2** | `get_imports` statically lists `flash_attn` even on CPU; also task-prompt-only, so you cannot ask it a free-form question at all |

`flash-attn` has **no official Windows wheel**. vLLM/SGLang/Triton are Linux-only in practice,
so any model whose only fast path is those loses its speed story here. **This is the
essentia pattern repeating**, and it is why the field splits cleanly.

Two things genuinely new: **Gemma 4** (2026-04-02, now **Apache 2.0** — Google dropped its
custom terms — with a configurable 70/140/280/560/1120 image-token budget) and **MiniCPM-V
4.6** (May 2026, **1B params**, Apache 2.0).

### ⚠️ A hardware correction that invalidates most published speed figures

**This machine has an AMD RX 6600 and `torch 2.11.0+cpu`. There is no CUDA path.** Every "fast
on a consumer GPU" number in the literature assumes NVIDIA and does not apply. The only real
accelerated option is `onnxruntime-directml`.

Related: `_vision_dataset/RESULTS.md` claims SigLIP is "far faster on CPU". **Measured, that is
wrong** — SigLIP ViT-B-16 is **3.75× slower** than ViT-B-32 (0.499 vs 0.133 CPU-s/image), and
under a probe they score 0.937 vs 0.929, i.e. the same. ViT-B-32 is the throughput swap.

*(Timing caveat, stated by the agent that measured it: the box was at 100% CPU with other
rounds' processes, so wall-clock was meaningless; it re-measured with `process_time()`
single-threaded and marked the derived per-image figures optimistic.)*

### Cheap non-VLM signals, and two more traps

* **`face_recognition` is an essentia-class trap.** It looks like a clean 15 KB pure-python
  wheel, but `dlib` 20.0.1 is **sdist-only, zero wheels for any platform.** The blocker is one
  level down the dependency tree.
* **`insightface` is no longer a Windows build problem** (1.0.1 ships `py3-none-any`), **but
  its pretrained weights including buffalo_l are non-commercial research only** — disqualifying
  for a lead-generation product. Use OpenCV **YuNet + SFace**: Apache-2.0, and both classes are
  *already in the installed `cv2`*.
* **PySceneDetect cut-rate is the strongest non-CLIP signal here** — the residual confusion in
  `RESULTS.md` is cinematic repost farms, and cut rate is the one axis a still frame
  structurally cannot see.

### Capture-and-analyse tools

**His TikTok method is already the industry standard.** Apify sells a "TikTok Frame Extractor"
that is exactly his approach — bulk cover images over plain HTTP, no browser. The only
alternative found is a fragile slider-CAPTCHA solver that trades a working path for a
ToS-violating one. **Recommend no change.**

A deliberate negative result: `pooriya-jamie/audit-pipline-demo`, which search engines surface
as "screenshot a TikTok and classify it", is a vanilla-JS animation that captures nothing and
calls no model. **The one project naming the exact target architecture does not implement it.**

One capability he does not have: HypeAuditor's audience-authenticity scoring — a kill signal a
screenshot physically cannot produce.

## The honest speed ceiling — no model choice moves it

**Target: 1,000 wanted pages in 10 minutes.** At the brief's 9% want rate that is **11,111
pages**, i.e. **1,111 pages/minute**.

| | actual | short by |
|---|---:|---:|
| TikTok vendor fetch | 5.05/min | **220×** |
| judging (paid API) | 10.18/min | **109×** |
| Instagram capture (free, Playwright) | 123.22/min | 9× |

**The bottleneck is different on each platform, and that matters.**

| 11,111 pages | TikTok fetch | Instagram capture | judging |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| hours | **36.7 h** | 1.50 h | 18.2 h |

* **On TikTok the vendor is the ceiling** — 36.7 h of fetching before a single judgement.
* **On Instagram capture is free and fast; judging is the ceiling** — 18.2 h against 1.5 h.

**A note on the 9%.** His own verdicts (BL-1303) measured **28% on Instagram and 37% on
TikTok**. At those rates 1,000 wanted pages is 3,571 and 2,703 pages — 11.8 h and 8.9 h of
TikTok fetch. The 9% looks like the rate at which pages *publish an address* (FACTS §1 puts
TikTok at ~10.6%), which is a different question. Both are computed in
`scratch/bl1320_ceiling.json`.

### What is actually achievable

| window | judge path | pages judged | wanted (IG 28% / TT 37%) |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 8-hour day | paid API | 4,886 | 1,368 / 1,808 |
| 8-hour day | free sub-agent | 3,101 | 868 / 1,147 |
| 24 h unattended | paid API | 14,659 | 4,105 / 5,424 |

**But TikTok fetch does not fit in any of those windows** — 4,886 pages needs 16.1 h of
fetching inside an 8-hour day. On TikTok, a realistic day is bounded by the vendor at roughly
**300 pages/hour, ~2,400 in an 8-hour day**, yielding ~890 wanted at his 37%.

**Ten minutes is not reachable by any route.** A realistic day is ~1,000–1,400 wanted pages on
Instagram, ~890 on TikTok — and the pre-filter above does not change fetch at all. **It buys
tokens and money, not time**, except on Instagram where judging *is* the ceiling and removing
85% of pages cuts 18.2 h to 2.7 h.

## What to do next, cheapest first

1. **Fit the linear probe on the embeddings already on disk.** 27 ms, free, and it moved AUC
   0.639 → 0.937 with false positives 53% → 10% in the measurement above. Confirm it on a
   held-out split before believing it.
2. **Buy confidence rather than asking for it** — 3 votes of Gemini Flash-Lite at $0.0000516
   each is $0.00015 per page, and *disagreement* is the routing gate that logprobs no longer
   provide.
3. **Add PySceneDetect cut-rate** for the residual cinematic-repost confusion a still frame
   cannot see.
4. **Spike Cloud Vision Web Detection** on 20 grids to see whether reverse image search
   *finds* the borrowed source — a different question, possibly the right one.

Do **not** start by downloading a VLM. On CPU-only AMD hardware, with the break-even at
$0.0348/image and a free probe already scoring 0.937, it is the most expensive way to answer
the cheapest question.
