# BL-1179 — the 17% is a ceiling, not a gap; and 0 of 722 meme pages can reach the send list

**2026-08-08.** Two losses were named in the brief. One of them turned out not to be a loss.
The other is total — not 1,249 rows out of many, but **every meme page the system has ever
found, without exception**, and the chain measurement is what proved it.

Everything below is measured. $0.2556 of vendor spend against a $2.00 cap.

> **Round id.** This is BL-1179, not BL-1178. BL-1178 is held by a live concurrent round —
> `scratch/bl1178_probe.json` was written at 23:37 while this round was reading the repo, and
> `clippershq/meme_finder.py` grew by 138 lines at 23:39 under it. Neither id was published on
> `origin/main`. That round is adding supply-discovery functions (`suggested_profiles`,

> writes, and no shipped module at all.

## 1. Only 17% of meme pages yield an email — and the brief's diagnosis was wrong

The brief says the 17% comes entirely from the bio and that nothing else is being tried. The
first half is a labelling artifact and the second half is false. Both were measured on the
real population: **373 pages that passed the meme filter**, one profile call each. 361
resolved; 12 came back private, deleted or not-found.

### 1.1 The business/contact button is already wired, and it is the BIGGER half

`ig_discovery.DEFAULT_FIELD_MAP["email"]` already reads `public_email`, `business_email` and
`contact_email` — the Professional-account contact button. `enrich_profile` then merges
those with addresses scraped from the bio *text* into one `emails` list, and
`meme_finder.resolve_contact` stamps the result `instagram:bio`.

So "all from the bio" is what the **label** says. It is not what the data says:

| path | hits | yield | marginal cost |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **A. business/contact BUTTON** (structured field) | 40 | 11.1% | $0 — rides the profile call |
| **B. bio TEXT** (address typed in the caption) | 30 | 8.3% | $0 — rides the profile call |
| A ∪ B — *what the code calls "bio"* | **61** | **16.9%** | $0 |
| — of which **button only** (no address in the bio at all) | **31** | 8.6% | |
| — of which bio-text only | 21 | 5.8% | |
| — of which both | 9 | 2.5% | |

**31 of the 61 addresses — 51% — exist only because of the contact button.** The brief's
instinct was right ("it often carries an email the bio does not") and the work is already
done; what was missing was the ability to *see* it, because one label covers two sources.
`email_finder.find_email` already distinguishes them (`:business-field` vs `:bio-text`);
`meme_finder.resolve_contact` does not. That is a one-line reporting difference, not a
funnel to build, and it is the reason two rounds could look at this number and conclude that
a path nobody had tried was sitting there.

### 1.2 Link-in-bio aggregators: 7 pages have one, and it is worth one address

Measured only where A and B were both empty (n=300) — crediting the link path for pages
whose bio already carried an address would be counting work it did not do.

| | pages | scraped ok | yielded an address | **survives the send-list quarantine** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **C. aggregator** (linktr.ee ×6, beacons.ai ×1) | 7 | 7 | 2 | **1** |
| **D. own site / other** | 29 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| social hop (tiktok/youtube/t.me/…) | 34 | — | — | — |
| **no link at all** | **230** | — | — | — |

Two corrections to my own first reading of this table, because both would have overstated it:

- **One of the two aggregator "hits" is not a lead.** `bccoberlin`'s linktree returned five
  addresses, all of them `…@ soundcloud.com` (`dsa-contact`, `tco-contact`, `contact`) —
  SoundCloud's own site boilerplate, scraped off an embedded player. This is exactly the
  population `send_list.py:300`'s bio-link custom-domain quarantine already exists for
  (measured there at 91% junk), so it would be cut before it ever reached a send file.
  Applying that same rule here leaves **one real address** — a gmail on `@audreyscinefile`'s
  own name, in `scratch/bl1179_paths.json`.
- **Path D's 0% is a refusal, not a measurement.** All 29 own-site links returned
  `unsupported` — `enrich_links.is_enrichable_link` declines them. Most deserve it
  (`bit.ly`, `apps.apple.com`, `etsy.com`, `maps.app.goo.gl`, `ig.me`), but roughly eight are
  real creator sites (`cyberprotec.site`, `bsapparel.shop`, `moviexsuggestion.com`,
  `nocrumbs.club`, `cardsheriff.com`, `acggoods.com`). Path D has **not** been shown to be
  worthless; it has been shown to be **switched off**. Its true ceiling is ≤8 pages
  (≤2.2pp), and `site_contacts` already exists to do the work if the host filter is widened.

**The marginal gain from every additional automated path, honestly counted: 1 page. +0.28pp.**
17.2% instead of 16.9%.

### 1.3 The real shape of the 83%

The reason there is so little left is not that the paths are weak. It is that **230 of 300
pages with no address publish no link of any kind**, and 34 more link only to another social
profile. There is nothing to follow.

### 1.4 DM-only pages are a category, not a failure

**27 pages (7.5% of resolved) state a DM or inbox contact route in the bio** — "dm for
promo", "business inbox", "dms open". **23 of them have no email on any path**, i.e. 7.7% of
the 300 "failures" are not failures at all: they are reachable, on a channel this funnel does
not use. The system already has `clippershq/ig_dm_list.py`.

So the honest denominator, on 361 resolved pages:

| | pages | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| an address on some path | 62 | **17.2%** |
| no address, but a **DM route stated** — reachable, different channel | 23 | **6.4%** |
| no address and no stated route | 275 | 76.2% |
| *(profile unresolved / private / deleted — 12 more, outside the 361)* | | |

### 1.5 Name search: 0 for 6, and the failure is identification, not coverage

My own shape rule (2–3 capitalised words, no page-word) matched 41 pages. **Reading all 41
by hand, only 14 are actually people** — the rest are brand names in title case ("History
Vault", "Scene Craft", "The Quirk Merchant"). I am quoting the hand count, 14 (3.9%), and
reporting the regex's 41 only as the thing it corrects.

Six of the 14 were searched. **Zero usable addresses. Six for six returned namesakes.**

- Five searches returned multiple same-name Instagram accounts, and in four of those the
  page's actual handle appeared in none of them.
- The only address any search produced was for **Alex Berisic** — from SignalHire, for a
  Building Serviceperson at the Toronto Transit Commission, a `ttc.ca` work address and
  near-certainly a different human. That is the vendor category this project already
  measured and refused, arriving through the back door.
- One search did something the paid call could not: **Ebony Moore**'s IG profile returned no
  `external_url`, but the search found her linktree and two websites. Still no address.

The path does not fail for lack of web coverage. It fails because **a name does not identify
the page owner**, which is the same wall BL-1167 hit from the other side (only a profile call
beats a namesake — and a name search is precisely the instrument with no profile call in it).
Ceiling if it worked perfectly on all 14: +3.9pp, every one of them unverifiable.

### 1.6 No email-finder vendor — reconfirmed, and it is worse than "won't work"

Not bought, and the brief's reasoning holds: that category is name + **company domain** →
permute-and-verify. **Of the 62 addresses found here, 44 (71%) are free-provider (gmail and
friends).** There is no domain to permute. The Alex Berisic result above is what the category
actually returns on this population.

### 1.7 The regex

Both live paths stayed bounded through 373 profile parses and 36 link scrapes. The cap fired
for real: `armra.com` returned 285,514 chars and `omniluxled.com` 289,832, both truncated to
`MAX_PARSE_CHARS`. My probe is a new caller and reaches the pattern only through
`bio_parser.extract_emails` and `enrich_links.enrich_link`, both of which own their bound.

The structural hole named in the brief is real and is now closed by a test rather than by a
comment: the cap lives in the *caller* (`_bounded`), not in the pattern, so nothing stopped
a future public function from reaching `_EMAIL_RE` directly and inheriting a silent
GIL-holding freeze. `tests/test_bl1179_gate_and_paths.py` parses `bio_parser.py`'s AST and
fails if any public function touches a quadratic pattern without calling `_bounded` first —
a text match would have passed on a comment. It also runs `parse_bio` on the 250,000-char
pathological input that once took 57.46s, with a 2.0s ceiling.

## 2. The verdict gate: what it actually tests, and who it cuts

### 2.1 The test

`send_list.py:339`, three lines:

```python
if kind == writer.LEAD_KIND_CLIPPER:
    verdict = str(r.get("verdict") or "").strip().upper()
    if verdict not in writer.BOT_READY_VERDICTS:     # {"EDITOR", "LIKELY"}
        cut_verdict += 1
        continue
```

`EDITOR`/`LIKELY`/`UNSURE`/`CREATOR?` are buckets of one number,
`quality_gate.verdict_label(editor_pct)`: ≥60 EDITOR, ≥40 LIKELY, ≥20 UNSURE, else CREATOR?.
So for a clipper row the gate means exactly **`editor_pct ≥ 40`**. It is clipper-only —
client rows skip it, correctly, since no client funnel computes an editor score.

Replaying the full filter ladder over the live master (`scratch/bl1178_gate.py`):

| stage | rows |
|---|---:|
| master rows | 61,079 |
| routable (`lead_kind` declared) | 61,019 |
| has an email | 5,800 |
| past the bio-link custom-domain quarantine | 5,745 |
| **cut at the verdict gate** | **1,269** |
| past the verdict gate | 4,476 |

1,269, not 1,249 — the number moved by 20 because today's meme run added rows.

### 2.2 It is not one population. It is four, and they are not the same decision

| verdict | rows | what it means | is the gate right? |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `UNSURE` | 618 | `editor_pct` 20–39. The classifier looked and said *probably not an editor* | **arguably yes** — this is the classifier doing its job |
| `CREATOR?` | 593 | `editor_pct` 0–19. The classifier looked and said *no* | **yes** |
| `MEME_PAGE` | 30 | `repost_finder` pages. **Never given an `editor_pct` at all** | **no** |
| *(blank)* | 28 | `meme_finder` pages, added today. **Never given an `editor_pct` at all** | **no** |

The bottom two rows are the whole argument. `MEME_PAGE` is not a low editor score — it is a
**different taxonomy sharing one column**. A meme page is not an editor you hire; it is a
repost partner you **pay**. The gate asks "is this an editor we can hire", and 58 rows are
cut for failing a question nobody asked them. All 58 are Instagram; 26 of the 30 `MEME_PAGE`
rows are on free providers.

The 1,211 `UNSURE`/`CREATOR?` rows are a genuinely different question, and I have no evidence
that opening them is right — they are TikTok rows the editor classifier examined and scored
below 40. Nothing in this round measured whether that classifier is well-calibrated.

### 2.3 The choice, with counts — for the operator, not for me

Two rounds refused to invent a verdict to move this number and were right to. This round does
not open the gate either: **no shipped module was modified.** Here is the decision, priced:

| | opens | what it means | risk |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **A. Meme pages only** *(the evidenced one)* | **+58** | Route `MEME_PAGE` and meme-funnel rows past a gate that was never about them. They are a paid-repost product, not an editor hire | low — they were selected *by* the meme filter, and the send copy for them is a different pitch |
| **B. A + `UNSURE`** | +676 | Also accept `editor_pct` 20–39 | medium — sends to people the classifier scored below the bar; nothing here says that bar is wrong |
| **C. Everything** | +1,269 | Delete the gate for clippers | high — includes 593 rows scored 0–19 |
| **D. Nothing** | 0 | Status quo | the meme funnel keeps producing leads that cannot be sent |

**Option A is the only one this round has evidence for**, and it needs an answer to one
product question I cannot answer: *do meme pages get emailed from the same send file as
editors, or their own?* They need different copy — one is a job offer, the other a rate
enquiry.

**Per the brief, the measurement ships and the gate stays shut until the operator answers.**
`tests/test_bl1179_gate_and_paths.py` pins all four populations separately, so whichever
option is chosen changes a named number in a test rather than silently changing who gets
emailed.

### 3.1 A second loss the chain surfaced: 4 paid addresses stranded by an 18-minute window

63 pages resolved an address; only 58 are on a master row that carries one. Chasing the gap:

- **`bccoberlin`** — correctly absent. Its "address" was the SoundCloud boilerplate from §1.2.
- **`meysamderis`, `rascals`, `songss`, `stayunsupervised`** — all four sit in master as
  `MEME_PAGE` rows with an **empty email column**. Verified as single rows, not a duplicate-row
  artifact of my index.

The cause is a timestamp, and it is the shape of
`a-fix-does-not-reach-a-running-server` again:

```
18:48–18:51   the four pages walked, addresses resolved
18:53:12      master append  ← ran here
18:57:52      master append  ← and here
19:11:31      commit 38a392a — BL-1176 fixes crossdedup.reconcile to CARRY the email on merge
21:36:02      master append  ← the 30 addresses landed here
```

**The fix is forward-only and these four fell 18 minutes on the wrong side of it.** They
merged by identity into their existing `repost_finder` rows and the email was dropped, which
is precisely the bug BL-1176 fixed — 12 minutes too late for them.

Worse, **the loss is sticky**: `meme_finder` filters candidates against the seen-set
(`run_funnel`, `already_seen`), and all four are recorded as walked. A normal re-run will
skip them forever. The addresses were nowhere on disk — the seen-file stores only a boolean.

**This round re-resolved all four**, and they are real:

| handle | address | path |
|---|---|---|
| `meysamderis` | `contact@` its own domain | business button |
| `rascals` | `team@` its own domain | business button |
| `songss` | `hello@` a company domain | bio text |
| `stayunsupervised` | `team.` + the handle, at a free provider | bio text |

*(Addresses withheld here on purpose — the secret scanner refuses live lead addresses in a
published report, correctly. All four are in `scratch/bl1179_paths.json`.)*

They are in `scratch/bl1179_paths.json`. **I have not written them to master** — it was not
in this round's claim, and more to the point it would change nothing: all four are
`MEME_PAGE` rows, so they would land straight back at the verdict gate. Recovering them is
worth doing *after* §2.3 is answered, not before.

### 3.2 Two small things worth a line

- `meme_pages_seen.json` holds two entries named `a` and `b`, which the API rejects as
  invalid usernames. Junk from a parser, harmless, 2 of 722.
- `enrich_links` extracted two addresses carrying a literal `u003e` prefix — a JSON-escaped
  `>` not unescaped before the email pattern ran, so the escape sequence was swallowed into
  the local part. Cosmetic here (those addresses were junk anyway), but a real address
  preceded by an escaped `>` in a JSON blob would be mangled the same way.

## 4. What was done, and what was deliberately not

**Written:** `reports/BL-1179.md`, `tests/test_bl1179_gate_and_paths.py` (8 checks), and six
read-only probes under `scratch/`. **No shipped module was modified by this round.**

BL-1178 landed two commits while this one was measuring (`41d2b7e`, `de4243a`), touching
`clippershq/meme_finder.py` (+552) and `tests/test_meme_finder.py`. Checked rather than
assumed: `git diff --name-only 0827f76..HEAD` touches **none** of the modules measured here —
`send_list.py`, `bio_parser.py`, `crossdedup.py`, `enrich_links.py`, `writer.py` and
`ig_discovery.py` are all unchanged, so every figure above still describes HEAD.

**Not done, on purpose:** the verdict gate is not opened (§2.3 is the operator's call); the
four recovered addresses are not written to master (blocked by the same decision); the
`is_enrichable_link` host filter is not widened (path D is worth ≤8 pages and belongs with
the gate decision, not ahead of it).

**Spend: $0.2556 of $2.00**, read off `spend.json` (19 autoflush rows tagged `BL-1179`), not
computed from my own call count. The two disagree and the ledger is right: **401 profile
calls, 426 billed requests.** `IgClient._bill_from` bills what HikerAPI's own `x-hiker-info`
receipt states rather than assuming one request per call, and ~25 profile responses cost two.
A round that quoted its own arithmetic here would have under-reported by 7%.

Of that, **$0.015 was wasted**: the first attempt crashed at call 25 on
`client.unflushed_requests()` — a property, not a method — and kept nothing, so the calls were
paid for twice. The probe now appends each paid row to a JSONL the moment it arrives and a
re-run skips what is already there. Link scrapes and the six name searches cost $0.

**Suites:** `python tests/run_all.py --jobs 4` — **ALL GREEN, 260/260 suites, 6,997 checks,
966.5s**, including this round's 8 new checks. **Campaigns:** unchanged (5 — ANIME15K,
DAYLIGHT, PANICBABY, STRAENGE, ZHUS). **Config:** `config.json` untouched (`git status`
clean), parses, 162 keys; `preflight` reports "All set".
