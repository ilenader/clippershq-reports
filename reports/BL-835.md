# BL-835 — Frame-by-frame video understanding: Gemini on VIDEO declines 9/9 where BL-731's thumbnails declined 0/10. Per-frame OCR is 12x cheaper than the brief assumed. Scene detection is refuted — 6 of 10 clips have no cuts at all

**Date:** 2026-08-01 · **Type:** Research · **Spend:** **$0.0197** of a $0.05 ceiling
**Read-only.** No pipeline file touched, no code written. Measured on 10 real MP4s already on
disk from BL-804 and on the live 617-clip library.

Tiers: **VERIFIED** (measured here) · **UNVERIFIED LEAD** (plausible, untested) ·
**REFUTED** (measured false) · **GAP** (not knowable from this round).

## Three premises in the brief are wrong, and they change the answer

**REFUTED — "the existing stage costs 16s per IMAGE, so 58 frames is 15 minutes per clip".**
`clip_walk.OCR_S_PER_IMAGE = 1.34`; the 16.08 s figure is **per CLIP** (1.34 × 12 frames).
Measured directly: easyocr median **1.397 s/frame** at 360×640 — BL-710's number confirmed.
So 58 frames is **~81 seconds** of CPU, not 15 minutes. Per-frame OCR was never infeasible;
it was mis-costed by ~12×.

**REFUTED — "scene-change detection is likely far cheaper and more informative".** Cheaper,
yes: **0.30 s/clip**. More informative, no. On 10 real clips:

| threshold | cuts found | clips with ZERO cuts |
|---|---|---|
| 0.2 | 53 (5.3/clip) | **6 of 10** |
| 0.3 | 7 (0.7/clip) | 8 of 10 |
| 0.4 | 2 (0.2/clip) | 9 of 10 |

The repost format is a **static caption bar over a small inset video**, so global frame
difference stays tiny and ffmpeg sees no cuts. A sampler that returns zero frames for 60% of
the library is not a sampler. Scene detection is still useful — as a *dedup prefilter*, not a
cadence.

**REFUTED — "a 58s median clip".** Library median is **40.7 s** (n=617, every clip has a
duration). 58 s is roughly p75. Mean 42.1 s, 84% of clips ≤ 60 s.

**Also REFUTED, from BL-733 — "cropping to the detected caption bar first".** Already
measured and rejected: cropped **CER 0.219 vs full-frame 0.112**. Cropping clips glyphs. The
brief lists it as a fix; it is a regression.

## 1. Frame sampling — VERIFIED

At the library's mean 42.1 s, per clip: **42 frames** at 1 fps, **21** at 1-per-2s, **8** at
1-per-5s. Library-wide that is **25,999 / 13,000 / 5,200** frames.

Extraction is free in practice — ffmpeg at **0.014 s/frame**, and a perceptual hash costs
**0.0088 s/frame**.

**Dedup is the real lever.** Consecutive 1-fps frames on this corpus are highly redundant:

| dHash hamming threshold | frames kept |
|---|---|
| > 0 (any change) | 87% |
| > 8 | 57% |
| **> 16** | **41%** |
| > 24 | 34% |

At >16 that is a **2.4× reduction for 0.0088 s/frame** — far more effective than scene
detection and it never returns an empty set.

## 2. OCR per frame — VERIFIED, and it is affordable in CPU but not free in wall-clock

| | median s/frame |
|---|---|
| easyocr @ 360×640 | **1.397** |
| easyocr @ 720×1280 | **3.808** (2.7×) |

Resolution, not frame count, is the dominant cost. Per 1,000 clips:

| cadence | @360×640 | @720×1280 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 fps, no dedup (42 fr) | 16.3 CPU-h | 44.4 CPU-h |
| **1 fps + dedup (17 fr)** | **6.6 CPU-h** | 18.0 CPU-h |
| 1 per 2s + dedup (9 fr) | 3.5 CPU-h | 9.5 CPU-h |

$0 in cash, hours in wall-clock, parallelisable.

**The downscale guard the brief asks for is already in place.** `ocr_one`'s `max_side`
defaults to `None` — BL-733's fix shipped. **New nuance:** on 360×640 frames, `max_side=512`
produced **character-identical output** on every frame tested, because that is only a 1.25×
reduction. BL-733's damage was measured on 540×960 → 288×512, a 1.875× reduction. The rule is
not "downscaling is bad" but "downscaling *past roughly 1.5×* is bad" — worth knowing before
anyone reintroduces it as a speed lever on high-res frames, where it would bite hardest.

**GAP — paddleocr cannot be evaluated in this repo.** It is installed but fails to import:
`clippershq/filelock.py` **shadows the PyPI `filelock` package** paddleocr depends on
(`ImportError: cannot import name 'FileLock' from 'filelock'`). Any engine-swap benchmark
needs that name collision resolved first. rapidocr-onnxruntime is not installed.

## 3. Gemini on video — VERIFIED, and it behaves materially better than BL-731's thumbnails

9 clips, `gemini-flash-latest`, structured output, temperature 0. Two questions plus a
timeline. **The control question is the point**: `posted_date` is physically not in the
pixels, so any answer at all is a fabrication with nothing to argue about.

| | result |
|---|---|
| **`posted_date` declined (unanswerable control)** | **9 of 9** |
| `source_title` declined | 1 of 9 |
| titles confirmed by the stored caption | 7 of 8 |
| titles **provably wrong** | **0** |

**This does not reproduce BL-731's failure.** There, a single thumbnail produced 0/10 declines
and a confidently wrong "Beast … the CGI lions". Here the model declined on 100% of the
unanswerable control and on the one clip whose content was unidentifiable (a crowd/mascot
clip with no film in it).

**The confound, checked:** could it be reading the title off the burned-in caption bar rather
than recognising the footage? Measured — of 8 titles, **only 1 (Apocalypto) had its title
visible in the on-screen text**. The other 7, including *Futurama*, *Modern Family*, *Temple
Grandin*, *The Dark Knight Rises* ×2 and *The Amazing Spider-Man 2*, were identified from
imagery alone and confirmed against a caption the model never saw.

### What it reliably produces

51 beats across 9 clips: **100% carry a timestamp, 100% carry an emotion, 94% carry
on-screen text**, with `who` / `action` / `setting` populated throughout.

### What it does NOT produce

- **Not second-by-second.** Mean **5.7 beats per clip** — beat-level, not per-second. Asking
  for per-second granularity is asking for ~42 entries; it returned 2–13.
- **`t_end_s` is unreliable** — several beats came back with an end of 0.0.
- **Its on-screen text is GARBLED and is not an OCR substitute.** Verbatim from the Futurama
  clip: `"Let me back AG No, use. your house 10"`, `"Hi, crackers hash?"`, `"p.m.. No."`.
  It gets the gist and mangles the characters. For anything that must be exact — a quote, a
  handle, a price — dedicated OCR is still required.

### Cost — VERIFIED tokens, GAP on price

Measured: **71.5 tokens per second of video** (the published docs page I checked claimed
5,792 tokens/sec and described video as billed on *output* tokens; that is garbled — the
measurement stands). At the library's 42.1 s mean: **~3,013 prompt + 625 output tokens/clip**,
**12.3 s latency**.

| price scenario (per 1M in/out) | $/clip | **$/1,000** | fits $2? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flash 2.5 rates $0.30 / $2.50 | 0.00247 | **2.47** | marginally NO |
| Flash 3.x rates $1.50 / $7.50 | 0.00921 | **9.21** | NO |
| Flash-Lite $0.10 / $0.40 | 0.00055 | **0.55** | YES |

**GAP: `gemini-flash-latest` will not say which model it aliases** — its metadata returns only
"Latest release of Gemini Flash". The token counts are hard; the dollar figure swings **5×**
on a fact the API does not expose. Pin an explicit model ID before costing this for real.

## 4. Subtitles — VERIFIED, the best ratio in the report

| | |
|---|---|
| `subtitles_available` TRUE | **126 / 617 = 20.4%** |
| accounts with any | 21 of 66 — **45 accounts have zero** |
| subtitled clips are longer | median 56.5 s vs 40.7 s library-wide |
| cost **at walk time** | **$0** — the URI is in a payload already paid for and the `.srt` GET is unbilled |
| cost **retroactively** | 1 call/clip to re-fetch the manifest = **$0.0756** for all 126 |

Consistent with BL-798's 12/72 (16.7%) and its account-concentration finding, on 8.6× the
sample.

It carries **spoken dialogue**, which is a genuinely different signal from both the burned-in
overlay (OCR) and the visual description (Gemini). BL-798 measured only **36% word overlap**
between the two. **Nobody has read a single one** — `subtitle_text` is populated on 0 of 617
clips.

## 5. Ranked by information gained ÷ cost

| # | option | cost / 1,000 | what it adds | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Subtitles at walk time** | **$0** | spoken dialogue, verbatim, timestamped | **DO IT.** Free, and the `.srt` is already timestamped — it *is* a second-by-second track for the 20% that have it. Only reaches 20.4%. |
| **2** | **Dedup + per-frame OCR** | **$0 cash, 6.6 CPU-h** | exact on-screen text, per frame, all clips | **DO IT** for verbatim text. Gemini cannot replace this. |
| **3** | **Gemini video, beat-level** | **$2.47** (or $9.21 — see GAP) | who / action / emotion / setting, and reliable declining | **FITS ONLY IF** the model pins to Flash-2.5-class pricing. At 3.x rates it is 4.6× over budget. |
| 4 | Gemini Flash-Lite | $0.55 | same shape, unknown quality | **UNVERIFIED LEAD** — priced, not tested. Worth one measurement round. |
| 5 | Scene detection as a sampler | $0 | — | **REFUTED.** Zero cuts on 60% of clips. Keep it only as a dedup prefilter. |

**They are complementary, not competing.** Subtitles give what was *said*; OCR gives what was
*written*; Gemini gives what is *happening* and who is in frame. The brief's goal —
"who is in frame, what they are doing, what emotion, what text appears when" — needs Gemini
for the first three and OCR for the fourth, because Gemini garbles text and OCR has no
semantics.

**Against the $2/1,000 budget:** options 1 and 2 fit with room to spare and cost no cash at
all. Option 3 fits only under the cheaper pricing scenario. Doing 1 + 2 + 3 together lands
between **$2.47 and $9.21 per 1,000** — the budget question is entirely the unresolved model
alias, not the design.

## Honest limits

- **n=9 clips from 7 accounts**, all English, all repost-format. The decline result is the
  strongest finding here and it rests on nine trials; BL-731's contrary result rested on ten.
  A 100-clip run would settle it properly, and would cost about $0.25.
- **Caption agreement is not independent ground truth.** It is written by the reposter and
  can be wrong. It is reasonable evidence because the model never saw it, but it is not a
  gold label.
- **One title is unverifiable**: "Lego DC Super-Villains" for a clip whose caption says only
  "Tensions in the Batcave | #lego #batman". Plausible, unconfirmed, and counted as neither
  right nor wrong above.
- **Zero declines on `source_title` for 8 identifiable clips is not proof of calibration** —
  the corpus was easy. The control question is what carries the finding, not this.
- **Timings came off a machine running other agents' work.** Individual OCR calls ranged
  0.85–2.24 s; medians are reported for that reason.
- **The 41% dedup figure is from 107 frames across 4 clips.** It will vary with how static a
  page's format is, and this corpus is unusually static.
- Nothing was implemented, as instructed.
