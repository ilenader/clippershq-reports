## Verdict

**BL-683's headline claim does not survive contact with real audio.**

| Claim | Synthetic (BL-683) | Real audio (this round) |
|---|---|---|
| Fabrication on no-step clips | **0/22 = 0%** | **19/19 = 100%** |
| Localisation within ±0.5s | 18/18 = 100% | **9/17 = 53%** |
| …on unmistakable steps only | — | **3/5 = 60%** |
| `shape` distribution | mixed (flat/ramp/spike/step) | **`step` on 36/36** |

The arithmetic BL-683 proved is real. What it did not prove — and what fails — is that the
arithmetic corresponds to what an editor would cut on. **The detector returned a timestamp
on every single one of 36 real clips, including all 19 where the envelope shows nothing to
cut on.** `NULL IS A FIRST-CLASS ANSWER`, per the module docstring, is currently unreachable
on real material.

**This detector should not ship in its current form.** Not because the maths is wrong, but
because on real audio it has no null.

## Method — how "by eye" was done, and its limits

Waveforms were rendered as RMS-dB envelopes (the module's own `envelope()`), six per page,
labelled only with the last 6 digits of the pk and the duration. **No detector marker, no
timestamp, no shape.** Those blind plots were reviewed and graded *before* the detector
output was opened, to avoid anchoring:

- **clear** — an unmistakable sustained level jump that holds
- **marginal** — a level change is arguably there, but not one to stake a cut on
- **flat** — no sustained up-step (uniform, or only dips, recoveries and ramps)

Result: **5 clear, 12 marginal, 19 flat.**

**The honest caveat, stated plainly:** this is a machine reading a rendered envelope, not a
human video editor watching a CapCut timeline. It is a materially better proxy than
synthetic ground truth — it is real music, judged without knowing the answer — and a
materially worse one than an actual editor. The fabrication result below is large enough
(100% vs 0%) that editor disagreement at the margins would not overturn it, but the 53%
localisation figure could move either way with a human grader.

Blind plots, marked plots, labels with per-clip notes, and raw detector output are all
committed alongside this report (see Assets).

## Agreement, where a step exists

Of the 17 clips where a step was marked (clear + marginal), **9 landed within ±0.5 s (53%)**,
median absolute error on those hits **0.30 s**.

The failures are not near-misses. They are gross:

| clip | eye | detector | error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 760030 | 2.7 s (near-silence → loud intro) | 50.99 s | **48.3 s** |
| 723159 | 8.5 s | 49.88 s | **41.4 s** |
| 163849 | 33.0 s | 53.29 s | **20.3 s** |
| 291659 | 41.5 s (flat bed → step, holds) | 27.35 s | **14.1 s** |
| 131735 | 3.5 s | 16.28 s | 12.8 s |
| 339612 | 30.0 s | 17.39 s | 12.6 s |

When it is right it is precise (median 0.30 s, well inside ±0.5 s). When it is wrong it is
not slightly wrong — it points somewhere else entirely. That bimodality matters downstream:
there is no "roughly right" mode to fall back on.

## Fabrication — the claim that degraded

**19 of 19 clips graded flat received a timestamp. 100%.**

Examples, with the detector's own confidence:

| clip | step_db | sep_db | confidence | eye |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 249750 | 17.14 | 10.59 | **1.000** | flat — floor ~-32 dB throughout, bursts only |
| 113528 | 13.32 | 7.15 | **0.980** | flat — brief plateau 12–16.5 s, not sustained |
| 624272 | 11.88 | 6.44 | **0.993** | flat — uniform |
| 671777 | 17.10 | 0.98 | 0.712 | flat — if anything a step *down* after 29 s |
| 029359 | 15.10 | 2.98 | 0.832 | flat — uniform, slight decline at end |

**Confidence is not merely uninformative, it is inverted.** Median confidence on eye-flat
clips is **0.712**; on eye-clear clips it is **0.758**. The single highest-confidence output
in the entire run — `1.000` on clip 249750 — is a clip with no visible step at all. A
downstream bot filtering on `confidence > 0.9` would select *preferentially* for fabrications.

---

## No threshold rescues it

A sweep over `min_step_db` ∈ {3…24} × `min_sep` ∈ {0…20} was run against the eye labels.
**No cell keeps ≥4 of 5 clear steps while nulling ≥15 of 19 flats.** The best combined point
(`min_step_db=18, min_sep=0`) nulls all 19 flats but keeps only **2 of 5** genuine steps.

The distributions genuinely overlap — this is not a tuning problem:

```
CLEAR  172311 step=25.00 sep=20.74      FLAT  249750 step=17.14 sep=10.59  conf=1.000
CLEAR  476545 step=20.51 sep= 4.29      FLAT  671777 step=17.10 sep= 0.98
CLEAR  291659 step=13.46 sep= 0.73      FLAT  029359 step=15.10 sep= 2.98
CLEAR  760030 step= 9.01 sep= 3.87      FLAT  688037 step=14.81 sep= 0.43
CLEAR  935884 step= 8.51 sep= 0.11      FLAT  960772 step=13.55 sep= 3.39
```

Three of the five real steps score *below* the loudest fabrication on `step_db`.

---

## Root cause — two findings, one of them about the corpus

**1. `MIN_SEP = 0.0` is not a gate on real audio.** The `sep` statistic (p25 after − p75
before) was designed as the transient killer, and on synthetic negatives it goes strongly
negative. On real music it is *slightly positive almost everywhere* — measured median 1.28 dB
on clips with no visible step, max 10.59. A gate of `sep > 0` therefore admits essentially
everything. This is why `spike_only` never fired: nothing was rejected.

**2. The corpus is dialogue, not music — and that is the deeper problem.** BL-682 established
these accounts post movie and TV scenes as `original_sounds` (`music_info` 0/108). Their
envelopes are speech-gated: deep troughs at every pause, recoveries after every silence. The
detector's `argmax` over sustained jumps reliably locks onto **the loudest recovery from a
dialogue pause**, which is arithmetically a real sustained step and editorially meaningless.

Most of the "marginal" grades in the label file are exactly this — *"quiet dip 15–17.5 s then
resumes; recovery, not a section change"*. That is the failure mode in one line.

BL-683's synthetic beds were stationary, so this class of confound could not appear. The
concern in the brief — *"a section change alters TIMBRE without necessarily altering LEVEL"* —
is real but turned out to be the *lesser* issue. The larger one is the reverse: **level
changes that are not section changes.**

---

## What would have to change

Not tuning. Three structural options, in order of cost:

1. **Make null reachable.** Require `sep` to clear a real margin (measured floor suggests
   ≥6–8 dB, not 0.0) *and* require the post-step level to hold for a multiple of the window.
   From the sweep this costs most of the true positives — meaning the honest output on this
   corpus is mostly `None`, which is the correct answer, not a failure.
2. **Gate on population before running it at all.** This detector assumes music with
   sections. Run it only where the audio is music — and per BL-682 that is a minority of
   this corpus. A speech/music discriminator would have to come first.
3. **Add the timbre view as a veto, not a bonus.** `flux_agree_s` is currently a +0.10
   confidence bonus that can never veto. On dialogue, spectral flux and RMS disagree at
   pause-recoveries; requiring agreement would kill a large share of the fabrications. This
   is untested and is the cheapest thing to try next.

## Limits

- **n=36 clips from 3 accounts**, all repost/clip pages posting dialogue-heavy scenes. A
  music-forward corpus would likely score better and must be tested before the detector is
  written off for *all* populations.
- Grading was done by machine vision on rendered envelopes, not by a human editor (see
  Method). The 100% vs 0% fabrication gap is robust to grader disagreement; the 53%
  localisation figure is less so.
- The detector was run **completely unchanged** — `scratch/energy_step.py` at its shipped
  defaults (`WIN_S=2.0`, `MIN_STEP_DB=3.0`, `MIN_SEP=0.0`, `RAMP_MAX_S=3.0`). No parameter
  was altered before measurement.
- Duration median (58.9 s) is from this 36-clip set only; `median_duration` is still
  populated on 0 of 56,173 master rows.
