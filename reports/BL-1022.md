## The headline: the operator's third command was a traceback

`python -m clippershq.outcome_loop analyse` **raised `AttributeError` on every invocation**,
including on an empty ledger, and has since the CLI was added:

```
  File "clippershq/outcome_loop.py", line 659, in _cli_analyse
    for name, r in sorted(res.items()):
AttributeError: 'list' object has no attribute 'items'
```

`analyse()` returns `{'metric', 'n_with_outcome', 'min_n_per_arm', 'questions',
'confounds'}`. `_cli_analyse` walked **that** dict as though it were the per-question map, so
`sorted(got.items())` handed `res` the alphabetically-first key — `confounds`, a list —
before a single line of output.

**`analyse()` itself was correct and covered.** Nothing exercised the command that reads it.
That is BL-972's shape one layer up: every piece of the loop built, committed, imported by
two modules and covered by two suites, and the operator had no working way to invoke it. The
sequence the runbook tells him to type is `export` → `import` → `analyse`; the third step
died.

Fixed, and the guards are **subprocesses** (`tests/test_outcome_first_data.py`), because an
in-process call would not have caught this and did not for however long it has been live.

## 1. The whole path, on a copy — `scratch/bl1022_roundtrip.py`, 33/33

265 render records copied out of the live ledger; 10 videos filled with realistic
log-normal-ish view counts, 1 row left deliberately blank, 1 filled with a genuine zero.

| proven | result |
|---|---|
| every metric and text field lands on the record it was typed against, **by `record_id`** | 10 records × 8 fields, **0 mismatches** — filled in reverse sheet order, so row position cannot be the reason |
| a **blank** row appends no outcome line | delta **0** |
| a blank row's metrics read `None`, **never `0`** | `views=None likes=None …` |
| a blank row is **offered again** next export | 265 → 255, the unfilled one still present |
| a genuine `0` **is** recorded | `views=0`, `has_outcome=True` |
| a metrics-only correction folds **per field** | `post_url`, `likes`, `posted_at` + 5 more survived |
| **negative control:** the old whole-line rule *would* erase them | highest-rev line carries only `['rev','views']` |
| a lower rev appended later does not win | `views` stays 31,200, not 999 |

### The per-field assertion is made through `resolve()`, and that is the whole point

BL-972's version of this test read the **rev-1 line off disk** — which append-only guarantees
will always be there — and never called `resolve()`. It passed for a reason unrelated to the
claim it made, while `resolve()` was in fact taking the highest-rev line *whole* and deleting
`post_url` on every routine view-count refresh. The feature and the thing that destroyed it
were the same keystroke. This round asserts through `resolve()` and adds the negative
control, so the test cannot pass without the behaviour.

### Two harness assertions were wrong, and the ledger corrected them

- **"the blank row appended no outcome line"** first read the *absolute* count and failed
  at 3. Those 3 are MEMEBOT-060 **join diagnostics** — `joinable`/`join_reason`, no metric —
  and one of them was written three times over. The right assertion is a **delta**. 22 of
  265 records carry such lines; a count that conflates them with numbers is the same
  `has_outcome` vs `has_numbers` confusion that once cut 22 videos out of the hand-entry
  sheet permanently.
- **"the rev-1 line still holds `post_url`"** failed because on a record that already carries
  join diagnostics the operator's **first import lands at rev 4**, not rev 1.

## 3. The statistics, stated in the output itself

Not in a report the operator will not open — in the block he sees and on the sheet he fills:

- **25 outcomes per arm** is the bar, and it clears only a **LARGE** effect
- a **MEDIUM** effect needs **~128 posts** per arm for a two-arm question
- **20 sends at 4–8% expects 1–2 replies**; the panel prints the expected range beside the
  observed count, because `5.0%` alone reads as a failure
- **`bias_map == {}` after the first import is CORRECT**, labelled as such in both surfaces

Arithmetic: `n ≈ 16/d²` per arm at 80% power, α=0.05 → d=0.8 → 25, d=0.5 → 64/arm (128 for
two arms), d=0.3 → 178.

## 4. The join survives fresh records — two windows, not one twice

Against the **live** `scratch/songs.json` (21 hook keys):

| fresh record | joins |
|---|---|
| `memebot/scratch/song01.mp3@0.427-18.701` | **yes** |
| `memebot/scratch/song02.mp3@0.36-9.981` | **yes** |

Two **distinct** windows on two **distinct** songs — re-proving one key shows the key still
works, not that the builder handles a window it has not seen. The suite adds a third window
on a known song, plus the refusal cases. Critically, `outcome_loop._grouper("hook")` and
`clip_pipeline.join_key_of` produce the **identical string** for both, which is the actual
failure mode: evidence attaching to a key nothing looks up.

The 48 historical outcome lines across 22 records remain marked `joinable: false`, every one
with a stated reason (`song is not in the store at all` ×30, `window is not one of its marked
hooks` ×11, `absolute song path` ×7).

through the bulk path end to end, not merely at the unit level where it was written.

`mark_bounced_from_csv` is called by **its own test suite and by nothing else** in the
shipping tree. No `[o]` menu entry, no CLI. It is BL-972's shape a third time. `docs/
AFTER_YOU_POST.md` therefore ships a working `python -c` one-liner (dry-run proven) and names
the gap; the fix is a fifth `[o]` entry, left to whoever holds `outcomes.py` next.
