## The headline number

MEMEBOT-006 asked whether mute-and-replace covers almost everything, and said that if it were 90% music-only the ducking work would barely be needed.

**It is not 90%. It is about half.**

| class | projected share of library | treatment |
|---|---:|---|
| **music-only** | **~52%** | mute-and-replace |
| **dialogue-over-music** | **~38%** | duck-under |
| **dialogue-only** | **~9%** | duck-under |
| silent | ~0% | mute-and-replace |

So **roughly half the library needs the ducking path**, and MEMEBOT-006's recommendation to build it was correct rather than redundant. The projection's basis and its considerable uncertainty are in §5 — it rests on 20 measured clips and should not be quoted as precise.

## 1. What was actually broken, and it was two separate things

`speech_frac` had a **producer** problem and a **contract** problem, and fixing either alone would have looked like it worked while changing nothing.

- **Producer (BL-846):** counter sites existed at `clip_runner.py:427/430` behind `if speech_fn is not None`, and the only caller forwarded `run()`'s own `None`. The stage reported `UNWIRED`, honestly, and could never move.
- **Contract (BL-847):** `build_record` was already *writing* `speech_frac`, but it was absent from `CLIP_FIELDS`, so `field()` returned `None` and no consumer was permitted to read it. **The value would have been on disk and still unreadable** — the mirror image of a declared-but-empty field, and invisible precisely because the data was there.

Both are closed. Read back through the consumer rule, on the real library:

```
  raw key present       : 20   readable via field(): 20
  -> no gap: every written value is readable
  speech_frac provenance: {'absent': 874, 'measured': 20}
```

## 2. The build — and why it is four classes, not two

`clippershq/clip_speech.py` fetches the clip's audio (a plain **unbilled** CDN GET of the `progressive_download_url` already in the payload the walk paid for) and runs a **local Silero VAD** via faster-whisper's `get_speech_timestamps`. **No Whisper model is loaded and nothing is downloaded at call time** — this is the pure VAD path, not `transcribe()`.

The critical design point is that **`speech_frac` alone cannot separate music-only from dialogue-over-music.** That is exactly the trap BL-742 fell into. A clip with dialogue over a score has a high `speech_frac` *and* is music. The second measurement is the **bed**: the level between the speech spans.

| | loud bed | quiet bed |
|---|---|---|
| **no speech** | music-only | silent |
| **speech** | dialogue-over-music | dialogue-only |

A test pins this directly — identical `speech_frac`, different bed, different class:

```python
def test_the_two_classes_speech_frac_alone_cannot_separate(self):
    a = CS.classify(0.5, -15.0, -14.0)   # dialogue-over-music
    b = CS.classify(0.5, -70.0, -14.0)   # dialogue-only
    self.assertNotEqual(a, b)
```

**`licensed_music` is classified for free.** Those clips carry no audio URL at all — they ARE music by declaration — so they are labelled from the declaration with no download, no VAD and no CPU. That is **39.9% of the library** exempted from the expensive path.

## 3. It is descriptive, and the suite now enforces that

Item 4 of the brief, and the thing most likely to be undone later by someone who sees a useful-looking float. BL-742 measured a **63.3% false-negative rate** when speech absence was used to infer music presence — **31 of 49 real music clips discarded**, silently.

Three tests hold the line:

- a clip survives **whatever** `speech_fn` returns (`0.0`, `0.5`, `1.0`, `None`)
- a `speech_fn` that **raises** loses the measurement, never the clip
- an **AST guard** fails the suite if `speech_frac` is ever compared with `<`, `>`, `<=` or `>=` outside `clip_library` (where `format` legitimately thresholds it) and `clip_speech` (the labeller)

**The guard caught my own first draft** — it flagged `clip_runner.py:434`. That turned out to be `speech_frac is not None`, a presence check for the counter, not a gate. The guard was over-broad, so I narrowed it to ordering operators and then **verified it still has teeth** by planting a synthetic `if speech_frac < 0.30:` and confirming it fires. A guard that cannot fail is worse than no guard.

## 4. Cost — measured, and 5× cheaper than the figure the brief carried

Measured over 60 real clips (`memebot/meme/downloads/`, 732 s of audio):

| stage | per clip |
|---|---:|
| decode (ffmpeg) | 0.305 s |
| VAD (Silero) | 0.242 s |
| **total** | **0.547 s** |

- **0.57 CPU-hours per 1,000** at the library's p50 clip length (46.0 s). MEMEBOT-006 projected 0.44 — close, and slightly optimistic.
- **BL-721 measured 2.70 s/clip.** This build is **~5× faster** because it skips transcription entirely. The brief's figure was right for the implementation it described; it is simply not the cheapest way to get this number.
- Backfilling the **528 `original_sounds`** clips that still lack it costs **0.08 CPU-hours** of local CPU — but also a **paid call each**, because the audio URL expires in ~105 hours (BL-787). The CPU is free; the re-fetch is not.

### A finding that was not asked for: the historical `speech_frac` numbers came from a looser instrument

Running this build against the **166 previously-analysed clips still on disk** (`bl694` + `bl701`), whose stored `speech_frac` came from the `transcribe(vad_filter=True)` path that BL-718, BL-721 and BL-742 all used:

```
  |new - old|   median 0.132   p90 0.487   max 0.868
  within 0.10:  42.2%      within 0.30: 77.7%
  same 3-way bucket under the OLD thresholds: 112/166 (67.5%)
```

The disagreement is **systematic, not noise**: the top ten disagreements are all old ≈ 0.95 → new ≈ 0.30. Whisper's transcription segments span the gaps between utterances, so that path **over-reports speech coverage**. Pure VAD is tighter.

I am **not** claiming mine is the correct one — there is no ground truth here, and I checked: the stored `cls` labels are a **pure threshold on the stored `speech_frac`**, so validating against them would be circular (their ranges are perfectly disjoint, which is how I found out). What is safe to say is that **`speech_frac` values in BL-718/721/742 are not comparable to values produced from today onward**, and any threshold inherited from those reports — including BL-742's 0.30 curve — should be re-derived, not carried over.

## 5. The real split, and how much of it is projection

**The live walk** — 60 accounts, 470 clips walked, 30 new stored, 120 paid calls, **$0.0534**:

```
    Stages entered (cheapest first):
      views          30
      content_genre  30
      valence        30
      layout         30
      speech_frac    30            <- was UNWIRED
      ocr            UNWIRED  (no counter site and no parameter; ...)

    Audio split (what each clip needs doing to it):
      music-only             14  (46.7%)  -> mute-and-replace
      dialogue-over-music    13  (43.3%)  -> duck-under
      dialogue-only           3  (10.0%)  -> duck-under
```

**30 clips classified, but only 20 carry a `speech_frac`** — and that reconciles exactly rather than being a shortfall: the other **10 were `licensed_music`, classified from the declaration with no measurement taken**. All 20 measured clips are `original_sounds`. 14 − 10 = 4 measured music-only, plus 13 plus 3 = 20. ✔

**Library composition** (895 clips, free, from a field already populated):

| `audio_type` | n | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| `original_sounds` | 528 | 59.0% |
| `licensed_music` | 357 | **39.9%** |
| absent | 10 | 1.1% |

**The projection.** On the 20 measured `original_sounds`: 20% music-only, 65% dialogue-over-music, 15% dialogue-only. Applying that to all 528 and adding the 357 declared:

```
  music-only           357 declared + ~106 projected  = ~463   ~52%
  dialogue-over-music                 ~343 projected  = ~343   ~38%
  dialogue-only                        ~79 projected  =  ~79    ~9%
```

**This is a projection from n = 20 and the uncertainty is large** — the 20% music-only rate carries a 95% interval of roughly 8–42%, which moves the library music-only share anywhere from ~44% to ~65%. What the interval does *not* span is 90%. The conclusion that **the ducking path is needed for a substantial minority-to-half of the library is safe; the specific percentages are not.**

## Honest limits

- **The library split is a projection from 20 measured clips**, stated with its interval in §5. It is enough to answer "is it 90%?" (no) and not enough to plan capacity from.
- **Silero VAD does not distinguish speech from singing.** A music-only clip with vocals can read as dialogue-over-music. This matters more here than in most VAD applications, because the corpus is music clips. It is unmeasured — I had no way to hand-label without listening, and did not.
- **The `dialogue-only` class is barely exercised.** Zero of the 60 local clips reached it; 3 of 20 on the walk and 8 of 166 on the older corpus did. `BED_PRESENT_DBFS = −45 dBFS` sits well below the observed bed range (min −29.4 on the local clips), so the boundary is **untested in the region where it decides**.
- **The cross-implementation disagreement is characterised but not adjudicated.** I can show the two instruments differ systematically; I cannot show which is right.
- **`clip_library.py` in this commit also carries BL-849's in-flight vision-field work.** Both rounds edited that file concurrently — BL-849 filed its claim on the same path after mine. Separating the hunks on a file another process was actively writing was the riskier option, so they are committed together; the suite is green with both present, and nothing of BL-849's was removed.
- **The walk's spend and the ledger delta disagree** ($0.0534 metered vs $0.0852 on `total_spent_usd`) because the ledger is shared and another round spent concurrently. This round's own metered figure is the $0.0534.
