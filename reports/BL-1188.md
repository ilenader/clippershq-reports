# BL-1188 — the creator rule, derived from his own marks; agreement 74 → 77 and pick accuracy 56% → 63%

**His biggest complaint had no rule at all.** 15 of his 100 marks say it in prose — *"this is a
person like a content creator this isnt meme page very bad"* — **all 15 are pages he wants
rejected**, and the filter passed 11 of them. That is **11 of the 36 rows he says it got wrong:
31% of all the disagreement, from one missing concept.**

This round derived that rule from the marks rather than inventing it, refused the version that
would have cost him rows 21 and 65, and found that the signal with the biggest raw separation
made the rule *worse*.

**Interpreter: every number below was produced by `.venv\Scripts\python.exe`, Python 3.11.9**
(bare `python` here is 3.13.14 and lacks the packages). Each script prints its interpreter at
run time. **Spend: $0.00 — no billed call was made; every input was already on disk.**

## 0. The field that is easy to read backwards, and I nearly did

`mark` in `ground_truth/review_marks.jsonl` is **not** "is this page good". It is **"was the
filter's verdict correct"**. Two figures prove it and neither works under the other reading:

| | |
|---|---:|
| `filter_verdict` = PASSED on | **31 of 100** → the 31.0% base rate |
| `mark` = GOOD on | **64 of 100** → the 64% agreement |

So his desired verdict is derived: `wants_pass = (filter said PASS) XOR (he marked it wrong)`.
Under the naive reading the file says "64 good pages", which matches nothing.

**103 lines, 100 rows.** Rows 18 and 24 were clicked more than once, seconds apart; last write
wins. Row 18 is kept as a **flagged conflict**: his prose says *"this is a person like a content
creator this isnt meme page very bad"* while his final click says the filter was right. That is
the words-vs-labels tension in one row, and it is reported rather than quietly resolved.

**The baseline, both halves:**

| | he wants PASS | he wants REJECT |
|---|---:|---:|
| **filter PASSED** | 13 | **18 wrongly picked** |
| **filter REJECTED** | **18 wrongly dropped** | 51 |

It passed **exactly 31 when he wants exactly 31** — a perfect-looking count — while only 13 were
the right pages. **Pick accuracy 41.9%.** A rate alone lies here.

## 2. The rule, derived — and what it refused to become

Measured over all 100 pages (`scratch/bl1188_signals.py`), the 15 creator rows against the 31
he wants:

| signal | the 15 creators | the 31 he wants |
|---|---:|---:|
| bio says repost/memes/credit/… | **0 of 15** | 35% |
| handle carries meme/clip/page/… | **0 of 15** | 19% |
| verified | 5 of 15 (33%) | 10% |
| display name is two words | 5 of 15 (33%) | 13% |
| **view/follower ratio, median** | **2.49** | **0.57** |

So: **no repost signal at all, AND behaves like a person.** Both halves are required — the first
alone rejects 70 of 100 and takes row 21 with it.

### The bar, and the honest answer to it

Base rate **31.0% [22.8, 40.6]** → a rule ships standalone only if its lower bound clears
**40.6**. Rejection counts at every threshold are in `scratch/bl1188_signals.json`. Summary:

| candidate REJECT rule | rejects | good lost | precision of survivors | 21/65 |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| IG `category` alone | 42 | 13 | 31.0% [20.6, 43.8] | **DROP / DROP** |
| creator-shape, v/f ≥ 1.5 | 39 | 7 | 39.3% [28.1, 51.9] | KEEP / KEEP |
| not-a-repost-page half alone | 70 | 17 | 46.7% [30.2, 63.9] | **DROP** / KEEP |
| is-a-person half alone | 50 | 12 | 38.0% [25.9, 51.8] | KEEP / KEEP |

**No creator rule clears the bar standalone**, and that is stated in the code beside the rule.
It ships because it is **additive** and moves agreement, which is the number that decides.
Quoting 39.3% as though it beat the base rate would be the exact error two earlier filters made.

Three *single* thresholds did clear the bar and all three were rejected on inspection:
`caption_len ≤ 400` (67.9% — but it judges the **caption**, which he said explicitly is not the
surface, and rejects 72 of 100), and `first_person_top` (59–70% — which is RULE 1's person
anchor under another name, so shipping it would double-count one signal).

> **A sweep pointed the wrong way is worse than no sweep.** The first pass swept `hashtags ≤ t`
> — rejecting pages that use *few* hashtags — when the medians say he wants the few-hashtag
> pages (1.0 vs 3.5). It reported the separation backwards. Directions are now derived from the
> medians, in code.

### Rows 21 and 65 — kept, not traded

Both are literally `Digital creator` in the IG category field, which is why BL-1187 refused to
ship category. **Both are KEPT.** Row 21 has no personal marks at all; row 65's bio matches the
repost vocabulary, so the first half never fires. `is_creator_page` **does not read `category`**,
and `test_bl1188_creator.py` fails if anyone reintroduces it.

### The strongest signal, measured and removed

The view/follower ratio has the largest separation in the table (2.49 vs 0.57) — and it made the
rule worse against his labels:

| variant | agreement | passes | pick accuracy | fires on |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| no creator rule (BL-1187) | 74 | 41 | 56.1% | 0 |
| + v/f ≥ 1.5, verified, name | 76 | 27 | 63.0% | **42** |
| + v/f ≥ 2.0, verified, name | 77 | 28 | 64.3% | 40 |
| **verified OR person-name** | **77** | **32** | **62.5%** | **22** |
| verified only | 77 | 36 | 61.1% | 13 |

Every variant lands at 76–77, so agreement cannot choose. What separates them is **how much they
destroy**: with the ratio the rule fires on 42 of 100 and costs 6 pages he wants; without it, 22
fires for the same agreement. **A signal that is real in the medians and useless in the labels
is a signal to drop.** The tie between the two survivors was broken on the concept itself —
`verified OR person-name` catches **7 of his 15 creator rows** against 5.

The ratio is still computed and reported in the rule's reason string, so a later round can
re-test it without re-deriving it. It just does not fire.

## 4. Every rule proven reachable, on every runner

`tests/test_rule_reachability.py` (18 tests) closes the class of bug behind `too_few_posts`
firing **zero times in 100 pages** — the parallel runner never passed `media_count` while the
sequential one did, so **which rules a run enforced depended on which runner launched it**.

1. **Reachability** — one test per rule constructs input that must trigger it. A rule with no
   such test fails the guard.
2. **Runner parity** — by AST, every shipping call site of `judge_page` must pass every
   rule-bearing keyword. The check understands `**bundle` forwarding by resolving the `dict()`
   it comes from, so the right pattern passes and a dropped keyword still fails.

**It caught two real defects on its first run**: four rules had no reachability test, and **two
call sites in `meme_finder.py` itself would have shipped the new rule silently off.** Both fixed.

`too_few_posts` now fires **7 times** on the 100. Firing counts after the change:

```
creator_page 22 | format_share 48 | low_views 14 | promotional 11 | too_few_posts 7
not_english 6 | news_media 3 | dialogue 2 | no_text 1 | thin_sample 1 | film_tv 0
```

**`film_tv` fires 0 times on these 100** — reachable (its unit test fires it) but never triggered
by this sample, despite film/TV being his 3rd-largest complaint at 7 rows. That is a real open
finding, not a pass.

One thing found on the way: `_SPEAKER_RE` is `MULTILINE`-anchored, so a two-speaker transcript on
**one line** is not detected as dialogue. That is why `dialogue` fires only twice.

**Stale cache: resolved.** Every verdict is recomputed from the posts; the stored `passed` is
loaded only to measure the drift. It **disagrees with fresh judgment on 21 of 100 pages** — the
size of the error that reading it would have introduced.
