```

## 4. The quotation-versus-assertion sweep — and the sweep made the same mistake

Three known instances: a `--force` guard firing on `# no --force, ever`; a ranked-runner test matching a docstring quoting the line it replaced; BL-890's header test flagging the five headers it had just corrected.

I swept every check in `tests/`, `tools/` and `clippershq/` that reads source text and asserts on it, classifying by whether it *can* tell an assertion from a mention.

**The first version reported 38 checked, 0 AT RISK** — because it counted "CONTEXT-AWARE" (the mere presence of a word like *mention*, *docstring*, *assertion* anywhere in the file) as mitigation. **That is the bug the sweep exists to find, committed by the sweep**: a keyword appearing in prose is not evidence the code does anything about it.

Tightened so only **parsing (AST)** or **stripping comments before matching** counts:

```
19 checked: 15 MITIGATED (parses or strips comments), 4 WEAK, 0 AT RISK
WEAK: clippershq/outcomes.py, clippershq/writer.py,
      tests/run_all.py, tests/test_quality_score.py

## 5. The disk-versus-HEAD split, recorded

`docs/CLAIMS.md` gains the reasoning verbatim:

> **A claim is a promise about code**, and the failure it prevents is BL-797's — work written, reported, then lost to `git reset --hard` because it was never committed. So a claim is verified against `git show HEAD:`, never the working tree, where *"I wrote it"* and *"it exists"* are indistinguishable.
>
> **A header is documentation about the manifest a person is reading**, and disk is what they read. Checking a header at HEAD would report a *corrected* caveat as a lie until somebody committed it — inverting BL-874's rule. A round fixing a stale caveat would be punished for the gap between the fix and the commit.

With the instruction not to "tidy" them into agreement: they answer different questions about different kinds of statement.

## Honest limits

- **I committed a manifest ahead of its code** and had to un-track it. Caught by the guard, in the round that documented the guard. The lesson is narrower than "be careful": *committing an untracked manifest is not the same act as committing a modified one*, and I treated eight paths as one operation.
- **Item 1 is untouched.** Second consecutive round blocked on `tools/claim.py`.
- **The one genuine grep-fragility is reported, not fixed.**
- **The sweep is a heuristic** and flattered itself until tightened. Even now it classifies by structural tells, not by correctness; a check that parses could still ask the wrong question.
