## 3. How a 19-second window is used — plainly

**It can never "place once in full", and nothing here pretends otherwise.**
`place_at_detail` refuses when `clip_len < 2 × hook_len`, so with `CEILING_S = 20.0` no hook
longer than 10.0 s can ever place once in full. A 19 s hook is far past that. Measured, not
argued: `strict_place_at_detail_ok` is **`false` on all 12** rendered clips, and every record
carries the reason string.

**What actually happens: the bed is aligned and it loops.** One continuous span of the song
opens at `33.71 − action_s`, so the drop sounds exactly on the action second. The span is cut
`clip_len + 1.0 s` long and looped only so the audio leg stays infinite — `-shortest` would
otherwise bound the *video* to a finite bed and truncate the picture. Because the period is
longer than the clip, **the first wrap lands past the end of the file and is never heard.**

**What it costs, and it is real.** The drop sits 17.71 s into a 19.0 s window, so the span
always runs past the window's end. Measured on the batch: `spill_before` **0.0 s on all 12**;
`spill_after` **2.23 – 13.71 s** (median 7.4 s). The spill runs into 35–48 s, which is the
continuation of the same drop section and is louder still — musically it is one unbroken
stretch — but it is outside the edges he marked, and that is the trade.

```
6.0 -11.3 | 7.0 -12.0 | 8.0 -11.5 | 9.0 -11.6 | 10.0 -11.7 | … | 14.0 -11.9
```

The build, the step, and no discontinuity anywhere after it.

### The correction

The brief expected that *"a 19-second window on an 8–20 second clip should make seams rare or
impossible"*. **The window length is not what does it.** Holding the bounds at 16.0–35.0 and
varying only the drop offset, under `MODE_WINDOW_LOOP` (BL-1226's rotation, the mode that can
seam at all):

| drop offset | seamless | seaming | seam seconds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.00** | **0 of 12** | **12** | 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 13 |
| 1.00 | 1 | 11 | 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 12 |
| 3.00 | 1 | 11 | 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 10 |
| 6.00 | 8 | 4 | 1, 3, 4, 7 |
| 9.00 | 10 | 2 | 1, 4 |
| 13.00 | **12** | 0 | — |
| **17.71** (shipped) | **12** | 0 | — |

The mechanism, exactly: `lead = min(offset, action)`, `delay = action − lead`, and
`t_seam = delay mod period`. The action second is bounded by the ceiling — `clip_len ≤ 20.0`
with `TAIL_GUARD_S = 1.0`, so `action ≤ 19.0`, and in this batch the largest was 13.0 s. An
offset at or above every achievable action second forces `lead = action`, hence **`delay = 0`,
hence no seam** — which is precisely BL-1229's control, *"the three clean ones were the three
with delay 0.0."* Here all twelve have delay 0.0.

So: **measuring the drop instead of assuming it is what bought the seamlessness.** Had I taken
the assumed 0.0, every one of the 12 would seam under the loop bed. Under the shipped
`MODE_CONTINUOUS` there is no rotation at all, so seams are impossible either way — but the
brief's stated reason was not the operative one, and a report that let that stand would leave
the wrong lesson in place.

## 6. Two things the code had backwards, and one bug I introduced and caught

**1. The comment that was refusing the rename.** `dropmark_routes.EDITABLE` said `hook_id` was
*"the key the outcome ledger joins on (`song_library.bias_map` groups on it)"*, and that was
the stated reason rename was not offered. It is **backwards**. `song_library.hook_key` is
`path@start-end` (`song_library.py:1031-1038`) and `bias_map` groups on exactly that
(`:1060`) — `hook_id` appears nowhere in it. The true dependencies are the other way round:

- **renaming** does not touch the outcome bias at all;
- **moving a bound** re-keys it, and the window's accumulated outcome evidence silently
  attaches to nothing.

`hook_id` is the join key for the *rotation* counter, `paste_batch._hook_uses`. So a rename
orphans rotation, which is recoverable — `rename_window` records `renamed_from` and the new
`paste_batch.fold_renames` reads through it, so the count follows the window. And a bounds edit
now returns `rekeys_outcome_bias: true` with a sentence saying so, because the old behaviour
was to say nothing and let the bias quietly stop firing.

**2. My own undo bug.** The first `_last_undoable` skipped only entries whose action was
`undo`, so pressing Undo twice re-targeted the *same* change; the second press tried to rename
a window back from an id that no longer existed and 404'd. Caught by `scratch/bl1241/ops.py` on
its first full pass. Fixed by recording the consumed journal **row index** — not `(ts, action)`,
because `ts` has one-second resolution and this round's own four deletes landed three to a
second. Regression test: `test_undo_twice_walks_back_two_changes`.

**3. A test landmine, in THREE suites, now removed.** Reducing song04 turned
`tests/test_bl1229_bed.py`, `tests/test_drop_placer.py` and `tests/test_place_at_fixed_target.py`
red. **None had a real regression in it.** All three read the operator's **live**
`scratch/songs.json` and asserted its 2026-08-01/08-12 constants — `sng_0004 h1` at
13.769–29.369, and twice over "the store has 21 windows, 4 of them placeable". Two of the three
docstrings openly invited the failure (*"if someone re-marks the windows this number moves"*),
and that was reasonable while re-marking meant hand-editing JSON.

It is not reasonable now. This round put add/edit/rename/delete on a page he is *meant to use*,
so the suite would go red every time he marks a window by ear — and a suite that reddens when
the operator uses a shipped feature is a suite he learns to ignore. That is a worse outcome
than a stale number in a report.

- `test_bl1229_bed.py` — the two historical tests now pin the 2026-08-12 window as a literal
  (`BL1229_SNG_0004_H1`). A past measurement must not move when he re-marks. A third test was
  added that keeps the *live* half by asserting the **property** — whatever he has marked today
  still yields a seamless continuous bed.
- `test_drop_placer.py` and `test_place_at_fixed_target.py` — both now assert the **invariant**
  they were really after (`placeable ⟺ hook_len ≤ CEILING_S/2 = 10.0`) over every live window,
