## WHAT KILLED THE JOB — named by evidence, not by guess

Four candidates were named. Three are **refuted by measurement**, and the fourth is confirmed
twice.

**A heartbeat process** was written that appends one line per second with **flush + fsync**, so
the last line on disk is the moment of death whatever kills it. It was launched two ways —
`nohup … &`, and the harness's own background mode — and left alone across many tool calls:

| arm | beats | last elapsed | outcome |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `nohup … &` | 601 | 619.6 s | **completed normally** |
| harness background | 601 | 619.7 s | **completed normally** |

So: **not reaped when a tool call returns. No watchdog at ten minutes. Not memory** — resident
size was flat throughout and neither process was touched.

**What is confirmed is the wall clock on the launching call.** A `--dry-run` pass wrapped in
`timeout 560` returned **`EXIT=124`** — the timeout command's own exit code — having reached
**link 11 of 35**. And while writing this report a plain `sleep 900` was itself moved to the
background by the tool at exactly 600 s. The render does **~50 s of real work per link even
with rendering skipped**, and 245 s per link with it, so 62 links is roughly two and a half
hours. **Any wrapper shorter than that kills the batch mid-flight, at whatever link the clock
happens to reach — and "killed at link 28 of 62" is precisely what that looks like.**

> **And the reason nobody could see it: Python buffers stdout when it is redirected.**
> `scratch/bl1323_rerun_log.txt` is **0 bytes** and `scratch/bl1323_rerun2_log.txt` holds one
> line, `EXIT=1`. The evidence was in a buffer that died with the process. Every subprocess in
> this round runs under `-u`, which is why the dry-run log is full of detail and the cause was
> visible in a single pass.

**Not one of the two prior runs was killed by a watchdog, the vendor, or memory. `taskkill` was
never run in this round.**

## THE FIX: DEATH IS NOW CHEAP INSTEAD OF IMPOSSIBLE

`clippershq/paste_batch.py` renders a batch in one call and has **no resume** — a grep finds a
comment and nothing else. So rather than trying to make a two-hour job unkillable, the unit of
loss was made smaller than the unit of work.

`scratch/bl1328_resume.py` renders **one link per `paste_batch` invocation** and writes
`_resume.json` with **flush + fsync + atomic replace after every single link**. A kill can now
cost at most the link in flight. It ran to completion — but if it had died at link 20 the next
pass would have started at link 21.

**It never re-bills.** A link that reached a verdict — rendered *or* physically refused — is
finished, and re-running it would buy the same resolve again for nothing. The 22 already on
disk and the 5 already refused were read from the previous batch record and never touched.

**The zip is written on demand, not on completion** — `--zip` rebuilds it from whatever is on
disk at that moment, so a partial batch still hands you something openable.

### The drawn line, and why "mismatch" is not the same as "defect"

`readback_matches` is false on some clips, so every difference was **classified** rather than
counted:

| verdict | clips |
|---|---:|
| **identical** | **39** |
| space-only — the OCR collapses spaces; the pixels are not implicated | 5 |
| case-only after ignoring spaces | 4 |
| confusable glyphs only, 1 character | 2 |
| **ellipsis added** | **0** |
| **truncated** | **0** |
| **words differ** | **0** |

Clip 1 is the shape: his line ends `instead of us:`, the read-back says `instead ofus:` — one
space. This project's OCR **drops spaces on 44.7% of caption lines** (BL-1318), so a space-only
difference is the reader failing, not the renderer drawing the wrong words. A difference in
letters would be the opposite, and there are none. The ellipsis case is separated out on
purpose, because clip 41 once shipped `In The Hobbit (2014)...` for a three-line source — a
truncation that reads plausibly is the dangerous kind. **There is not one in this batch.**

### AAC coverage by frame count — and a rule that cannot be met on short clips

Coverage is `decoded AAC frames × 1024 ÷ sample rate` over the video duration. **The
container's declared duration is never used as the numerator.** 45 of 50 land inside
99.8–100.2%. Five do not, and they are all worth one sentence each:

| n | shortcode | coverage | short by | one AAC frame is |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | DbDPnKQs2sd | 99.78% | 21.7 ms = **0.93 frames** | 0.24% of the clip |
| 35 | DbYO4D0RxCH | 99.78% | 19.0 ms = **0.82 frames** | 0.26% |
| 40 | DaDlsPVBo_D | 99.72% | 22.3 ms = **0.96 frames** | 0.29% |
| 46 | DblWOFBox54 | 99.79% | 21.3 ms = **0.92 frames** | 0.22% |
| 54 | DVUjllKjeFp | 99.72% | 22.3 ms = **0.96 frames** | 0.29% |

**Every one is short by less than a single AAC frame**, and on clips this short one frame is
0.22–0.29% — **wider than the entire ±0.2% tolerance.** You cannot emit a fraction of an AAC
frame, so on any clip under about eleven seconds the band is sub-frame and unreachable by
construction. Nothing is faded, looped or missing; this is the encoder's tail quantisation.
**The rule would be better written as "within one AAC frame" than as a percentage** — as a
percentage it will keep reporting false failures on exactly the shortest clips.

### What was NOT measured from the shipped file, said plainly

Four checks are render-time instruments and cannot be recovered from a muxed mp4: drawn pixels
outside the strip, username-bar coverage, source-ink coverage and seam, and dead canvas
measured **on magenta**. The renderer records each per clip and those records are carried in
`scratch/bl1328_verify.json`; the shipped-file equivalent for canvas — aspect against the
source — **was** measured, on all 50. A verifier that reported a clean result it never computed
would be worse than no verifier, so these are listed as not measured rather than passed.

## TWO DEFECTS OF MY OWN, BOTH CAUGHT HERE

**1. The zip swallowed itself and reached 17 GB.** `make_archive` was pointed at
`FINAL/<name>.zip`, which makes the destination a member of its own source: the walk reaches
the part-written archive and compresses it into itself. Caught on the file listing, deleted,
and fixed — the archive is now written to the **parent** directory and any stale copy inside
the folder is removed first. The correct zip is 399.4 MB.

**2. The resume table dropped fields and made good clips look broken.** `render_one` copied a
hand-picked subset of each item and silently omitted `ruler`, `type_target_px`, `bed_seamless`,
`bed_spill_*` and `hook_choice_honoured` — so the verifier read them as absent and reported
clips as failing checks the renderer had passed. Fixed to keep the whole item, and
`scratch/bl1328_backfill.py` repaired the 35 already-rendered links **from files already on
disk, adding zero billed calls.**
