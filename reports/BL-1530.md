## THE VERDICT FIRST

**The button works, and the shape of it was decided by a defect nobody had named.** The obvious
implementation, a loop over the existing clip rejection route, **would have banned the payee and then made
the owner's own payout refuse itself with a 409.** That is BL-826's permanent jam arriving by a new road, and
it is the reason this action does not call that route at all.

Three defects in this round's own code were caught before it shipped, each by a different reviewer, none by

## PART 0 — exactly which clips, and why the narrow one

**Scope is `PayoutRequest.clipIdsSnapshot` and nothing else.** Not every clip on the campaign, and not every
clip the person ever posted.

`clipIdsSnapshot` is written once, at request time, at `payouts/route.ts:1079`, as the de-duplicated union of
the clipper's own clips on that campaign and the marketplace clips they created. It is never rewritten. It is
the only stored link from a payout to the clips behind it.

**Why not wider.** The owner is judging THIS payout: he looked at these views and decided they were bought.
Clips on other campaigns are money out of other budgets he has not looked at, and a clip submitted AFTER the
request was made was never part of what he is refusing. **The owner's intent about other campaigns is not
clear from the brief, so this is the narrow version**, and widening is a second deliberate press on their
next payout rather than a silent extension of the first.

### The null case is REFUSED, not widened, and that is the whole point

`admin/payouts/[id]/adjust/route.ts:240` handles a null snapshot by falling back to **every approved,
non-frozen clip that user has on that campaign** — including clips submitted after the payout. Inheriting
that fallback here would reject clips the payout never funded, silently, on the one action whose entire risk
is scope.

**MEASURED against production:** 222 payout rows, **179 carry a snapshot, 43 are null, and ZERO of the 43 are
in a status this button can act on.** Refusing on null blocks nothing that exists today and fails closed.
Proven live: a payout with a null snapshot returns 400 `NO_CLIP_SNAPSHOT`, and **not one of the three approved
clips on that campaign was touched.**

### The scale this had to be designed for

| live payouts with a snapshot | clips in it |
| --- | --- |
| APPROVED, 1 row | **642** |
| REQUESTED, largest | 188 |
| PAID, largest ever | 251 |

The worst live case is **642 clips carrying $1,460.11 of earnings, against $491.54 already paid to that
clipper on that campaign.** That single row is why nothing here fits in one transaction and why the paid
question is not academic.

### What happens to a clip already paid for

Its status becomes REJECTED and its earnings go to zero, exactly as a single rejection does today.
**Nothing is clawed back, and that is arithmetic rather than a promise:**

- `effectivePaidOut` (`balance.ts:234-258`) bounds every campaign at `min(paidGross, payableEarnings)` at
  `:256`. Zeroing the clips drives that campaign's payable to zero, so what is SUBTRACTED goes to zero too.
  Money already paid stops offsetting instead of eating into anything else.
- `available = max(approvedEarnings - effectivePaidOut - locked, 0)` at `:320-321` floors at zero, which is
  BL-627's property.
- `paidOut` is computed from payout rows, never from clips, so the clipper is still shown every dollar they
  received.

**`balance.ts` was not edited. The floor already existed; this round proves it holds.**

### The floor, proven by breaking it

A guard nobody has watched fail proves nothing. `scripts/sandbox/bl853-floor-demo.ts` builds a clipper with
**$120 on campaign A of which $100 is already PAID, and $80 on campaign B that nobody is rejecting**, then
rejects campaign A's clips with the real function and runs three times.

| run | `effectivePaidOut` | available after | result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | the bound intact | **$80.00** | 4 passed, 0 failed |
| 2 | **`min()` REVERTED to `total += paid`** | **$0.00** | **3 passed, 1 FAILED, exit 1** |
| 3 | restored | **$80.00** | 4 passed, 0 failed |

`balance.ts` restored and verified **byte-identical by blob OID** (`81a683c1…` on both sides).

**The demo states a distinction the obvious reading gets wrong, and states it in its own output.** Case 1,
"the balance never goes negative", PASSES WITH THE BOUND REMOVED TOO, because `computeBalance` floors at zero
regardless. That is BL-627's property, not BL-824's. What BL-824's per-campaign bound actually prevents is a
payment made against one campaign reaching across and eating another's earnings, and that is case 2: without
it the clipper loses $80 on a campaign nobody rejected. **A demo that only asserted case 1 would have been a
guard that could not fail**, which is exactly what BL-835's guard demo was caught doing.

## PART 1 — one action, the blast radius, and how it behaves when it breaks

`GET /api/payouts/[id]/reject-botted` returns the blast radius and **writes nothing at all**. Proven: a
sha256 fingerprint over every clip and payout in the world is byte-identical across the call.

**The figures shown before he confirms**, each recomputed independently in the harness and asserted, not
merely checked for being non-zero:

| shown | live example from the proof |
| --- | --- |
| Clips to reject | 14 |
| Already rejected | counted and excluded |
| Earnings removed | the exact sum over the clips that will change |
| Already paid on this campaign | $100.00 |
| Their balance now, after, and the drop | through the real `computeBalance` |
| A note when the record ends below money paid | fired, and asserted true |

**Every figure comes from the same helper the balance itself uses.** BL-728's rule, quoted in the route's own
header: a blast radius computed by a different rule than the gate would be worse than no number.

**The confirmation is typed.** The phrase is `fake <count>`, derived from the target so it differs every time
and cannot become muscle memory, and distinct in shape from the three phrases already on that page (the void
amount, `below <amount>`, and a username). The owner's optional note sits ABOVE the phrase field, never
below, so the last thing before the button is the thing that unlocks it.

### Atomic where it matters, resumable everywhere else

One clip rejection is about seven database round trips. 642 of them do not fit in one interactive
transaction: Prisma's default budget is 5 seconds and the payout review route sets no timeout at all.

1. **The payout is rejected first, in its own short transaction.** That is the half that must never be half
   done. The reverse order would leave a pile of rejected clips under a live payout request.
2. **The clips follow, one short transaction each, with absolute writes.** This is
   `campaign-freeze-undo.ts:542-546`'s settled answer for this exact shape, in its words: the failure mode is
   slower, not half-frozen.

**THE HALF DONE STATE, NAMED EXACTLY.** The payout is REJECTED. Some clips are REJECTED with the code and the
reason; the rest are untouched and still APPROVED. **No clip is half done**, because each is its own
transaction. The clipper's balance is correct at every instant. **And pressing the button again finishes it**,
because every write is absolute rather than a delta, so a done clip is skipped and an undone one is
completed. No job table, no double counting. Proven: the second press returns 200 with `rejected: 0`,
`already` equal to the clip count, and **no clip's `reviewedAt` moved.**

## PART 2 — the reason, stored consistently

**A new column, `Clip.rejectionCode`, nullable, additive**, applied through `run-schema-sql.js` and never
`prisma migrate`. NULL on every human-typed reason, which is all 413 distinct strings BL-775 measured and
every row that existed before this round.

**Why a column and not a magic string.** The owner may append his own note, so an exact match on the sentence
would break the moment he does. A separate code is exact, queryable, and leaves the 413 human strings
untouched and unclassified.

The stored sentence is a constant:

> The views on this clip did not come from real people, so it was not paid.

**The note is appended and never replaces it.** Proven: with a note set, the stored reason starts with the
constant, contains the note, and `rejectionCode` is still exactly `VIEW_BOTTING`.

**The clipper sees it plainly**, rendered as `Reason: <sentence>` on the clips page
(`clips/page.tsx:476-477`) and on the clip card (`ClipCardNew.tsx:512`). It says what happened and it does
not call anybody a fraud.

## PART 3 — what the clipper experiences

**Their clips turn red with a reason on each one, their balance drops, and they get one notification.** One,
for one action, not one per clip.

**Is a botted rejection distinguishable to them from any other rejection? No, and that is stated rather than
implied.** The clip card renders the reason text and nothing else; there is no badge, no colour and no label
separating it. The only difference they can see is the wording of the sentence.

**The earnings page.** BL-818's existing block explains money that left the balance because clips STOPPED
earning, and tells the reader it "is from finished campaigns". **That copy does not fire for this case and is
therefore not misleading**: it is gated on `videoUnavailable && status === "APPROVED"`
(`api/earnings/route.ts:224`), and a bot-rejected clip is REJECTED with `videoUnavailable` false. **It is
silent, and silence is its own problem on the page where somebody watches their balance fall.** So this round
adds its own sentence beside it, never folded into it, gated on a count of clips carrying the code.

**A count and deliberately no dollar figure.** The rejection zeroed `Clip.earnings`, so by the time that page
loads there is nothing left to add up; the amount lives in the audit row the action wrote. Inventing a figure
would mean deriving it from a different source than every other number on that page.

## PART 4 — the sandbox, and the defect that decided the design

**75 checks, 0 failures**, twice, by real HTTP request against the production build with real minted cookies
and `DEV_AUTH_BYPASS=false`.

### THE FINDING: the obvious implementation would have jammed the payout it was rejecting

`clips/[id]/review/route.ts:1476` calls `checkAndApplyRejectionPenalty`. That helper counts REJECTED clips in
a rolling window (`excessive-rejections.ts:96-103`) and at the threshold writes a `MarketplaceStrike`
carrying a `bannedUntil`. **That strike is read by `isUserMarketplaceBanned`, which is exactly what BL-851's
payee standing gate consults at `payouts/[id]/review/route.ts:195.`**

So a loop over the clip review route would ban the person within the first few clips and then refuse the
owner's own payout with a 409, for a decision he made once. **And the clip count had no reason filter at all**
— the cascade exclusion BL-849 added applies only to marketplace submissions — so even without calling the
helper, the next ordinary human rejection would have swept all 642 into the window and banned them
retroactively.

**Closed in two places, because one would only hold until the next caller.** This action never calls the
penalty helper, and `excessive-rejections.ts` now excludes `rejectionCode = VIEW_BOTTING` from the clip count,
matched on the code so the exclusion survives the owner appending a note. **One button press is one judgement
about one payout.** Counting it 642 times is the same error BL-849 fixed on the marketplace side, in its own
words: a cascade is an event, not a judgement. The owner can still ban this person deliberately.

**Proven: zero marketplace strikes after 20 clips were rejected in one sitting; still zero after an ordinary
human rejection immediately afterwards; and the clipper ACTIVE with both marketplace ban stamps NULL.**

### The five failure paths, each run

| path | result |
| --- | --- |
| **a clip already paid for** | rejected, earnings zeroed, `paidOut` unchanged at $140, the PAID payout row untouched and still stamped `paidAt`, the campaign record left below money sent, **and the balance floored rather than going negative** |
| **a clip on a completed campaign** | rejected like any other. There is no campaign-status branch anywhere in this path, and the harness asserted coherence rather than forcing an expectation |
| **a clip already rejected** | counted in `already`, **its human reason and NULL `rejectionCode` NOT overwritten**, and the blast radius told the owner in advance it would not change |
| **a payout already paid** | 400 `INVALID_STATUS`, payout still PAID and still stamped, **no clip touched**, and the GET says so in advance with `canRun: false` |
| **an interruption partway** | a clip deleted mid-run is counted honestly in `missing`, the payout is still REJECTED, every other clip completes, and a second press finishes cleanly |

**No out-of-scope clip was touched.** Four clips on the same clipper and the same campaign, deliberately left
out of the snapshot, are **byte-identical including `updatedAt`, with `rejectionCode` still NULL** — the new
column was written nowhere it was not asked to be.

**No real clipper was touched.** Every fixture is `isTestUser` on an `isTestCampaign`, minted through
`sandboxId()` and ledgered at creation. Live coded rejections in production: **0**. Earnings invariant: **0
violations**.

## THREE DEFECTS IN THIS ROUND'S OWN CODE, CAUGHT BY THREE DIFFERENT REVIEWERS

**1. The resume path was a lie.** The result copy promises "press the button again to finish the rest", and
the status guard refused anything not in REQUESTED, UNDER_REVIEW or APPROVED. **After the first press the
payout IS REJECTED, so the second press was a 400** and the `payoutAlreadyRejected` branch written for exactly
that case was unreachable. Found by the accessibility review reading the copy against the guard. REJECTED is
now an accepted entry status.

**2. The preview disagreed with the act.** The blast radius counted a clip as already rejected from its
STATUS; the executor skipped it only when the status AND the machine code both matched. **So a clip a human
had rejected earlier, carrying their typed words and a NULL code, was shown as "will not change" and then
changed**, overwriting somebody's reason with the machine sentence. Found by the harness author while writing
the test, who asserted the brief's expectation rather than the implementation. Already rejected now means
already rejected, whoever did it and for whatever reason.

**3. The resume control did not render.** The server was fixed to accept a second press; the row still
offered the button only on the three live statuses, and a run leaves the payout REJECTED. **The sentence
pointed at a control that did not exist.** Found by the render pass, measured on screen: 3 rejected rows, 0
carrying it. A rejected row now carries its own control, labelled "Fake views: finish clips".
