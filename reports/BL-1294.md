# 1. THE WAY IT WORKS TODAY — INSTAGRAM, FOR 1,000 GOOD PAGES

## The walk

Imagine a conveyor belt. Pages come along it, and at each station something happens.

> **Station 1 — FIND.** Instagram is asked *"who is posting under this hashtag?"* One question
> comes back with about 18 accounts, so one account costs one eighteenth of a question.
> **0.043 questions per page. Free-ish: $0.00004 a page.**
>
> **Station 2 — IS IT FRESH?** A free check on data already in hand. On Instagram this station

>
> **Station 3 — BUY THE PROFILE.** *"Who is this?"* Follower count, biography, whether there is

> on **every single page**, before anyone has looked at anything. **1.000 question per page.**

> purchase** — same account, different question, no cache. It is skipped only when the profile
> already said the account is empty, which is 12 pages in 100. **0.880 questions per page.**

> covers. **Instagram is not charged.** $0.00.

## ⚠️ A CORRECTION, MEASURED TODAY, TO THE STORY I WAS ASKED TO TELL

**I was asked to explain that TikTok is cheaper because "stale pages die before they buy capture
or profile". BL-1291 measured exactly that this evening and it is false.** Its report landed
while this one was being written, so I am reporting the measurement rather than the premise.

| the 62-day freshness gate | with it | without it |
|---|---:|---:|
| **dollars per 1,000 wanted** | **$1.94** | **$1.94 — identical** |
| calls per wanted page | 3.23 | 3.23 |
| pages judged per wanted | 2.02 | 2.10 |
| million tokens | 17.29 | 17.97 |
| **wanted pages it destroys** | **34 of 84 = 40.5%** [30.6, 51.2] | — |

**The gate saves no money at all.** It buys **3.9% of the token bill** and costs **40.5% of the
pages he wants**. The want rate even *falls* through it, 0.344 → 0.312. And it is not an artefact
of one sample: inside one stratum it kills 4 of 12 wanted, inside the other 30 of 70, and the
median days-since-newest-post is **10.5 for WANT against 10.4 for REJECT** — staleness barely
separates the two populations at all.

**So the gate reduces absolute throughput, not waste.** BL-1291's own sentence is the right one:
*"TikTok is cheap because its want rate is high and its call count is low — not because of this
gate."*

**Why the dollars do not move:** capture and profile are already bought only where they are
needed. Dropping a page at Station 2 saves its *judging*, not its purchases — because on TikTok
nothing has been purchased for it yet beyond its share of the search. **That is the real lesson,
and it is the same lesson as §3:** the saving lives in *when you buy*, not in *how many pages you
throw away early*.

*(An independent check that both of us are computing the same funnel: BL-1291's row A1 reads
3,533 walked / 2,022 judged / 3,231 calls / $1.94 / 17.29 M tokens / 5.2 h. My `bl1294_walk.py`,
written before I read it, gives 3,533 / 2,022 / 3,230 / $1.94 / 17.3 M / 5.2 h. Two rounds, two
implementations, one funnel.)*

## So why IS TikTok three times cheaper?

**Two reasons, and neither is the freshness gate.**

**Reason one: the want rate. 49.5% against Instagram's 19.0%** — two and a half times as many
pages are ones you want, so you walk far fewer to collect 1,000.

**Reason two, and it is the one that transfers: TikTok buys the profile LAST.** 3.23 questions per
*wanted* page, because the only expensive purchase happens after something has looked at the page.
**That is precisely the change §3 proposes for Instagram.**

**And underneath both sits the reason Instagram cannot copy reason one for free:**

| | TikTok discovery | Instagram discovery |
|---|---|---|
| follower count | **free on all four endpoints, real values** | **absent — no count of any kind** |
| post count | **free on 3 of 4** | **absent** |
| biography — the email surface | **free on 3 of 4** | **absent** |
| recency | `create_time` **65/65**, median **84.94 days**, **67.7% older than 62 days** | `taken_at` 27/27, median **0.18 days**, **0 of 27** older than 62 days |

*(BL-1287, and the recency row is the one that matters.)* **The same field name, opposite value.**
Instagram's hashtag endpoint returns *recent* posts by construction, so its date field is 100%
present and tells you **nothing** — every page looks fresh because only fresh pages are returned.
TikTok's search is not sorted that way, so two thirds of what it returns is genuinely stale and
can be dropped for free. **Instagram's Station 2 is empty because Instagram's answer has no number
in it to gate on.** That is not effort anyone can apply.

**And a second free gift, which is money rather than tokens: TikTok's biography is free on three of its four discovery endpoints.** On
`/v1/user/suggested/by/username` the bio comes back non-empty on **118 of 144 = 81.9%**, and
carries an address outright on **11 of 144 = 7.6%** — against **11.1%** from the paid profile
call. **You can get two thirds of the email yield for nothing.** And nothing in `clippershq/`
calls that endpoint at all.

**So TikTok already does the thing §3 proposes for Instagram.** It buys the profile last, only
for winners. **$1.94 already clears your $3.00 ceiling, and no reordering there would save a
cent** — the ordering win has already been collected.

**What IS still on the table for TikTok is plan capacity, not money.** BL-1291 measures
retiring the freshness gate and adding a share-per-play signal at **17.29 → 11.81 M tokens
(−32%)** and **5.2 → 3.6 judging hours**, with dollars unchanged at $1.94 and 40.5% of wanted
pages given back. That is a different decision from the one this report is about, and it is
BL-1291's to make — noted here only so the two are not confused.

## Why this is a safe number to trust

**It rests on counting purchases, not on estimating a rate.** Every other saving anyone has

a quarter of where you already are. It has four times the headroom it needs.

## Is it safe? It has already been tested

The obvious worry is: *if the judge does not have the biography and the follower count, does it get

**And the finding that matters most: the shipped free judge never receives those fields anyway.**

# 4. THE THING YOU HAVE ASKED FOR TWICE — JUDGE FEWER PAGES

You want the judge to see only the uncertain middle. **This was built and measured. Here is where
it honestly stands.**

The idea is three buckets: **CLEARLY BAD** (throw away in code, never judged), **CLEARLY GOOD**
(keep in code, never judged), and **THE MIDDLE** (judged). Tuned on one set of 474 pages, scored
**once** on a completely separate set of 100 that shares **zero handles** with it.

| bucket | tuned (474 pages) | **held out (100 pages)** |
|---|---|---|
| **CLEARLY BAD — dropped in code** | 25 of 474 = **5.3%** | 1 of 100 = **1.0%** |
| ↳ **wanted pages lost** | **0** | **0** |
| **CLEARLY GOOD — kept in code** | **0** | **0** |
| **THE MIDDLE — still judged** | 449 = **94.7%** | 99 = **99.0%** |

> ### **On today's data the confident buckets hold 5.3%, and the judge still sees about 95%.**

**Two rules survived**, and only two: a page whose profile did not render or whose grid is empty,
and a page with 5 or fewer items in the discovery answer — too few to *be* a repost page. Twenty-five
fires, twenty-five correct rejects, **zero wanted pages killed**. A follower floor was tried and
**measured out**: on the tuning set a floor kills a wanted page at *every* threshold that fires on
more than one account.

## Why CLEARLY GOOD came out empty — and this is a measurement, not laziness

The best free signal available was long captions: `cap_mean_len >= 1200` fires on 22 pages at
**77.3% precision**.

**77.3% looks shippable and is not.** It was measured on a review sheet whose base want rate is
**49.4%**. Your production base rate is **19.0%**. Carrying the lift across gives about
**30% precision in production** — meaning **roughly seven in ten pages in that bucket would be
pages you do not want**, arriving on your send list **having never been looked at by anyone**.

A second, independent check says the same thing: the model's discriminating power collapses from
0.727 on the tuning set to **0.597** on the held-out set — barely better than a coin.

## What the 5.3% is worth, if it were wired

| | pages judged | tokens | saving |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| today | 5,263 | 45.0 M | — |
| with the drop bucket at its tuned 5.3% | **4,984** | **42.6 M** | **2.4 M tokens, 0.7 h** |
| with it at the held-out 1.0% | 5,211 | 44.5 M | 0.45 M tokens, 0.1 h |

**And it is not running.** `clippershq/triage.py` has **no caller** — verified today across
`clippershq/`, `tools/` and `dashboard/`. Even the 5.3% is on the table, not on the belt.

## What WOULD change this

**One thing, and it is not tuning.** The confident buckets are small because Instagram's discovery
answer contains almost nothing to be confident about — **no follower count, no post count, no
biography**. You cannot sort on numbers you were not given.

**Two things that would genuinely move it:**

* **Read the profile page you are already photographing.** The browser is on the page. Pulling
  `bio` and `full_name` out of it is free, and it would make every biography-based signal testable
  on held-out data — which today it is not, because the field does not exist in the data set.
* **Change what discovery returns**, i.e. find pages through an endpoint that carries counts. That
  is a discovery project, not a filter project.

## Does TikTok differ? Yes — completely

**TikTok's free data is far richer, and a three-bucket split there is a genuinely different
proposition.** Its discovery answer already carries the follower count on **all four** endpoints,
the post count on **3 of 4**, and the biography on **3 of 4 at 81.9%**. It already drops
