## 3. Is it worth it? Buy the media. It will not settle the question.

**No, spending on media does not unblock a real answer** — but it costs so little that this is
not a trade-off, so the honest recommendation is *do both, and do not confuse them*.

### The instrument has never been established

| judge | n | postable | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| BL-1083 | 176 | 103 | **59%** |
| MEMEBOT-074 | 30 | 3 | 10% |
| MEMEBOT-119 | 30 | 10 | 33% |
| BL-950 | 24 | 0 | **0%** |

And the agreement evidence behind those numbers is thinner than the numbers suggest:

```
POOLED over ALL overlapping judgements
  n = 24   raw agreement = 70.8%   kappa = 0.391
  95% CI on raw agreement: [51%, 85%]   <- chance level here is 52%
```

**The entire agreement evidence base in this project is 24 overlapping judgements.** Pairwise:
`BL-1083 x BL-950` n=5 κ=0.000, `x MEMEBOT-074` n=3 κ=0.000, `x MEMEBOT-086` n=2 κ=0.000,
`x MEMEBOT-119` n=13 κ=0.435, `BL-950 x MEMEBOT-074` n=1. Only **24 of 243 judged clips (9.9%)**
have ever been seen by two judges.

The lower bound of that interval sits **below chance**. A κ of 0.391 on n=24 is not a
measurement of judge agreement; it is a rumour with a decimal point.

### So what does unblock it

More labels at κ≈0.39 buy nothing: any pooled significance is a judge artifact, which is
exactly why the per-account ranking came back at chance and 17 of 20 fields flip sign between
audits. Those were never data problems.

**BL-1085 is landing the fix right now** — `ground_truth/RUBRIC.md` v2, written *from the seven
recorded disagreements* rather than from first principles, with `rubric_version` per row so v1
rows are never silently pooled with v2. That is the correct instrument fix and it costs $0.

**Recommendation:** take **option B ($0.30, 2.3 GB)** — media is a precondition for any future
judging and is effectively free. Then judge under the v2 rubric, and **deliberately overlap**:
9.9% double-judged is why nothing is decidable. A hundred clips judged twice under a shared
rubric is worth more than two thousand judged once.

Spending on media buys *material*. It does not buy *agreement*, and only agreement makes any of
it mean anything.

## 4. The two count defects

### Defect 1 — the provenance block poisons every text scan (REAL)

Every clip row carries a `provenance` sub-object keyed by the **same field names** as the clip,
whose values are the literal words `declared` / `absent` / `derived` / `measured`. A scan for
`"clip_id": "..."` returns a clip whose id is `declared`; a scan for `"account"` returns
accounts named `declared` and `absent`.

`read_all` + `field()` are immune. Anything built on grep is not. Pinned by three tests that
assert the naive route **does** produce the ghost, so they cannot pass vacuously.

### Defect 2 — I was wrong. This one is my harness, not the product.

BL-1094 reported that last-row-wins *"silently loses four accounts"* so a filter could not reach
their clips. **That is incorrect and I am correcting it.**

`clip_library.read_all()` returns **all 244 accounts**, including all four. The four clips have
rows in **two shards** — a dated one climbing to rev 5, and `clips-unknown.jsonl` holding a
rev-1 row with a blank account. My BL-1094 harness iterated shards in **sorted filename order**,
so `clips-unknown` sorted last and its rev-1 row overwrote rev 5. `read_all` breaks ties on
**rev**, so it never had the bug I attributed to it.

**What is real, found while checking:** 87 of 2,731 clips have rows in more than one shard,
always paired with `clips-unknown.jsonl`. `_latest_in_shard` reads **one** shard, so the
write-time merge cannot see the other shard's rows — the guarantee that "nothing anyone wrote is
dropped" has a hole for those 87. I checked whether it has cost anything: **it has not.** For
all 87 the winning row is a superset. The hazard is **latent**, and it is `clip_library.py`'s to
fix — held by BL-1107, so I did not touch it.

A third trap earned its own test while I was in there: **`field()` returns None for a value with
no provenance tier**, so a synthetic fixture without `provenance` reads as empty no matter what
it stores — which looks exactly like the loss the test is hunting. The first version of my
fixture had that bug and accused `read_all` of a defect it does not have. That is now documented
in the fixture itself.

`tests/test_library_scan_traps.py`, 7 tests, green.
