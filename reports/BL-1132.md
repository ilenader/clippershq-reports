# BL-1132 — can the model we already pay for hear the clip?

**Measured. The answer is no — and it does not know that it cannot.**

85 paid calls, **$0.0472 of a $0.50 cap**, receipts not constants. 20 clips × 3 same-condition
runs, plus a stripped-audio arm and a swapped-audio control.

---

## The verdict in one table

| control | result | what it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Is audio uploaded and billed?** | **+500 tokens median = 25.0 tok/s** (Google publishes 32) | **Yes.** The audio really is being sent and charged for. |
| **Does it notice a clip with the audio physically removed?** | **1 of 20 — 5.0% [0.9, 23.6]** | **No.** It is deaf to its own deafness. |
| ...and what does it claim anyway? | **16 of 20 (80%) reported speech**, 5 of 20 reported music, **mean self-reported confidence 0.90** | It invents sound from the picture, confidently. |
| **Swapped-audio control (5 clips)** | **3 of 5 gave the ORIGINAL's mood** despite a completely different track playing | It is not listening to the track that is there. |
| **Mood reproducibility, 3 runs, same input** | **25.0% [11.2, 46.9] unanimous** | **Below the 40% floor** that closed audio classification last time. |
| **Agreement with mood read off the clip's own text** | **25.0% [11.2, 46.9]** | On a 5-way vocabulary that is chance. |

## 1. The raw answers (arm A — the clip exactly as production sends it)

Some are genuinely impressive and I want to be fair about that. Clip 02: *"A male voice is
speaking in **German**"* — you cannot get German off a picture of Jackie Chan on a roof.
Clip 12: *"commentary in **Mandarin Chinese**"*. Clip 07: *"A male voice (Spiderman) says
'Venom! Get ready for a world of pain.'"* Those read like hearing.

But look at the same column against the local VAD, which is ground truth this model never sees:

| | |
|---|---|
| clips with a VAD class | 15 |
| model said **speech present** | **15 of 15** |
| VAD says speech present | 9 of 15 |
| **false positives** | **6** |

`speech_present` is not a measurement. It is the constant `true`.

Selected rows, with the VAD's verdict beside the model's:

```
01 VAD music-only          model: speech=True  "A young boy (Malcolm) ... expressing distress"
05 VAD music-only          model: speech=True  "grunts, yells, and sounds of impact"
09 VAD music-only          model: speech=True  "Two male voices ... discussing Batman"
13 VAD music-only          model: speech=True  "A sergeant is interrogating a private"
16 VAD music-only          model: speech=True  "stern and commanding tone ... orders or a decree"
19 VAD music-only          model: speech=True  "Jamie Foxx is speaking about ..."
```

Every one of those six is a music-only clip being described as dialogue — and every
description is a fluent paraphrase of **what the burned-in text already said**.

`music_present` came back **false on 14 of 20** — including clips whose VAD class is
`music-only`, which is to say clips that are *definitionally* music.

## 2. The stripped-audio arm — the one that settles it

The same 20 files, re-encoded with `ffmpeg -an`. **No audio track exists.** The prompt says
so explicitly and gives it an out: *"Some of these clips have had their audio REMOVED... if
you cannot actually hear anything, set `heard_nothing` true... Guessing is worse than saying
you heard nothing."*

| | |
|---|---|
| set `heard_nothing` (the honest answer) | **5.0% [0.9, 23.6] — 1 of 20** |
| claimed **speech present** with no audio | **80.0% [58.4, 91.9] — 16 of 20** |
| claimed **music present** with no audio | 25.0% [11.2, 46.9] — 5 of 20 |
| gave a non-null **mood** with no audio | 30.0% [14.5, 51.9] — 6 of 20 |
| **mean self-reported confidence** | **0.90** |

The very first pair in the smoke test was already the whole result: on a file with no audio
stream at all, it returned *"A young boy is talking, expressing distress and confusion about
his lisp and how his mother will react. **The dialogue is in English**"* — at **confidence
1.0**.

### The noise-floor test, which is the rigorous version

A/B agreement alone would be misleading: if A and B disagree, that could be audio doing
work — or it could be the model disagreeing with itself. So compare A-vs-B against
**A-vs-A**, the same input run three times.

| field | A vs B (audio removed) | A vs A (identical input) | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| **mood** | 40.0% | **50.0%** | **indistinguishable from noise** |
| **energy** | 35.0% | **50.0%** | **indistinguishable from noise** |
| genre | 60.0% | 75.0% | indistinguishable from noise |
| music_present | 70.0% | 86.7% | small real difference |
| speech_present | 80.0% | 100.0% | small real difference |

**On mood and energy — the two fields the operator actually needs — removing the entire
audio track moves the answer less than running the identical file twice does.** You cannot
detect a signal smaller than your instrument's own noise.

## 3. The swap control — nobody passes this by guessing

Five clips, audio replaced with a known, wildly different track (`song01`, a slow sad piano
ballad; `song04`, fast aggressive percussion).

| clip | swapped in | A said | C said | what C described the music as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | song01 *(sad ballad)* | neutral | **neutral** | *(nothing)* |
| 02 | song04 *(aggressive)* | neutral | **neutral** | *(nothing)* |
| 03 | song01 *(sad ballad)* | hype | **hype** | *"Dramatic and **epic orchestral** music with a strong beat and **soaring strings**"* |
| 04 | song04 *(aggressive)* | funny | null | *(nothing)* |
| 05 | song01 *(sad ballad)* | null | **hype** | *"a dramatic, instrumental track with a **driving beat**"* |

**3 of 5 [23.1, 88.2] returned the original clip's mood** with a different song playing.

Clips 03 and 05 are the ones to read twice: a **slow sad piano ballad** was playing, and the
model described **"epic orchestral… soaring strings"** and **"a driving beat"**. It was
describing the Spanish Armada and a fight scene — the *pictures*.

## 4. Reproducibility, against the 40% floor

Three runs, same clip, same condition, same prompt.

| field | 3/3 identical | pairwise |
|---|---|---|
| speech_present | 100.0% [83.9, 100.0] | 100.0% |
| heard_nothing | 95.0% [76.4, 99.1] | 96.7% |
| music_present | 80.0% [58.4, 91.9] | 86.7% |
| genre | 70.0% [48.1, 85.5] | 75.0% |
| **energy** | **30.0% [14.5, 51.9]** | 50.0% |
| **mood** | **25.0% [11.2, 46.9]** | 50.0% |

**Mood is 25% reproducible. The floor it had to beat was 40%. It did not beat it — it is
worse.** The fields that *are* reproducible are reproducible because they are near-constant:
`speech_present` is 100% stable because it is always `true`, including on files with no
audio.

## 5. Does it add anything over the source's own text?

20 clips hand-labelled sad / funny / hype / nostalgic **from the burned-in line alone,
recorded and timestamped before any call was made** (`scratch/bl1132_labels.json`).

**Agreement: 25.0% [11.2, 46.9] — 5 of 20.** On a five-value vocabulary that is chance.

| | distribution |
|---|---|
| text-derived (human) | funny 10, hype 5, sad 3, nostalgic 2 |
| model, from audio | neutral 7, hype 4, funny 3, **null 6** |

**The model returned `null` or `neutral` for mood on 13 of 20 clips.** It has no opinion two
times out of three.

Every disagreement went the same way — the text was right and the model was vague:

```
01 text=funny      model=neutral   "Malcolm struggles to pronounce the letter S"
02 text=sad        model=neutral   "Jackie Chan became the victim of the very fight he thought he could win"
05 text=hype       model=null      "He challenges the abusive champion and triggers a brutal fight"
07 text=nostalgic  model=hype      "PLEASE THIS PEAK NEEDS A REMAKE"
08 text=hype       model=null      "Metallica's opinion on Lady Gaga during their 2017 Grammys collab"
```

The operator's insight holds exactly as he stated it. *"when you hear the girls ranking the
ugliest guy and you're at the top"* is unmistakably sad however the picture looks — and this
model would have called it `neutral`.

From receipts (`usage.cost`), not the config constant.

| arm A, 20 s clip with the audio prompt | **$0.000679/call** (5,516 prompt + 121 completion tokens) |

**It folds into the existing call almost for free** — the video is already in the upload, so
asking costs ~140 extra prompt tokens and ~121 answer tokens. **$0.17 for the whole library.**

That is the cruel part of this result: **it is cheap, and it does not work.** Cost was never
the obstacle.

## The verdict

**The model receives the audio, is billed for it, and cannot use it.**

It fails every control that distinguishes hearing from inference:

1. it does not notice when the audio is **entirely absent** (5%),
2. it reports the **original** mood when a different track is playing (3 of 5),
3. its mood is **less reproducible than the 40% floor** that closed this question before (25%),
4. and removing the whole audio track changes its answer **less than re-running the same
   file does**.

Some individual answers really are heard — the German, the Mandarin, the quoted line. But
**there is no field where hearing can be told apart from confabulation**, and a signal you
cannot separate from invention cannot route a song.

### The recommendation stands where it was

**Read the source's own on-screen text.** It is free — RapidOCR is vendored and local, about
46 minutes of CPU for the whole 2,731-clip library, **$0.00**. It is 100% reproducible
because it is not a judgement. And on this sample the human reading of that text produced a
usable mood on **20 of 20** clips where the model produced one on 7.

**And strip the audio from the vision upload.** It buys nothing and costs **$0.26 per full
library pass**. That is the only actionable saving here, and it is small — I am naming it
because it is measured, not because it matters much.

**No audio API is proposed.** Refused with numbers in BL-1129 and nothing here changes it: a
fingerprint identifies the track the *reposter* laid over the clip — the same proposition
`track_title` gives free at 28.7% fill, and precisely the signal that put a football song on
a fake-note scene.

## Off-brief, stated

* **Every arm was trimmed to the same 20-second window.** Token count is a function of
  duration, so an untrimmed comparison would let a 90 s clip and a 12 s clip contribute
  unequally to the same average. This departs from production, which sends whole files
  (mean 38.2 s) — the cost-at-scale section prices **production** durations, not these.
* **The swap control is n=5.** The brief specified five; the interval [23.1, 88.2] is
  correspondingly enormous and the qualitative evidence (a sad piano ballad described as
  "epic orchestral… soaring strings") carries more weight than the ratio.
* **I am the labeller for the text-derived moods**, with no second reader, and 5 of 20 clips
  had OCR too garbled to label from — I read those off the frame instead. Labels were
  recorded and timestamped before the first call.
* **The 20 clips are not a random sample of the library.** They were drawn for
  *verifiability* — every one carries readable burned-in text — and stratified across audio
  classes so the speech question did not rest on one clip.

## Got wrong

* **I nearly reported the A-vs-B mood difference as evidence of hearing.** A/B agreement on
  mood is 40%, which looks like the audio doing work — until you put it beside A-vs-A at
  50%. The third same-condition arm is the only reason that reads correctly, and it is the
  reason two-arm comparisons are retired here.
* **My first instinct was that `speech_present` was the model's strongest field** — 100%
  reproducible. It is 100% reproducible because it is always `true`, including on 20 files
  with no audio track. Perfect stability and zero information.
