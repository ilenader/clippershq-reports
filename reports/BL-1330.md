## 2. What it did differently

### Discovery — the real difference

| | old tool | today (live) |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram | **`/v2/fbsearch/accounts` keyword search** + **BFS snowball over `/v2/user/suggested/profiles`** | **hashtag media drain** (`/v2/hashtag/medias/<kind>`) |
| needs hashtags | **no** | yes |
| ordering | best owned leads → resolve pk → crawl suggested → free filter → survivors become the next layer | paginate a hashtag, take the authors |

Its premise, quoted from the module: *"seeding on an editor returns mostly editors, and every
editor found seeds ~42 more."*

It survives at `C:\Users\Game Centar\OneDrive\Desktop\clipper finder\clippershq\crawl_suggested.py`,
wired to Control Panel menu 9. **It is off:** `ig_crawl_enabled = False`, and
`ig_crawl_max_depth = 1` — depth 1 is not a snowball at all.

**The measured claim inside it**, from a 2026-07-22 probe:

| query vocabulary | clipper-persona purity |
|---|---|
| **fandom tokens** (scenepack, fanedit, animeedit, velocityedit…) | **~72–89%** [Wilson 62–94, n=90] |
| **craft tokens** (editor, aftereffects, videoeditor, vfxedit) | **~0%** [Wilson 0–5] |

> **These are NOT want rates and must not be set against the 9%.** They measure a *clipper-persona*
> label on an *editor* population; the 9% measures *his WANT verdict* on *meme-repost pages*. Two
> labels, two populations — the exact comparison that took share/play from AUC 0.818 to 0.548.

What transfers is the **mechanism, not the number**: on one endpoint, the *query language* moved
purity from ~0% to 72–89%. **That is not seed ranking.** Seed ranking asks which known account to
start from and has been refuted three times; this asks what words to search, which none of those
refutations touched.

### Filtering — tested against his marks, and it is NOT the find of the round

The brief said a filter that does not kill wanted pages would be the find. So I ran it.
`crawl_suggested.free_keep` = drop verified/private, then `main._ig_free_score >= 30`.

Against **his 100 marked TikTok pages**:

| inputs supplied | wanted killed | precision of what it keeps |
|---|---|---|
| handle + name only | 35 of 37 = 94.6% [82.3, 98.5] | — |
| **+ followers (100/100), avg_views (76/100)** | **13 of 37 = 35.1% [21.8, 51.2]** | **24 of 62 = 38.7%** |
| *shipped filter, for comparison* | *17.4% [10.2, 28.0]* | — |
| *base rate on this set* | — | **37.0%** |

The first row is not a fair test and is shown only to explain the second: the score distribution
was **bimodal — 93 pages at exactly 0.0 and 7 at exactly 45.0** — the signature of one component
firing while four inputs were missing. Reporting 94.6% would have been scoring a crippled version
of the filter.

**The fair answer: it kills twice as many wanted pages as the shipped filter, and lifts purity
1.7 points over the base rate.** 38.7% against 37.0% is not a lift. *Caveat:* this is an
Instagram-**editor** scorer run on TikTok **meme pages** — it measures reuse safety on today's
target, not how good it was at its own job.

### Speed and cost

Old: **sequential by design** — `crawl_suggested` runs "capped + sequential". Today's finders have
lane pools (and BL-1327 is live right now adding one to `tiktok_finder.py`). **On concurrency the
current code is ahead; there is nothing to take.** The old tool's cost lever was structural
instead: the **contact-mode switch** (`main.py:4247`) still offers *"Instagram CHEAP (handles only,
no IG profiling → DM_LIST)"*, documented at **~10–30x cheaper** because it makes zero profile
calls.

## 5. The thing I did not expect: it contradicts my own last round

`ig_dm_list.py`'s docstring states plainly:

> **"TikTok restricts DMs from non-followers, so a cold DM to a TikTok lead does not arrive.
> Instagram DMs do."**

**BL-1321 was me, last round, concluding "TikTok is a DM channel."** I reached that from four
converging lines — the platform gives personal accounts nowhere to publish an address, the payload
has no address field, 0 of 37 wanted bios publish one, and 4 of 37 explicitly invite a DM.

Every one of those still holds. **What I did not check is whether the channel I recommended is
deliverable.** If cold TikTok DMs do not arrive from a non-follower, then those pages are asking
for a channel outreach cannot use, and the route is the Instagram handle they published — which is
exactly the file above. The same module records **~3.3% of TikTok leads publish an IG handle in
bio**, consistent with my 0-of-37 on an all-WANT draw of 37.

I have not re-verified TikTok's DM policy this round, so I am reporting the contradiction rather
than resolving it. **It should be resolved before anyone builds TikTok DM outreach**, and it was
sitting in a docstring in this repository the whole time.
