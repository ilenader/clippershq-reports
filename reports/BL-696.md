# BL-696: the energy-step detector fabricates on music too — the reframe did not save it, but flux-as-veto is the first thing that produces a null

**Date:** 2026-07-29
**Spend:** **$0.00** of a $0.03 budget. No API call was made — track audio came from CDN URLs already known to need no auth, and BL-684's payloads were reused.
**Pipeline code:** untouched. `scratch/energy_step.py` was run **completely unchanged** at shipped defaults (`WIN_S=2.0`, `MIN_STEP_DB=3.0`, `MIN_SEP=0.0`, `RAMP_MAX_S=3.0`). The flux veto was applied **post-hoc** to its own recorded `flux_agree_s`, which is arithmetically identical to gating on it, so nothing was tuned before measurement.

---

## The answer in one line

**Fabrication is 100% on music, exactly as on dialogue.** The reframe was a good hypothesis and it is refuted: the detector's failure is not caused by the dialogue corpus.

| | dialogue (BL-690) | dialogue (re-graded here) | **music (new)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **fabrication on eye-flat** | 19/19 = **100%** | 29/29 = **100%** | **4/4 = 100%** |
| **localisation ±0.5 s on eye-clear** | 9/17 = 53% (clear+marginal) | 1/1 | **4/8 = 50%** |
| emitted a timestamp at all | 36/36 | 35/36 = 97% | **17/17 = 100%** |

On music the detector returned `shape='step'` on **17 of 17 tracks**. Not one null. Its own docstring says *"NULL IS A FIRST-CLASS ANSWER… Uniformly-loud audio with no build must return None."* On real audio of either kind, it never does.

---

## Method (BL-690's protocol, reproduced)

17 licensed tracks from the Instagram sound library — pop, phonk, Bollywood, soundtrack, lo-fi; 78–301 s; `clips_count` from 293 to 1,067,605. Envelopes rendered as RMS-dB with **no detector markers**, labelled only with the last six digits of the id and the duration, six per page. Graded **clear / marginal / flat** with an eyeballed step time read off the axis, and the labels written to disk **before** the results file was opened.

Blind grades: **8 clear, 5 marginal, 4 flat.** (Music has far fewer flats than dialogue — songs have intros.)

Then the same thing for a rebuilt dialogue corpus: 36 clips from BL-690's three accounts, audio re-derived from saved payloads. Blind grades: **1 clear, 6 marginal, 29 flat.**

> **Grader difference, stated openly.** BL-690 graded 19 of 36 dialogue clips flat; this pass graded 29. Different grader, stricter bar — dialogue envelopes are spiky with no sustained plateaus, and I counted "recovery from a pause" as flat. Four clips BL-690 named as flat (671777, 113528, 249750, 624272) were independently graded flat here, so the corpora agree where they overlap. The direction is identical; only the count moved.

Blind plots, marked plots and both label files are committed to `scratch/` (`bl691_blind/`, `bl691_blind_dlg/`, `bl691_marked/`, `bl691_blind_labels.json`, `bl691_dlg_labels.json`).

## What music changed, and what it did not

### The confidence inversion is NOT universal — it is a dialogue pathology

| corpus | median conf, eye-flat | median conf, eye-clear | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| dialogue | **0.852** | 0.518 | **INVERTED** |
| music | 0.815 | **0.998** | correctly ordered |

On music, confidence ranks the way it should: flat 0.815 < marginal 0.957 < clear 0.998. BL-690's most alarming finding — that a `confidence > 0.9` filter selects *preferentially* for fabrications — **does not reproduce on music**. There, 9 of 11 high-confidence outputs are genuine.

This is the one real win from the reframe, and it is worth recording. But it does not rescue the detector, because…

### …the absolute floor is still far too high

Median confidence on tracks with **nothing to cut on** is **0.815**. A fabrication with `conf=0.974` (`Beautiful Home`, eye-flat) outranks a genuine step at `conf=0.749` (`Happier Than Ever`, eye-clear). The distributions are ordered but heavily overlapped, so no confidence threshold separates them.

### Localisation did not improve either

4 of 8 eye-clear tracks landed within ±0.5 s — statistically the same as dialogue's 53%. The failures are gross, not near-misses: median error 0.70 s but **max 204 s**. `Where Is My Mind?` was graded clear at 15.5 s; the detector returned **219.87 s**. Two others were ~18 s and ~6.5 s off.

---

## The flux requirement: the first change that produces nulls

`flux_agree_s` currently grants +0.10 confidence and can never veto. Requiring agreement (`flux_agree_s ≤ 0.5 s`) instead:

| corpus | fabrication (eye-flat) | total timestamps emitted |
|---|---|---|
| **music** | 4/4 = 100% → **0/4 = 0%** | 17/17 → **6/17** |
| **dialogue** | 29/29 = 100% → **6/29 = 20.7%** | 35/36 → **7/36** |

**On music it is a complete fix for fabrication.** Every eye-flat track is nulled. The separation is clean and not a threshold artefact — flux disagreement by grade:

```
clear     0.05, 0.12, 0.26, 0.35, 0.39, 1.28, 4.48, 154.46
flat      1.11, 8.41, 20.78, 56.49        <- not one below 1.0
```

On dialogue it removes 23 of 29 fabrications but leaves 6, so dialogue still fails.

### The catch, and it matters

**Flux agreement means "the two views agree on a time", not "that time is right."** On music the veto also cut localisation from 4/8 to 2/8, because it keeps three badly-mislocated tracks whose views happen to agree:

- `Where Is My Mind?` — flux agreement **0.35 s** (excellent), detector 219.87 s, eye 15.5 s → **204 s wrong, and kept**
- two more kept at ~2 s when the eye says 20.5 s and 8.5 s

Both RMS and spectral flux lock onto the same late, loud, arithmetically-real section. Corroboration between two views of the same signal is not evidence of editorial correctness when both views share the same bias.

So the veto trades fabrication for silence: on music you get **0% fabrication and 25% correct localisation on the obvious cases**, with 11 of 17 tracks returning nothing.

---

## Verdict

**The detector is not saved by the reframe, and it is not dead everywhere either.** Precisely:

1. **At shipped defaults it is unusable on both populations.** 100% fabrication, zero null capacity, on music and dialogue alike. Nothing downstream can distinguish its guess from a measurement, which is the exact failure its own docstring warns against.
2. **On dialogue it should be gated off entirely.** Even with the flux veto it fabricates on 21% of flat clips, and its confidence is inverted there — the one condition under which a filter actively selects for garbage.
3. **On music, with flux agreement REQUIRED, it becomes safe but weak.** No fabrications, correct confidence ordering, and roughly a quarter of the obvious steps located correctly. It is honest silence most of the time. Whether "right about 2 of 8 obvious cases, silent otherwise" earns its place is a product call, not a measurement one — but it is no longer *harmful*, which is a change of kind.

**Recommended if it ships at all:** flux agreement as a hard requirement, a population gate restricting it to licensed-music audio, and the output named for what it is — a candidate, not a drop. It must never run on dialogue audio.

**Recommended against:** further threshold tuning. BL-690 swept `min_step_db` × `min_sep` and found no configuration that keeps the real steps while nulling the flats; this round adds that the same overlap exists on music (a 0.974 fabrication above a 0.749 true positive). The distributions genuinely overlap in both populations, and the flux veto works precisely because it is a *different view*, not a tighter threshold on the same one.

## Open

1. **n is small on the decisive cell** — 4 eye-flat music tracks. The 100% fabrication figure is unambiguous but rests on four tracks; a larger music corpus would tighten it.
2. **Only one eye-clear dialogue clip**, so the dialogue localisation column is not meaningful this round.
3. **The flux threshold was not swept beyond 0.25–5.0 s.** 0.5 s is BL-690's own agreement window, not an optimum.
4. **Mislocalisation is unexplained.** The veto addresses fabrication only; why both views agree on a wrong late section (204 s off on one track) is untouched, and is the next thing worth understanding if this is pursued.
