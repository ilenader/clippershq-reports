## Why it is flat: the library was built to exclude the bottom

`clip_walk.walk_account` yields *"this account's clips in descending view order until the floor is cleared"*, `DEFAULT_VIEW_FLOOR = 20000`, stopping after 3 consecutive sub-floor clips.

**VERIFIED against the data:** the minimum `play_count` in the entire library is **20,029**. Thirty-nine clips sit under 25k. The floor is not approximately observed — it is exact.

And for a large minority of accounts the ordering is still visible in the file:

| rho(file append order, `play_count`) | accounts (n ≥ 8) |
|---|---|
| ≤ −0.7 (stored in descending view order) | **11 of 29** |
| −0.7 to 0 | 13 |
| > 0 | 5 |

Four accounts — `darkframemovie`, `richardthedis6067`, `culturemeetsonline`, `clipsvaultex` — are at rho = **−1.000**: perfectly descending. Those pages contributed their **top twelve clips**, not twelve of their clips.

This reframes the brief's question. "What separates the top from the bottom" cannot be answered here, because **the bottom was never collected.** The library holds each account's above-20k slice. Range restriction at the bottom is the single best explanation for a table of forty nulls, and it is a property of the collector, not of the content.

### ARTEFACT — `follower_count`, r = −0.566, is measuring the crawler

The largest within-account effect in the entire round is `follower_count` at **r = −0.566, p < 0.0001** — apparently saying a page's clips get *worse* as the page grows. It is not a finding.

- Within an account, `follower_count` has a **median spread of 1.1%** and typically **2 or 3 distinct values**. It is nearly constant.
- On the *same rows*, `posted_at` — the variable that would actually carry account growth — gives **r = −0.056, p = 0.325**. Nothing.
- The mechanism, visible in the raw rows:

```
richardthedis6067      followers      play_count
  6198                              1,929,194     <- walk page 1, ranked descending
  6198                              1,136,043
  6198                                802,324
  6198                                794,436
  6198                                620,873
  6198                                619,003
  6198                                580,924
  6247                                567,624     <- walk page 2, follower count re-read
  6247                                544,465
  6247                                473,867
  6247                                471,553
  6247                                442,748
```

`follower_count` is re-read from each API response, the walk pages through clips **in descending view order**, and so the field increments exactly at page boundaries — which are rank boundaries. It is a **walk-progress clock**, and correlating it with `play_count` is circular.

> **Do not rank on `follower_count` at clip level.** Its within-account variation encodes which API page the row came from, not anything about the account or the clip. The v1 cut of this analysis also reported `follower_count` at r = −0.012 with p = 0.0098, which was a second, different artefact — a p-value computed against a degenerate null because the predictor was pooled raw while the outcome was ranked within account. Both are recorded here so neither is rediscovered.

The shape is a shallow U with its peak at the shortest bucket, which is why a rank correlation reports nothing (r = −0.095, q = 0.157) — Spearman is monotone-only and cannot see a U. The bucket omnibus test, built for exactly this, gives p = 0.057 / q = 0.199: **not significant**.

## 5. Layout — the field MEMEBOT-003 flagged, and why it cannot carry a ranking

MEMEBOT-003 called `layout` the best-attributed signal in the spec: 67.4% fill, `measured` tier, library-wide. That is all true — this snapshot puts it at **67.7% (429/634) across 62 accounts**, and it is the only genuinely library-wide `measured` field. **Best-attributed is not the same as discriminative,** and this is the round that separates them.

**Reason one: it barely varies.**

| `layout_class` | clips (all) | share | median play (raw) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **caption_bar_top** | **340** | **79.3%** | 318,793 |
| caption_bar_bottom | 25 | 5.8% | 394,410 |
| other_band | 25 | 5.8% | 299,271 |
| blank_frame | 24 | 5.6% | 228,576 |
| **full_bleed** | **15** | **3.5%** | 261,314 |

Classes follow `repost_layout`'s own calibration: `bar_top_frac ≥ 0.10` is its primary rule, letterbox is excluded via `_letterbox_pair` because symmetric black padding is an aspect-ratio artefact rather than a composited strip, and `blank_frame` catches near-uniform cover frames with no imagery to judge.

Four in five clips are the same class. This is a library of repost pages, and repost pages caption the top of the frame — that is what makes `repost_layout` a good *detector*. A field that is 79% one value has almost no variance to rank on, no matter how well attributed it is.

**Reason two: the specific comparison is UNDERPOWERED, and that is not the same as "no effect".**

Only **7 accounts** post both a caption bar and a full-bleed clip, covering **71 clips of which 10 are full-bleed**. Simulated power for that comparison, at an uncorrected α = 0.05 — the most generous case:

| true shift in within-account rank | P(detect), caption-bar vs full-bleed | P(detect), caption-bar vs everything else |
|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 0.05 | 0.07 |
| 0.10 | 0.09 | 0.24 |
| 0.15 | 0.14 | 0.40 |
| 0.20 | 0.34 | 0.76 |
| 0.30 | 0.70 | 0.99 |
| 0.50 | 0.99 | 1.00 |

A genuine one-decile advantage for caption bars would be missed **91% of the time**. The observed Δ = +0.061 at p = 0.524 is therefore consistent with a small real effect and equally consistent with nothing.

> **Do caption-bar clips outperform full-bleed? This library cannot say.** It is not a null result; it is an absence of evidence, and reporting it as "layout doesn't matter" would be wrong.

**One thing layout *is* clean on, which is worth recording:** the two dominant accounts have **no** layout at all. `loste1980` and `movies.avengers` — 32.6% of the library — carry `layout = None`. So layout's confound share from the top-2 accounts is **2.6%**, against 47–65% for the declared panel. Layout is the least account-confounded field in the library. It is simply too invariant, and too thinly crossed within accounts, to rank on.

**To answer this properly** would need roughly 25–30 full-bleed clips spread across ≥15 accounts that also post caption bars, which means targeting the collection rather than waiting for the walk to produce it.

## 6. What does not predict anything

Stop the orchestrator ranking on these. Each was tested within account, permutation-null, BH-corrected:

- **`valence_text`** — all three levels flat (q = 0.81 / 0.87 / 0.96). Positive, neutral and negative clips rank identically inside their own accounts. Raw, positive clips do 0.84× — *worse* — which is itself an account effect.
- **`audio_type`** — original_sounds vs licensed_music, Δ = ±0.015, q = 0.799. Raw says licensed music does 1.28×; 51.1% of licensed-music clips are one account.
- **`content_genre`** — Δ = +0.003, q = 0.987, and BL-847's confound is worse here: **64.0% of clips carrying a genre are `loste1980`**, only 11 accounts have it at all. It cannot support a library-wide claim in either direction.
- **`franchise`** — Δ = +0.024, q = 0.743. 45.4% fill, 41% one account. Raw ratio 0.92 — franchise-tagged clips do slightly *worse* library-wide.
- **`caption_chars` / caption length** — r = +0.006, q = 0.987. Caption line count likewise (r = +0.047, q = 0.751), despite a raw ρ of +0.203.
- **Posting hour** — r = −0.001, p = 0.986. The flattest result in the round. *Caveat, and it matters:* hour is UTC and the audience timezone per account is unknown, so a null here is weaker than it looks — a real local-time effect would be smeared across UTC hours by accounts in different regions. This is a **GAP**, not a clean refutation.
- **Day of week** — r = −0.110, p = 0.017, **q = 0.084**. The only field that got close and did not make it. With 40 hypotheses in the family, the chance of at least one p < 0.05 by luck is 87%. Do not ship this; it is the one worth re-testing when the library doubles.
- **`subtitles_available`** — q = 0.199, and power for this contrast is good (0.90 at a 0.15 shift), so this null is a genuine one rather than a coverage problem.
- **Aspect ratio** — r = +0.056, q = 0.751.
- **Clip age** — r = −0.008, q = 0.987. Older clips have not accumulated measurably more views within their own account, which is mildly surprising and consistent with reel views saturating early.
- **`saves_per_view`, `reshares_per_view`** — r = −0.001 and +0.069. Flat, and they are the fields that *would* have separated clip quality from clip reach if anything did.
- **`follower_count`** — see the ARTEFACT above.

**Untestable, not tested:** `format`, `speech_frac`, `imdb_rating`, `arousal_audio`, `affect_conflict`, `subtitle_text`, `content_medium`, `genre_derived`, `medium_declared`, `medium_derived`, `why_watch` are at **0% fill**. `vision_*` is at 4.3% (27 clips, BL-849 in flight), `caption_chars` / `is_templated` / `valence_score` / `genre_conflict` / `medium_conflict` at 2.7% (17 clips), and the IMDb-style declared panel (`title`, `cast`, `storyline`, `summary`, `ratings`, `runtime`, `release_date`, `language`, `hashtags`) at 0.2–0.3% — **1 to 2 clips each.** None of these can be tested at all. The ~30-field declared panel that BL-838→BL-843 rescued from being discarded is, on this snapshot, present on two clips.

## 7. Recommended ranking function

Given that 78.7% of variance is the account, nothing content-side is measurable within account, and the library never collected sub-20k clips — the ranking function should be **short**, and it should mostly not be a prediction.

```
score(clip) = log10(play_count)                       # the only term with strong evidence
            + 0.15 if media_duration_s < 10           # PROVISIONAL: q=0.037, n=24, 10 accounts
            - 1e9  if play_count is None              # unrankable, do not guess
            - 1e9  if layout_class == "blank_frame"   # no usable cover frame (24 clips)
```

**The evidence behind each term.**

- **`log10(play_count)` — the whole function.** This is not a prediction, it is an observation the library already paid for, on 634/634 clips at `declared` tier. Every content field tested adds nothing to it within account, and every engagement count is proportional to it (per-view ratios flat at r ≈ 0). Spread is 3.33 orders of magnitude, so the log scale is required — on the raw scale one 42M-view clip outweighs the bottom 300 combined.
- **`< 10s` bonus, +0.15, provisional.** Δ = +0.184 within-account rank at q = 0.037, 9/10 accounts agreeing, survives leave-one-account-out. Sized *below* the measured effect deliberately: it rests on 24 clips and most contributing accounts supply one. **Re-test it before trusting it**; if it fails on the next 200 clips, delete the term and the function still works.
- **Both hard exclusions are data-quality gates, not quality judgements.** A missing `play_count` is unrankable and must not be scored as 0. `blank_frame` means the cover frame carried no imagery, so anything downstream reading the frame is reading nothing.

**Explicitly excluded, with the reason:**

| term | why not |
|---|---|

**The load-bearing caveat for whoever wires this.** Ranking clips by their own observed `play_count` selects clips that already worked *on the page that posted them*. Whether a clip that did 3M for `clipcapture.tv` will do anything at all when remade on a different page is **not measured anywhere in this library**, and the 78.7% account share is direct evidence that the posting page matters more than the clip. If the orchestrator is choosing source material to remake, the honest reading of this round is:

> **Source from the accounts, not from the clips.** Account medians span a factor of 105 and account identity explains three quarters of everything; within-account clip choice explains almost none of it and is the part this library cannot measure.

## Corrections to figures in circulation

**"755 clips" is a row count, not a clip count.** `clip_library.py`'s own docstring says *"Measured fill on 755 clips"* and MEMEBOT-006 quotes *"767 library rows"*. On this snapshot: **799 non-empty lines, 634 distinct `clip_id`s** — 165 clips carry a `rev` 2 from BL-849's vision-labelling pass, and `read_all()` correctly last-wins them. MEMEBOT-003 measured **629 clips on 2026-08-01** using the same dedup and is consistent with this round. Percentages quoted against 755 are quoted against the wrong denominator by ~19%.

**Two estimator errors were made and fixed inside this round**, recorded so they are not repeated:
1. Ranking the outcome within account while pooling the predictor raw produces a degenerate permutation null — a rho of −0.012 came back at p = 0.0098. Rank the predictor within account too.
2. Simulating power for a binary predictor by splitting at `x > median(x)` yields an all-zero predictor whenever the binary is mostly 1s, and reports power = 0.000 at every effect size including shifts nothing could miss. That very nearly became "the library cannot answer the layout question" — which is the right conclusion, reached from a broken number.

## Method, reproducibility, and what would change the answer

- **Snapshot:** whole `clip_library/` tree frozen together (36 files, 3,481,625 bytes, newest shard mtime 2026-08-01 15:23:38). BL-849 was writing to it live. Per-file `read_snapshot.snapshot()` would not have been enough — a per-account numerator and denominator drawn from two shards must come from one version of the tree.
- **Outcome:** within-account rank percentile of `play_count`, accounts with **n ≥ 8** (29 accounts, 511 clips). Ties share the mean rank.
- **Null:** permutation shuffling the outcome **within account**, 20,000 draws (5,000 for the bucket omnibus), seed 850. Both the within-rank correlation and the pooled group-mean difference are monotone in `T = Σ x·y` once the within-account multiset of y is fixed, so each test permutes one scalar vectorised over all draws.
- **Multiplicity:** Benjamini-Hochberg over all 40 hypotheses.
- **Power:** simulated by injecting a known shift into permuted outcomes.
- **Scripts:** `scratch/bl850_snapshot.py`, `bl850_shape.py`, `bl850_countcheck.py`, `bl850_viral.py`, `bl850_viral2.py`, `bl850_probe.py`. Results in `scratch/bl850_results.json`, `bl850_results2.json`, `bl850_probe.json`, `bl850_fill.json`.
- **Claimed as BL-850**, read-only, empty `will_write`. Seven other rounds were in flight; no path conflicts.

**What would actually change these conclusions**, in order of value:

1. **Collect below the floor.** Walk a handful of accounts with `view_floor = 0` and no patience cap. Everything null here is null on a sample whose bottom was removed by design; 200 sub-20k clips from 10 accounts would test that directly and cost one walk.
2. **Targeted full-bleed collection** — ~25–30 clips across ≥15 accounts that also post caption bars, which is the only way to answer the layout question that MEMEBOT-003 raised.
3. **Finish the vision pass** (BL-849, 27/634 today). `vision_scene` and `vision_beats` describe what actually happens on screen and are the only fields in the schema that could plausibly separate clips within an account. Nothing in this round tested content — only its metadata.
4. **Re-test day of week** at q < 0.05 when the library doubles. It is the one near-miss.

## Verdict

1. **Within account, nothing predicts.** 40 hypotheses, one survivor: clips under 10 seconds, +0.18 rank percentile, q = 0.037, on 24 clips. Provisional.
2. **78.7% of the variance is the account.** Source from pages, not from clip features.
3. **The library never collected the bottom.** `DEFAULT_VIEW_FLOOR = 20000`, minimum observed 20,029, 11 of 29 accounts stored in descending view order. "Top vs bottom" is unanswerable on winners-only data.
4. **Layout is underpowered, not refuted** — 79.3% one class, 7 crossing accounts, 91% chance of missing a real one-decile effect.
5. **`follower_count` at clip level is a crawler artefact.** It encodes walk page order.
6. **Engagement counts add nothing to `play_count`** — their per-view ratios are flat.
7. **The ranking function is one term**, `log10(play_count)`, plus one provisional bonus and two data-quality gates.
