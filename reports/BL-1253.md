## 3. THE GUARD NOW CHECKS EVERY SURFACE — AND THE OLD HOLE WAS BIGGER THAN "THREE FILES"

The brief says the guard checks three files. It is worse than that, and the reason matters.

**The existing guard asks: "is this ADDRESS outside the primary classified?"** It walks every
top-level CSV and all of quarantine, so its *scope* was never three files. But its *question*
cannot see the hazard:

> **`PANICBABY_SEND.csv` contributes ZERO addresses outside the primary, because all 1,396 of
> its addresses are inside it. So the guard finds nothing, and passes — while the file sits in
> `output/` looking exactly as sendable as the primary.**

**A pure subset is the most dangerous file and the least visible one.** Measured: **16
top-level CSVs are 100% subsets, covering 5,560 addresses**, and every one of them was
invisible to the set-based check by construction.

So I added a second question — **is every file that carries an address accounted for, by
name?** — as `EverySendSurfaceIsAccountedForByName` in `tests/test_send_superset.py`.
**The suite goes from 9 to 15 tests, all green.**

**It is not an allowlist, and that distinction is the whole design.** Each registry entry
states a property that is **re-verified every run**, so listing a file buys it no exemption:

- `AUTOMATION_MIRROR` — `LATEST_BOT_READY.csv` must still be **byte-identical** to the
  primary. Verified by sha256 (both `6d7d15ff9e41ac7a` today). If it ever drifts it has
  become a real second list and the BL-787 contract stops describing it, so the suite goes
  red. **This is BL-787's deliberate n8n contract and I did not "fix" it.**
- `SUPERSEDED_SLICES` — each must still hold **nothing the primary lacks**. One unique address
  and it is no longer superseded, and the test says so with fingerprints.
- `test_a_classified_file_is_still_scanned_for_dead_addresses` — a classified file is still
  walked by the suppression scan, so classification cannot become the back door

- `test_the_file_level_check_can_detect_a_new_file` — plants an unclassified CSV in a **temp

**Red with its name, demonstrated rather than asserted:**


The failure then tells the operator to put it in exactly one of the three buckets, and says

## 4. THE BLOCKER — three lines, and the refusal is verified

`send_identity.json` **does not exist**, so nothing can send. That is the system working: it
refuses rather than mailing `[[RATE]]` at 3,978 people.

> **Create `send_identity.json` (copy `send_identity.example.json`) and fill five values:**
> 1. **`SENDER_NAME`** — the real first name you sign off as, and **`REPLY_TO`**, a mailbox
>    you actually read. Both are printed in every message.
> 2. **`RATE`** — what you pay an editor, in the unit you will honour (e.g. `USD 40 per
>    finished short`) — and **`PRICE`**, what you charge a client, which is a different
>    number (e.g. `USD 150 for 10 clips`). Client copy never quotes RATE.
> 3. **`TURNAROUND`** (e.g. `48 hours from brief`) and **`PAYMENT_TERMS`** (e.g. `PayPal, on
>    delivery`) — vague payment terms are the most common reply-killer.
