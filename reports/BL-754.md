## Headline

Specifying the audit against real data found things, which is the best available evidence that it works. Running its six sections by hand over the last two Spotify runs surfaced:

- **Email fill fell 58.9% → 47.6%** between 27 and 30 July (−19.2% relative) and **the no-contact pool nearly tripled**, 7.2% → 18.9%. Both are in shipped deliverables. Neither was flagged anywhere.
- **125 artists were re-processed across the two runs. 124 of 125 (99%) got the identical answer, 102 hit the PAID resolve step again, and the number whose answer changed was zero.**
- **`world_rank` — recommended in BL-744 as a free signal — is empty on all 12 artists sampled.** A presence-only field audit would have sent someone to build on it.
- **7 artists had their country lookup give up**, logged as *"country unknown for a reason we caused"*, and they sit inside BL-744's 415 country-unknown leads wearing the same label as "MusicBrainz has no record".

And it independently reproduced the leak BL-747 fixed today: **777 of 1,527 delivered Spotify leads (50.9%) never reached master**, against BL-747's 52.4%.

## The design decision that matters most

**Probe the raw response, not the adapter output.** In `spotify_finder.make_live_fns`, `artist_fn` is an adapter that projects the rich `Artist` object down to a 7-key dict before `find_contact` ever sees it. `find_contact` reads 6 of those 7.

| where the probe sits | fields seen | reported coverage | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `find_contact` (consumer) | 7 | 6/7 = **86%** | **false all-clear** |
| **`client.get_artist` return (raw)** | **14** | **8/14 = 57%** | correct |

An audit wired at the obvious place — the consumer — reports the funnel healthy. The loss happens one layer up, in the projection. **The proxy must wrap the value returned by the network client, before any adapter runs.**

### Record reads, don't grep for them

[BL-749](BL-749.md) did this sweep by hand and was explicit that its grep makes the unread list a **lower bound** — "any appearance of the name anywhere in those modules counts as read" — and that it could not tell `False` from missing. An in-run proxy that records `__getitem__`/`__getattr__` removes both weaknesses: the read set is exact, and a present-but-falsy field is distinguishable from an absent one. It also needs no saved payloads, which is what limited BL-749 to Instagram and TikTok.

### Rank by fill rate, or the audit lies to you

Measured live on 12 real artists from this run's own log:

| field | read | fill | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `followers` | **no** | **100%** | 293,738 on Jordan Ward — the finding |
| `top_tracks[].play_count` | **no** | **100%** (120/120) | free traction signal |
| `share_url` · `uri` · `url` · `images` | no | 100% | duplicates/plumbing — noise |
| `world_rank` | no | **0%** | **always empty** |

`followers / monthly_listeners` is a free loyalty ratio: Wolf Alice 0.279, Blondshell 0.221, Jordan Ward 0.185.

**`world_rank` is the reason fill rate is mandatory rather than nice.** Sorted by fill, the real finding lands first and the decoy sinks. This is a correction to BL-744, which lists it as free and worth reading.

## The six sections, against real artifacts

### 2. Drop ledger — counts already ship, examples don't

All four contact funnels maintain `stats["filter_reasons"]` as a `reason -> count` dict (`spotify_finder.py:671`) and already print it. Spotify's real vocabulary: `low_listeners`, `unknown_listeners`, `too_many_listeners`, `non_target_country`, `non_target_language`, `stale_release`, `error:blocked_403`, `error:<Type>`, `timeout`.

What's missing is **3 examples per reason**, and the brief is right that it's the difference between a number and an action. BL-744's re-check fired `too_many_listeners` exactly once: **Barry Can't Swim at 3,015,808 against a 3,000,000 ceiling — 0.53% over.** The count reads as a working filter; the example shows a rounding error deciding a lead.

**This run's own drop counts do not exist anywhere.** They were printed to console and discarded, which is why the sample output's section 2 is labelled as coming from BL-744's separate 56-artist check. That is the argument for persisting the block.

### 3. Found but not saved — proven, not inferred

| | |
|---|---|
| delivered across all six Spotify runs | **1,527** |
| master rows attributable to Spotify | **750** (all email-bearing) |
| gap | **777 = 50.9%** |
| emails delivered across all runs | **758** |
| agreement with master row count | **1.1%** |

A lead reached master **only if it had an email**. The 335 handle-only leads on the last run had already been through a *paid* Instagram resolve, then were dropped at the door — invisible to crossdedup, re-discovered next run, unsendable because send lists build *from* master. **Fixed today in `append_found_to_master` ([BL-747](BL-747.md)).** My measurement is of the pre-fix artifacts and corroborates it independently.

**The second-order cost is the 125 re-processed artists above** — 99% identical answers, 102 paid resolves, zero new information.

### 4. Consistency checks — four pass, one fails

```
PASS  status buckets sum to rows        1,000 = 1,000
PASS  email-bearing statuses = emails     476 = 476
PASS  artists unique                    1,000 distinct
FAIL  every delivered lead in master      476 of 1,000
```

**Specify the identity key exactly, or the check invents bugs.** Case-folding artist names reports a collision between **MALIA** (552,246 listeners, has a handle) and **Malia** (60,758, no contact) — two different artists. Uniqueness must key on a stable id, and *the output sheet has no artist-id column*, so today it can only run on display name.

### 5. Cost and time — money ships, time doesn't

`spend.json` already records the run: **704 calls, $0.4224, all Instagram** → $0.00042 per delivered lead, $0.00089 per emailed lead. No new plumbing, just division.

Time needs per-stage stopwatches. Reconstructed from the log, the MusicBrainz country phase ran 14:09→17:01 and spent **599.7 s purely waiting on the 1 req/sec courtesy limit** across 264 waits, plus **110 HTTP 503 retries across 113 artists**.

Buried in that: **7 artists whose country lookup gave up.** They are 1.7% of BL-744's 415 country-unknown and currently indistinguishable from "MusicBrainz has no record". Split the bucket into *no record* vs *lookup failed — retry*.

### 6. Delta vs last run — fires twice on real data

| metric | 27 Jul | 30 Jul | move |
|---|---|---|---|
| delivered | 304 | 1,000 | +229% |
| **email fill** | **58.9%** | **47.6%** | **−11.3 pts = −19.2%** |
| **no contact** | **7.2%** | **18.9%** | **2.6×** |

*Threshold honesty:* −19.2% sits just under a 20% rule and would have been **missed**. The 2.6× move catches it. Use "any of N metrics over threshold" rather than trusting one.

## Limits

- **Nothing was implemented and nothing was benchmarked.** The proxy's overhead is asserted negligible, not measured.
- **Field coverage was measured on Spotify only**, live, **n=12**. Other funnels are unmeasured here; BL-749 covers IG and TikTok and found no saved Twitch/YouTube/Google Play/Spotify payloads on disk.
- **Section 2's sample counts are BL-744's 56-artist re-check, not the audited run** — that run's drop counts no longer exist. The sample output says so on its face.
- **The nested-field case is underspecified.** `top_tracks[].play_count` is a list-of-objects path; the proxy as described tracks top-level access. Recursive tracking needs a depth cap or it produces the 600-field noise lists BL-749 reported.
- **The 5h34m Spotify window overlaps other work** (Twitch runs and probes ran inside it), so it is not a clean per-funnel duration — which is itself why section 5 needs real stage timers rather than log archaeology.
- **My own per-funnel table was wrong about the repost finder in three of five columns** before I read the module instead of trusting a grep — the same failure mode section 1 exists to remove.
