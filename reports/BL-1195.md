# BL-1195 — four new reads built, three measured, and NOT ONE of them clears the base rate

**Nothing was wired into the gate, and nothing should be.** Three of the four reads were
measured against all 149 labels with a held-out split, and **not one clears its base rate out
of sample.** The fourth could not be measured at all, and says so rather than returning a zero.

That is the result. A round recently wired a positive signal before measuring it and agreement
fell 71.1% → 69.8%; this round's entire value is that it did the measuring first.

Every figure came from **`.venv\Scripts\python.exe`, Python 3.11.9**. Bare `python` is 3.13.14
and lacks the packages; it was used only for the stdlib-only claim readers.

---

## 0. The axis, and the bar — fixed before any read was written

`mark` says whether **the filter's verdict was correct**, not whether the page is good. So the
label every read is scored against is **derived**:

| filter | mark | he… |
|---|---|---|
| PASSED | GOOD | **wants it** |
| PASSED | BAD | does not |
| REJECTED | GOOD | does not |
| REJECTED | BAD | **wants it** |

149 distinct handles (154 rows, last-mark-wins). **46 wanted, 103 not — a base rate of 30.9%.**
One fixed train/test split (99/50, seed 1195) is shared by all four reads, so they are four
measurements of one experiment rather than four experiments.

**A read ships as a gate only if its precision interval's LOWER bound clears the base rate's
UPPER bound.** The bio pattern was 6-for-6 at 31 labels and collapsed to 53% [32, 73] at 149.

## 1. Music versus speech — the highest-value read, and it FAILS

His distinction is about energy, not presence: *"a little dialogue is fine when the song is the
main focus… what's bad is when the dialogue IS the main event."* And presence provably cannot
work — moviezar (his "a lot of dialogue" example) and a page he called BEST both run **6 of 10
talking**.

`clip_speech` already measures both sides, so the read is their difference:
**`contrast_db = speech_dbfs − bed_dbfs`.** Low = the bed is as loud as the voice (song-led);
high = the voice sits above a quiet bed (dialogue-led).

| median contrast | |
|---|---:|
| pages he wants | **1.9 dB** |
| pages he does not | **2.7 dB** |

**They sit on top of each other.** The sweep, 70 pages, low-contrast = wanted:

| t (dB) | picked | rejected | precision | 95% Wilson | agreement |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 0 | 15 | 43 | 20.0% | [7.0, 45.2] | 56.9% |
| 2 | 24 | 34 | 29.2% | [14.9, 49.2] | 55.2% |
| 6 | 39 | 19 | 25.6% | [14.6, 41.1] | 39.7% |
| 12 | 48 | 10 | 29.2% | [18.2, 43.2] | 37.9% |

Base rate **27.6% [17.8, 40.2]**. **No threshold clears it — in-sample, out-of-sample, or
overall.** Precision is at or below chance everywhere.

**Why it fails, and it is not a bug:** the "10 of 12 dialogue clips under 6 dB, median 2.0 dB"
figure already on record turns out to describe *both* classes. Social video is loudness-
normalised and almost universally music-bedded, so speech-to-bed contrast is nearly constant
across the population and carries no information about whether he wants the page.

**A prediction from the brief that did NOT hold:** ~35% of clips were expected to carry
xHE-AAC that ffmpeg cannot decode. **72 of 72 clips decoded — 0% unmeasurable.** The machinery
to mark them UNKNOWN is built and tested regardless, because the next batch may differ.

## 2. The caption signal — his idea, and what the data said back

**The mechanism he proposed does not separate.** Tested directly on the 149:

| phrase | wanted | unwanted | lift |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| "follow for more" | 10 | 8 | 2.51 |
| "credit" | 6 | 6 | 1.94 |
| "link in bio" | 2 | 4 | **0.90** |
| "pov" | 0 | 3 | **0.00** |

Mining n-grams by lift instead surfaced rare content words — *fantasy, hero, interesting fact,
directors* — which scored **92.0% in-sample and 66–77% out-of-sample, clearing the base rate on
no out-of-sample threshold.** That is memorisation, and the split is what exposed it.

**But the surface he pointed at is real; the mechanism is length, not shared boilerplate:**

| median mean caption length | |
|---|---:|
| pages he wants | **605 chars** |
| pages he does not | **109 chars** |

**5.5x.** Scored properly:

| | best threshold | precision | 95% Wilson | base rate | clears? |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| in-sample (99) | 500 | 82.1% | [64.4, 92.1] | 38.6% [29.1, 49.1] | **yes** |
| **out-of-sample (50)** | 500 | 42.9% | **[21.4, 67.4]** | 25.5% [15.3, 39.5] | **NO** |
| all 149 | 500 | 69.0% | [54.0, 80.9] | 34.1% [26.6, 42.4] | yes |

It is the strongest of the three measured reads and **it still fails out of sample.** 14 pages
write no captions at all and are UNKNOWN, never zero.

## 4. Face recurrence — NOT MEASURED, and that is different from measuring zero

**`cv2` on this box is 5.0.0, which has no `CascadeClassifier` at all.** The Haar XML still
ships in `cv2.data.haarcascades`; the class that loads it is gone. The 5.0 replacement,
`FaceDetectorYN`, needs a YuNet ONNX model that is not bundled.

So all 70 pages returned `face_share = None`, the scorer reported **"nothing scoreable"**, and
**that is the honest state**. The first version of this code swallowed the `AttributeError` and
would have handed back a face_share of 0.0 — which scores as "no faces recur" and would have
rejected every creator page for a reason that has nothing to do with faces. `detector_status()`
now returns the reason instead.

**What WAS built and tested** is the half that governs the read — row 41's exemption. A
recurring face from a movie is a meme page, not a creator, and the exemption is unit-tested
both ways. Of 70 pages, **36 name screen content in their captions**, so the exemption would
have been load-bearing on more than half the sample had the detector existed.

**Cost to finish it: one 340 KB ONNX model.** That is a dependency decision, not a mid-round
improvisation, so it is surfaced rather than taken.

## 5. The ceiling — he wants 95–97%, and this sample cannot demonstrate it

At **n=149** a 97% result reads **[93.3, 99.0]** and a 90% result reads **[84.1, 93.8]**.
**Those overlap.** No result on this sample can distinguish "we hit the target" from "we are
seven points short", whatever the true value is.

To separate 97% from 90% with non-overlapping 95% intervals needs roughly **450–500 labels**;
to put a ±2-point interval around 96% needs about **900**. The 149 took two review rounds, so
the honest statement is that **the target is not demonstrable at the current label budget**,
independent of how good the filter becomes.

**And what these reads buy: nothing yet.** Three measured, three failing out of sample. The
value delivered is negative-shaped and real — four plausible ideas are now priced, and none of
them would have improved the gate if wired in on their in-sample numbers.
