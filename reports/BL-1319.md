## §1 — THE 70/30 MIX, AND THE CLAIM UNDER IT

His justification was specific and testable: editors are almost always approved **and editors
publish emails far more often**. The second half is the one that would pay for the allocation,
and it runs straight into BL-1317's finding that on TikTok **publishing an address
anti-correlates with being wanted** — 0 of his 37 WANT pages have one against 9 of 63 REJECTs.
That reproduced here exactly.

**The label had to be made.** Nothing on disk marks a page as an editing page: `edit_page` is
**void** as a TikTok disqualifier (brief C17), the old chip fired on 5 of 159 pages and does not
track his verdict — of the three inside his marked hundred he split them WANT / REJECT / REJECT
— and his own reason field is empty on 91 of 100. So **22 blind agents** classified 169 grids
against his verbatim C17 test — *"could this account put OUR SOUND over THEIR video?"* — knowing
neither his verdict nor whether the page publishes an address.

| class | n | publishes an address | rate | 95% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **EDITING** (edit + both) | 16 | **1** | **6.3%** | [1.1, 28.3] |
| **QUOTE / MEME** | 95 | **3** | **3.2%** | [1.1, 8.9] |
| **NEITHER** | 58 | **22** | **37.9%** | [26.6, 50.8] |

**Editors vs quote/meme: +3.1 points, Fisher p=0.468.** The point estimate is marginally higher
and the evidence does not support the claim. Stated honestly: this test could only have caught
an editor rate of **18.75% or above**, so it does not prove editors are *no* better — it proves
the "far more often" he is relying on is not there to be seen at this sample size.

**NEITHER against the other two combined: p ≈ 0.** That is the mechanism behind BL-1317's
anti-correlation, and it is not a coincidence. **An account publishes a business address because
it is a business** — a person selling their own face, a shop, a label. Those are exactly the
pages the rubric kills. On the marked subset the NEITHER class runs a **3.2% want rate**.

**What the 30% does buy.** Editors have the highest want rate of the three: **58.3% [32.0, 80.7]
against quote/meme's 50.9% [38.3, 63.4]** (p=0.755 — again not significant, but the direction
he expected). So:

> **The 30% allocation is defensible on approval rate and indefensible on addresses. It will
> buy pages he likes. It will not buy more ways to contact them.**

**And the supply is not there yet.** Editors are **12 of the 100 he marked (12%)** and **16 of
169 (9.5%)** across everything classified. Reaching 30% is a change to what is *sourced*, not a
re-weighting of what already arrives.

**Redaction is structural, not textual.** Every script here reads `p_email_present`, a
**boolean**. No address string is opened at any point, so there is no code path that could emit
one. That is a stronger guarantee than a masking function, which can be wrong — as one was.

## §2 — THE TWO-MONTH GATE: HE IS RIGHT, BL-1291 IS RIGHT, AND THE CODE HAS NEITHER

### Why he sees three-year-old pages

```
clippershq/tiktok_finder.py:226      "created": m.get("create_time") or m.get("createTime"),
```

That is the only appearance of the key in the module. Nothing reads it. `clippershq/
tiktok_triage.py` does implement `days_old(newest_epoch)` with `STALE_DAYS = 62`, and its only
importers are its own test and two scratch sweeps — **no production caller**.

**This is not a mis-tuned gate. It is an absent one.** The corpus shows the consequence: median
age 25.6 days, **p90 902 days, oldest 2,230 days — 6.1 years**, 16 pages over three years.

### Why BL-1291 measured a 40.5% massacre, and why that was true

BL-1291 scored the gate on `output/bl1291_agent_a/dataset.jsonl`, whose 257 labels are **251
JUDGE_PROXY and 6 HIS_MARK** — it said so itself: *"only six human TikTok marks exist in this
repo."* Its 40.5% priced the pages the **judge** wanted. His own 100 arrived later in BL-1298,
and **all 100 join this corpus**, 98 with a date. So the gate can now be priced in his currency
for the first time.

| cut | pages dropped of 98 | **his wanted killed of 37** | precision on the drop |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 30 d | 54 | **17 (46.0%)** | 0.685 |
| **62 d — his stated rule** | **46** | **13 (35.1%)** [21.8, 51.2] | 0.717 |
| 90 d | 39 | 11 (29.7%) | 0.718 |
| 180 d | 28 | 6 (16.2%) | 0.786 |
| 365 d | 23 | 4 (10.8%) | 0.826 |
| 730 d | 15 | 2 (5.4%) | 0.867 |
| **1095 d — three years** | **5** | **0 (0.0%)** | **1.000** |

**BL-1291's conclusion survives on his own marks: a two-month gate destroys a third of what he
wants.** 35.1% here against 40.5% there, intervals overlapping.

### The rule that does what he actually wants

**Cut at three years.** It drops 5 pages, every one of them a page he rejected, and costs
**zero** of his 37 wanted. The reason it is safe is visible in the data: **the oldest page he
marked WANT was last posted 1,011 days ago** — `funny.memes584` at 1,011 days and `michtmp` at
972 are pages he wants from accounts nearly three years dead. The tightest zero-loss cut is
**1,013 days**; 1,095 sits just above it with margin.

His instinct and his number are different things. *"A three-year-old page is worthless"* is
correct and now costed. *"Posted in the last two months"* is the same instinct with a number
that removes a third of his own picks.

### The honest caveat, and why it does not sink the recommendation

**94 of the 98 dates come from `PAID_recency_probe_bl1270`, which read `max(create_time)` over a
relevance-ranked `/v2/search` sample capped at 20 results before author filtering** — not over
the account's post feed. Nothing guarantees the newest post is in that sample. The evidence it
sometimes was not: pages the probe called STALE returned a median of **6** own videos against
FRESH pages' **10**, and on the 7 handles with an independent free clock (`aweme_id >> 32`,
validated to within a minute) **2 disagreed by 40–52 days, both in the false-STALE direction**.

**The error is one-directional, and that is what rescues the rule.** Taking the max over a
*subset* of posts can only under-state recency, so **measured age is an upper bound on true
age**. Therefore a "drop if measured age > 3 years" rule **never misses a genuinely dead page** —
it can only over-drop. And the over-drop is measured directly against his verdicts: 5 pages, 0
wanted. The recommendation stands; the input deserves fixing anyway, and `aweme_id >> 32` is
free.

**Also on the record: 38 of 249 ages are negative**, minimum −8.1 days — posts dated in the
future. Reported, never clipped to zero, because a rule is about to be built on this field.

## §3 — WHAT THE CODE DECIDES, AND WHY THE 30% CHANGES IT

BL-1318 shipped one code-only decision: **reject a page whose grid carries fewer than three
drawn-text lines** — 14.0% and 30.0% of two Instagram hundreds, **zero wanted lost** on both.

**It had never been run on TikTok, because it cannot be.** The shipped capture's
`on_screen_texts` is **empty on all 100** of his marked TikTok pages (`has_on_screen_text` true
on zero). The text was therefore read here with the same reader, floor and parsing BL-1318 used.

**Pooled on his TikTok 100 (want rate 37.0%): 15 decided by code, 85 reach the judge, precision
93.3%, and 1 of 37 wanted pages lost** — no longer zero.

**The pooled number hides the whole story:**

| class | n | decided by code | **his wanted lost** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **EDITING** | 12 | **6 — 50.0%** | **1 of 7 (14.3%)** |
| QUOTE / MEME | 57 | 1 — 1.8% | 0 of 29 |
| NEITHER | 31 | 8 — 25.8% | 0 of 1 |

**The rule fires on half of all editors and on 1.8% of meme pages — 28 times more often — and
C17 says exactly why: an edit page needs no written line at all.** *"Do not require a hook line.
Do not require a meme format. Do not require text of any kind."* A rule that rejects pages for
having no text is a rule that rejects editors.

**At his 70/30 mix: 16.2% decided by code, 83.8% reach the judge, ~2,011 tokens saved per page
judged — 2.01M per 1,000 pages — at a cost of 2.5 wanted pages per 100.** The alternative is to
scope the rule to the quote/meme lane, where it is free: 1.8% decided, zero wanted lost. That is
a much smaller saving honestly earned, and it is his call which he prefers.

**The threshold was not re-tuned.** BL-1318 showed K=9 is zero-loss on its tuning set and costs
a held-out wanted page. Re-tuning K here and reporting the best number would repeat that overfit
exactly.

### Per-tile OCR: it works, and it makes the shipped rule worse

Three arms, so tiling and resolution are separable. **A** the whole grid as BL-1318 read it,
**B** the whole grid at 2×, **C** nine cells at 3× with 12% overlap.

Of the 10 pages that read empty, **9 return something under per-tile**. What they return is:

```
"I=I"   "JDPR"   "日"   "H"   "D"   "BIGWIN"   "业"   "AIK"
```

Single glyphs and logo fragments — one plausible line in ten pages. And:

- **every one of the 10 empty pages is a page he REJECTED. Zero wanted pages were recovered.**
- the whole-grid read rejects all 10 correctly. Per-tile leaves only 2 under the threshold:
  **8 correct rejections lost, 0 wanted pages gained.**
- on 20 controls the mean line count rises 22.8 → 29.2, which pushes yet more pages over any
  line-count threshold.

> **The named cheapest gain is real as a text-recovery measure and negative as a system change.**
> It inflates the input to the one rule that ships, in the direction that switches the rule off,
> for noise.

**The caveat it deserves:** this crops the *stored downscaled* grid, so it upsamples noise. The
original cover bytes are never written to disk — `build_contact_sheet` pastes them into one PNG
and discards them. A true per-tile capture at native resolution might recover real text rather
than glyphs. That is untested and would need new capture, and this result is not evidence
against it.

rule scored above counts **lines, not words**, so it is unaffected — but anyone reusing this text
for a word rule must match both forms or repeat BL-1318's false refutation.
