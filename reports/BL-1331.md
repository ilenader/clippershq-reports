## 2. Does a cold TikTok DM arrive? Supported, not proven — and it barely affects the sheet

### The sheet is 100% Instagram

**Of the 1,669 DM targets: 0 are TikTok, 1,669 are Instagram.** Every row is a TikTok-discovered
lead whose DM target is an Instagram handle the owner published. `ig_dm_list.py` is named
accurately. **Nothing in the delivered spreadsheet depends on the TikTok DM question.**

### The exposure is in master, and it is enormous

| a TikTok lead in `master_leads.csv` (55,249 rows) | n | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| has an email → email it | 2,990 | 5.4% |
| no email, has an IG handle → the sheet above | 1,486 | 2.7% |
| **no email, no IG handle → TikTok DM is the only route** | **50,773** | **91.9%** |

**91.9% of the TikTok pool is reachable only by TikTok DM, or not at all.**

### What the documentation establishes

From a **primary source** — TikTok's own advertiser help for Messaging Ads:

> **"Set `Allow direct messages from` to `Everyone`."**

Two things follow without trusting anyone else: the setting exists and is named
`Allow direct messages from`, and **`Everyone` is not the default** — a platform does not
instruct users to opt in to a value they already have.

**Consistent across secondary sources** (not verbatim from TikTok): the options are Everyone /
Suggested friends / **Friends (mutual followers, the reported default)** / No one; DMs are 16+
only; 16–17s default to Friends and cannot choose Everyone.

**What I could not get:** a verbatim TikTok statement of the default. Every official page
redirects to `tiktok.com/support/faq_detail?id=…`, and **those pages render client-side** — a
fetch returns a shell containing the words "TikTok Support" and nothing else. *The same defeat
this round measured on profile pages: TikTok's documentation is as unreadable to a script as its
profiles are.*

**Verdict.** `ig_dm_list.py`'s docstring — *"TikTok restricts DMs from non-followers, so a cold
DM to a TikTok lead does not arrive"* — is **supported but not proven**. And **BL-1321's "TikTok
is a DM channel" was reached without checking deliverability at all.** Its four supporting lines
all still hold; none of them is evidence that a message *arrives*.

**What would settle it, cheapest first:**
1. **Open the app on a fresh adult account** → Settings and privacy → Privacy → Direct messages,
   and read `Allow direct messages from` *before touching it*. One minute, free, primary. **Do
   this one.**
2. **Two of our own accounts**, A not following B, B left at default, one message. Definitive
   for the default case — a send, so it needs your go-ahead, but it is a send to ourselves.
3. Even a confirmed default leaves the real question: **what share of the 50,773 opted in to
   Everyone?** Nothing in the payload exposes that setting, so it can only come from send
   outcomes — measure on a small batch first.

**Until step 1: do not count those 50,773 as reachable leads.**

## 3. The free profile read: works on TikTok, dead on Instagram

**130 fetches, 120 handles across both platforms, plus 10 browser-UA controls. Zero cost.**

| | Instagram | TikTok |
|---|---:|---:|
| n | 60 | 60 |
| **OK — counts parsed** | **0 — 0.0%** | **57 — 95.0%** |
| SHELL — 200, no `og:description` | 60 — 100.0% | 0 |
| generic placeholder — 200, og tag, no counts | 0 | 3 — 5.0% |
| HTTP 404 / 429 / 5xx / timeout | **0** | **0** |

**Failures by cause, not as one rate.** The three TikTok non-successes are **not** shells and
**not** 404s: TikTok returned HTTP 200 with its generic `og:description` —
*"Watch, follow, and discover more trending content."* — carrying no counts. Deleted, private,
banned or region-blocked are indistinguishable from that response, so it is reported as its own
bucket rather than folded into an account explanation.

**Volume behaviour: no degradation.** TikTok first half 96.7%, second half 93.3% — noise at
n=30. Instagram was **flat 0% from the very first request**, which is a constant, not a decay
curve: it was already failing before this run started.

**Rate limiting: none observed.** Zero 429s across 130 fetches at ~1.9 s/fetch.

**Browser-UA control: 10 of 10 SHELL.** The User-Agent is doing the work where the trick works
at all.

### Does it match the paid vendor? Yes — and it is fresher

TikTok, 57 handles with a paid follower count already on disk:

| | |
|---|---|
| median relative difference | **4.1%** |
| within 10% of the paid value | 42 of 57 = 73.7% |
| more than 10% apart | 14 of 57 = 24.6% |
| **of those 14, how many are free HIGHER** | **14 of 14 = 100%** |

**Every large disagreement runs the same way: the free read is higher.** A noisy or wrong read
would scatter in both directions. One-directional divergence is a growing account measured more
recently — **the free read is not disagreeing with the paid data, it is more current than it.**
Exact equality is mostly unavailable because the platform rounds (`30.0k`, `1.1m`); the closest
five agree to within 0–3 followers.

### A parser bug I shipped and caught, which is the reason for the third bucket

My first smoke test reported **6 of 6 SHELL across both platforms** and I nearly concluded the
trick was dead everywhere. It was not. TikTok writes
`30.0k Followers, 9530 Following, 1.1m Likes` — **commas** — and my pattern expected a period
before `Likes`. **Every working TikTok read parsed to `None` and was bucketed as "the page gave
us nothing".** A live, correct, free answer recorded as an absence.

It was caught by the recovery probe, which only asks *"is there an `og:description` at all"* and
disagreed with the parser. The fix is not just the regex: `SHELL` (no tag) and `SHAPE_CHANGED`
(tag present, unparseable) are now **different buckets**, because merging them is what hid this.

### Verdict: wire nothing, and turn one thing OFF

**It cannot replace the calls you might hope.** `tiktok_finder.profile_of` is *"ONE billed
request → {bio, followers, videos_total, emails, …}"* and **it is bought for the bio** — that is
where the email comes from. The free read returns followers / following / likes and **cannot see
the bio**. So:

| call | can the free read replace it? |
|---|---|
| TikTok `profile_of` (the funnel's per-lead call) | **No** — bought for the bio; free read has no bio |
| Instagram profile call | **No** — 0% success today |
| An auxiliary lookup needing only follower/post counts | **Yes**, on TikTok, at 95% |

**Saving per 1,000 pages in the shipped funnel: $0.00.** Every one of those calls is still
needed. The saving is real only in auxiliary use — refreshing a stale follower count, or the
kind of identity work in BL-1321 where follower and post counts were the decisive evidence — and
there it is one avoided TikTok call per page, **$1.00 per 1,000 at 100% coverage, $0.95 at the
measured 95%**. That is worth having, but it is not a funnel saving and should not be booked as
one.

**And Instagram's scraper is currently ON and returning nothing.** `ig_follower_scrape_enabled`
is `True` in `config.json`, capped at 100/run, and measured today at **0 of 60**. Its failure
handling is sound — *"a failed scrape leaves followers UNKNOWN (never cuts)"*, and only
successes are cached — so it is not corrupting anything. But it now spends up to 100 HTTP
requests per run for zero return, against an IP Instagram is already shelling, and its own
docstring warns: *"If IG flags/limits that residential IP it can spill into the operator's
personal IG/FB sessions on the same connection."*

**Recommendation: set `ig_follower_scrape_enabled` to `false`.** I have not flipped it — it is
your config, two other rounds are live against it, and the risk it names is yours to weigh.

**One honesty note:** BL-1330 (mine, earlier today) tested this on three handles, got three
successes, and recommended reusing it. Three was not enough, exactly as you said. The
transition to 0% happened somewhere between that test and this one, and I cannot pin whether it
was cumulative requests from this machine or a platform-side change — Instagram was already at
0% on the first request of this round, which is more consistent with the latter.
