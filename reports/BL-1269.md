## The answer in one sentence

**A 70% pass rate cannot be filtered into existence.** From today's 19% base rate it needs a
pre-sort that kills **89.9%** of rejects at perfect recall, and the measured text/DOM ceiling
is **44.2%** — so the pass rate tops out at **29.6%** no matter how good the filter is.
**70% needs the base rate itself at ~57%, a 3.0× on candidate share, which is a discovery
problem.** And the pre-sort he was told "loses nothing" loses **13 of 148 wanted pages out of
sample**, so it ships as an **ordering**, never as a gate.

---

## 1. The arithmetic that decides the round

With a pre-sort killing fraction *k* of rejects at perfect recall, from base rate *b*:
`pass = b / (b + (1−b)(1−k))`.

| base rate | kill needed for 70% | within the 44.2% text/DOM ceiling? |
|---:|---:|:--|
| **19.0%** (today) | **89.9%** | no |
| 30.0% | 81.6% | no |
| 50.0% | 57.1% | no |
| 55.0% | 47.6% | no |
| **56.6%** | **44.2%** | **the break-even** |
| 60.0% | 35.7% | yes |

And what filtering actually delivers from today's supply:

| | pass rate |
|---|---:|
| today, no pre-sort | 19.0% |
| BL-1261's union (28 of 81 rejects) | **26.4%** |
| the text/DOM ceiling at perfect recall | **29.6%** |

**Discovery must multiply candidate share by 3.0× before the filter has anything to work
with.** Everything below follows from that.

## 2. Why the "loses nothing" pre-sort must not be a gate

BL-1261's own artefact says of its four signals: *"in-sample: these four were CHOSEN for
killing no WANTs on this same set"*, and two of the four carry a **FITTED** cut point. A rule
selected for scoring perfectly on a set, and then reported at its score on that set, has no
error bar. This round re-scored it on a population it was never fitted to: **922 browser-judged
pages, 148 wanted, deduped by handle.**

| | in sample (his 100) | out of sample (n=922) |
|---|---|---|
| `news_category` (prespecified) | 5 killed, **0 WANTs** | *not testable — `category` is not recorded in the older corpus* |
| `follows_1500_plus` (prespecified) | 14 killed, **0 WANTs** | 72 killed, **8 WANTs**, 88.9% [79.6, 94.3] |
| `ratio_under_5` (FITTED) | 7 killed, **0 WANTs** | 31 killed, **3 WANTs**, 90.3% [75.1, 96.7] |
| `two_business_words` (FITTED) | 9 killed, **0 WANTs** | 29 killed, **2 WANTs**, 93.1% [78.0, 98.1] |
| **UNION** | 28 killed, **0 WANTs**, pass 26.4%, **SHIPS** | **124 killed, 13 WANTs**, 89.5% [82.9, 93.8], pass 16.9%, **DOES NOT SHIP** |

The ship bar is BL-1261's own: precision lower bound must clear the reject base rate's upper
bound (86.2% out of sample) **and** zero wanted pages killed. **The union clears neither.** It
loses **8.8% of wanted pages** and buys **0.8 points** of pass rate.

I swept ten candidate signals against the same 922. **Not one ships.** The closest, an empty
bio, reaches 96.9% [84.3, 99.4] — its lower bound still misses 86.2%.

> This is the same failure the existing code gate has, only smaller: `_ig_prescreen_full` kills
> **17.4% [10.2, 28.0]** of wanted pages, this union kills **8.8%**. The bar is zero, so
> neither is a gate. **`presort.py` therefore returns two lists and discards nothing** —
> `presort(authors) -> (judge_first, deferred)`, and concatenating them restores the pool
> exactly. A test pins that.

### Corroborated independently, on a different platform

The BL-1260 resume ran the same four signals against its TikTok population in parallel with
this round and found them killing **3 of 13 wanted pages** there — including a polarity trap
where `follows_1500_plus` *inverts* on TikTok. Two populations, two platforms, two rounds that
did not consult each other, **and the same conclusion: the pre-sort does not transfer off the
set it was fitted to.**

### A defect this found in my own module

The first out-of-sample run showed `follows_1500_plus` and `ratio_under_5` firing **zero
times on 922 pages** — which reads like a clean negative result. It was a broken predicate:
the older corpus stores `followers`/`following` as **digit strings** (`'56000000'`, `'1003'`),
and an `isinstance(v, (int, float))` test silently disabled two of the four signals. Fixed
with `_as_number`, which also reads the `1.5K` / `2.3M` forms the DOM renders, and still
refuses anything unreadable. The numbers in the table above are post-fix. `tests/test_bl1269_presort.py::CountsArriveAsStrings` pins it in both directions.

## 3. Every correct reject, grouped by cause

Two populations, never summed, because their labels differ in quality.

*(The brief asked for "the 211 earlier verdicts". I could not reproduce a 211: the browser-judged
corpus with written reasons is **671 distinct handles carrying 503 rejects** across 13 files,
and the BL-1240/BL-1242 iteration files alone are 297 distinct handles. I used the widest
defensible corpus rather than guess which subset 211 meant, and both counts are stated so the
figure can be reconciled.)*

| cause | **A. his 100** (n=79 correct rejects, ground truth) | **B. earlier browser verdicts** (n=503 rejects / 671 handles, advisory) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `creator` | **30 — 38.0%** | **217 — 43.1%** |
| `business` | 19 — 24.1% | 76 — 15.1% |
| `not_english` | 8 — 10.1% | 16 — 3.2% |
| `dialogue` | 6 — 7.6% | 65 — 12.9% |
| `news` | 4 — 5.1% | 47 — 9.3% |
| `edit_page` | 4 — 5.1% | 12 — 2.4% |
| `topical_aggregator` | 3 — 3.8% | 28 — 5.6% |
| `lyrics_ambient` | 3 — 3.8% | 8 — 1.6% |
| `promo` / `no_video` / unchipped | 2 — 2.6% | 34 — 6.8% |

**His estimate, checked:** he said *"roughly half are personal creators and most of the rest
businesses or news."* Measured: **personal creators 38.0% on his own marks and 43.1% on the
wider set — directionally right, materially high.** Business + promo + news + aggregator is
**34.2% / 31.8%**. Together those two buckets are **72.2% / 74.9%** of all rejects, so the
remaining quarter — dialogue, not-English, edit pages, ambient-lyrics — is a real tail that no
"creators and businesses" rule reaches.

**The cheapest signal per cause, and why the big ones stay expensive:** `creator` (the largest
bucket at 38–43%) is decided by *whose footage it is*, which is a picture question — the DOM
tells you a category, not authorship, and category is the signal that turned out to be
unrecorded on 100% of the older corpus. `not_english` and `news` are the only causes with a
genuinely cheap tell, and together they are **15.2%** of his rejects. That is the mechanism
behind the 44.2% ceiling: **the causes that dominate are the ones text cannot see.**

## 4. Discovery: the question is currently unanswerable, and that is the finding

Ranking paths by candidate share needs to know which path found each page. **It is not
recorded for 70.1% of them** — 1,885 of 2,688 pages in `meme_pages_seen.json` carry no
`found_via` at all.

| path | n | candidates | share | 95% CI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `known_repost_account` | 41 | 12 | **29.3%** | [17.6, 44.5] |
| *(unrecorded)* | 371 | 62 | 16.7% | [13.3, 20.8] |

*(browser-judge labels; every other named path has n < 8 and is not reportable. The
pipeline-flag view shows `known_repost_account` at 100%, which is circular — that pipeline
auto-passes known repost accounts — and is excluded from any conclusion.)*

**So the one path with enough data does beat the unattributed pool — 29.3% against 16.7% —
but the intervals overlap and n=41 cannot carry a 3× supply decision.** The prerequisite for
§3 is instrumentation, not analysis: **record `found_via` at the moment of discovery for every
page.** Until then no discovery strategy can be compared to another on candidate share, and
the 3.0× the target needs cannot be steered.

Two things worth keeping from the existing evidence while that gap is closed: seeding from
**random hop-1 candidates** returned 77.1% [75.5, 78.7] fresh against 46.0% [44.2, 47.9] from
known-good pages, because good pages' neighbourhoods are already mined — but **fresh is not
the same as candidate**, and only the join above can tell them apart. And **follower size was a
proxy, not the mechanism**: his 16 reference pages have a **median of 13,350 followers**, and
only one sits in the 250k–1M band the 874-address harvest was ordered into.

## 5. The contamination guard, and it is now a test

Brief v7 named **six** of the hundred pages it was scored on, with their tile counts and
verdicts written out, so its score was never blind. `scratch/bl1269_leakguard.py` refuses
that, and it runs in the suite:

| brief | result |
|---|---|
| `bl1242_brief_v4.md` | **CLEAN** |
| `bl1262_brief_v7.md` | **LEAKS 6** — `acog.hitbox`, `thedunkcentralhq`, `maestremoros`, `aboutfacts_786`, `cultr.ent`, `filmoviment` |
| `bl1265_brief_v8.md` | **CLEAN** |
| `bl1260_brief_addendum.md` | **CLEAN** |

The guard matches **handle-shaped** occurrences only. One of the hundred handles is literally
`drop`, and a naive substring check flags the phrase *"dropping the gate to one"* — a guard
that cries wolf is a guard someone switches off. So a short all-alphabetic handle counts only
when decorated (`@drop`, `` `drop` ``); a handle carrying a dot, underscore or digit counts
bare. Both directions are pinned, and **v7 is kept on disk as the counter-example: if the test
ever passes on v7, the guard is broken.**

## 6. End to end, and the number he asked for

Real batch: his 100, labels = his marks, judging at his stated 10.18 pages/min.

| | pages in | deferred | judged | wanted found | pass rate | wanted/hour | wanted lost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **before** | 100 | 0 | 100 | 19 | 19.0% | **116.1** | 0 |
| **as a GATE** *(not recommended)* | 100 | 28 | 72 | 19 | 26.4% | **161.2** | 0 in sample / **13 of 148 out of sample** |
| **as ORDERING** *(ships)* | 100 | 28 → judged last | 100 | 19 | 26.4% first tranche | **161.2** over the first tranche | **0, by construction** |

**Ordering buys the identical 1.39× — time-to-all-wanted 9.82 min → 7.07 min — and cannot lose
a page, because the deferred pile is judged last rather than never.** That is the whole
argument for it: the speed-up does not depend on the signals being right, only the *order*
does. On the free sub-agent path (6.46 pages/min at 12 lanes, BL-1267) the same 1.39× gives
73.6 → 102.3 wanted/hour.

**Pass rate reached: 26.4%, against his 70% target.** The gap is not closable here.

## 7. What I did not do, and why

**`clippershq/main.py` was declared and deliberately left unchanged.** Its only pre-judge gate
is `_ig_prescreen_full`, the one measured killing one in six wanted pages, and wiring a second
filter beside it would have made this module a gate — which §2 shows it must not be. There is
no product-code judging queue to order; judging is driven from the scratch harnesses. **The
honest wiring is the module plus its tests, and a call site added when the judge queue itself
moves into product code.** Shipping `presort.py` as a gate today would have been the same
mistake, with better manners.
