# BL-1129 — what we are paying for, and whether anything hears audio

Read-only audit. **No paid call was made; every figure is from disk.** No credential value
appears anywhere in this report.

---

## The short answer

**You are not paying for a sound API. There is no music-recognition vendor in this system,
and there never has been.** Nothing on the ledger identifies a song, a genre, a mood or a
beat from a waveform.

But the premise needs one correction, and it is the useful half:

**Things DO hear audio here — three of them — and all three are local and free.** What is
missing is not ears. It is *identification*. Nobody is being paid to tell you what song is
playing, and nothing on this machine is trying to.

And there is one line where you are paying for audio you do not use:

> **Every clip sent to the vision model is sent with its audio track attached, tokenised and
> billed — and the prompt never asks about sound.** That has cost **$0.20 – $0.25** of the
> $11.72 lifetime bill.

**MusicBrainz is the one most likely to be mistaken for a sound API and it is not one.** It
is a music *metadata* database queried by identifier and text — artist, release, country,
URL relationships. No audio is uploaded. **AcoustID**, the MusicBrainz project that *does*
fingerprint audio, appears nowhere in this codebase.

### The one that receives audio and bills for it

`control._vision_ask_fn` base64-encodes the **whole MP4** and sends it as
`data:video/mp4;base64,…`. Google's own documentation states Gemini processes *"information
from both the **audio and visual** streams"* and tokenises **audio at 32 tokens per second**
alongside 258 tokens per video frame at 1 fps.

The prompt asks for `scene`, `title`, `on_screen_text`, `posted_date`, `beats`. **Not one
word about sound.** No stored field holds an audio answer.

**Measured, from a stored probe (`scratch/bl960_cost.json`) — not from list price:**

| | |
|---|---|
| a 30 s clip | **8,497 prompt tokens = 283.2 tok/s** |
| Google `countTokens` on the same clip | 9,121 = 304.0 tok/s |
| video-only tokenisation would be | 258 tok/s |
| **the excess is the audio track** | 25.2 tok/s measured, 32 tok/s published |
| audio share of billed prompt tokens | **8.9% – 11.0%** |
| **cost of that audio, lifetime** | **$0.201 – $0.249** |

**The rate question is settled by the receipt.** Google lists audio input at **$0.30/M**
against **$0.10/M** for video, which would have made audio 27% of the input bill. The stored
OpenRouter receipt shows `usd_in_per_m = $0.10` for the whole video-plus-audio prompt — so
the 3× audio premium **does not apply on this path**. The cheap reading is the correct one.

**What it would save to strip it:** ~$0.22 to date, and about **$0.0001 per future label**.
That is not a reason to change anything on cost grounds. It is worth knowing because it is
the only line in the bill with the word "audio" attached to it, and the answer to *"am I
paying for sound?"* is *"only this, only by accident, and only about 22 cents of it."*

### What actually hears audio — all local, all free

| | cost | what it decides |
|---|---|---|
| `clip_speech.py` — Silero VAD via faster-whisper | **$0**, on this machine | `speech_frac` and the four-way audio class (music-only / dialogue-over-music / dialogue-only / silent). **This is what gates dialogue clips out of selection.** |
| `edit.py` / `duck.py` — ffmpeg `volumedetect`, `sidechaincompress` | **$0** | source loudness, bed gain, ducking threshold, and the 40–250 Hz correlation the verifier uses to prove the configured track is really in the finished file |
| `song_loudness.py` | **$0** | per-song LUFS / LRA, which decides whether a track is too dense to sit under dialogue |

## 2. `track_title`, traced end to end

| | |
|---|---|
| **where it comes from** | Instagram's own metadata: `clip_metadata.music_info.music_asset_info.title`, read at `clip_runner.py:711`. It is **the song the original poster picked in Instagram's audio picker** — a string they selected, not anything measured. |
| **which vendor** | HikerAPI, on the `/v2/user/clips` page that is already being fetched |
| **what it costs** | **$0 marginal.** It rides on a page billed at $0.0006 whether the field is read or not. "Believed free" is confirmed. |
| **fill** | **784 of 2,731 clips = 28.7%** |
| **how far it reaches** | maps to a mood for **206 clips = 7.5% of the library**, through a 19-entry `track_title_mood_map` (melancholy 109, warm 68, triumphant 15, hype 14) |
| **is it still read?** | **Computed, then refused.** `song_library._match_any_tier` still evaluates the tier, but `match(content_evidence_only=True)` parks it. |
| **who disabled it** | **BL-1127**, after your fake-note complaint. `CONTENT_EVIDENCE_TIERS = ('VISION_RULE',)`. |
| **where it is refused** | `clip_pipeline.pick_song` (the render path) and `clip_pipeline.servable_by` (the ordering predicate) both pass `content_evidence_only=True` |
| **also forbidden in ranking** | `clip_pipeline._RANK_FORBIDDEN` has carried `track_title` since before this round |

Verified live on a real clip:

```
match(default)          -> TRACK_TITLE_MOOD  'track_title:Song for Denise -> mood:warm'
match(content_evidence) -> PARK  "TRACK_TITLE_MOOD is not evidence about this clip's
                                 picture -- refused [...] (spec 7: never force a match)"
pick_song (RENDER path) -> tier=none
```

**One caller still uses the loose default:** `dashboard/server.py:1331` calls
`song_library.match(r, store)` without the flag, so the dashboard's mood/renderable panel
still counts track-title matches the renderer will refuse. It overstates supply. Named in
BL-1128 as still-broken; this is the exact line.

## 4. What it would cost to actually identify the song, at 2,731 clips

Two things are **closed with numbers** and are not re-proposed: asking a language model to
classify a clip's audio genre or mood measured **40% reproducibility** (the same clip
returning different answers on repeated calls), and automatic drop detection from audio
returned a confident timestamp on **all 36** test clips including **all 19 with nothing
there** — 100% fabrication.

| option | per query | **at 2,731 clips** | no-match billed? | sped-up / pitched edits | egress |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AudD** | **$5 / 1,000** = $0.005; 300 free; volume discount to $2/1,000 | **≈ $12.16** | **not stated in their docs — I could not verify it** | not addressed in their docs | **audio file or URL leaves the building** |
| **ACRCloud** | **behind a login — I could not verify it from a primary source.** A secondary source reports ¥320 / 10,000 (~$0.0044) | **≈ $12 if that figure holds** | unverified | marketed as robust; unverified | audio or a fingerprint leaves the building |
| **Panako** (open source, AGPL) | **$0 licence** | **$0 licence + your CPU** | n/a | **explicitly designed for it — reliable to ±10% pitch and time-scale modification** | **none — runs local** |
| **Chromaprint / AcoustID** | **$0**, public database | **$0** | n/a | **the docs make no excerpt or speed/pitch claim**; it is built for whole-file identification of known recordings | none — fingerprint only |

**The structural point that decides this table.** Open-source fingerprinting matches only
against a reference database **you already hold**. Panako will tell you a clip contains
*song01* because song01 is on your disk — which you already know. It cannot name a
commercial track you do not have. **The licensed catalogue is exactly what AudD and ACRCloud
sell**, and it is not substitutable by running software locally.

**Data egress, flagged as asked:** AudD takes the audio file or a URL, so clip audio — content
you do not own the rights to — leaves the building to a third party. ACRCloud's flow is the
same class of decision. Panako and Chromaprint keep everything local. If the audio-ID route is
ever taken, that is a decision to make deliberately, not a side effect of a library call.

**And $12.16 is more than this system has spent in its entire life** ($11.72).

## 5. Is it worth it? No — and the reason is not the price

**Identifying the song answers a question the matcher has already ruled inadmissible.**

An audio fingerprint on a source clip tells you *what track the reposter laid over it*. That
is **the same proposition `track_title` already gives you, free, at 28.7% fill** — and
BL-1127 refused it as evidence three days ago, for a reason that does not change if the
signal gets more reliable:

> `track_title` is the reposter's own background audio. It says what the poster laid over
> the clip, never what happens in it. *"Mother Nature → warm"* is a fact about a production
> music library, not about a boy holding a forged banknote.

Six of ten renders in the batch you rejected were matched that way. **Paying $12 to buy a
higher-fidelity version of a signal we just threw out is spending money to re-acquire a
rejected input.** A perfect answer to the wrong question is still the wrong answer.

The mood mismatch is not caused by not knowing the source's music. It is caused by not
knowing what the **clip** is about.

### What to do instead, and it is free

**The source's own on-screen text is the signal, and it is already extracted.**

* RapidOCR is vendored, local, **$0**, ~1 s per clip. BL-1128 already ran it over 295 clips.
  A full sweep of 2,731 is **~46 minutes of local CPU and no money at all.**
* BL-1128 measured that **9 of 60 clips (15%)** carry a real hook burned into the video while
  the caption we currently read is a film blurb or a hashtag block — *"I wasn't ready to cry
  this hard today"* sitting under a Futurama cast list.
* That text describes **what is happening in the clip**, which is the question the matcher
  actually asks. It is the only free signal in the system that does.

**The honest cost comparison:**

| | cost | answers |
|---|---|---|
| audio fingerprinting, 2,731 clips | **$12.16** + third-party egress | what the reposter's backing track is — *a signal already refused* |
| OCR sweep, 2,731 clips | **$0.00**, ~46 min local | what the clip's own text says it is about — *the question being asked* |

**Recommendation: buy no audio API.** Spend the round on reading the text that is already
on the screen, and on adding songs — BL-1128 measured that the four-song library, not any
filter, is what caps output at 7.9% of meme-format clips.

  vision bill, because audio input lists at 3× video. The stored OpenRouter receipt shows the
  whole prompt billed at the video rate, which is 2.5× lower. The receipt beat the price list.
