### Why it was wrong

`measure_label_card` finds the card by walking out from the ink band while rows are STATIC.
A letterbox bar is exactly as static as a caption panel. Run against all twelve of his staged
sources, the live code returned **card top = 0 on twelve of twelve**. `LABEL_TOP_CLEAR_FRAC`
then took 20% of that, so the strip was being sized to the letterbox rather than to his text.

An independent frame measurement says the same thing from the other side: on six of seven
pairs the strip top sat at **exactly 20.0% of the letterbox** and its height at **80.0%**,
padding symmetric to the pixel, and on five of seven **more than half the white was empty**.

The three fixtures in `tests/test_label_offset.py` all read `card = (0, 0, ...)`. Every clip
he personally reviewed had this, and the assertion above them could not see it, because it
computed `(strip_top - card_top) / strip_height` and required 0.20-0.30 — which under the old
policy is the identity `0.2ch / 0.8ch = 0.25` for every card that has ever existed. It passed
on arithmetic and could not fail on geometry. It is rewritten to bound the gap above **his
ink**, in pixels.

### What shipped, and what I tried first and threw away

Shipped: a clamp in `label_strip_from_card`. The strip starts at most
`min(40 px, 0.28 x his ink height)` above his first row of ink, applied before the existing
coverage clamp so coverage still wins. It cannot refuse a clip and cannot return None.

Rejected: gating the card walk on the row's TONE as well as its motion, which is the deeper
fix. It finds his real card exactly — on @theclipbuzzz it returns top 420, matching the frame
measurement to the row — but it returned **None on seven of twelve**, because a
high-percentile tone tracks the surface under dark-on-light type and not under light-on-dark,
and a card that cannot be measured is a clip that gets refused. Kept with its row-by-row tone
traces at `scratch/bl1215/clip_pipeline_TONEGATE_rejected.py`.

## 4. The ENOSPC: named, proven, and fixed

**It was never the disk.** `fc#0` in *"Error sending frames to consumers: No space left on
device"* is the AUDIO filter graph — `-loglevel verbose` prints
`Binding input with label '1:a'`, which is the song bed. The bed leg is infinite by
construction (`aloop=loop=-1`) and `hook_chain` front-loads it with `adelay=<place_at_s>` so
the drop lands on the action. `adelay` generates that silence as fast as the CPU allows, so
the audio races ahead of the video by the whole placement offset. ffmpeg holds that lead in a
sync queue capped by `-shortest_buf_duration`, **default 10 seconds**, and reports overflow as
`AVERROR(ENOSPC)`.

Measured on his own failing clip, same command, only the flag changed:

| buffer | result |
|---|---|
| default (10 s) | ENOSPC, truncated to 12.98 s |
| 2 s | ENOSPC, truncated to 12.82 s |
| **30 s** | **OK, 18.60 s** |
| **60 s** | **OK, 18.60 s** |

**Duration was a red herring.** The 9.78 s clip that always shipped fine fails at
`adelay >= 9500`; the 18.62 s clip that failed renders cleanly at every delay up to 12000 and
dies only at 12770. Longer clips only correlate because the drop lands later in them. The
output always ended at `adelay + ~0.05 s` — the exact moment the silence stops and real bed
samples arrive. It also explains why the same clip rendered at 17:36 and failed at 20:26 from
a byte-identical config: at `adelay=10770` it sits on the cap and the config randomises the
x264 preset, so the outcome flipped with encoder speed.

Fixed in `memebot/scraper/edit.py` by passing `-shortest_buf_duration 300`, sized against the
20.0 s ceiling rather than the observed 12.8 s failure. The buffer is a cap, not an
allocation.

**And it is proven through the pipeline, not just on the bare command.** @wasted
(`DWH-XU-gByq`, 18.6 s, `adelay=12770`) had failed with ENOSPC in every batch since 17:15 the
previous day, including the first batch of this round, which started before the flag went in.
In the verification batch, with the flag:

```
ok -> 07_wasted__OURS.mp4  (When she liked a "when he" reel but I never did that)
```

**It is a partial fix and I will not overstate it.** The other long clip, @animeotaku
(`Db1Jihts6Au`, 18.8 s, `adelay=10770`), **still fails with the same ENOSPC** at
`-shortest_buf_duration 300`:

```
2026-08-11T12:48:23  instagram/animeotaku_amv10k  edit:fail
    [fc#0] Error sending frames to consumers: No space left on device
```

So raising the buffer closes the larger offset and not the smaller one, which means the cap
is not the only bound in play and my model of it is incomplete. The second remedy the
diagnosis produced is untried: bound the audio leg explicitly with `-t <video duration>`
rather than relying on `-shortest` at all, which also rendered the failing clip in isolation
(`outdur=18.621995` exactly). That is the next thing to do and it is not done.

## 5. The stutter: OneDrive is not the cause

Tested and **not supported**. Reads of the same files inside the sync root and outside it:

| metric | in sync root | outside | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| cache-bypassing read | 470-1117 MB/s | 707-1773 MB/s | out 1.5x faster |
| **first 2 MB latency** | 1.21-1.92 ms | 0.93-2.32 ms | **1.01x, no difference** |
| worst paced stall, all files | **15.15 ms** | 30.50 ms | in was better |

There is a real placeholder penalty on the throughput path, up to 3.3x on the largest file.
It is irrelevant at this scale: these videos need **0.07-0.43 MB/s** and the slowest in-root
read was ~470 MB/s, three orders of magnitude of headroom. The metric that would actually
cause his symptom, time to the first 2 MB, is identical. A visible pause needs hundreds of
milliseconds; the worst stall measured was 15 ms.

**Moving renders out of the sync root buys throughput nothing in this workload can spend.**
This batch was written outside it anyway, so he can compare directly, but I am not claiming
it as the fix.

**One system-health finding worth his attention.** OneDrive.exe has burned **50,820 s of CPU
in 15.3 hours — about 92% of one core, continuously** — and did not convert a newly written
40 MB file to a placeholder within 15 minutes. It is badly backlogged. On a low-end PC that
is a plausible cause of a whole machine feeling sluggish during playback, and it is a CPU
problem rather than a file-read one. Not proven to be his stutter; worth killing and retrying
as a two-minute test.
