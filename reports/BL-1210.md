# BL-1210 — the 34% was a reading error. The filter scored 72%, the prediction was right, and nothing I tried beats it

**The fresh 50 did not measure 34%. It measured 72.0% [58.3, 82.5].** The last round
predicted 74% [66, 81]. The intervals overlap almost entirely and the prediction was
correct.

The 34% comes from reading his `mark` field as a verdict on the page. It is not one. It
records whether **the filter's call was right**, and under that reading every correct
rejection — the majority of a filter's work — is counted as a disagreement.

So this round changes **no rule**. I built and scored four candidates including his own
stated capability test, and not one beats the shipped filter out of sample. That is the
finding, with numbers.

Every figure came from **`.venv\Scripts\python.exe`, Python 3.11.9**. Bare `python` is 3.13
and lacks the packages. **Vendor receipt: $0.0000 — this round made no paid API call.**

## 1. The gap, reconciled before anything was touched

Three candidate causes, all separable, all tested.

### A. THE READING — this is the cause

`output/fresh50/REVIEW_50_FRESH.html` line 361, the sheet he actually used:

```js
tag.textContent = (m.m==='GOOD') ? 'saved: RIGHT CALL' : 'saved: WRONG CALL';
```

and `OPEN_FRESH_50.bat` documents the keys as `G = right call`, `B = wrong call + why`,
`R = right call, WRONG reason`. So `mark:"GOOD"` means **the filter's verdict was correct**,
and his desired verdict has to be *derived*:

```
wants_pass = (filter_verdict == "PASSED") == (mark == "GOOD")
```

Every formula, computed on the same 50 rows:

| how `mark` is read | agreement |
|---|---|
| **CORRECT — "was the call right"** | **72.0% [58.3, 82.5]  36/50** |
| read as a verdict on the page | 40.0% [27.6, 53.8]  20/50 |
| same, over the raw 52-row file | 30.8%  16/52 |
| "of the rows he keyed GOOD, how many were PASSED" | 44.4% [29.5, 60.4]  16/36 |
| of what the filter PASSED, he wants it (precision) | 61.5% [42.5, 77.6]  16/26 |
| of the pages he wants, PASSED (recall) | 80.0% [58.4, 91.9]  16/20 |
| **base rate — what chance scores** | 40.0% [27.6, 53.8]  20/50 |

**Every page-verdict misreading lands in a 31–40% band. The correct reading lands at 72.0%.**
"Roughly 34%" sits inside the misreading band and nowhere near the correct one.

The sheet itself never displays an agreement figure — it shows only saved/waiting counts —
so the number was computed outside it, from the file, with `mark` taken at face value.

## 5. A bug of mine that inverted the round, caught before publishing

My first pass reported that **68% of the corpus had been judged on three posts** and built a
whole diagnosis on it. It was wrong, and it was mine.

`scratch/bl1196_pages.jsonl` and `scratch/bl1201_pages.jsonl` hold **the same 425 handles**.
bl1196 carries **twelve** posts for 379 of them; bl1201 is a truncated re-fetch carrying
**three** for 411. My loader took the first file listed, and bl1201 was listed first, so 411
pages were judged on a three-post truncation of a twelve-post fetch.

bl1196 is deeper on **409 of the 425** shared handles. The loader now keeps the **deepest
record per handle**, which cannot be got wrong by reordering a list. The true depth:

| posts fetched | pages |
|---|---|
| 12 | **549** |
| 11 | 13 |
| 1–10 | 48 |
| 0 | 13 |

**567 of 623 pages have ≥10 posts.** Every number in this report is from the corrected load;
the wrong version never left this round.

## 8. The honest answer: nothing ships

**No candidate beats the shipped filter out of sample.** Three tie, two lose, one fails to
clear the base rate at all. Under this project's own bar — the agreement interval's lower
bound must clear the base rate's upper bound on held-out data — the shipped rules already
clear it and nothing I built improves on them:

| | agreement | base rate (upper) | clears |
|---|---|---|---|
| all 623 | 69.7% [**65.9**, 73.1] | 48.9% | yes |
| held-out TEST (n=242) | 70.7% [**64.6**, 76.0] | 49.7% | yes |
| fresh 50 (temporal holdout) | 72.0% [**58.3**, 82.5] | 53.8% | yes |

Split by how deeply each page was sampled, the picture holds:

| stratum | n | agreement | base rate | clears |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≥10 posts (the real corpus) | 567 | 67.7% [**63.8**, 71.4] | 48.7% [44.6, **52.8**] | yes |
| <10 posts | 56 | 89.3% [78.5, 95.0] | 7.1% [2.8, **17.0**] | yes — these are mostly dead accounts, correctly rejected |

**And one rule is dead.** `dialogue` (RULE 8) fires on **0 of 623 pages** — never, on either
stratum. Meanwhile "too much dialogue" is the reason he typed **11 times**, and it accounts
for **3 of the 10 false accepts on the fresh 50**. The rule exists, is documented, and cannot
fire. That is the single clearest gap this round found: not a threshold to tune, a detector
that never triggers on the thing he keeps rejecting pages for.

So: **the filter is not at chance and was never at 34%.** It sits around 70% against a 45%
base rate, and it has sat there for several rounds. The remaining error is not one bad rule —
it is ten false accepts on the fresh 50 spread across four different causes, three of which
(*"not a meme page"*, dialogue the rule missed, stills) no current rule addresses at all.

**What I would do next, and did not do here:** stop tuning thresholds. The measured ceiling
of the existing signals is roughly where the filter already is. The next real gain is a
detector for the thing he keeps typing in the box — *"this is not a meme / repost page"*, 40
marks, zero of them pages he wants — and that needs a read of the VIDEO, not the caption.
