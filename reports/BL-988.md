## 2. Method — and the constraint that shapes every number below

**The library stores no per-clip frame.** `media_renditions` holds a codec/bandwidth
inventory with no image URL, so hand-labelling 200 clips visually means re-fetching them at
~$0.003 each — about $0.60 against a $0.20 budget. Labelling therefore reads the **fields**:
`vision_scene`, `vision_on_screen_text`, `caption`. The brief permits "read frames/fields";
this is the fields half, said plainly rather than implied.

That forces an honest taxonomy of what a precision number here can mean:

| kind | meaning |
|---|---|
| **INDEPENDENT** | signal from a field no model produced and I did not label from (`account`, `duration`, `is_templated`). A real precision estimate. |
| **SHARED** | signal from one vision output, label from another. Correlated through one model call. **Upper bound.** |
| **RULE-ONLY** | signal from the SAME field I labelled from. Measures whether the regex finds what a careful reader finds **in that text** — it says nothing about whether the text is complete. |

**No signal was scored against another detector.** That circularity is what produced the
fake 4.15× in this project's history. **116 clips were hand-labelled** across the sheets.

**`vision_on_screen_text` emptiness is never evidence**, in either direction: it is missing
on 92.3% of the clips it calls empty. §6 shows that limit biting on live data.

## 3. Per-signal precision and recall, against hand labels

### static non-clip — 40 clips, 11 positives

Re-sampled from clips that *have* a `vision_scene` (a clip with no scene cannot be judged
static at all). The first random sheet gave only 3 positives; a "100% precision" computed on
two true positives is not a measurement and would not have been safe to gate 2,661 clips on.

| signal | kind | precision | recall | tp/fp/fn |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **`vision_scene` names a still image / grid / split image / graphic** | RULE-ONLY | **100.0%** | **90.9%** | 10/0/1 |
| beats ≤ 1 AND a static word | RULE-ONLY | 100.0% | 81.8% | 9/0/2 |
| beats ≤ 1 | SHARED | 43.5% | 90.9% | 10/13/1 |
| duration < 8 s | INDEPENDENT | 54.5% | 54.5% | 6/5/5 |
| `is_templated` | INDEPENDENT | — | 0.0% | 0/0/11 |

**`beats ≤ 1` is the intuitive signal and it fails at 43.5%** — most low-beat clips are
ordinary short footage. **Both INDEPENDENT signals fail outright**, which is the
uncomfortable part: the only term that clears is one read from the same field as the label.

### third-party watermark — 36 clips, 20 positives (4 unlabellable: text empty)

| signal | kind | precision | recall | tp/fp/fn |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **on-screen text has an @handle or a domain** | RULE-ONLY | **90.0%** | **90.0%** | 18/2/2 |
| on-screen text has any @handle | RULE-ONLY | 93.3% | 70.0% | 14/1/6 |
| handle/domain NOT matching the account | RULE-ONLY + INDEPENDENT `account` | 81.8% | 45.0% | 9/2/11 |

### non-English caption — 40 clips, 31 positives (enriched pool)

| signal | kind | precision | recall | tp/fp/fn |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **non-Latin letters >5% OR a romance/Indonesian stopword** | RULE-ONLY | **85.3%** | **93.5%** | 29/5/2 |
| non-Latin letters >5% | RULE-ONLY | 100.0% | 54.8% | 17/0/14 |
| romance/ID stopword | RULE-ONLY | 70.6% | 38.7% | 12/5/19 |
| **caption contains any non-ASCII byte** | RULE-ONLY | **77.1%** | 87.1% | 27/8/4 |

**The naive non-ASCII rule fails the bar at 77.1%, and it fires on 2,277 of 2,661 clips —
because emoji are not a language.** The shipped rule counts non-Latin *letters*, which emoji
and punctuation cannot move.

## 4. Where NO signal clears — refused, with the measurement

**Two of the five clip-side categories are left ungated. An ungated category with the reason
recorded beats a noise gate.**

### inherited mirroring — NOT GATEABLE

The whole library yields **3 text candidates, and all 3 are false**:

```
cawncept        "a muscular deer ... lifting weights, working out"
cinemapmagazine "humorously describing her process of getting ready"
popcorncliip    "Nolan really flipped a full-size semi-truck for real"
```

*Flipped* describes something that happened **in** the video. A mirrored clip shows
**reversed text in the frame**; nothing in the library records frame orientation, and the
vision model transcribes what it reads without noting it was backwards. **0 true positives
exist to measure against**, so there is nothing to set a threshold on. MEMEBOT-098 saw 2 of 30.

### content safety — NOT GATEABLE on the present evidence

**4 candidates in 2,661, all 4 false positives**, and the failure is instructive:

```
smoovereactss   "...Cheetos Co Chile, the Fleming Hot..."          <- 'spic' inside SPICY
xvibe07  (x2)   "...warning a co-guest that spicy chips are..."    <- same
screenjunkiebe  "...Queson? Cheetos..."
```

A word-boundary fix removes all four and still leaves **zero true positives**. The real case
is a slur burned into a **subtitle**, which needs on-screen text that is missing on 92.3% of
the clips it calls empty. MEMEBOT-098 noted "no gate exists"; that is still true, and it is
an **OCR-coverage problem, not a vocabulary problem**.

## 5. The gate as landed — pool sizes

`clippershq/clip_postable.py`. Pure stdlib, no config, no I/O. Every term is toggleable, and
a disabled term still **flags** — a gate you cannot turn off cannot be measured against the
thing it is meant to improve.

| term | excludes | % of 2,661 | precision | reason in the decision log |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `non_english_caption` | **289** | 10.9% | 85.3% | 1 of 30 shipped Persian |
| `third_party_watermark` | **99** | 3.7% | 90.0% | 4 of 30 advertised a competitor |
| `static_non_clip` | **57** | 2.1% | 100.0% | 5 of 30 were not clips at all |

```
POOL                       clips     %          MATCHES A SONG      clips      %
  before the gate           2,661  100.0%         before             448    16.8%
  AFTER the gate            2,234   84.0%         AFTER              388    17.4%
  removed                     427   16.0%         matched lost        60
```

**The gate keeps 84% of the library — it has not traded away supply.** The matched-to-song
pool falls 448 → 388, but the matched *rate* rises 16.8% → 17.4%: the clips it removes were
slightly more likely than average to have a song, which is the correct direction — it is
removing things that would otherwise have been rendered.

**78 clips carry a SELF-watermark and are FLAGGED, NOT CUT.** A page's own sign-off is a
weaker objection than a competitor's mark and cutting it costs real supply. §6 shows that
decision has a cost.

## 7. What I got wrong

1. **My first hypothesis for the static category was the wrong fields.** I expected
   `imdb_rating` / `ratings` / `where_to_watch` to mark the IMDb ratings-grid infographics.
   They are present on 214–364 clips and **absent from all 17 clips of the account that
   supplied four of the five failures**. Checked before it became a gate term.
2. **My first sheets were too thin to gate on** — 3 static positives and 1 non-English. I
   re-sampled rather than publish a 100% precision computed on two true positives.
3. The static vocabulary was **tuned on the sample it is scored against**, so its 100% is an
   upper bound. Recorded in the decision log, not only here.
4. **I opened under an ID another round had already published.** Caught by checking origin
   before writing a byte.
5. **I first quoted my spend as $0.0401 — a delta in the SHARED `spend.json` total.** Another
   round (`BL876_VISION`) was metering concurrently. The receipted figure for this round is
   **$0.0204, 34 calls, campaign `BL980_PREVIEW`**. Reading a shared total as one round's
   cost is the same attribution error BL-979 had to correct on a matched-clip count; a
   per-campaign receipt is the only honest source.

## 8. Still broken, and whose file it is

- **`clip_pipeline.py` is held by BL-899**, so the gate is not wired into the render path.
  The patch is one condition in the clip-selection step: drop clips where
  `clip_postable.classify(clip)["postable"]` is False. Nothing else changes.
- **On-screen-text coverage is the binding limit on two of three terms.** The watermark gate
  and any future content-safety gate both read a field that is empty on most clips.
  **Improving OCR coverage would do more than any new rule.** Unowned.
- **No term for dead canvas or unreadable footage**, which the frames say is now the largest
  surviving defect class. No signal was sought this round.
- **Account concentration is ungated** — 6 of the top 30 from one page.
- **Every landed term is RULE-ONLY.** Both INDEPENDENT signals tested failed. A gate whose
  terms all read the same fields they were validated on is a gate that will drift silently
  when the vision model changes.
