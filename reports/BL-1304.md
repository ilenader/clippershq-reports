### There were TWO writers with the hole, not one

| path | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| **CLI** `all_bot_ready.main()` | 2 CSVs written, **1** meta | 2 CSVs, **2** metas |
| **menu** `control.py` `[b]` | 2 CSVs written, **0** metas | 2 CSVs, **2** metas |

## 3. The guard — and proof that it bites

`tests/test_bl1304_meta_companion.py`, **7 tests**. It checks a **relational** invariant —
*whatever the CSV holds, the companion must say so* — rather than pinning a constant, because
`row_count == 4270` would go red on the next legitimate rebuild and be deleted within a week.

- Red **5 of 7** against the broken state before the fix.
- Green **7 of 7** after.
- **Proven to bite**: I built a throwaway `output/` with a lying meta (`row_count: 2`,
  `campaign: DAYLIGHT`) and ran the guard against it — **exit 1, three assertions fired**
  (`row_count_matches_the_csv_beside_it`, `source_names_a_file_that_exists`,
  `the_mirror_meta_is_not_still_the_daylight_snapshot`). A guard nobody has watched fail is
  not evidence.

It also includes an **AST** check that each writer emits at least as many companion metas as
CSVs — a text search for `write_bot_ready_meta` matches the docstring explaining the bug and
the comment fixing it, neither of which is a call.

> **One finding the guard forced into the open: the four send files ship THREE different meta
> schemas.** `ALL`/`LATEST`/`MEME` use `row_count` + `source_csv`; **`TIKTOK` uses `kept` +

> it fails a companion that is *wrong* rather than one that is merely *differently worded* —
> but the divergence is real and worth normalising in a future round.

## 4. The AST sweep — and the shape it had to learn

Parsed **178 Python files** across `clippershq/`, `tools/`, `dashboard/` with `ast.parse`,
never a text search.

(`calls & DATA_WRITERS`) sees `all_bot_ready.main()` calling both a CSV writer and a meta
writer and reports "paired". The actual defect was **two CSV writes and one meta write inside

**Proof it now catches it**, by re-parsing the pre-fix file from git:

|---|---:|---:|:--:|
| `all_bot_ready.main()` at HEAD (pre-fix) | 2 | 1 | **YES** |

**Findings across the tree:**

| kind | site | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| SPLIT | `all_bot_ready._self_test` writes a CSV fixture, `main` writes the meta | **benign** — a test helper |
| COUNT_MISMATCH | `writer._write_bot_ready()` — 2 CSVs, 1 meta | **benign, checked**: writes a per-run *timestamped archive* plus `LATEST`, and the one companion describes `LATEST` while naming the archive as `source_csv` |

### ⚠️ Both redaction guards fired on THIS round's own output, and both were right

**1. The publish scanner rejected the report.** I had quoted two addresses from `writer.py`'s
comment explaining the bio-link quarantine. The scanner joined them against master and found
**both are real people**. Replaced with a description of the shape; the point survives, the
person is not published.

**2. The commit guard rejected `scratch/bl1304_fifty.json`** — *"gitignored lead data has
reappeared at a path that IS committed"*, 50 email-shaped literals, **2 matching master**.
My `_redact()` was `local[:2] + "@" + domain + "#" + sha6`, and it had two defects:

- it still emits an email **shape**, so anything scanning for addresses treats it as one — correctly;
- **for a local part of two characters or fewer, `local[:2]` is the entire local part**, so
  the "redacted" string was the complete real address, character for character. That is
  exactly how the 2 matches happened.

Now `ab~domain.tld#6hex` — no `@`, and the prefix is dropped entirely when the local part is
short. The full-address sha256 still carries identity. **0 email-shaped literals remain.**

> This is the same failure the brief warned about — *"the guard recently fired on a report's
> own text, where 3 of 8 email literals were real master rows"* — and it happened again here,
> in a round that was being careful about it. The redaction has to be **structurally** unable
> to reproduce an address, not merely intended to shorten one.
