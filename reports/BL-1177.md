# BL-1177 — the filter was reading the wrong text, and his 37 reels say what the right one looks like

**The caption and the burned-in top text disagree, and every previous version of this filter
judged the caption.** Measured on the operator's own example page `memes.entertainment.daily`:

| | |
|---|---|
| caption | "Adrien Broner and DeenTheGreat showed why they are the best duo in streaming after dancing in sync on stream…" — **a synopsis** |
| burned-in top text | "This gotta be the BEST duo in streaming" — **the line he picked as an example** |

He judges the drawn line. Two rounds judged the typed one. That is why BL-1175 scored 65%
(circular by its own admission) and BL-1176 scored 25.0% against a 26.5% base rate — *worse
than passing every page*.

Every figure here came from **`.venv\Scripts\python.exe`, Python 3.11.9**. Bare `python` is
3.13.14 and lacks the packages; it was used only for the stdlib-only claim readers.

## 1. What his 37 examples actually are — measured, not assumed

39 reel URLs supplied → 38 fetched (one private/deleted) → **37 with readable burned-in top
text**. Fetched via `/v1/media/by/code` (flat payload, verified by probe first), video pulled
from the CDN for free, 6 frames per reel through RapidOCR, blocks sorted into reading order.

| property | share of his 37 |
|---|---:|
| first- or second-person anchor (`me/my/I/we/you/your/ur`) | **81.1%** (30/37) |
| a frame word (`POV`, `when`, `imagine`, `how it feels`, `what if`) | 43.2% (16/37) |
| a quoted line answered by a reaction | 24.3% (9/37) |
| a colon — the video finishes the sentence | 45.9% (17/37) |
| **any of the four** | **86.5%** (32/37) |

Median **69 characters, 13 words**. Median **1,474,869 plays**, minimum 17,687. 32 distinct
posting accounts.

**The derived rule, and the test that makes it a rule rather than a hunch:** both rejections
he wrote out score FALSE on all four marks, and all three hooks he wrote out score TRUE.

```
Walt confronts Jesse as his father tries to force entry   -> SYNOPSIS
He showed who is in charge here                           -> SYNOPSIS
POV - that joke hit too hard                              -> HOOK
my girl when she leaves me                                -> HOOK
me when bro's toxic ex tries to get back with him         -> HOOK
```

A third-person pronoun does **not** disqualify — 43.2% of his own examples say he/she/they
("When she moved on… but you're still stuck on her"). What disqualifies is third person with
no anchor and no frame, which is precisely the shape of both his rejections.

The 5 unmatched are OCR fragments of heavily stylised type (`GTANyTC`, `ADEGA`) and one
opinion-claim. They are instrument misses, not counterexamples.

## 2. The six rules, each a check, synopsis first

| # | rule | how |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **synopsis vs hook** | `is_hook()` — the four marks above, on the **top line** |
| 2 | film/TV promotion | `Genre:`, `Starring`, `IMDb`, `Season N`, `Streaming on`, `n/10`, `Trailer` |
| 3 | English only, Spanish tolerated | full-Indic + CJK + Cyrillic + Arabic script detection, then stopword scoring |
| 4 | dialogue clips | two speaker labels, or a long quote nobody answers — a quoted *set-up* survives |
| 5 | dead/tiny | avg views over posts **older than 48h** must be ≥ 2,000 |
| 6 | stale format | only the **latest 10** posts are read |

**Rule 5's recency grace is his, and it matters:** a post younger than 48h is *excluded* from
the average, never counted as a zero, so "ten posts in the last hour" cannot kill a live page.
And a **photo post carries no `play_count` at all** — counting its absence as zero views
returned avg=0 across twelve posts on a live dog page and would have killed it. Absent means
unknown; an explicit 0 on a video is a real zero.

### A bug the labels caught

The first scored run cut exactly **one** page for language while `7stargadag` (Kannada),
`gazal_khari_kay` (Marathi), `insta_mudhalvan` (Tamil) and `i_support_imran_khedalwala`
(Gujarati) all passed. Two causes, both fixed: the script range covered Devanagari only when
Indic scripts run U+0900–U+0DFF continuously, and the language check was reading the **OCR**
of stylised type — which comes back as garbled Latin — instead of the typed caption. Language
is judged on the caption now; hook-ness on the top text.

The curated repost pool was exhausted (287 of 288 resolved last round) and hashtags are

**33 seed accounts → `/v2/user/suggested/profiles` → 1,326 NEW candidate pages.** That is a

| | |
|---|---:|
| new candidates discovered | **1,326** |

| rejected by the free gates (language / dead / film / advert) | 77 |
| OCR'd (only survivors — free but slow) | 143 |

**The order is what makes the OCR affordable.** Reading burned-in text costs ~15 seconds a
page and nothing in money; the cheap gates run on data the `medias` call already paid for, so
the slow signal is spent only where it can still change the answer. 77 of 287 pages never
needed a video downloaded.

**Email rate among pages the filter passed: 21 of 62 = 33.9%** — against 3.1% for
hashtag-discovered pages and 14.3% for the curated pool. The filter is now selecting pages
that also happen to be contactable.

Master: **21 offered → 20 appended, 12 merged by identity** across the two batches.

Stopped at the page limit, not the cap: **$0.3240 of $5.00 spent, $4.68 left unspent.**

### What I got wrong

**The header row of the first delivered .xlsx was overwritten by the first data row.**
`ws.append([])` does not advance `max_row` for an empty row, so `row_i = ws.max_row` returned
row 1 for the first page. Worse, **the checker reported success**: with the headers gone it
found "0 data rows", examined nothing, and every per-column assertion passed vacuously. Both
fixed — the writer counts its own rows, and the checker now FAILS on an empty sheet. A check
that cannot fail has not run.

**The language gate rejected exactly one page of seventy on its first scored run** while four
Indic-script pages sailed through, because the script range stopped at Devanagari and the
check was reading OCR of stylised type instead of the typed caption.
