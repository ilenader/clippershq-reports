# BL-961 — the email exists

BL-956 measured the send path end to end and found the plumbing sound, the file safe, and
step 6 of the runbook reading `NOTHING EXISTS. You write this by hand.` 58,000 leads, 2,980
in the send file, zero emails ever sent, and no template, subject line or greeting anywhere
in the repo. The merge-field columns had been added to the export and no consumer was ever
built.

**This round built the consumer.** `clippershq/message_template.py`, 57 tests in
`tests/test_message_template.py`, and `docs/SENDING.md` step 6 rewritten from "you write
this by hand" to a command.

**All 2,980 rows now render a finished email.** 1,908 of them are rows a merge built
straight on the columns could not have sent. The commercial terms — rate, turnaround,
payment — are deliberately unfilled placeholders and the renderer refuses to ship them.

---

## 1. Two audiences, and no default between them

`lead_kind` splits the file 1,578 `clipper` / 1,402 `client`. A clipper is someone we want
to **hire** to cut clips; a client is someone we want to **sell** clipping to. Sending
either one the other's email is not a cosmetic mis-merge — it is offering to pay a customer
and invoicing an employee.

`render()` branches on `lead_kind` first. The two messages share no sentence and no subject
line. A row whose kind is neither is **refused** — `render()` raises — because
`writer.bot_ready_rows` already excludes unroutable rows, so the only way one reaches here
is by hand, which is exactly when a silent default would do damage.

```
clipper   →  recruiting.  "ClippersHQ pays editors to cut short-form…"
client    →  sales.       "you send the footage, we send back cut, captioned clips"
```

Asserted across the whole file: every clipper message contains "pays editors" and no client
message does.

### The clipper message is a recruiting message

Recruiting is the highest-replying cold category at 4–8%, and the reason is that the offer
is concrete and the evidence is specific. Both are load-bearing in the copy: the first
paragraph is this person's own numbers, and the terms are a three-line table, not a
paragraph, so the offer reads in two seconds. The close converts a non-answer into a cheap
answer ("if you're booked up, say so and I'll leave it there").

The evidence is there to carry it. On the clipper half: **followers 99.0%, clip views
98.4%, clip date 98.4%, median 93.5%, recency 98.5%.**

### The client message cannot be personalised on numbers, and does not pretend to be

This is the finding that shaped the client templates. Split by kind, the file is two
different files:

| field | clipper (1,578) | client (1,402) |
|---|---|---|
| followers | 99.0% | **1.4%** |
| clip_views | 98.4% | **0.0%** |
| clip_date | 98.4% | **0.0%** |
| rank_median_views | 93.5% | **0.0%** |
| days_since_post | 98.5% | **0.0%** |

BL-956's "50.5% lack median views" is a whole-file average that hides this: the missing
numbers are almost entirely the client half. There is nothing honest to compliment a client
on, so the client copy leads with the mechanic and personalises on **segment** instead.

## 2. `fandom` holds the source funnel — read it for what it actually says

100% of client rows (804 `spotify_finder`, 517 `twitch_finder`, 61 `google_play`, 20
`youtube_finder`) plus 38 clipper rows (`suggested_crawl`) carry a funnel name where a
fandom should be. A merge on it reads *"we cut short-form for Youtube_Finder."*

The fix is not to blank the field. The funnel name is a **recorded fact about which search
ran** — the same reasoning `writer.lead_kind_from_source` already uses — and it says
exactly what kind of client this is:

| funnel | rows | segment | the message talks about |
|---|---|---|---|
| `spotify_finder` | 804 | musician | live sets, studio footage, music videos |
| `twitch_finder` | 517 | streamer | VODs and stream highlights |
| `google_play` | 61 | developer | screen capture, app footage |
| `youtube_finder` | 20 | creator | long-form uploads |
| unmapped | — | *(generic)* | "the footage you already have" |

An unmapped funnel is **never guessed** — it gets the generic client message. The funnel
list is asserted against `writer._SOURCE_TO_KIND` so the two cannot drift, and retired
`kick_finder` is listed so its residue can never surface as a fandom either.

**Three independent guards, and the third raises.** Client templates never read `fandom`;
prose fandom comes from an allowlist that contains no funnel name; and `_assert_clean()`
greps the *finished text* for every funnel token and raises. That third one is the
[MEMEBOT-057](https://github.com/ilenader/clippershq-reports/blob/main/reports/MEMEBOT-057.md)
lesson applied — BL-956 recorded this defect as prose in a report, and prose does not stop
a send.

## 3. `Theodyssey` — why the fandom is an allowlist and not a splitter

`fandom_display()` title-cases an unbroken lowercase run, which is how `theodyssey` became
`Theodyssey` in a real message. Two repairs were tried against the 374 distinct clipper
fandoms in this file and both fail:

* **A leading-article rule** fixes `theodyssey` and leaves `thegreatgatsby` → "The
  Greatgatsby" and `theoc` → "The Oc". Partial repair is worse than none: it looks
  deliberate.
* **LENGTH DOES NOT SEPARATE** run-together tags from real single-word titles.
  `Lalaland`(8) and `Insideout`(9) are broken while `Euphoria`(8), `Gladiator`(9),
  `Casablanca`(10) and `Heartstopper`(12) are correct; `Theodyssey`(10) sits between
  `Bridgerton`(10) and `Terminator`(10). This is the same negative result BL-794 recorded
  for character tags, on the same column — which is why `writer.py` hand-maintains a list
  too.

So prose fandom renders from a **measured allowlist and nothing else**: 196 tags mapping to
167 distinct works, plus 4 genre tags rendered lowercase ("your football edits"). It
resolves **70.7% of clipper rows** (1,115 of 1,578). A tag not on it does not render; the
row drops from FULL to REACH and loses one sentence.
Cruft is stripped first and repeatedly, so `demonslayertiktok`, `demonslayerfan` and
`demonslayeredits` are all "Demon Slayer". `fandom_is_character` is honoured, not
re-decided — writer already made that call.

## 5. Six defects found by reading the rendered output

This is the part that could not have been done any other way. Each was invisible in the
columns and visible in the first message that contained it.

| # | what rendered | why | fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `did 4.4m views` | `str.capitalize()` capitalises the first character **and lowercases the rest** — it ate the `M` off the number in the one sentence whose job is to prove the numbers are real | `_ucfirst()` |
| 2 | `a 8.1M median` | the article has to agree with how the number is **spoken** ("eight point one"), not its first character | `_article()`: 8*, or exactly 11/18 |
| 3 | `the one from 2026-06-14` | a raw ISO date reads like a database export, which is what the reader must not think they got | `month_year()` → "June 2026" |
| 4 | `Hi X,` from `X ORIGINAL(HONG KONG) LIMITED` | a company was first-named | strip the legal suffix, never first-name an entity |
| 5 | `did 906 views … a 123 median — a real audience, not one lucky post` | every number true, the sentence false — the worst thing a personalised cold email can be | `QUOTABLE_MIN_VIEWS = 4000`, the configured business floor |
| 6 | `Hi indiakatona` sent to that creator's *manager's* inbox at a talent agency | 211 rows are agency inboxes; the manager is the contact | neutral greeting + third person by @handle, switched **together** |

Two more came from linting all 2,980 rather than reading 40:

* **`.ae` is After Effects, not the United Arab Emirates.** `writer._DOMAIN_NAME` lists
  `ae` as a TLD. 60 handles and 11 display names in this file end `.ae`/`.aep` — every one
  an editor advertising the tool they cut in. Treating them as domains rejected 6 greetings
  outright and, because the handle fallback runs the same test, sent those rows all the way
  to GENERIC. Fixed here, in the template layer; `writer`'s own use is a different question
  and a file this round does not own.
* **`Hi B    T    E    Z,`** — one row, letter-spaced with padded runs. Whitespace collapsed.

And one that reading 40 messages produced but only a measurement could settle:

* **`Hi After,` from `After Credits`.** `After Credits` and `Austin Wartes` are the same
  shape and no rule about words separates them — `After` is not a rarer English word than
  `Grace`, `April` or `Hope`, all of which *are* first names in this corpus. What separates
  them is evidence the row already carries: `austinwartes@…` contains "austin";
  `ilgincfilmbilgileri@…` does not contain "after". **A first name is now used only when
  the email address confirms it.** Measured: of 253 rows that would be first-named, 141 are
  confirmed and 112 are not — and the 112 are where every bad greeting lived (`Hi Trapped`
  from *Trapped in Anime*, `Hi Demon` from *Demon Slayer Edits*, `Hi Lyrics` from *Lyrics
  Galore*, `Hi grandma` from *grandma deb*). Those now greet the whole name, which costs a
  little warmth on the handful of real people whose address abbreviates their name rather
  than spelling it, and is never wrong.

## 6. The fallback ladder — a visible generic message beats a broken merge

```
FULL     name + a real title + reach numbers        (clipper only — no client row
REACH    name + reach numbers, no fandom line        carries a single reach number)
NAMED    a usable name, nothing else
GENERIC  no usable name — "Hi there", no personalisation at all
```

The GENERIC message is a **real message** — same offer, same terms, same close, 300+
characters — not a stub. 16 rows get it. Every one of them would otherwise have been
`Hey Cykl0psFilm()`, `Hey UCdMFy5IMQF-PpiMLBcPOgYA` or `Hi heatherrobertsoncom`.

Agency mode is the one variant that can decline itself: it needs a @handle to refer to,
because "I found them through their edits" with no antecedent refers to nobody. A flagged
row with no handle falls back to the direct message rather than to a dangling pronoun.

## 7. The operator fills the commercial terms

This file does not invent a rate. A number written here becomes a number a real person is
quoted.

```
clipper   [[SENDER_NAME]] [[REPLY_TO]] [[RATE]] [[TURNAROUND]] [[PAYMENT_TERMS]]
client    [[SENDER_NAME]] [[REPLY_TO]] [[PRICE]] [[TURNAROUND]] [[PORTFOLIO_LINK]]
```

They render **visibly**, `unfilled()` lists every one still standing, and
`render(..., strict=True)` refuses to produce a message that contains any. `Operator()`
rejects an unknown field rather than silently ignoring it. A test asserts no currency
amount appears anywhere in either template.

## 8. Proof

**57 tests**, `tests/test_message_template.py`, offline, no key, no paid call. The
load-bearing ones run over the **2,980 real rows** — a fixture cannot supply
`X ORIGINAL(HONG KONG) LIMITED` or `B    T    E    Z`, and every defect above came from a
real row. The corpus tests skip **loudly** if the export is absent (docs/TESTING.md rule 6).

**A lint of all 2,980 finished messages, 13 checks, 38,740 assertions: 0 hits.** No funnel
name, no platform ID, no broken-merge artifact, no unsubstituted `{field}`, no raw ISO
date, no lowercased `M`, no article disagreement, no sub-floor view count, no
one-character greeting, no run-on paragraph, no manager addressed as the creator.

**Mutation-checked** (docs/TESTING.md rule 1) — two one-line reversions, both detected:

| mutation | result |
|---|---|
| `QUOTABLE_MIN_VIEWS` → 0 | **RED** — sub-floor numbers reappear |
| funnel guard off *and* funnel names added to the title allowlist | **RED** — `_assert_clean` raises; `test_every_row_renders` and the funnel test both fail |

The first attempt at this check reported both as green. It was wrong: the probe read
`mt.QUOTABLE_MIN_VIEWS`, the constant the mutation had just changed — **the instrument
shared state with the subject** (docs/TESTING.md rule 4). Re-run with pinned literals.

**Suite: 126 of 127 green (934.5 s).** The one red is `tests/test_claims_manifest.py`,
failing on `docs/claims/MEMEBOT-067.claims` — a manifest committed by a concurrent round at
12:38 today whose header says it cannot be verified and which then verifies cleanly. It is
not this round's file, this round writes no manifest, and the failing assertion names that
file and nothing else. 12 rounds were in flight.

## 9. What is still open

1. **You must supply rate, turnaround and payment terms.** Nothing sends until you do.
2. **BLOCKERS 3, 5, 6, 7 of `docs/SENDING.md` are untouched** — `ALL_BOT_READY.csv` is not
   a strict superset (51 addresses live in 5 other files), `touch_number=2` cannot be
   recorded, suppression removes only ~1.2% against an observed ~10% bounce rate, and
   nothing tracks sends outside master.
3. **26 greetings are letter-spaced or initial-suffixed** (`J J E D I T Z`, `M I Y A H`,
   `Paige G`). They render as the person wrote them, which is defensible — about half are
   plainly fine (`Hi Paige G`) — but joining them would mangle `m 𖦹 l l y`. Left alone
   deliberately, not missed.
4. **`writer._DOMAIN_NAME` still treats `.ae` as a TLD.** Worked around here; the upstream
   question is whose to answer.
5. **Some `youtube_finder` clients look like clippers** (`jectroeditz_24`, `cranime_editz`
   are being pitched clipping services). That is a `lead_kind` routing question in the
   funnel, not a template question, and `writer` owns it. 20 rows.
6. **No A/B variant, no follow-up sequence.** One message per audience per tier. A second
   touch also has no recording path (blocker 5).
