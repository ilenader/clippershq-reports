# BL-1134 — free open-source audio analysis: what actually hears, on this machine

**The operator is right. Purpose-built audio models really do hear — all five I tested passed
the control that the vision model failed, at 100%, and four of five are perfectly
reproducible against a 40% floor.**

**And none of them answers the question he asked.** The tools that hear give *genre and
content tags*, not mood. The one that gives mood gets 1 of 4 known tracks right and calls
digital silence "warm". The purpose-built music-mood models that would answer it — Essentia's
MTG-Jamendo heads — **cannot be installed on Windows**, and that is very likely the whole
difference between this machine and his acquaintance's.

$0.00 spent. Nothing uploaded. 2.5 GB installed into one directory, deleted.

---

## 1. The survey — 38 candidates, install verdict decided by the machine

`pipguard --check-only` was run against every pip-installable candidate. It answers a
narrower question than a web search: not "does this project support Windows" but "does this
package, on **this** Windows CPU-only host, with **these** pins, resolve without dragging
numpy back across the 2.0 ABI boundary". `pipguard` exists because two audio installs
(inaSpeechSegmenter → TensorFlow, laion-clap → numpy<2) nearly took out OCR, thumbnail
vision and the repost funnel at once.

## 2. Five tested on our own clips

Test set: 10 real library clips (spread across the VAD's four classes), the same 10 with
their audio **replaced** by a known store track, the 4 store tracks unmixed, and 20 s of
digital silence. All 16 kHz mono WAV, all local.

### A. Is it listening? — swap vs pure, byte-identical audio

| tool | agreement |
|---|---|
| librosa | **100.0% [72.2, 100.0]** 10/10 |
| audioflux | **100.0%** 10/10 |
| PANNs | **100.0%** 10/10 |
| AST | **100.0%** 10/10 |
| CLAP | **100.0%** 10/10 |

**Every one of them follows the waveform.** This is the control BL-1132's vision model
failed — it noticed a *completely absent* audio track 5% of the time. The operator's belief
that this is possible is **correct**, and the last round's negative result must not be read
as a statement about purpose-built audio models. It was not one.

### B. Is it right? — the 4 store tracks, ground truth the operator wrote

| track | truth | PANNs top tags | AST top tags | CLAP mood |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| song01 | melancholy *(slow sad piano ballad)* | Music of Asia, Zing, Singing, Pop | Singing, Pop, R&B | **warm** ✗ |
| song02 | triumphant *(female rap anthem)* | **Rapping, Hip hop music** ✓ | **Hip hop music, Rapping** ✓ | **warm** ✗ |
| song03 | warm *(summer/football)* | Vehicle, Car, Disco | Afrobeat, Dance, House | **hype** ✗ |
| song04 | hype *(fast aggressive battle)* | Music of Africa, Afrobeat, Reggae ✗ | **Reggae** ✗ | **hype** ✓ |

**Mood, scored against truth: PANNs 0/4, AST 0/4, CLAP 1/4, librosa and audioflux emit no
mood at all.**

The AudioSet taggers *have* mood classes — `Sad music`, `Happy music`, `Exciting music` — and
**none ever exceeded 0.05**. They are genre/content taggers. On genre they are right about
half the time: both nailed the rap track, both called an aggressive battle track *Reggae*.

### C. Is it honest? — 20 s of digital silence

| tool | answer |
|---|---|
| **PANNs** | top tag **`Silence` 0.4055**, Music 0.107 ✓ |
| **AST** | top tag **`Silence` 0.4367**, Music 0.350 ✓ |
| librosa / audioflux | RMS ≈ 0 — arithmetic, cannot lie ✓ |
| **CLAP** | **mood = `warm`** ✗ |

PANNs and AST **know when there is nothing there.** CLAP cannot abstain — a softmax over
four prompts always elects a winner — which is the same structural failure as the vision
model's confidence 0.90 on an empty track, arriving by a different route.

### D. Is it stable? — 3 runs, same file (the floor to beat is 40%)

| tool | 3/3 identical |
|---|---|
| librosa | **100.0% [78.5, 100.0]** 14/14 |
| audioflux | **100.0%** 14/14 |
| PANNs | **100.0%** 14/14 |
| AST | **100.0%** 14/14 |
| CLAP | 92.9% [68.5, 98.7] 13/14 |

**This is the cleanest win in the round.** The 40% reproducibility that closed audio
classification was a property of *asking a language model*, not of audio analysis.
Deterministic local models are stable by construction — and it is measured here, not assumed.

### E. Music vs speech, against the local VAD (which the tools never see)

PANNs **70.0% [39.7, 89.2]**, AST **80.0% [49.0, 94.3]**. Real, but Silero VAD already does
this for free and better — it is the ground truth in this table.

## 3. The cost, in CPU — p50 and p99, never a mean

| tool | p50 | p90 | p99 | first call (model load) | **2,731 clips @ p50 / @ p99** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| librosa | 0.18 s | 0.31 s | 4.61 s | 4.6 s | **0.1 h / 3.5 h** |
| **PANNs** | **0.41 s** | 0.49 s | **0.51 s** | 80.4 s | **0.3 h / 0.4 h** |
| CLAP | 0.30 s | 0.40 s | 12.76 s | 12.8 s | 0.2 h / 9.7 h |
| audioflux | 0.63 s | 0.66 s | 1.04 s | 1.0 s | 0.5 h / 0.8 h |
| AST | 1.12 s | 1.33 s | 1.44 s | 44.3 s | 0.8 h / 1.1 h |

**PANNs has the tightest distribution — p99 is 1.2× its p50.** librosa's and CLAP's p99 are
dominated by their first call (import, numba JIT, model load); over a batch that cost is paid
once, which is exactly why the mean would have flattered them and the p99 exposes it.

**Every one of these is cheap.** Under an hour of local CPU for the whole library. Cost is
not the obstacle here either.

## 4. Scored against what already works

The free baseline is the source's own on-screen text: on 20 clips a human reading it produced
a usable mood **20 of 20**, where the vision model's audio guess managed 7.

| | mood on the 4 known tracks | mood on real clips | reproducible | abstains |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **reading the on-screen text** | n/a | **usable 20/20** | 100% (not a judgement) | yes — no text, no answer |
| CLAP | **1/4** | always answers | 92.9% | **no** |
| PANNs / AST | **0/4** | no mood emitted | 100% | **yes** |
| librosa / audioflux | no mood | no mood | 100% | n/a |

**Nothing here beats reading the text on mood.** Not close.

**What audio adds that text cannot** is real but narrow: **tempo and energy**. librosa gives
BPM and RMS for free at 0.1 h across the library. One caveat measured: song01, a slow ballad,
came back at **161.5 BPM** — a textbook octave error. Tempo needs a half/double sanity check
before anything trusts it.

## 5. The recommendation

**Adopt none of them for mood today.** But this is not the same refusal as BL-1129 or
BL-1132, and the difference matters:

> **Audio analysis works. The tools hear, they are stable, they are free, they are fast, and
> they run locally with nothing uploaded. What is missing on THIS machine is the one class of
> model that answers the mood question — and it is missing for a platform reason, not a
> capability reason.**

**If the operator wants to pursue this properly, here is the precise route.** Essentia's
MTG-Jamendo mood/theme heads (`mood_happy`, `mood_sad`, `mood_aggressive`, `mood_relaxed`,
`danceability`) are the standard answer and are what a working system almost certainly uses.
They need `essentia-tensorflow`, which **has no Windows distribution at all** — pip finds
nothing, and plain `essentia` falls back to a source tarball whose build fails here. **That
requires Linux: WSL2 or a container.** If his acquaintance is analysing audio successfully,
that is very likely the difference, and it is a one-evening setup rather than a research
project.

**What is worth taking now, regardless:** nothing for mood. **librosa tempo + RMS energy** is
free, already installed, 0.1 h for the library, and gives the two dimensions text genuinely
cannot express — with the octave caveat above.

### The strategic point, which no tool changes

Even a *perfect* mood reading of the source's audio tells you the mood of the track **the
reposter chose**. That is the same proposition `track_title` supplies free at 28.7% fill, and
BL-1127 refused it after it put a football song on a fake-note scene.

I want to be fair rather than re-refuse by reflex: a **waveform measurement is strictly
better evidence than a title string**, and BL-1127's objection was that a title is not
evidence — which is a weaker objection to a spectrogram. If the operator judges that "match
the mood the original poster picked" is a good rule, audio analysis is the right way to
implement it, and PANNs/CLAP-class tools can do it locally and free.

**But that is his call, not mine.** The defect he has been rejecting — a sad clip getting the
football track because the frame showed Messi — is a *picture-reading* defect, and the fix
already shipped is to read the clip's own text. Audio would be a second opinion on a
different signal, not a repair of that one.

## Off-brief, stated

* **I tested 5 of 38, not more.** The five were chosen because they install and run here;
  several strong candidates (Essentia's mood heads above all) could not be tested at all on
  this host, so "not tested" is not "does not work" for those.
* **n=4 on the ground-truth tracks.** Four tracks is what the operator's store contains. Every
  accuracy figure in section B rests on four items and the intervals say so — 0/4 and 1/4 are
  directions, not measurements.
* **The mood mapping for PANNs/AST is mine.** I mapped AudioSet's `Sad music` / `Happy music`
  / `Exciting music` onto the store's vocabulary at a 0.05 threshold. A better mapping might
  extract more; the classes never exceeded 0.05 on any clip, so I doubt it, but I built that
  mapping and it is a possible source of the 0/4.
* **The install directory is deleted and verified.** 2.5 GB peak (1.5 GB HuggingFace models,
  313 MB PANNs checkpoint, the rest wheels), one directory, `HF_HOME` and `TORCH_HOME`
  redirected into it so nothing leaked to `~/.cache`. `panns_inference`, `audioflux` and
  `essentia` all confirmed **absent from site-packages** afterwards. Disk 116 G free before,
  115 G after — the 1 G difference is another round working concurrently, not residue.

## Got wrong

* **I read `pipguard --check-only` as "this installs".** It said essentia "resolves clean" and
  I reported that as a surprise contradicting the docs. It validates **dependency
  resolution**, not that a binary exists — pip had silently fallen back to a source tarball,
  and the build then failed. The web search was right and I was wrong for about ten minutes.
  A resolution check is not a build check.
* **I called CLAP with `audios=`**, deprecated in transformers 5.x, and lost 25 calls to a
  `ValueError` before noticing. The tool was fine; the caller was not.
