# BL-858: I opened 30 confirmed pages. 21 are the format you asked for, not 29 — the gate's 96.4% does not survive re-measurement, because it tests format consistency and never tests whether the clip was reposted at all

**Date:** 2026-08-01 · **Type:** Read-only audit · **Spend:** **$0.0192 · 32 paid calls** (cap $0.02)
Claim filed via `tools/claim.py` (`.claims/BL-858.json`, 12 other rounds in flight, no path conflicts). Read-only: nothing in `clippershq/`, `config.json`, `master_leads.csv` or `clip_library/` was written. Every measurement reads through `read_snapshot` so a live writer could not move a denominator. `git -C` throughout.

Audits the repost finder's page pool. Acts on nothing; **recommends** but does not implement.

---

## The hand-read verdict, first

I fetched the 12 most recent posts for 30 confirmed pages, rendered each page's covers as a grid, and looked at all 30 by eye. The label is mine, not the detector's.

| verdict | n | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| **wanted** — reposts other people's footage under a caption bar, film/TV/anime | 16 | 53.3% |
| **off-niche** — correct bar format, subject is not film (finance, motivation, streamer gossip, general viral) | 5 | 16.7% |
| **marginal** — some posts qualify, some do not | 3 | 10.0% |
| **wrong** — not a repost-with-caption-bar page at all | 6 | 20.0% |

**21 of 30 (70.0%) are the format the funnel claims to find**, 95% CI **[52.1%, 83.3%]**. Counting the three marginals as wins gives 80.0% [62.7%, 90.5%]. If you require film/TV/anime *and* the format, it is **16 of 30 (53.3%)**.

**The shipped claim is 96.4%. That is outside the confidence interval on either reading.**

### What the six wrong ones actually are

| page | gate score today | what it is |
|---|---:|---|
| `@sniper_yatinn` | **1.00** | a PUBG player captioning **his own gameplay**, in Hinglish |
| `@primecuttv` | **0.88** | a Farsi film-analysis page posting **designed poster graphics**, not captioned clips |
| `@screenrant` | 0.50 | ScreenRant — a professional publisher posting its **own interview footage** with branded cards |
| `@theoff.recordfiles` | 0.33 | political conspiracy text cards, including an antisemitic trope. **Brand-unsafe** |
| `@zackrawrrshorts` | 0.33 | Asmongold's shorts channel — the streamer's **own webcam reactions** |
| `@factofthefilm12` | 0.00 | a real repost page, but the text is burned **over** the frame in coloured fonts — no bar |

The top two are the important ones. `@sniper_yatinn` scores a **perfect 1.00** and `@primecuttv` scores 0.88 — these are not near-misses that a threshold tweak would catch. They are pages the detector is certain about and wrong about.

### The one-line diagnosis

**The account gate measures whether a page's posts share a caption-bar layout. It never measures whether the footage under the bar came from somewhere else.** "Reposts other people's clips" is the actual definition in `repost_finder`'s own docstring, and no code tests it. A streamer captioning his own gameplay every day is, to this gate, indistinguishable from a page reposting studio footage every day — and scores higher, because his format is more consistent.

That is why the precision drifted. The gate was calibrated against a hold-out labelled by the same layout intuition it encodes, so it kept scoring well on the thing it measures while the population drifted toward pages that satisfy the layout without satisfying the definition.

### The English gate leaks, in both directions

5 of 30 delivered pages (16.7%, CI [7.3%, 33.6%]) are **not English** — Portuguese, Tamil/Telugu, Farsi, Indonesian, Hinglish — while the same gate rejected **49 accounts** for language. `@cenadofelix` is a Brazilian telenovela page whose caption bar literally reads *"SE VOCÊ É FÃ DO FÉLIX…"* and it passed. The gate classifies the **IG caption text**, not the burned-in text a viewer sees, so a page with sparse or English-ish captions and Portuguese on-frame text sails through.

## 2. What the gate is actually testing — re-measured

`account_gate` applies four tests: distinct-frame ratio, ≥6 scored posts, caption-language, and format consistency ≥ 0.40. **Provenance is not among them**, and neither is niche, follower count, or content safety.

Re-scoring the 30 confirmed pages today against the live gate:

- **4 pages the gate passes today, I labelled wrong or marginal** (`@screenrant`, `@primecuttv`, `@sniper_yatinn`, `@pr_photography_anime.official`).
- **3 pages the gate would now reject** are in the pool (`@factofthefilm12` 0.00, `@theoff.recordfiles` 0.33, `@zackrawrrshorts` 0.33). Two of those three I also labelled wrong — so re-scoring is doing useful work, just too late.
- **`@reelspottv` is a false negative in waiting**: visually the right format, scored 0.25, because its caption sits on a dark-blue background rather than a white or black bar. The geometry rule needs a *distinguishable* band; a coloured backdrop defeats it.

**Precision drifted, recall has a shape problem.** The 96.4% figure was measured on a 71-account hold-out drawn from 24 tags on 2026-07-28, when the pool was 41→187. The pool is now 189 confirmed pages and the tag mix has changed. Nothing was wrong with that measurement when it was taken; it simply has not been re-taken, and the docstring still presents it as current.

## 3. What is being rejected — and the structural problem underneath

**Rejections are never persisted.** `repost_finder` counts them (`gated_out`, `non_english`, `thin_accounts`) and `emit()`s a line to the console. `emit` defaults to a no-op, `logs/run.log` contains **zero** repost-finder lines, and no artifact records a rejected handle. The only reason I could sample rejections at all is that four scratch grow-runs happened to redirect stdout to a file.

That is the finding: **you cannot audit this filter from its own output.** Recovering the rejected accounts would mean re-running discovery and paying again, because the handles are gone.

From the surviving logs, 113 rejections:

| reason | n | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| format consistency below 0.40 | 59 | 52% |
| **captions not English** | **49** | **43%** |
| too few distinct clips | 5 | 4% |

**The language bucket is the story.** It is 43% of all rejections, and it is dominated by Portuguese and Spanish:

```
  @topfilmesplus       lang:portuguese      @dicadefilmes        lang:portuguese
  @primecine.ofc       lang:portuguese      @cafepost_br         lang:portuguese
  @jogadaderiscoserie  lang:portuguese      @destinazionememe    lang:italian
```

Those handles read as exactly what the funnel wants — `topfilmesplus`, `dicadefilmes` ("movie tips"), `primecine` — Brazilian movie repost pages. I could not open them to confirm the format, because they were never persisted and my budget was committed to the confirmed sample. **Judged on the evidence available, these rejections look wrong for a format-based funnel and right only if the campaign is English-only by policy.** That is a business decision, not a detector question, and the measured size of it is 43% of everything the gate throws away.

The 59 format rejections are more defensible: the consistency distribution tops out at **38%**, just under the 0.40 bar, with the bulk at 8–33%. Nothing was rejected at 39%. But I could not visually verify a single one — same reason.

The pattern you warned about — a filter that looks correct while deleting good leads — **does apply here**, and it applies to the language filter, not the format one.

## 4. The per-tag yield has not inverted — the measurement was comparing different denominators

BL-829 ranked `moviefacts` first at **333.3 pages/1k clips**, and BL-855 is currently carrying that number forward. **It is an artefact.**

`bl829_tagrank.py` parses its denominator from two logs:

```python
LOGS = [... ("bl806_grow3.log", "bl829_tagmeasure.log")]
```

`moviefacts` appears in **five** logs, at 60 clips each:

```
  bl806_bounded.log  60    bl806_grow.log  60    bl806_grow2.log  60
  bl806_grow3.log    60    infra002b_a.log 60          TOTAL = 300 clips
```

So the numerator counted **every moviefacts page ever found (22)** while the denominator counted **one run's clips (60)**. Corrected, `moviefacts` is **73.3 pages/1k**, and it falls from **rank 1 to rank 14**.

Re-ranked on the full denominator:

|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | **ryangosling** | 12 | 60 | **200.0** | 2,303,086 |
| 2 | scifi | 11 | 60 | 183.3 | 726,304 |
| 3 | gothamcity | 9 | 57 | 157.9 | 488,532 |
| 4 | **brucewayne** | 8 | 57 | 140.4 | 1,582,190 |
| 5 | memereels | 8 | 57 | 140.4 | 294,760 |
| … | | | | | |
| 14 | **moviefacts** | 22 | 300 | **73.3** | 189,716 |

**"moviefacts found 67% of new pages" is an exposure effect.** It was walked four times on 2026-07-31 and produced 20 pages; walked again on 2026-08-01 it produced **2**. The other tags in the table were walked **once**.

Same two-log undercount, opposite direction.

## 5. What makes a good page: followers, and essentially nothing else

142 pages with ≥4 clips in the library. Outcome = the page's **median** `play_count`. Spread between pages is enormous — top decile / bottom decile = **295.8×** — which is what makes page selection worth doing at all.

| predictor | Spearman ρ | p | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **follower count** | **0.820** | <0.0001 | 140 |
| span of posts (days) | 0.535 | <0.0001 | 142 |
| median gap between posts | 0.523 | <0.0001 | 142 |
| clips retrieved | 0.173 | 0.038 | 142 |
| view CV within page | −0.366 | <0.0001 | 142 |
| median caption chars | −0.119 | 0.156 | 142 |
| **caption templating** | 0.062 | 0.463 | 142 |
| **caption-bar fraction** | −0.085 | 0.314 | 140 |

**Posting frequency is a confound, not a signal.** It correlates with followers at ρ=0.571, and inside follower terciles its correlation with views **vanishes**: ρ = 0.082 (p=0.59), 0.201 (p=0.17), 0.189 (p=0.19). Rank on it and you are ranking on followers with noise added.

**Templating is null again** (ρ=0.062, p=0.46) — consistent with BL-692, which refuted it. **The detector's own bar fraction predicts nothing** (ρ=−0.085, p=0.31): how strongly a page matches the caption-bar geometry says nothing about how well its clips do.

### Two objections, both checked

- *Is this BL-850's `follower_count` artefact?* **No.** That artefact is about `follower_count` varying **within** an account as the walk crosses view-ranked page boundaries, and `clip_walk.py` says so explicitly: *"It remains correct and useful at ACCOUNT level."* I used one **median per account** and correlated **across** accounts. Within-account spread is a median of **1.3%**, though 10% of accounts vary by >4.5× — using the median, not any single row, is what makes it safe.
- *Does the view floor manufacture this?* **It attenuates it.** `DEFAULT_VIEW_FLOOR = 20000` ends the walk, so every page's clips are its above-floor slice. 16 of 140 pages (11%) have medians near the floor and their true medians are unmeasured. Truncating the outcome biases a correlation **toward zero**, so ρ=0.820 is a floor, not an inflation.

### The actionable part

**`followers` is empty for all 189 confirmed pages in `master_leads.csv`.** The finder never records it, so the strongest page-level selector it could have is discarded at write time — while the account-gate response it already pays for carries it.

| follower floor | pages kept | share of pool | median page views | lift |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| none | 140 | 100% | 213,418 | 1.0× |
| ≥ 1,000 | 86 | 61.4% | 756,393 | **3.5×** |
| ≥ 10,000 | 48 | 34.3% | 2,074,314 | **9.7×** |
| ≥ 50,000 | 18 | 12.9% | 4,053,330 | 19.0× |

**38.6% of the confirmed pool has under 1,000 followers** and a median of 91,266 views.

## 6. The pool is not saturating — the gate is the binding constraint

**Saturation shows up as a rising dedup rate, not a falling confirm rate.** Those are different failures: dedup climbing means there is nothing new left to find; confirm falling means the gate is rejecting more.

| run | pass | format-fail | non-Eng | thin | dedup | confirm | dedup% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| bl806_grow3 | 30 | 4 | 12 | 1 | 5 | 63.8% | 9.6% |
| bl829_tagmeasure | 116 | 54 | 36 | 4 | 10 | 55.2% | **4.5%** |
| **all runs** | **153** | 59 | 49 | 5 | 26 | 57.5% | **8.9%** |

**Marginal confirm rate is 52.4%** — the carried-forward figure is 52.2%, so it has **not moved**. **Dedup is 8.9%**, and 4.5% on the largest run. Nine in ten accounts surfaced are still new.

The pool is not running out of pages. **It is losing 45% of everything it pays to gate** — 59 format, 49 language, 5 thin.

Per-tag, saturation is real but local, and only `moviefacts` has been walked enough to show a curve: 83.3 pages/1k on its first-day walks, **33.3 on its fifth — 40% of the first-day rate.**

### Headroom

Modelling each further walk of a tag as returning 40% of the last (the measured `moviefacts` decay), from a measured first-walk yield of 7.25 pages/tag:

```
  remaining tail per already-walked tag  :  4.8 pages
  20 live tags, all further walks        :   97 pages
  8 never-walked bank tags (discounted)  :   58 pages
  CEILING FROM THE CURRENT BANK          : ~344 pages
```

**~155 more pages from the current bank, and the tail is front-loaded:**

| after N more walks of every live tag | new pages | pool |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | +58 | 247 |
| 2 | +23 | 270 |
| 3 | +9 | 279 |
| 5 | +1 | 285 |

**One more full pass of the 20 live tags is worth 58 pages; the fourth and fifth passes are worth 5 between them.** Deeper pagination is a one-shot, not a strategy — the bank has only ever been walked `pages_per_tag: 2` deep (~60 clips per tag).

## 7. What would find better pages, ranked by expected pages per dollar

Baseline, measured on the one fully-logged run: 241 paid calls → 116 pages = **$0.00125/page, 802 pages per dollar**.

| rank | option | pages/$ | basis |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | **Accept non-English** | **1,050** | 36 pages in that run were found, **paid for, and discarded**. Zero new calls. |
| 2 | **Suggested-profiles snowball** | *unmeasured* | `crawl_suggested.py` already exists: ~40–78 clustered accounts per $0.0006 call, seeded on the 189 confirmed pages. |
| 3 | **8 never-walked bank tags** | ~481 | Frame hit-rates measured, page yield never. Cheapest *measured-shaped* new supply. |
| 4 | **Deeper pagination, live tags** | ~321 | Same machinery; 40% decay per walk. Worth exactly one more pass. |
| 5 | 247 bare title tags in `PANICBABY_STRAENGE_hashtag_bank.xlsx` | ? | 961-tag bank, **one** tag overlaps the repost bank. But its `<title>edit` tags target **editors**, not repost pages. |
| 6 | Follow who these pages repost **from** | low | See below. |

**On #1** — this is not a recommendation to drop the language gate; it is the observation that the gate's cost is 43% of all rejections and its benefit has never been measured against outcomes. The pages are already paid for at the moment they are discarded.

**On #2, why I rank it above new tags despite it being unmeasured.** Hashtag discovery pays one call per ~30 clips and then pays again per candidate. The suggested-profiles endpoint returns 40–78 accounts *already clustered around a page you have confirmed*, for the same $0.0006. The prior for "similar to a confirmed meme page" is far better than "used #joker". It needs a measured pilot before it gets a number — and note `@sniper_yatinn` and `@clipbycastle` are in the pool, so seeding indiscriminately would snowball their neighbourhoods too. **Seed it on the follower-floored subset.**

**On #6, the option that looked good and is not.** The clips these pages repost are overwhelmingly **studio footage** — there is no IG account to follow back to. Where a source *is* visible it is another repost page: `@jack.potpulse`'s posts carry a `netflixnmovies` watermark. So "follow who they repost from" collapses into "find more repost pages", which options 1–3 already do more cheaply. The watermark trail is real but it is a same-population lateral, not a new surface.

---

## What I recommend, in order

1. **Record `followers` on confirmed pages** — it rides free on a call already paid for, it is the only page-level predictor that survived, and a ≥10k floor keeps 34% of the pool at 9.7× the median clip views. This is the highest-value change in the audit and it is a write, not a filter.
2. **Add a provenance test to the account gate.** Watermark presence, or a same-frame check against the seed, or simply demoting pages whose posts are dominated by a single recurring face. The gate currently cannot tell reposted footage from own footage, and that is what let a PUBG player in at 1.00.
3. **Persist rejections** — handle, reason, consistency, one thumbnail URL. Without it this filter cannot be audited except by accident, as here.
4. **Re-rank the tag bank on the corrected denominator** and tell BL-855 before it acts on 333.3.
5. **Decide the language question deliberately.** 43% of rejections is too large to leave as a side-effect of a default.

## Honest limits

- **n=30, and the interval is wide.** 70.0% [52.1%, 83.3%] is a clear refutation of 96.4% and a poor estimate of the true rate. A 100-page read would cost ~$0.06.
- **The labels are mine alone.** No second reader. `off-niche` versus `wanted` is a judgement call on several rows — `@paxtoprime.in` posts motivational text over Batman clips and a different reader could call that either way. The `wrong` six I am confident about.
- **I read 8 covers per page, not 12.** IG's CDN was slow enough to hit a 75-second per-account budget; 8 pages of the 30 were judged on 2–3 covers. A page with a mixed feed could be mislabelled from a thin sample — this cuts both ways and I have not bounded it.
- **I could not open a single rejected account.** They are not persisted, and re-discovering them costs money I did not have. The rejection analysis is from log lines and handle names only, and the claim that the Portuguese pages look right is **inference from handles, not a hand-read.**
- **Page quality is measured on above-floor clips.** `DEFAULT_VIEW_FLOOR` ends the walk, so 11% of pages have medians pinned near 20k and their true medians are unknown. Direction of the bias is toward zero; magnitude is not established.
- **The tag-value table conflates two denominators.** Pages per tag is all-time; clips surfaced is what the logs recorded. Tags walked in unlogged runs are under-counted, which inflates their yield — the same class of error I am correcting in BL-829, bounded here by every tag having near-identical logged exposure (57–60 per walk). **The ranking is robust; the absolute pages/1k figures are not.**
- **`source_tag` is unreliable per-page.** `@clipbycastle`, a Kick-gossip page, was found via `#onepiece`. Pages spam unrelated tags, so per-tag *quality* (§4) inherits that noise even though per-tag *counts* are sound.
- **29 of 189 pages predate `source_tag`** and are excluded from every per-tag figure.
- **The headroom model has one measured decay constant, from one tag.** 0.40 comes from `moviefacts` alone. If other tags decay slower the ceiling is higher, and ~344 is the number I would least defend in this report.
- **$0.0012 was spent unmetered and then metered by hand.** My first attempt hung on a CDN socket and I killed it, skipping the `finally` that records spend. Two paid calls had already left. They are in `spend.json` under `bl858_handread_killed`. The harness now saves after every account.
