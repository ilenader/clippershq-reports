## WHAT LANDED

### 1. The scramble: clip 02 is a ONE-PIXEL fuse, and it was already fixed — but its twin was not

**Clip 02 (@apexsyndicate), measured live off its own five OCR frames this session:**

```
y 306-374  x 142  'Me discovering that my poker'
y 373-451  x 121  'rival at the casino is actually my'      <- overlaps the row above by 1 px
y 453-518  x 185  'son that I lost gambling 23'
y 535-594  x 407  'years ago'
```

Rows 1 and 2 overlap by **one pixel of a 68-pixel line** — ratio **0.0147**. Under the
pre-BL-1211 rule (`not (y1 < L.y0 or y0 > L.y1)`, true on a single shared pixel row) they
fuse; the fused blob is then sorted left-to-right, x0 121 sorts ahead of x0 142, and the two
lines swap. **That reproduces the shipped string character for character:**

> `rival at the casino is actually my Me discovering that my poker son that I lost gambling 23 years ago`

This is the same defect as the earlier fuse-and-swap, at one pixel instead of five.

**The important part: `assemble_reading_order` was already fixed** — BL-1211 gave it
`LINE_OVERLAP_MIN = 0.40`, and both clips read correctly in the two most recent batches
(`outputs_paste_20260811_121208`, `…_122622`). The scrambled strings come from the three
**older** batches (`20260810_171047`, `_172851`, `_201912`).

**But `line_heights()`, one function below it in the same file, still ran the OLD rule** —
and its docstring asserted the opposite:

> *"Grouping is the same vertical-overlap rule `assemble_reading_order` uses, so the two
> cannot disagree about what a line is."*

That sentence was false for four rounds. Measured on clip 02:

| | line heights |
|---|---|
| old rule (shipped until now) | `[145, 65, 59]` — rows 1+2 fused into a 145 px "line" |
| **fixed** | `[78, 68, 65, 59]` |

**`line_heights` is the drawn type size.** Its caller takes the median, so on a two-line card
the fused value is the majority and is elected outright — **the type was being set from two of
his lines added together.** That is a direct cause of §2, and nothing was measuring it.

Also fixed in the same function's neighbourhood: `sorted(L["items"])` sorted the whole
`(x0, text)` tuple, so two blocks at the same left edge — stacked centred short lines, a real
case — were ordered **alphabetically by their text**. Nobody chose that; it is what tuple
ordering does when the first element ties. Now `key=lambda i: i[0]`.

### 2. Type size and line count: two causes, both fixed

**Cause A — the strip was starving the fitter.** `compute_label_layout_verbatim` starts at his
measured size and then walks **downward** until the wrapped block fits `box_h - 2*pad`. So the
strip height is the binding constraint on type size, and BL-1215's (correct) win of pulling the
strip tight to his ink is exactly what took the room away — strip 25.5% → ~16% of frame, and
the type fell with it: his 52.5 px read 32 px of ours, his 49 px read 37 px.

Fixed by **ordering, not by a new number**: `read_source_line` now runs *before*
`label_strip_from_card`, and the strip reserves the height his own line needs at his own size
across the strip's width (`type_block_height_px`, mirroring edit.py's wrap). Demonstrated:

```
his ink low in the card, tightening applied:
  OLD strip (0, 485, 1080, 115)   height 115   <- fitter must shrink his type
  NEW strip (0, 436, 1080, 164)   height 164   = exactly what his type needs
  still covers his ink: top 436 <= ink_top 500
```

The reservation is applied **last**, after the coverage clamp, so it can only ever make the
strip taller — it can never uncover his words. Where there is spare room BL-1215's tightening
is untouched.

**Cause B — `max_lines` capped his line count at 3.** Under verbatim the text is *his* sentence
at *his* size, so the row count is a measurement of his layout, not a liberty. His busiest clip
sets four rows and the cap forced ours to three, which dragged the type down to fit.
`LABEL_MAX_LINES_CARBON = 6` now raises the cap to whatever his line wraps to at his own size,
bounded so an OCR misread cannot paint twenty rows. Measured:

| | rows | size drawn | his target |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| old cap (3) | 3 | **44 px** | 50 px |
| **with the raise** | **4** | **51 px** | 50 px |

And with the box no longer binding, the fitter now returns his size exactly — 52→52, 49→49,
69→69 on the three sampled lines.

**I have not re-rendered, so I cannot give you the per-clip delta in px off new frames.** The
old deltas stand as the target to beat: 52.5/32, 49/37, 69/86, 50/52, 15/54.

### 3. The guard that could not fail — the one the brief warned about

`paste_batch.py` recorded:

```python
_set(idx, state=DONE, ..., his_line=src_line.text, our_line=src_line.text,
```

**The same variable on both sides.** The check that consumes them —

```python
if (i.get("his_line") or "") != (i.get("our_line") or ""):
    A.append("> **THESE TWO MUST BE IDENTICAL.** They are not, and that is a defect …")
```

— is `x != x`. It was **unreachable on every `done` item**, and it read TRUE through all three
batches that shipped a scrambled line. This is exactly the failure the brief described, and
BL-1211 fixed the *cause* of one instance while leaving the *detector* inert.

`our_line` is now **OCR'd back off our own rendered mp4**, in the same band we drew into, so the
two sides have independent origins. It never refuses — pasted links are pre-approved and a
readback miss is our problem, not his clip's — and it never goes silent: a failure returns
`readback: <why>` rather than a bare `None`, because *"Errors: 1"* with no reason is the defect
this project keeps re-finding and a `None` here is the same shape one layer down.

`batch_dir` defaulted to `REPO_ROOT` — which is
`C:\Users\Game Centar\OneDrive\Desktop\clipper finder`, **inside the sync tree**. Every mp4 a

**This is worth doing whether or not it is the stutter.** A render is a build artefact; syncing

playback with him** — that needs him at the machine — so the stutter itself remains open; what
is closed is that renders no longer touch the sync engine.

## WHAT I DIAGNOSED BUT DID NOT FIX

### 5. The residual ENOSPC on @animeotaku

BL-1215's mechanism is right and I re-verified its foundations: **C: has 270 GB free**, so this
is not a disk, and ffmpeg 8.0 accepts `-shortest_buf_duration 300` without complaint. The bed is
infinite by construction (`aloop=loop=-1`) and `adelay` front-loads silence, so with `-shortest`
the audio races ahead of the video by the whole placement offset and the sync queue reports
overflow as `AVERROR(ENOSPC)`.

**What I did not do is find why 300 s is insufficient for `Db1Jihts6Au` at `adelay=10770`.**
My reading of the mechanism says the fix is structural rather than a bigger cap: **bound the bed
to the video's known duration** (`atrim=end=…`, or an explicit `-t` on the audio leg) so
`-shortest` has no infinite stream to race. A cap is a limit on a symptom; an unbounded
generator is the cause. I did not implement or test that, so it is a recommendation, not a
result. BL-1215's own note that the same clip flipped outcome with the randomised x264 preset is
consistent with the cap never having been the real lever.

### 6. Clip 08 passes its vote 3–2, which is a coin flip

Clip 08's frames disagree with each other. Live OCR of its five sampled frames:

- frames 0, 3 → `Real reason why bro is not sending g me reels` (a **detector split** — the
  box `is not sending` and the box `g me reels` are two halves of one row)
- frames 1, 2, 4 → `Real reason why bro is not sending me reels`

The vote is **3–2 against `MIN_FRAMES_AGREEING = 3`** — it passes by exactly zero margin, which
is why the same clip shipped both strings on different days with no code change. The grouping is
*correct* on frames 0/3 (those two boxes overlap 74 px of a 74 px box, ratio 1.00, and fuse in
the right order); the spurious `g` is the detector splitting a word, not an assembly fault.

**So clip 08's symptom is not a dropped word and not a merge bug.** If he is seeing words
*missing* rather than a stray `g`, the mechanism is the band construction, and the near-miss is
measurable: `'years ago'` on clip 02 clears `MIN_W_FRAC = 0.18` by 0.07 and `MIN_ASPECT = 3.0`
by 1.61. A shorter trailing line — `"23:"`, `"me:"`, one word — fails both, its band never
enters the cluster, the ink band ends above it, and `blocks_in_band` then discards it **with no
refusal and no log**. That is the word-drop path and it is one short word away. I did not widen
it, because widening a band filter without frames to check it against is how the last three
regressions happened.
