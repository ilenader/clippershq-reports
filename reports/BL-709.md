## 0. A corpus correction that invalidates BL-704's denominators

**VERIFIED by [BL-705](BL-705.md) (concurrent agent), and confirmed here.** `bl703_concentration.json` walked `_probe_samples/`, which is dominated by **per-account media pulls**. One account reusing a royalty-free library put "Apollo Keys" at 20 of 98 licensed clips — 20% of the entire clip weight on a single library composer. In the hashtag corpus that artist is 4 clips.

Measured consequence: **the 48 artists probed in BL-703/704 account for only 26 of 348 licensed clips (7.5%) of the hashtag corpus.** Every clip-weighted figure in BL-704 is therefore unvalidated on the corpus that matters, and this report re-measures rather than defends them.

Hashtag corpus (from `scratch/bl701/`): **1,462 unique clips, 348 licensed with title+artist, 263 distinct artists, 83% appearing exactly once**, top-50 covering 38.8%. BL-705's refutation of the concentration hypothesis is stronger than BL-704's and supersedes it.

## 1. Wikipedia / DBpedia infobox genre — **REFUTED as an improvement**

BL-704 called this its most promising open lead: infobox genre is CC BY-SA (share-alike + attribution but **not** NonCommercial), and editors write infoboxes long before anyone models a Wikidata P136 statement.

**VERIFIED — live probe of all 48 BL-704 artists against the en.wikipedia Action API:**

| view | result |
|---|---|
| artist-weighted genre | 25/48 (52%) |
| **clip-weighted genre** | **29/98 (30%)** |
| resolved cleanly | 41/48 |
| UNRESOLVED (HTTP 429) | 7 artists / 8 clips |

**Identical to Wikidata's 30%.** Even granting every rate-limited name a genre the ceiling is 38%. It recovers a few tail artists Wikidata lacks (adore, Gold-Tiger, Temper City, Alok/Alan Walker) and loses others, netting zero.

**It fails on the same head, for the same reason: notability.** Of the four artists carrying 49% of the old corpus, Wikipedia has no usable genre for any — Trikstar has no article at all, and the "kowlys" hit was a wrong-article match with no genre. An encyclopaedia will never carry a royalty-free library composer no matter how many Reels use them.

**Licence, stated plainly:** CC BY-SA 4.0 permits commercial use — the obligations are attribution (credit Wikipedia and the article, conventionally with a link and the licence name) and share-alike on *adaptations of the licensed text*. Extracting a short factual label sits closer to fact extraction than to text reuse, and bare facts are not copyrightable, but a stored database reproducing infobox phrasing at scale is where share-alike starts to bite. **Since it delivers exactly what CC0 Wikidata already delivers, there is no reason to take the obligation on.**

**Two bugs of my own, both silent:**
1. My first extractor matched `| genre = …` only to end-of-line. Modern infoboxes span lines (`{{hlist| [[Pop music|pop]] | [[disco]] }}`), so it returned **empty for exactly the best-maintained articles** — ABBA and The Rolling Stones both read as "no genre". A brace/bracket-depth scanner fixed it (ABBA → *Pop, disco, pop rock, Europop*). This looked like missing data, not a broken parser.
2. Rate limiting again: 12 names 429'd on the first pass, 7 on the second even at 2.6 s spacing — all held as UNRESOLVED, never counted absent, per BL-704's warning.

**Extraction-quality caveat:** editor comments leak into naive parsing — "Kendrick Lamar, SZA" extracted as `R&B, hip-hop, CITED TO A RELIABLE SOURCE, BE…`. Production use needs comment stripping and a genre allow-list.

## 2. Discogs — **BL-704's inference is REFUTED. Genre is CC0.**

BL-704 was blocked by HTTP 403, inferred from a secondary quote that genre/style were probably Restricted Data, and flagged it as the report's most important unresolved question. **The primary document overturns that.**

**VERIFIED — API Terms of Use** (direct fetch and browser-UA fetch both returned 403 from Cloudflare; retrieved via a text-extraction proxy):

> "The Content includes the following JSON- and XML-formatted, CC0 data (the 'CC0 Data'):"
> — Release titles, notes, dates, format, track listings, barcodes and other identifiers, credits, versions, URL links to third-party sites
> — **Artist names, notes, associated releases**
> — Label, producer, manufacturer, distributor, etc. names and contact information, notes, and associated releases

> "Our API provides access to the following restricted use Content (data that is not CC0) (the 'Restricted Data')."
> — **Discogs User Data** (username, user images; optionally real name, homepage, location, biography…)
> — **Marketplace Data** (inventory, orders, lists, fees, pricing suggestions, sales history)
> — **Images** (release, artist, label, user images)

**The words "genre" and "style" appear nowhere in the document**, and the Restricted list is a closed set of three categories — user data, marketplace data, images. Genre is in none of them.

**Corroborating primary source:** `data.discogs.com`, which serves the monthly database dumps (whose XML carries `<genres>` and `<styles>`), states: *"This data is made available under the CC0 No Rights Reserved license."*

**Commercial clause, for the record:** prohibited are *"Selling or giving to any third party Our API, the Content, or access to Our API or the Content"* and *"Charging a fee to use or access any part of Your application that integrates with Our API or the Content if we provide that access to users free of charge, without Our express written permission."* That bars **reselling or paywalling Discogs data** — not internal enrichment of your own clip library. And the **dumps route sidesteps the API terms entirely**: CC0 files, no API, no rate limit, no ToU acceptance.

**GAP — coverage is unmeasured, and it is the only thing left to check.** Discogs search requires a token, so a keyless probe was impossible under the $0 constraint. The open question is precisely the one that killed Wikipedia and Wikidata: **does a collector-built release catalogue carry royalty-free library music and Indian film playback singers?** Its electronic/hip-hop taxonomy is not in doubt; its coverage of this corpus is. A free personal access token makes this measurable at $0.

## 3. Comma-split against iTunes — **worth 3× the estimate**, and iTunes re-measures at 97%

BL-704 estimated comma-splitting multi-artist credits would recover ~8% of licensed clips. **Measured on the unbiased corpus: 15 artists / 37 clips = 25.5% of the measured slice.**

**VERIFIED — top 60 hashtag-corpus artists (145 of 348 licensed clips = 41.7% of the corpus), 60/60 resolved, zero rate-limit contamination:**

| source | artists with genre | **clip-weighted** |
|---|---|---|
| **iTunes** (with comma-split fallback) | 57/60 | **140/145 = 97%** |
| **Wikidata** | 16/60 | **44/145 = 30%** |

Every recovered split is a real artist credit, not a fuzzy guess:

| credit string | split to | genre |
|---|---|---|
| Sai Abhyankkar, V.M. Mahalingam, Paal Dabba | Sai Abhyankkar | Tamil |
| Arijit Singh, Harshdeep Kaur, JAM8 | Arijit Singh | Bollywood |
| KR$NA, Dhanda Nyoliwala | KR$NA | Hip-Hop/Rap |
| Kumar Sanu, Alka Yagnik, P. Sunanda | Kumar Sanu | Worldwide |
| tienanh109, HDN, MC K3 | tienanh109 | Funk |

**Wikidata's 30% is remarkably stable** — the same figure on two different corpora — but the re-probe exposes *why* it is 30%: this corpus skews heavily to **Indian/Tamil film music and small electronic producers** (BL-705 observed the same), and Wikidata is `absent` for nearly all of them while carrying the Western famous names. iTunes returns *Tamil, Bollywood, Regional Indian, Música Mexicana, K-Pop, Worldwide* — a vocabulary that actually fits the corpus. Coarse, but fitted.

## 4. TheAudioDB — terms located, permissive with mandatory attribution

**VERIFIED — `theaudiodb.com/docs_terms_of_use.php`:**
- *"You can scrape, copy and modify any content returned from the API, as long as you use the official end points"*
- *"You cannot resell our API in any way without specific permission"*
- *"You must mention us as the source of the data"* (paid users)
- App-store publishing requires a paid subscription; free key 30 req/min, Premium $8/mo 100/min, Business 120/min
- No named Creative Commons licence; **no explicit clause on storing data in your own database**

Workable for an internal pipeline at $8/month with a visible attribution, and well inside budget. **GAP: coverage unmeasured**, and given it is also a community-built catalogue it likely shares Discogs' uncertainty about library music.

## Where this leaves the decision

1. **Measure Discogs coverage with a free token** — it is now the only source that is both provably CC0 and taxonomically strong. If it carries this corpus's Tamil/Bollywood and library artists, it wins outright and the licence question is already settled.
2. **Ship the comma-split regardless of source** — free, and worth 25% of the slice on its own.
3. **Do not adopt Wikipedia infobox.** Same 30% as Wikidata with an added BY-SA obligation.
4. **iTunes stays blocked on a licence read, not on merit** — 97% is the best coverage anyone has measured here, and it is one legal opinion away from being usable.
5. Genre remains null on most clips regardless: only ~23% of clips carry a track at all, and BL-705 further found that **14.3% of `licensed_music` clips carry no title**, server-side and variably.
