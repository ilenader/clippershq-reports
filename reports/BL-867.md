## I was wrong about the estimator, and the correct answer was already landing

BL-865 asked whether `estimated_pages()` mattered — it counts chronologically newer clips while `retrieve()` walks a feed sent with `sort_by_views=true`. I measured 93.9% against a 95.2% ground truth, called the difference immaterial, and recommended leaving it alone. **That recommendation was carried into two subsequent briefs. It was wrong.**

**The test was confounded.** My ground truth was *clips that had been labelled at `max_pages=1`* — a population that is on page 1 **by construction**. I asked the model to predict page 1 for clips already known to be on page 1, and it obliged. I never tested the clips it called **deep**, which is precisely where an ordering error shows up.

BL-921 ran that test. Of 12 clips the function called unreachable at estimates of 3, 5, 5, 5, 7, 7, 7, 9, 9, 9 and 10 pages, **every one was found on page 1 or 2** — the docstring's promise that "true depth is worse, never better" held for **0 of 12**. BL-925 then rewrote it to count clips of the same account with a higher `play_count`, matching the ordering the feed is actually sent in.

**Verified independently here, against BL-866's own recorded actuals (n=261 clips carrying both an estimate and a billed page count):**

| estimator | total pages | per clip | vs actual | exact | under | over |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **OLD** (recency axis) | 399 | 1.53 | **−43%** | 32.6% | 64.4% | 3.1% |
| **NEW** (views axis) | 640 | 2.45 | **−8%** | **89.3%** | 9.6% | 1.1% |
| actual billed | 697 | 2.67 | — | — | — | — |

**Item 2 asked me to correct the estimator or apply the measured 1.75× multiplier at every caller. I did neither, and the multiplier would have been a mistake.**

A flat 1.75× on the old estimator gives 399 × 1.75 = 698 pages against 697 actual — the aggregate lands almost perfectly. **But the per-clip number stays wrong 67% of the time**, and per-clip is what `should_skip()` uses to decide whether a clip is worth paying for. A multiplier would have made the *budget* look right while leaving the *decision* broken, and it would have hidden the axis bug behind a correction factor that appeared to work. The axis fix is correct where the multiplier is only correct on average.

**The residual −8% is an honest lower bound**: we hold a subset of each account's clips, so clips we do not hold can also outrank a given one. A modest safety factor covers it; a 1.75× multiplier on top of the new estimator would now over-budget by 60%.

## Item 3's correction, restated — because it was also incomplete

BL-865 offered "the average rises as coverage grows." BL-866 added "the mix moved the other way and the bound is loose." **Both were reaching for a mechanism that did not exist.** There were never two mechanisms. There was one:

> The estimator reported position in a **chronological** ordering while the fetcher walked a **view-sorted** one. Those orderings are unrelated, so the output was not a weak or conservative depth signal — it was a number about a different question. It ran low in aggregate on BL-866's pool and high on BL-921's excluded set, which is what an uncorrelated estimate does, not what a lower bound does.

"The bound is loose" was my attempt to explain a 72% miss without questioning the axis, after I had already told two rounds the axis was fine.

## The 4 re-openable retirements: 3 rescued

`vision_attempts >= MAX_ATTEMPTS` is a permanent tombstone. That is right for a media fault and wrong for a fault in **our own tooling** — a clip retired for "unparseable response" was retired because the parser of the day could not read a reply **we had already paid for**. BL-889 later replaced every runner's private regex with a shared `clip_vision.parse_vision_json`. The ceiling does not know the parser changed.

**The split now applied:**

| class | reasons | policy | at ceiling |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **PERMANENT** | no `video_dash_manifest`, no rendition with a url, larger than 18MB | retire and mean it | **13** |
| **TOOLING-DEPENDENT** | unparseable response | **re-openable when the tooling changes** | **4** |
| transient | not found in N pages, download failed | normal retry ladder | 3 |

Re-opening is an ordinary append carrying `vision_reopened_by` and `vision_reopened_why`, so the reversal is auditable rather than a lost tombstone.

**Outcome of the retry — 3 of 4 labelled, and none was still unparseable:**

| clip_id | outcome | beats |
|---|---|---:|
| `3067724123984853772_614559053` | **labelled** | 12 |
| `3817607684854834528_80072930752` | **labelled** | **67** |
| `3923007571933207741_227282247` | **labelled** | 16 |
| `3895677589679106698_79050325291` | HTTP error — transient, stays re-opened | — |

**Zero re-retirements.** The parser fix was the entire cause, and the policy had been destroying labellable clips.

Note the 67-beat row. At the old `max_tokens=2000` that response would have truncated and been recorded as unparseable — which is very likely how it was retired in the first place. **Two separate tooling limits produced the same tombstone**, and both have since moved.

BL-866 named 8 such clips; only 4 remained. Of 35 clips whose failure history mentions unparseable, **30 are now labelled** — other rounds rescued them incidentally once the shared parser landed. That is the same effect arriving by accident rather than by policy, which is the argument for making it policy.

## The SHA is not drift — it is a hashing convention change

The brief expects `7a029ee5447cddd8`; my formula returns `8e02f8d6f6307ae8`, the value every prior round matched. **The campaigns block is byte-identical** — 5 campaigns, `ZHUS / PANICBABY / STRAENGE / DAYLIGHT / ANIME15K`:

| hashing of the **same** block | digest |
|---|---|
| `json.dumps(sort_keys=True)` — my formula | `8e02f8d6f6307ae8` |
| `json.dumps(sort_keys=True, separators=(',',':'))` — **compact** | **`7a029ee5447cddd8`** |

Someone standardised on compact separators. **Both digests describe the same bytes, and nothing in the campaigns config changed.** Flagging it so the next round does not read a convention change as a config drift and go hunting.
