| 5 | delivered, ready-to-post | **UNBACKED** | renders land in a scratch dir named for *our* run. No per-client location, no hand-off, no record of what went to whom — until this round's delivery record |

**Two of seven are served. One is human. Four are unbacked or undecided.**

The honest summary: the render pipeline is a **meme-repost** pipeline. It takes a scraped
Instagram clip, lays a licensed song under it and burns a frame. A client's footage is a
different input, a different edit, and a different output destination. The overlap is the
canvas size and ffmpeg.

## The intake — `clippershq/client_intake.py`

One JSON per client at `output/clients/<client_id>.json`. **Not a portal.** Five required
answers, because an editor cannot start without them:

| field | why it is required |
|---|---|
| `source` | where the footage IS. Free text on purpose — a musician answers with a Dropbox link, a streamer with a VOD list |
| `platforms` | which of tiktok/reels/shorts. Does **not** change the cut (same canvas); it sets expectations and is what the delivery record joins on |
| `do_not_use` | the rule that makes a deliverable unusable if broken |
| `contact_email` | who the clips go back to — **the 184 agency rows are where this misdelivers** |
| `segment` | copied from the funnel so the intake stands alone |

`brand_notes` and `reference_links` are optional and stay optional.

**`do_not_use` has no safe default.** An empty list cannot be told apart from a question
nobody asked, and those are different facts: one is a client who said there are no
restrictions, the other is an unasked question that surfaces later as an unusable clip. So it
must be a non-empty list **or** the explicit `NOTHING_WITHHELD` sentinel. Same rule
`clip_speech` applies to a missing audio class.

`client_id` is derived and readable (local part `jo` at domain `vellum-mgmt.example` → `jo-at-vellum-mgmt`), not
hashed — an operator must know whose file it is from the filename, and the address is already
in `master_leads.csv`.

## The delivery record — `clippershq/client_delivery.py`

**ONE MECHANISM, NOT TWO.** A client deliverable *is* an outcome-loop record. It carries the
same `record_id`, derived from the output path exactly as `outcome_loop.record_id_for` does,
and its metrics arrive through `outcome_loop.append_outcome` unchanged. This module adds only
what outcome_loop has no opinion about: **which client** it was for and **which editor** made
it.

A second results table would fork the loop — two shapes, two importers, and statistics that
silently cover half the posts.

```
batch      batch_id, client_id, created_at, state, editor, brief_note, deliverables[]
deliverable record_id, output, source_ref, platform, duration_s, captioned, delivered_at, note
states     planned -> delivered -> posted
```

`captioned` is **per clip and defaults FALSE**, because that is what was measured — a
deliverable is uncaptioned until a human captions it, rather than inheriting the promise as
if it were a fact.

### The asymmetry, which is not a defect

| | who sees the result |
|---|---|
| our own memes | we post → we know the url immediately |
| client clips | **the client posts** → we know nothing unless they tell us |

"We send back … ready-to-post" means the client publishes. So `post_url` and every metric on
a client deliverable are **reported, not observed**, and `delivered` and `posted` are separate
states. A deliverable can sit in `delivered` forever without that being our failure.
`join_outcomes` returns `outcome: None` for an unreported clip — **distinct from zero**, so an
unreported clip is never counted as a zero-view one.

## The gap list — "a client says yes tomorrow" to "the client has clips"

Ranked by what blocks first. **The human steps are the answer, not a failure.**

| # | gap | blocks | who |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Price and turnaround are undecided.** Cannot answer "how much / how fast" | the reply to the first yes | **operator** — a decision, not code |
| 2 | **No ingest.** No way to receive footage: no link convention, no folder, no size limit, no format check | everything | operator (a shared folder is enough) + a convention |
| 3 | **No editor.** Somebody cuts three clips by hand. The brief another round is building is the input to this | the trial | **human** |
| 4 | **No captioning.** The most labour-intensive part of short-form, promised in the copy, with zero implementation | promise 4 | human today; ASR is a build |
| 5 | **No delivery channel.** Where do finished files go? A link, a folder, an email | promise 5 | operator convention |
| 6 | **No portfolio.** `[[PORTFOLIO_LINK]]` is unfilled and the copy offers samples | the reply | operator |
| 7 | **Results depend on the client telling us.** No mechanism asks them | measurement | design — a follow-up, not a scraper |
| 8 | **No pricing model per segment.** A 3-hour VOD and a 3-minute music video are not the same job | scaling past the first client | operator |

**Gaps 1, 2, 5 and 6 are operator decisions and could be closed this afternoon with no code.**
Gap 3 is a person. Gap 4 is the only one that is genuinely a build, and a human with a
captioning tool serves the first ten clients without it.

## What this round did NOT build, on purpose

No renderer, no ingest, no captioning, no delivery channel. The brief said design and report;
the code is the intake schema and the delivery record, with 36 tests. Anything else would
have been a pipeline nobody asked for, aimed at a promise whose price is still blank.

## What I got wrong on the way

**My own validator refused a legitimate client.** `segment` is required, and `""` is a
LEGITIMATE value — it is the generic key in `_CLIENT_COPY`, the copy that sold the job. My
first `validate()` looped over `REQUIRED` rejecting anything empty, so it refused the exact
client the template already serves. Caught by
`test_the_generic_segment_is_a_legitimate_answer`, which I wrote before running it because
`SEGMENTS` mirrors `_CLIENT_COPY` and `""` is one of its keys. Required-to-be-PRESENT and
required-to-be-NON-EMPTY are different constraints and the loop conflated them.

`test_the_segments_match_the_copy_that_sold_the_job` now asserts the two sets are equal, so
the intake cannot drift from the copy — a client sold one thing and briefed another is the
failure mode that matters here.

## Suites, campaigns, config

**162 of 165 suites green.** One of the three red was mine and is fixed; two are not.

| red suite | mine? |
|---|---|
| `tests/test_clip_pipeline.py` | **YES — fixed.** Its guard scans every `clippershq/*.py` for the render ledger's filename and reports any module that is not one of two sanctioned writers. My delivery module named it **in a comment** saying its own ledger is a different file. The scan cannot tell prose from a call. I **reworded the comment rather than adding an exemption** — the guard is right, and my module writes nothing to that file: it appends to its own and reads the other through `outcome_loop.resolve()`, which is the sanctioned door. Weakening a correct guard to accommodate a correct module is how the next real third writer gets in. |

scan. Not my file and not a defect that bit anyone but me, so it is noted, not changed.
