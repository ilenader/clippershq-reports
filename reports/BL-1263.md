# BL-1263 — The 43.7% was a cap set below an invisible tail; the lane plateau was 12 mod 8

**Budget: $1.00 hard cap, declared before spending. Spent $0.886195, enforced in the
harness, never breached.** 24 API calls, all on `anthropic/claude-opus-5`, every dollar from
the vendor's own `usage.cost`. Ledger: `spend.json`, vision vendor, via `record_aux_spend`.

---

## 1. The waste: the cap was not mean, it was pointed at the wrong half of the reply

BL-1258 sized `max_tokens` at 1,500 for an expected ~600-token reply. Five of twelve calls
hit the cap exactly, were billed in full, and returned truncated JSON that would not parse:
**$0.228, 43.7% of that run, paid for nothing.**

### The cause, measured rather than assumed

The reply has two halves and only one of them is visible. Across nineteen completed replies
(BL-1258's seven clean + this round's twelve) the **visible JSON never overran**:

| | min | median | p90 | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| visible JSON tokens (n=12, this round) | 305 | 528 | 567 | **602** |
| visible JSON tokens (n=7, BL-1258) | 273 | 476 | — | 515 |
| **reasoning tokens** (n=12, this round) | 112 | 680 | 1,366 | **1,502** |
| **total output** (n=12, this round) | 417 | 1,187 | 1,932 | **2,104** |

A 1,500 cap is **2.5× the largest visible reply ever observed**. It looked generous. It
failed because reasoning is a second, unbounded stream billed in the same envelope, and
`max_tokens` caps the two together. Two of BL-1258's five failures (`acknowledge.ai`,
`menacesoccerhq`) spent the **entire** 1,500 on reasoning and emitted *zero characters* of
content — a full-price call that returned nothing at all.

**So "size it from the measured output distribution — median 857 chars of written reason"
measures the half that never overruns.** The written reason is stable and small. The tail
that broke the run is invisible in the response body and only shows up in
`usage.completion_tokens_details.reasoning_tokens`.

### The fix, and why it is close to free

**Output is billed per token *generated*, not per token *allowed*.** Raising the cap costs
nothing on a reply that was going to be 900 tokens; it only stops a reply that wanted 1,900
from being cut at 1,500 and thrown away. The 43.7% was never the price of a generous cap —
it was the price of a mean one.

Cap set to **3,000**: 1.43× the measured maximum (2,104), 2.5× the median. Result on the
same twelve pages:

| | BL-1258, cap 1,500 | BL-1263, cap 3,000 |
|---|---:|---:|
| pages | 12 | 12 |
| **usable verdicts** | **7** | **12** |
| truncated | 5 | **0** |
| paid for nothing | **$0.228 (43.7%)** | **$0.000 (0.0%)** |
| receipt | $0.522053 | $0.499388 |
| $ / page | $0.043504 | $0.041616 |
| **$ / usable verdict** | **$0.074579** | **$0.041616 (−44.2%)** |

Per-page cost barely moved, because we now pay for the full replies we used to cut off. **The
number that moved is cost per usable verdict, −44.2%**, and that is the honest one: the old
run bought seven verdicts with twelve pages of spend.

### Truncation is now detected and retried, not dropped

`finish_reason == "length"` is a first-class outcome, distinct from a format miss (BL-1258
could not tell them apart — both surfaced as `parse_error`). Proven by forcing it:

```
capehq, max_tokens=600  ->  truncated at 600 tok  -> RETRY at 1200 -> ok, verdict WANT
  paid-for-nothing this run: $0.015000 (13.8%)   <- priced, not vanished
```

Every attempt keeps its own receipt; a page that truncates *again* is reported as
`truncated_after_retry` rather than disappearing. `wasted_on_discarded_output_usd` is now a
field on every run, so this failure can never be silent again.

### The budget is enforced in the harness, not in the plan

BL-1258 breached $1.00 and reported $1.048 afterwards. A budget checked after the money is
gone is not a budget. `--budget` holds a running total of the vendor's own `usage.cost` and
**refuses to start a call whose worst case** (`max_tokens` fully generated) would cross the
line. This round: **$0.886195 of $1.00**, zero refusals needed.

## 2. Lanes: there was never a ceiling at six — the arithmetic was 12 mod 8

BL-1258 measured 5.91 / 11.91 / 23.42 / 33.96 / 33.48 pages/min at 1/2/4/6/8 lanes and read
"local saturation at 6". The harness split work with `handles[i::lanes]` — a **static**
partition — over 12 pages:

| lanes | pages per lane | critical path | wall | wall ÷ critical path |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 12 | 12 pages | 121.8 s | 10.15 s/page |
| 2 | 6,6 | 6 pages | 60.4 s | 10.07 s/page |
| 4 | 3,3,3,3 | 3 pages | 30.7 s | 10.23 s/page |
| 6 | 2×6 | 2 pages | 21.2 s | 10.60 s/page |
| 8 | 2,2,2,2,1,1,1,1 | **2 pages** | 21.5 s | 10.75 s/page |

Eight lanes had the **same two-page critical path as six**, because 12 does not divide by 8.
The wall clock could not fall and it did not. Per-page service time was **flat at
10.07–10.75 s across every arm** — the signature of nothing saturating. The plateau was the
page count, not the machine, and the number that would have said so was already in the file.

### What the page actually spends its time on

`bl1257_capture.capture_one` sleeps **7,200 ms per page on a clock**, unconditionally:
3,500 settle + 400 after Escape + 600 after the dismissal + 4×450 scroll sweep + 900
scrolling back. Measured per phase over 8 pages, one lane:

| phase | shipped (ms) | condition-waits (ms) |
|---|---:|---:|
| goto | 675 | 835 |
| **settle** | **3,512** | **164** |
| escape | 434 | 20 |
| dismiss JS | 29 | 7 |
| **dismiss settle** | **610** | **8** |
| **scroll sweep** | **1,889** | **67** |
| **scroll top** | **925** | **6** |
| await decode *(already a condition — the control)* | 54 | 26 |
| measure | 33 | 11 |
| **TOTAL** | **8,161** | **1,240** |

**76% of the page was the harness waiting on a clock for things that had already happened.**
Replacing each sleep with a poll for the condition it stood in for — *keeping the original
sleep as the cap*, so it can never wait longer than the shipped path — gives **6.58×**:
8.16 s → 1.24 s per page, with **12 tiles on 8 of 8 pages and zero fidelity regressions**.
That is the number that mattered; BL-1257 paid for the tile-loss lesson twice already, so the
arms are compared on tiles and grid geometry, not on time.

### The new scaling curve — 48 pages, shared work queue, no static split

Lane count no longer quantizes the critical path. `page_s` is the saturation test: throughput
is bounded by ⌈N/lanes⌉ whatever the machine does, so only per-page latency can tell you
whether a resource is under pressure.

| capture | mode | lanes | pages/min | speedup | page_s | vs 1 lane | free RAM |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| fast | procs | 1 | 32.59 | 1.00 | 1.5 | 1.00 | 2,506 MB |
| fast | procs | 4 | 99.03 | 3.04 | 1.9 | 1.25 | 441 MB |
| **fast** | **procs** | **8** | **123.22** | **3.78** | 2.6 | 1.71 | 348 MB |
| fast | procs | 12 | 115.74 | 3.55 | 2.7 | 1.78 | 848 MB |
| fast | procs | 16 | 110.86 | 3.40 | 4.9 | 3.22 | 353 MB |
| fast | procs | 24 | 110.22 | 3.38 | 7.8 | 5.10 | **15 MB** |
| fast | procs | 32 | 89.52 | 2.75 | 8.8 | 5.75 | 99 MB |
| fast | **contexts** | 16 | 24.14 | 0.74 | 17.4 | 11.41 | 505 MB |
| fast | **contexts** | 32 | 17.08 | 0.52 | 24.6 | 16.08 | 67 MB, **7 errors** |
| shipped | procs | 4 | 24.33 | 0.75 | 8.9 | 5.82 | 5,017 MB |
| shipped | procs | 8 | 45.97 | 1.41 | 9.0 | 5.87 | 3,431 MB |

**Peak 123.22 pages/min at 8 lanes — 3.63× BL-1258's best (33.96 at 6 lanes).**

**What saturates: memory, and it is unambiguous.** Free RAM falls 2,506 → 441 → 348 MB and
reaches **15 MB at 24 lanes** on a 16 GB machine. CPU sat at 85–100% in *every* arm including
the 1-lane one, because three other rounds were in flight, so CPU cannot be cleanly
attributed; memory can. Per-page latency rising 1.5 s → 8.8 s (5.75×) is real saturation —
exactly the signal BL-1258's flat 10.07–10.75 s did *not* show.

Note also that on the **shipped** capture with a proper queue, 4→8 lanes still gives
24.33 → 45.97 = **1.89×, near-linear**. There was no ceiling at six on the old code either.

### Does he need to open six Chrome windows himself? No.

- **The old two-window ceiling was the extension's** — only the active tab could be
  screenshotted, background tabs failed 0 of 3 with 30 s CDP timeouts. **Playwright removed
  it entirely**; lanes are a parameter and this round ran 32 of them.
- **Sharing one browser is worse, decisively.** One Chromium with N contexts over CDP:
  24.14 pages/min at 16, and at 32 it produced **7 failures and lost 8 grids (40 of 48)**.
  One browser *per lane* — what already ships — is the right architecture.
- **The useful setting is 8 lanes on this machine**, and it is a flag, not a person.

### Throttling: none observed, at any lane count

Every `procs` arm returned **47 of 48 grids and 0 errors**, at 1, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24 and 32
lanes. The one grid-less page is the same handle in every arm (dead or private), not a
throttle. No `http_*` status other than 200, no rate-limit or login markers. The only errors
anywhere were in the 32-**contexts** arm and were local 45 s `goto` timeouts under memory
pressure — our machine, not Instagram's answer.

**The caveat still stands and is not disproven by this:** 48 pages in ~30 s is a small
sample against a per-IP policy. The instrumentation to catch it is now in place and reports
per arm; a 1,000-page run should be watched, not assumed safe.

## 3. What else is left on the per-page cost

**The biggest remaining lever is the half of the bill nobody can see.** Reasoning is 808 of
1,311 output tokens per page (62%), and output is ~75% of the bill — so **reasoning alone is
~46% of every page.**

| | default effort | effort=low | change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| reasoning tokens/page | 808 | **0** | −100% |
| output tokens/page | 1,311 | 407 | −69% |
| **$ / page** | **$0.041616** | **$0.018332** | **−56%** |

**It is not proven safe, and I am not going to say it is.** On 8 pages the verdicts agree
**7 of 8 (87.5%)**. The single disagreement, `footyeight`, went WANT → REJECT — the
expensive direction, a missed lead. So I re-ran that page **at default effort**: it returned
**REJECT**, disagreeing with its own earlier default-effort WANT. **The one flip is a page
the judge does not agree with itself on, so this sample cannot attribute it to the effort
setting** — and equally cannot clear it. n=8, and none of these handles carry an operator
mark. Testing this properly needs marked pages; until then it is a measured 56% saving with
an unquantified accuracy risk, and that is how it should be reported.

**Carried forward, already measured, not re-litigated here:** the capture floor is 467²
(`mcu_universe__` flips REJECT→WANT at 374² because its dialogue tiles stop resolving, so the
disqualifier never fires — **gate on legibility, since illegible-tile count rises before
agreement falls**); 560² ran live here at 419 tokens; followers/bio/email are DOM text and
need no pixels; grid-only gives 9 tiles at 1,164 tokens instead of 6 at 1,844.

**The brief was not trimmed and must not be.** Measured 83.3% → 75.0% and 76.5% → 64.7%
against his marks, and the lean variant collapsed every reject to `creator`.

## 4. The real rate

**Judging is now the bottleneck, and that reverses BL-1258's premise** ("capture is the slow
half"). Capture got 6.58× faster; judging did not move.

| stage | setting | rate | 1,000 pages |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| capture | 8 lanes, condition-waits | **123.22 pages/min** | **8.1 min** |
| judge | 4 lanes, cap 3,000 | **10.18 pages/min** | **98.2 min** |

Judging runs at **2.54 pages/min per lane**; matching capture's 123 pages/min would need
~48 concurrent judge lanes. That is the next measurement, and it was not made this round.

### Cost per page, and the number that matters

Pass rate **20/100 = 20.0%**, Wilson **[13.3, 28.9]** — so cost per wanted page is an
interval, not a point.

| | $ / page | $ / **wanted** page @ 20% | @ 20%, interval | @ 50% (pre-sort) | $ / 1,000 pages |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| default effort | $0.041616 | **$0.2081** | $0.1440 – $0.3129 | **$0.0832** | $41.62 |
| effort=low *(unproven)* | $0.018332 | $0.0917 | $0.0634 – $0.1378 | $0.0367 | $18.33 |

**A pre-sort that lifts the pass rate 20% → 50% takes cost per wanted page from $0.2081 to
$0.0832 — a 60% cut, and it is the largest single move available**, larger than anything left
in the request shape. It does not touch accuracy on the pages that survive it.
