### Could a bot pick a clip and rebuild the edit?

**Partly — and the missing half is not the half BL-784 predicted.**

It can **obtain the pixels**. I retrieved **10 of 10** clips from stored identity alone
(`clip_pk` + `account_user_id`), at 540×960–720×1280, one paid call each. That **refutes
BL-784 blocker #1** — "records store `permalink` only… a bot cannot obtain pixels" — and
closes BL-802's step 2, which listed the retrieval arguments at 0% and "derivable from
`clip_id`". They no longer need deriving: they are stored explicitly on 200/200, and the
retrieval they enable is demonstrated rather than argued.

What it cannot do is know **what kind of edit a clip is** before paying. `layout`, `format`
and `speech_frac` are null on 200/200, so a consumer must download every clip to find out
what it looks like. The library says a clip exists, how it performed, and how to fetch it.
It does not say what it is.

## 4. `layout`, `format`, `franchise` — none of them populate

- **`layout` — still 0%.** `clip_record()` passes `layout=None` literally. The stage costs
  0.032 s/clip and does not run.
- **`format` — still 0%, and wiring `layout` would not fix it.** `build_record()` derives
  `format` from `speech_frac`, which `clip_record()` also passes as `None`. `format` is
  downstream of the 1.6 s/clip speech stage, not of the 0.032 s layout stage.
- **`franchise` — still 0%.** `content_genre.label()` finds a franchise on **0 of 200**
  captions. BL-784's "extractable at $0, the caption literally begins `Title:`" was true of
  `clipcapture.tv` — an account this run never reached. On the captions that *did* land the
  titles are present but shaped differently: **20%** match `<Title> – ` on line 1, **50%**
  carry a `(YEAR)`. Extractable in principle; not by the current labeler.

## 5. Ten permalinks, opened as pixels rather than captions

The library stores retrievable identity, so "is this the format wanted?" was answered from
frames, not inference: 10 clips retrieved, 2 frames each, contact-sheeted.

**9 of 10 are exactly the format described — a text caption bar above an inset sourced clip
on black.** The tenth (`darknightjoe`) is a stacked webcam-reaction over LEGO footage: a
different format entirely. Sample of 10, eyeballed — which is precisely the job `layout`
exists to do at scale and 0.032 s a clip.

### BL-784's "only 1 of 7 accounts is templated" generalises wrongly

My own first measurement repeated the error: I grepped for `Title:` and got 0/200, which
would have gone into this report as "the library is 0% templated". Measured against markers
actually present, `loste1980` is **98% templated** (`Main Cast:`, `IMDb:`, `Streaming on:`,
`Genre:`, `Premise:`) and the library is **58.5% templated** overall — every one of them from
that single account; the other five are 0%.

Templating is **per-account with different templates**. Searching for one page's template
finds one page. BL-784's underlying warning — the library is not uniformly one format —
holds, and is if anything stronger than stated.

### Two accounts in the library are one operation

`theelitescenes` and `theelitescense` — one letter apart, different user IDs
(`46406032196` / `16171807964`), different `clip_pk` — posted the **identical caption and a
visually identical clip**. `crossdedup` did not merge them, correctly by its own rules
(distinct accounts, no shared contact), but for a clip library that is duplicate supply.
One pair in 200 clips — though it is 1 of the 7 clips those two accounts contributed, and it
surfaced in the first ten I opened.

## 6. Two more things the run settled

**`duration_s` at 40.5% is structural, not a bug.** It fills **81/81** for `original_sounds`
and **0/118** for `licensed_music`, because `original_sound_info` exists only for the former.
`media_duration_s` from the DASH manifest covers 196/200, and between them **every clip has a
duration**. A consumer wanting duration should read either field, not `duration_s` alone.

**Rendition quality is better than the first record suggests.** Best offered across the
library: **1080×1920 on 64 clips, 720×1280 on 116**, 360×640 on 13. Codecs are mostly VP9 and
AV1. Four clips carry no renditions at all. Subtitles are available on **48/200 (24%)** —
consistent with BL-798's 16.7% live figure and its finding that coverage clusters by account.

## Honest limits

- **200 clips from 6 accounts, 191 of them from two.** This is not a representative sample of
  the 42 confirmed pages, and every fill rate here is dominated by `loste1980`'s template.
  A run that walked all 42 would produce different numbers for `content_genre`, `track_title`
  and the templated share.
- **The format verdict is 10 clips, eyeballed.** 9/10 is an impression, not a measurement.
- **The stopping rule was barely exercised.** All 12 below-floor rejections came from four
  small accounts; the two large ones never dipped below the floor.
- **`run_stages()` was not exercised at all.** The live ladder evidence covers the walk's view
  filter, not the stage machinery BL-784 tested with synthetics.
- **Nothing was fixed.** `layout`, `format`, `franchise` and the five engagement counts are
  each a small wiring gap in `clip_record()` — all free, all on data already paid for — but
  the brief was read-only on pipeline code, so they are reported, not closed.
