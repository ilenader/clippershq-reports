## The number

**One finished video costs $0.0195 fully loaded, at today's measured selection.**
**1,000 of them cost $19.48 and 28 hours — and cannot be made from the library that exists.**

| | $/video | $/1,000 videos | labelling hours /1,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 in 5 selected | $0.01306 | **$13.05** | 19.3 |

| 1 in 10 selected | $0.02611 | **$26.11** | 38.6 |
| 1 in 20 selected | $0.05222 | **$52.22** | 77.1 |

Everything upstream of the choice is paid on clips you discard, so cost per video is simply
the cost of one labelled clip multiplied by the selection ratio. **The ratio is the whole
model.** The 1-in-7.5 row is not a scenario — it is what the library measures right now.

### What is metered, what is estimated, what is missing

**Estimated is a label heuristic, not a flag.** Not one of the 171 entries carries
`estimated: true`. All nine are caught by `_label_is_estimated()` matching words in the label,
and every one is a reconstruction of a killed run. That heuristic also catches
`CORRECTION_killed4_was_not_killed_double_count` — a *correction*, counted as estimated
because its label contains "killed" — and `bl858_handread_killed`, which may well have been
metered. The 3.94% is right to within a rounding of these; the classification underneath it
is a substring match on a human-written string.

**And then the missing money.** `main.record_aux_spend()` takes `ig_calls`, `tt_calls`,
`tikhub_calls`, `link_calls`. There is **no parameter for vision spend**, and its docstring
says the ledger splits "by the SAME three vendors." OpenRouter is not one of them.

> **Vision cannot be recorded in this ledger even deliberately.** It is not that the runners
> forget — there is no field to put it in.

Neither `bl872_label.py`, `bl864_ranked_label.py`, `clip_vision.py` nor `clip_media.py`
contains a single `record_aux_spend`, `metered_spend_fn` or `IncrementalMeter` call, and all
four reach `ig_client`. That confirms BL-873's retrieval finding independently — and shows it
is the smaller half.

Vision is **52% of the cash and 96% of the clock**. Retrieval is another 31%. The two paid
legs are 83% of the bill and the ledger records neither of them properly.

**Render is the one stage with no number anywhere.** Nothing in the tree times it and nothing
declares a video finished. I priced it at $0 because ffmpeg is local, which is safe; I did
*not* invent a wall-clock figure for it, so every hours-per-1,000 figure here is a labelling
floor and excludes rendering entirely.

**OCR is in the cost table but not in the pipeline.** `clip_walk.STAGE_COST_S` carries
`ocr = 16.08 s/clip` — larger than vision — but BL-851 established the stage is declared
UNWIRED and did not run. It is excluded above. If it is ever switched on it more than doubles
the clock.

## 3. The selection ratio is the dominant term — and the real one is not a choice yet

The figures at the top are the answer to item 3. What makes them concrete is that **the ratio
is currently set by the song store, not by a quality threshold.**

- 2,003 clips · **1,505 vision-labelled (75.1%)** · 985 speech-labelled (49.2%)
- 4 songs · **208 clips match a mood (10.38%)**
- **Of the labelled clips, 202 of 1,505 — 13.4% — match a mood. That is 1 in 7.5.**

So today's effective selection is 1-in-7.5 *before anyone applies a quality bar at all*. Ranking
does not choose it; the mood map does. Add a 1-in-5 quality selection on top of that eligibility
and the real ratio is 1-in-37 — **$0.097 per finished video, five times the headline.**

This is why the threshold is not the first decision to make. **Adding songs moves this number
harder than any ranking change**, because it moves the denominator of every stage at once.

## 4. Two premises corrected

**Ranked labelling costs 15% MORE per clip, not less.** $1.57 per 1,000 labelled against $1.36.
Ranking on `log10(play_count)` selects high-view clips; those are longer and richer and cost
more tokens. The case for ranking survives intact — but it was never the unit price. The saving
is *volume*: $2.16 for all 1,591 against $0.27 for the top 200. Any claim that ranked labelling
is cheaper per clip is refuted. **Caveat carried forward: $1.57 is n=32 against n=436, and the
length mechanism is asserted, not correlated.**

**The predicted page-1 depth collapse did not happen.** Ranking by views was expected to select
recent clips, land them on page 1 and collapse retrieval depth. Measured: **1.55 pages per
clip — worse** than the unranked baseline. The speech pool skews older and that dominated.
There was no depth saving to collect.

> **A denominator problem underneath that second correction.** The "library average" it is
> worse than is quoted three different ways: 1.00 (BL-864), 1.18 (quoted by BL-873), and
> **1.345 — which is what BL-854's own page distribution actually implies** (76.4/15.5/8.1 at
> cap 2 gives 1.236 pages per attempt and 1.345 per success). I recomputed it from the
> published distribution and got the third number. The correction holds under all three, so
> nothing here changes — but three values are in circulation for one quantity and only one can
> be right.

## 5. What 1,000 finished videos cost

| selection | labelled clips needed | money | labelling hours |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 in 5 | 5,000 | $13.05 | 19.3 |
| **measured, 1 in 7.5** | **7,460** | **$19.48** | **28.8** |
| 1 in 10 | 10,000 | $26.11 | 38.6 |
| 1 in 20 | 20,000 | $52.22 | 77.1 |

**The money is not the constraint. Two other things are.**

**Supply.** 1,000 videos needs 7,460 labelled clips. The library holds 2,003 clips and 208
mood-matched ones. That is a shortfall of **5,953 clips — 3.7× the entire library** — and the
$19.48 above already includes discovering and walking them.

**Time.** 28.8 hours single-threaded, and that is the *labelling floor*: it excludes rendering,
which nobody has timed. At the observed 13.3 s/clip it is 27.6 hours of vision alone. The
figure is 2× worse if the higher observed rates (20.6 s, 21.9 s) are the true ones rather than
contention artefacts.

**So: $20 and roughly a working week of machine time, and it is not reachable from today's
library without a 3.7× supply increase.** Money has never been the reason this does not scale.

## 6. Where the remaining waste is

Applying the known shapes, with one that is larger than all of them together.

| shape | instance | cost |
|---|---|---|
| **work discarded after payment** | **1,305 of 1,507 labelled clips match no mood** | **$2.82 total · $1.87 per 1,000 labelled** |
| paying before filtering | labelling everything, then ranking | $2.16 vs $0.27 per pass |
| retrying billable errors | 6.1% unparsed responses, recurring every re-run | $0.15 per 1,000 |
| work discarded after payment | 8.1% of clips unreachable at cap 2 — paid, yields nothing | $0.10 per 1,000 |
| paying twice | 429 retries billed 3× (counter increments before send) | up to 2× the bill, only under overlap |
| paying for data in hand | page-1-all-known page buys | ~$0.02 per 1,000 |
| paying twice | 15 of 120 clips failed and were paid for anyway | 1.15× factor |
| *closed* | `user_pk` resolves, 30% of walk calls | **$0.00** — fixed |

**The first row is new and it dwarfs the rest.** Previously identified leakage totalled ~$0.25
per 1,000. Buying a vision label for a clip that no song can ever be chosen for costs
**$1.87 per 1,000 labelled — seven times all of it combined** — because it wastes the two most
expensive stages in the pipeline, vision and retrieval, in full.

It is also the cheapest to fix, and not by making anything cheaper: **the mood check is free,
local and pure, and it currently runs after the paid call instead of before it.** Nothing in
`clip_vision.select()` consults the song store. 86.6% of vision spend is currently buying
labels for clips the matcher will reject for nothing, a second later.

## Honest limits

- **Cost per finished video remains a projection.** Only a handful of finished videos exist. The
  per-labelled-clip figure of $0.002611 is measured; multiplying by a selection ratio is
  arithmetic, not observation.
- **The library moved while I measured it.** Labelling is running: the labelled count read 1,505
  at the start of my pass and 1,507 by the waste calculation minutes later. Shards were frozen
  per read, so no single ratio mixes versions, but the figures are a snapshot of a moving thing.
- **Vision unit cost has a 2.3× unreconciled spread** — $0.000681, $0.001355, $0.001365,
  $0.001570, $0.001583 across five measurements. I used $0.001355 (n=380, the largest clean
  sample). At the top of the range every dollar figure here rises about 15%.
- **Wall clock per labelled clip has a 2.2× spread** — 10.0 s, 13.3 s, 20.6 s, 21.9 s. I used
  13.3 s, which is itself derived from file timestamps rather than instrumentation, and one
  round disowned its own 21.9 s as contention-contaminated. **The hours figures are the softest
  numbers in this report.**
- **Render is entirely unmeasured** — $0 is safe, the time is simply absent. Every hours figure
  excludes it.
- **Retrieval depth is a model, not 2,003 measured retrievals**, and its own authors call it a
  lower bound. My 1.345 is recomputed from their published distribution.
- **I did not verify the 15% ranked premium is caused by clip length.** It is asserted upstream
  and I carried it; nobody has correlated token counts against duration.
- **The eligibility ratio depends on a mood map being actively edited this hour.** 13.4% is
  today's rule, not a constant, and it should rise as songs are added — which is the point.
- **TikHub is 12.5% of the ledger and appears in none of the ten cost reports I reconciled
  against.** I have left it in the ledger totals and out of the per-clip model, because nothing
  documents which stage it belongs to. If it is on the clip path, the per-clip figure is low.
