# BL-1010 — the four client decisions, and which two the code will not stop you getting wrong

**`docs/CLIENT_DECISIONS.md` ships: price, ingest, delivery and portfolio as four questions with
the consequence of each answer and the file+line the answer goes into. The finding that reorders
them: `strict=True` already refuses `[[PRICE]]`, `[[TURNAROUND]]` and `[[PORTFOLIO_LINK]]`, so
three of the four cannot be forgotten. Ingest and delivery are gated by nothing — and they are
the two that fail silently, in front of a client who has already said yes.**

No code was built for captioning, by instruction. No paid calls, no emails sent, nothing under
`memebot/`. All client-side files were FREE and clean at the precondition check;
`clippershq/clip_pipeline.py` is held by BL-899 and was read-only.

> **The brief arrived truncated** mid-sentence at item 5 — *"…and the overlap is"*. Rather than
> guess the missing clause I measured the overlap (§5). It comes to the same answer BL-1006 gave.

## 1. The decision page

`docs/CLIENT_DECISIONS.md`. Four decisions, each with the copy it is promising verbatim, what
exists today, the options with their consequences, and where the answer goes.

**The ordering finding.** These are not four equivalent blanks:

| decision | what stops a send without it | how it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Price | `render(strict=True)` refuses `[[PRICE]]` | **LOUDLY** — the send is impossible |
| Turnaround | `strict=True` refuses `[[TURNAROUND]]` | **LOUDLY** |
| Portfolio | `strict=True` refuses `[[PORTFOLIO_LINK]]` | **LOUDLY** |
| **Ingest** | **nothing** | **SILENTLY**, when a client asks where to send footage |
| **Delivery** | **nothing** | **SILENTLY**, after they have already sent it |

Measured: `render(row, strict=True)` on a client row reports
`['SENDER_NAME', 'PRICE', 'TURNAROUND', 'PORTFOLIO_LINK', 'REPLY_TO']` and raises. Nothing
anywhere reports an absent ingest or delivery convention. **So the portfolio gap is, uniquely,
already closed by the code** — a showreel is guaranteed to exist at send time; what the code
cannot check is whether it is any good.

Do ingest and delivery first. That inverts the brief's order and it is the point of the page.

## 2. Captioning — specified, not built

**What the copy promises** (`message_template.py:1084`): "we send back cut, **captioned**,
ready-to-post clips." **1,392 people.** There is no implementation anywhere.

**`subtitles_uri_of` is not a way in.** It reads `video_subtitles_uri` — *a URI Instagram
attached to a scraped clip* (`clip_media.py:221`) — and generates nothing. Client footage has no
Instagram media object and therefore never has this field. The one thing in the tree with
"subtitles" in the name is unreachable from the client path by construction.

| option | real cost | what it buys | what it costs you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Burned-in from an existing transcript** | **Not available.** The only transcript source is Instagram's own attachment, which client footage never has | — | — |
| **B. Paid transcription vendor** | Per-minute, billed on audio length. A 30-second clip is 0.5 min; **three trial clips ≈ 1.5 min**. At the ~$0.004–0.006/min band the vendors advertise, a trial is well under a cent and 1,000 clips of 30 s is single-digit dollars. **Prices must be re-checked at signup — they are quoted from memory, not measured here.** | Real captions, no infrastructure | A vendor account, an API key in the key path, and a per-clip paid step in a pipeline that currently has none on this branch |
| **C. Local ASR** | `faster_whisper` and `whisper` **are installed**, but **no model is cached** (only PaddleOCR models are), so first use needs a ~150 MB–1.5 GB download. **Throughput unbenchmarked — I did not measure it and will not quote a figure I have not run.** | $0 per clip, no vendor, no key | CPU time per clip, a model download, and a benchmark before anyone promises a turnaround |
| **D. Remove the promise** | **$0, one template edit** | The copy stops outrunning the system today | A narrower offer: "cut, ready-to-post" instead of "cut, captioned, ready-to-post" |

**D is legitimate and is the honest default until B or C is measured.** A promise with no backing
is worse than a narrower offer — it converts a client who says yes into a client you disappoint,
and the disappointment lands *after* they have sent you their footage.

**Note the asymmetry that makes captioning worse than the other gaps:** ingest and delivery fail
at the moment of contact, where you can improvise. Captioning fails at the moment of *delivery*,
after the work is done, and there is no improvising a transcript by hand at volume — it is the
most labour-intensive part of short-form editing, which is exactly why it was promised.

## 3. Copy that outruns the system

The client message, rendered verbatim (segment `creator`, one of 1,392):

> Hi @jectroeditz_24,
>
> I'm `[[SENDER_NAME]]` at ClippersHQ. We turn your long-form uploads into short-form for
> TikTok, Reels and Shorts — you send the footage, we send back cut, captioned, ready-to-post
> clips. Shorts that feed the main channel.
>
> `  Price:       [[PRICE]]` · `  Turnaround:  [[TURNAROUND]]` · `  Samples:     [[PORTFOLIO_LINK]]`
>
> If you want to see it on your own material rather than a showreel, reply and I'll cut three
> clips from your footage first.

This is the false-median shape again — **every clause is grammatical and confident, and most of
them are not backed.** Sentence by sentence:

| clause | backed? | why |
|---|---|---|
| "short-form for TikTok, Reels and Shorts" | **YES** | 1080×1920 is the correct canvas for all three |
| "We turn **your** long-form uploads into…" | **NO** | presupposes ingest; no path receives client media |
| "**you send the footage**" | **NO** | an instruction the system cannot honour |
| "we **send back**" | **NO** | no delivery channel; renders land in a scratch dir named for our run |
| "**cut**" | **partial** | ffmpeg trims; choosing the window is human (drop detection was closed at 100% fabrication) |
| "**captioned**" | **NO** | zero implementation (§2) |
| "**ready-to-post**" | **NO** | depends on cut + captioned |
| "**Shorts that feed the main channel**" *(and the 3 sibling outcome claims)* | **NO — and this is the worst one** | a claim about **results**. No client has ever been delivered to, so there is no clip that fed any channel. It is stated as fact to 1,392 strangers |
| "rather than a **showreel**" | **YES, by construction** | `strict=True` refuses an unfilled `PORTFOLIO_LINK`, so one exists at send time |
| "I'll cut **three clips** from your footage first" | **NO** | the only quantity in the message, blocked on ingest + caption + delivery |

**Proposed edits — not applied. The template is green (91 tests) and stays green.**

1. **Drop "captioned"** until §2 option B or C is measured: *"we send back cut, ready-to-post
   clips"*. One word, and it retires the largest unbacked promise.
2. **Soften the outcome claims** from result to intent — *"clips that feed the main channel"* →
   *"cut to work as channel feeders"*. Same pitch, no assertion about outcomes nobody has
   observed. This is precisely the fix BL-992 applied to the clipper branch's median claim.
3. **Make "you send the footage" concrete or conditional.** Once ingest is decided it becomes
   *"send a folder link and…"*. Until then it promises a mechanism that does not exist.
4. **Leave the trial sentence alone** — it is the strongest line in the message and it becomes
   true the moment ingest and delivery are answered.

Edits 1 and 2 are safe today and require no operator decision. 3 waits on §1.

## 4. The two design points still hold

Verified behaviourally, not by reading the docstring (`scratch/bl1010_verify.py`, 6/6):

| check | result |
|---|---|
| `do_not_use=[]` refused as ambiguous | **PASS** — *"An empty list cannot be told apart from a question nobody asked"* |
| `do_not_use=NOTHING_WITHHELD` **accepted** | **PASS** |
| `do_not_use=["unreleased single"]` **accepted** | **PASS** |
| the None-vs-zero asymmetry documented at `join_outcomes` | **PASS** |
| unreported deliverable → `outcome is None`, not `0` | **PASS** — returned `[None]` |

Both acceptance cases are here deliberately: a validator that refuses everything passes a
refusal test while being useless.

**A correction to my own harness.** My first run scored 1b and 1c as FAILURES. `validate()`
returns a **list of errors** — empty means valid — and I had assumed a dict with an `.ok` key.
The product was right and my reader was wrong; read the return shape rather than assuming it.

## 5. The one-line truth

**The render pipeline is a meme-repost pipeline. A client's footage is a different input, a
different edit and a different destination — the overlap is the canvas size and ffmpeg.**

Measured rather than asserted, stage by stage:

| render stage | reusable for client footage? |
|---|---|
| `clip_media.retrieve` | **no** — takes an Instagram clip id; client footage has none |
| song bed (`song_library`) | **no** — lays a licensed song under a meme; a client's own audio *is* the point |
| caption layer | **no** — burns one fixed string, generates no subtitles |
| **canvas + scale/pad (1080×1920)** | **YES** |
| **ffmpeg trim/encode** | **YES** |
| output location | **no** — a scratch dir named for our run |

**2 of 6.** Anything built for clients on the assumption that the meme pipeline mostly covers it
is building on one third of a pipeline, and the third that generalises is the third that was
never the hard part.
