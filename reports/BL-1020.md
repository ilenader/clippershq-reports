## 1. The ordering, and the invariant that makes it safe

`scratch/bl1020_interleave.py` — round-robin by account, best-ranked first within each
account. **An ordering, never a filter.** That is BL-999's rule, learned when "unwalked
first" risked becoming "never refresh": a caller cannot tell an ordering that quietly drops
rows from a filter, so this one must not.

```
[OK ] SET UNCHANGED on empty / one row / one account / many accounts / skewed 10-1-1 / blank account
[OK ] one clip per account before any account's second: ['b0', 'm1', 's1']
[OK ] the top-ranked clip is still first
[OK ] rank order preserved inside an account
[OK ] a single-account pool comes back as PLAIN RANK ORDER, not starved
[OK ] top-10 accounts: rank order 1 -> interleaved 10
[OK ] SET UNCHANGED with a cap applied     [OK ] input list is not mutated
15 checks, 0 failures
```

**The degenerate case (item 3) needs no branch, and that is the point.** A single-account
pool round-robins into exactly plain rank order — no starvation, no short batch — because the
permutation invariant makes returning fewer *impossible*. A guard that "degrades to rank
order" would be describing what the algorithm already does when there is nothing to
interleave. Asserted directly rather than argued.

The account owning the single best clip still leads the batch: accounts are visited in the
order their **first** clip appeared, not by bucket size. Visiting by size would hand the lead
to the largest account, which is the thing being fixed.

promoted  clips carrying a hand verdict : 0 of 16   ->  none
mean rank score  7.5087 -> 7.3061        (97.3% of the ranker's own signal retained)

**All three displaced clips that anyone has judged were judged "not postable"** — two by
BL-950, one more from `moviezar`, the account losing 8 of the 16 slots. The promoted clips
are unjudged, so this is *not* evidence they are better; it is evidence that what was lost
was not good. Combined with keeping 97.3% of the rank signal, the trade is cheap.

**Honest about the power here:** 3 of 16 is a thin overlap, and the verdict set is
one-sided (50 "no", 8 "post", 3 "fix" across four judges with a moving bar). This supports
*"the displaced clips were not better"* and cannot support *"the promoted clips are better"*.
Nobody has watched the promoted 16.

## 3. Did the gate cause it? **No — the ranker did.** (item 4)

The suspicion was reasonable: a gate that filters by style will concentrate by account as a
side effect. It measures both ways at once.

**The gate does filter by style — but not the accounts that dominate.** Overall removal rate
16.5%:

| account | had | removed | rate | |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| matheuxmendex | 28 | 22 | **78.6%** | hit hard |
| weareassassin | 28 | 14 | **50.0%** | hit hard |
| loste1980 | 117 | 5 | 4.3% | spared |
| dirtyclips67 / culturemeetsonline / dopameme.yt_ | 27/27/24 | 0 | **0.0%** | spared |

The two hit hardest are non-English pages, so **the gate concentrates by language** — real,
worth naming, and a live risk if more language-shaped terms land.

**But it is not what drove 6 → 10**, and the reason is one column:

| account | gate removal | median rank score of survivors |
|---|---:|---:|
| moviezar | 23.1% | **7.586** |
| cawncept | 0.0% | **7.371** |
| songss | 16.7% | **7.299** |
| matheuxmendex | 78.6% | 5.917 |
| weareassassin | 50.0% | 5.854 |
| loste1980 | 4.3% | 5.192 |
| **the pool** | 16.5% | **5.432** |

The three accounts owning the top 30 have median rank scores **~2 points above the pool
median**, and `rank_score` is `log10(play_count)` with no per-account term. The accounts the
gate hit hardest were never contending for the top 30 — their medians sit *below* the pool's.
`loste1980` owns 117 clips, the most of any page, and never appears in the top 30 at all.

**The concentration is the ranker doing exactly what it was asked: order by play count.
Three pages post the highest-play clips, so three pages own the batch.** MEMEBOT-110's terms
moved pool-wide top-2 share only 9.4% → 10.6%.

## 4. Pool-wide, the statistic the walk fixed one stage earlier (item 5)

| | clips | accounts | top-2 share | max per account |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gated pool, before MEMEBOT-110's terms | 2,061 | 201 | **9.4%** | 117 |
| gated pool, after | **1,720** | 179 | **10.6%** | 112 |
| *the clip walk, one stage earlier* | | | *95.5% → 5.0%* | |

**The pool is not concentrated — the top 30 is.** 179 accounts share 1,720 clips and the top
two hold a tenth of them. The walk's fix already worked at supply; what was missing is that
*selection* re-concentrated it, because ranking by a single popularity signal necessarily
picks the accounts that are popular. Interleaving is the same correction applied where the
cut actually happens.

## Limits and what I got wrong

- **NOT LANDED.** The ordering is a patch, not a commit. `scratch/bl1020_rank_interleave.patch`
  carries the function, the exact call site (after the `servable` sort, before the `limit`
  cut, and *why* that order), three tests, and a verification step. **Owner: whoever next
  holds `clippershq/clip_pipeline.py` — currently BL-1013, actively writing it.**
- **I published a wrong number to myself before catching it.** The first run of the harness
  read `cand["score"]` — a key that does not exist on a candidate, which is
  `{clip, clip_id, rank, score_why}` — through `.get(...) or 0.0`, got 0.0 for every clip, and
  reported *"mean rank score 0.0000 → 0.0000"*, i.e. that interleaving is free because the
  ranker is flat. The ranker is not flat: there are **2,718 distinct `rank_score` values**
  across the library. **A zero that comes from asking the wrong question looks exactly like a
  zero that is true**, and the only reason it did not reach this report is that "the ranker
  is flat" was too convenient to accept.
- **The verdict overlap is 3 clips.** Enough to say the displaced were not better; not enough
  to say anything about the promoted, and not enough to detect a subtle effect. The set is
  one-sided and has four judges on a moving bar.
- **`cap` is implemented and unused.** The patch calls `interleave_by_account` without one,
  because plain round-robin already yields max 2 per account in the top 30. If a future pool
  is thinner, `cap` moves the surplus to the tail rather than dropping it — but that path is
  tested only synthetically, not on the real library.
- **No frames were watched.** Whether a batch from 25 pages *renders* better than one from 11
  is a viewing question this round did not touch.
