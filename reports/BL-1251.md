### As a sorter — the different test, and the bar I used

**17 of his 25 WANTs land in the top half: 68.0% [48.4, 82.8].** A coin flip puts 50.0% there.
**The lower bound, 48.4%, does not clear 50.0%.**

I used the coin-flip bar rather than the base-rate bar, and the reason is the brief's own: a
sorter deletes nothing, so it cannot lose him a page the way the code filter does. It does not
have to beat the base rate to be useful — it has to beat *no sorting at all*, which is a random
order, which is a coin flip. It does not, at this n.

**There is real signal, though — the quartiles are monotone**: **9/12 | 8/13 | 5/12 | 3/13**
best-first. That is not noise-shaped. At the observed 68%, the lower bound clears 50% at
**n = 50 WANTs** (~100 pages). So this is a *"not proven at n=50"*, not a *"measured to be
worthless"* — and it would cost about two more hours of local time to settle.

**One caveat on its own limits:** it used only 6 distinct scores of 11 (0, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10) and put
46 of 50 pages at 5 or above. It is reluctant to say "no", which is exactly why the classifier
thresholds below 6 reject almost nothing.

**p50 7.6 s · p90 8.9 s · p99 20.0 s · min 4.0 · max 20.0** — p99 is the first call, before the
model warms.

**473 pages/hour at p50; 180 at p99.** That is **5.3× a single browser judge** (90 pages/hour),

## 7. Verdict — REFUSED as a pre-sorter, with the arithmetic

Using **this round's measured 7.6 s**, not the 31.9 s figure:

| | hours for 874 pages |
|---|---|
| Qwen sort of the whole queue | **1.85 h** |
| Browser judge, one judge | **9.71 h** |
| **sort, then judge** | **11.56 h** — 1.19× *slower* than judging alone |
| two browser judges in parallel, no sorter | **4.86 h** |
| three browser judges in parallel | **3.24 h** |

**A sorter deletes nothing, so the judging work is unchanged and the sort is pure addition.** It
pays only if he stops early: break-even is judging **less than 81%** of the queue and binning the
rest. But the queue exists *because* the code filter already bins too much — 17.4% [10.2, 28.0]
of the pages the browser wants. Stopping early is the same failure with a nicer name.

**The saving quantified: there isn't one.** Adopting it costs **+1.85 h** and buys an ordering
whose advantage over random is not statistically established. Adding one more browser judge costs
**nothing but a second Chrome profile** and saves **4.85 h** — and BL-1248 is measuring exactly
that right now.

**What I would do instead, and it is free:** the strongest number here is that the *cheap first
pass* is the problem, not the judge. The code filter deletes; a sorter does not. If a pre-sort is
wanted, the cheapest honest next step is to finish this measurement at n≈100 pages (~2 h local,
$0.00) and adopt only if the top-half lower bound clears 50%. **Do not put it in front of the
judge on today's evidence.**

**Not re-proposed** (all previously closed, none re-tested here): local VLMs at 20.3 h/sweep and
0.158 precision; language-model audio classification at 40% reproducibility; the 0.197 temporal
persistence ceiling; OCR resolution 736→1440 making accuracy *worse* (58.3%→51.4%); and the
cheaper paid model at 70.0% vs 74.0% with no significant gap. `claude-video` was **not tested** —
rejected on cost and fit as instructed.
