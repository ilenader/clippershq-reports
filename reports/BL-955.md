## 2. I wrote the ceiling test wrong, and that is the finding

My first version asserted the **thread count** stays bounded:

```
  AssertionError: 8 not less than or equal to 4 :
  8 abandonments under a ceiling of 2 grew the thread count by 8 -- the ceiling is not bounding
```

**The test was wrong, not the code.** Reading the implementation: past the ceiling the caller waits *one more deadline* and then returns anyway, leaving that worker alive. It never claimed to cap the absolute count.

**Asserting a stronger safety property than a mechanism provides is worse than asserting nothing, because it reads as a guarantee.** A green suite would then have been evidence for something untrue about a live funnel.

What the ceiling actually bounds is the **rate**: abandonment cannot proceed at full speed indefinitely. The absolute count is held down by the underlying calls' own deadlines — *"an abandoned worker dies in seconds, not minutes"* — and the semaphore is the backstop stopping them arriving faster than they retire. The test now says that, and proves it by **timing**: six abandonments under a ceiling of two cannot complete in less than six deadlines, because four of them had to wait.

Nine tests in total — all four sharing one implementation; the ceiling consuming **and releasing** its slots (a ceiling that never releases is a deadlock, not a bound); the caller demonstrably waiting past the ceiling; and normal behaviour unchanged: fast call, no deadline, exception propagation, timeout reporting.

## Honest limits

- **The ceiling bounds the rate, not the thread count.** Under a pathological hang, threads still accumulate until their own network deadlines fire. The semaphore stops them arriving faster than they retire; it does not cap how many exist. My first test claimed otherwise and was wrong.
- **The ceiling of 24 is inherited, not derived.** twitch chose it against an incident with ~61 piled-up threads; nothing measures whether 24 is right for spotify, youtube or google_play, whose call profiles differ. It is a bound where there was none, which is the improvement — not a tuned one.
- **No live funnel run.** The change is proven by unit tests and a full suite, not by a real paid walk. The behaviour it alters only manifests under repeated timeouts.
