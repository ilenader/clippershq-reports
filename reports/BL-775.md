## 1. The four contradictions, resolved

### 1a. Ready-to-send — reproduced the whole ladder today

Every figure is a different filter depth. I recomputed all of them against today's master:

| definition | reported | **today** | by |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| every unique valid address in master | 4,217 | **4,186** | BL-757 |
| …minus role inboxes + agency addresses | 3,659 | **3,628** | BL-743 |
| …what `bot_ready_rows()` actually emits | 1,576 | **1,620** | BL-751 |
| …minus domains with no mail route | **1,600** | **1,600** | BL-764 |
| union of both export files | 1,623 | **1,623** | BL-765 / BL-771 |

Every step reproduces. The small deltas are master growth (57,386 rows today) — BL-764 already attributed BL-751's 1,576 → 1,620 to rows appended by BL-760/761, and that holds.

**Verdict: no report was wrong. Every report used a different definition and called it the same thing.** The defect is the *label*, not the arithmetic: "ready to send" was applied to the raw address universe, to a quality-filtered view, to code output, and to a deliverability-filtered view.

**BL-764 already fixed this** and named the number to retire ("BL-757's 4,217 is the number to stop quoting"). That reconciliation is the single most valuable document in the corpus and it is one report among 258 — nothing links the four superseded reports to it. A reader who finds BL-757 first has no way to learn it was superseded.

### 1b. Spotify seed pool — a real error, correctly caught

BL-766 measured ~452 in-band seed artists and concluded a 1,000-lead target was 2.2× the pool. BL-769 re-measured at the correct depth and got **~777** (95% CI 659–857), reducing the ratio to 1.29×.

**Verdict: 777 supersedes 452.** The correction is in BL-769's own title — *"I measured the pool at the wrong depth in BL-766"* — and it states plainly which conclusion survives and which does not: *"The direction survives; the magnitude does not."*

It also refuses to close a gap it cannot close:

> *"BL-766's mixing model implied only ~267 of 1,000 leads came from seeds, yet ~777 in-band seed artists were available. Both cannot be right… the honest position is that the two estimates disagree and the per-lead `source` field BL-761 added is what settles it on the next run."*

**This is the behaviour that should be the norm.** The number is corrected, the surviving conclusion is separated from the dead one, and the open tension is named with the mechanism that will settle it. The 17-point expansion penalty was measured directly and is unaffected either way.

### 1c. `save_count` 66.7% → 0%

**Verdict: both correct; the field is account-specific.** 66.7% on the first three accounts, **0.0% on all 48 clips across four new accounts**, pooled 28.6%. The second round was run specifically to test whether the first generalised. It does not.

**Neither number should be quoted as "the fill rate"** — the honest statement is that some accounts report the field and others never do, so `saves_per_view` cannot be ranked on. The reachable ratio is `engagement_per_follower`, whose inputs are 100% across all seven accounts.

### 1d. Description-email rate — four denominators, one measurement

| figure | denominator | n | source |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 10.0% | all channels, pre-gate, one niche | 50 | BL-748 |
| 8% | brief's derivation from the n=50 sample | 50 | — |
| 12.5% | all channels, pre-gate, four niches | 200 | BL-755 / BL-762 |
| 8.1% | raw regex hits after the country gate | 99 | BL-755 |
| **7.1%** | **usable addresses after the country gate** | **99** | BL-755 |
| **13.3%** | **usable addresses after size + country gates** | **45** | BL-762 |

**Verdict: not a contradiction. The same 200 channels, counted at four positions.** 12.5% and 7.1% are the *same measurement* before and after gating — BL-755 says so in its title. BL-762's 13.3% is that same set of survivors against a tighter denominator (size + country, not country alone): **7 usable of 99, then 6 usable of 45.** Six addresses, two denominators.

The operationally honest figure is **6 usable per 200 channels sampled = 3.0%**, or 13.3% of channels that actually ship. BL-762 makes the point explicitly: *"Parsing before the gate would have reported a 12.5% fill rate against a population that never ships."*

**The lesson is the same as 1a: the rate is meaningless without its denominator, and the denominator was in the prose, not in the number.**

**Set on all four campaigns. Read by nothing.** The only occurrence of the string anywhere in the repo:

## 5. What a new reader gets wrong — the real cost

### The big one: the reports repo is two projects under one numbering sequence

Classifying all 258 reports by which system they describe:

| | reports |
|---|---:|
| **the payments / clipper-earnings system** | **165** |
| **this lead-generation repo** | **70** |
| ambiguous | 23 |

**A new reader opening `clippershq-reports` finds that 2 out of every 3 reports describe a codebase that is not this one** — payouts, CPM, withdrawals, Supabase, the earnings dashboard — interleaved in the same `BL-NNN` sequence with no marker distinguishing them. BL-661 (clipper payouts) sits between BL-660 and BL-662; BL-770 (junk sweep) and BL-771 (dedup) are this repo. Nothing in the filename, the header or the directory says which.

This is also why the numbering collides: BL-769 was taken by another agent mid-write during BL-771, because both projects draw from the same counter.

**Consequences a reader inherits:**
- 19 of the "missing evidence" files aren't missing — they live in the other repo. A reader concludes this project loses its data. It does not.
- Cost figures, funnel names and module names from the payments system read as if they describe this one.
- The single most useful document here — BL-764's reconciliation — is 1 report in 258 with nothing pointing to it.

### Four more things a new reader would believe that are false

1. **"`LATEST_BOT_READY.csv` is the live send list."** `STATE.md:129` says n8n emails it. It holds 2 rows and is 13 days stale. A reader pointing automation at it sends to two people.
2. **"Ready to send is one number."** Depending on which report they open first: 4,217, 3,659, 1,576, 1,623 or 1,600 — all correct, all differently defined, only one reconciled.
3. **"`ig_niche_check` is a working per-campaign toggle, and it's tested."** It is set on four campaigns, read by nothing, and its "test" tests something else.
4. **"The Spotify seed pool is ~452."** Superseded by ~777 in BL-769 — but a reader who finds BL-766 first sees a confident measurement with no forward pointer.

### The pattern behind all of it

Every failure in this audit is a **linking** failure, not a measurement failure. The measurements are sound (item 3: 100% reproducible), the corrections happen (items 1b, 2), and the arithmetic reconciles (item 1a). What is missing is any mechanism by which a superseded number knows it has been superseded — no forward pointers, no project tag, no `SUPERSEDED BY` header, no index.

**The corpus is accurate and unnavigable.** That is a cheaper problem to fix than an inaccurate one, and it is the one actually costing you.

Not implemented — audit only, as instructed.

## Limits

- **The 165/70/23 project split is a keyword heuristic** over report text, not a hand-classification. Spot-checked at the boundaries (BL-661 payments, BL-743/748/755/762/764/765/771 lead-gen) and it holds, but individual assignments will be wrong.
- **Item 2's sweep only catches comments that state a number matching a config key by name.** A prose claim ("caps a page at ~13s") has no key to compare against and is invisible to it — those were found by reading, and I read a fraction of 187 `MEASURED` comments.
- **"Dead" means no static reference.** A key read via a computed name or an external consumer would be missed. I checked `meme_*` and `ig_niche_check` by hand; the other 11 rest on the sweep.
- **The 89 silent defaults are not defects** — they are the population defects come from. No claim is made that any specific one is broken.
- **Reports were read selectively.** I read the reports behind the four named contradictions in full and sampled others; 258 reports were searched, not read.
- **Snapshot.** Master and reports are both being written by other agents during this audit.

## Method

Cloned `clippershq-reports` (271 files, 258 `BL-*.md`) read-only into a scratch directory. The four contradictions were resolved by reading the reports in full and **recomputing every figure against today's master** using the shipping code (`writer._first_email`, `role_policy.kind/looks_agency`, `bio_parser.valid_email`, `all_bot_ready.build`).

Item 2 compared every numeric-bearing code comment against live `config.json` by key name. Item 3 extracted all repo-relative artifact paths cited by any report via regex and tested each for existence, then ran `tests/test_repost_captions.py` against the real fixture. Item 4 ran the repo's own `scratch/scan_dead_knobs.py` plus a config-side sweep of all 638 keys against the concatenated source and test trees. Item 5's split classified report text by project-specific vocabulary.

Zero API calls, $0 spent, no repo writes, no credential read or printed.
