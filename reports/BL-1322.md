## 1. What I actually tested, and why it is not what previous rounds tested

Every prior cheap-model test asked it to replicate the full judge — *"is this a page he wants"* —
and every one failed. This round asked six one-glance questions instead, each scored
**separately**, because a model may be excellent at one and useless at the rest and that alone
would carry the value.

Three ingredients, all measured:

- **Reference labels** — the premium free judge answering the same six questions on the same
  grids. 120 images, 40 per set, deterministic sampling. **$0.00**, 8,326 subscription tokens
  per image (7.8× cheaper than a judged page — one sub-agent labels *eight* images for about
  what one judged page costs).
- **Grids already on disk** — `output\bl1296_fresh100\shots`, `output\bl1298_mark_ig\grids`,
  `output\bl1298_mark_tt\grids`. **Nothing was fetched.**
- **His own marks**, and only his: `mark_kind: OPERATOR_OWN_VERDICT` on the two BL-1298 sets,
  and his direct TSV on BL-1296. Want rates **9.0% / 28.0% / 37.0%**, stated on every table.

## 2. Three of the six questions were badly posed, and one polarity was backwards

This came out of the reference labelling and it changes what can be claimed:

| task | what is wrong with it |
|---|---|
| **`person_on_camera`** | **A spec defect, not a vision problem** — flagged by 11 of 15 labellers. A fan/repost/update page satisfies *"the same individual recurs across tiles"* perfectly while being the **opposite** of a creator: one page reposts a single clip nine times with different captions; another shows the same Brad Pitt scene five times. The wording must exclude reposted third-party footage before these labels are creator-class truth. |
| **`any_drawn_text`** | **Near-degenerate** — 107 of 120 true, 39 of 40 on TikTok. Answering "true" every time scores **89.2%**. |
| **`stills_not_video`** | **Measured off platform furniture, not content.** Labellers were reading play icons. If the renderer ever drops the icon layer, every answer flips. Mixed grids (7 stills + 2 reels) have no correct binary answer. |
| `business_page` / `news_page` | 5 true each in 120 — too imbalanced to earn a usable recall estimate. |
| `animals_present` | An unstated cartoon/depicted boundary — SpongeBob, a wolf *printed on a shirt*, a husky mask. Labellers were openly inconsistent. |

**And I had one polarity backwards.** I listed `any_drawn_text` as a drop signal. Used that way
it kills **100% of his wanted pages** — obviously, in hindsight: drawn text is the thing he
*wants*. It is a **keep** signal. Every task below is therefore scored **both ways**, because
scoring only the polarity the author had in mind is exactly how that error survives.

cheap model beats a perfect one, so a task that fails here is dead regardless of accuracy.

any tile".

**The union of every zero-kill polarity** — `business_page`=true, `news_page`=true,

> **0% JSON at 3.5× the price**.
> **The fence.** Only 2 of 6 honour "no markdown fence". A parser trusting strict JSON would
> score four models at 0%.

## 5. The shipped model, scored — read every figure against its constant baseline

**Four of six tasks are won outright by answering the same thing every time.** Any accuracy not
compared to its own constant is meaningless.

| task | constant baseline | model accuracy | precision (true) | recall (true) | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `animals_present` | 74.2% | **98.2%** [93.6, 99.5] | **100%** [83.9, 100] | 90.9% | **beats it clearly** |
| `any_drawn_text` | 89.2% | **95.7%** [90.4, 98.2] | 97.2% | 98.1% | **beats it** |
| `news_page` | 88.3% | 95.5% | n/a | **0.0%** | beats it **by never saying true** |
| `business_page` | 85.8% | 93.5% | 40.0% | 80.0% | marginal |
| **`person_on_camera`** | **71.7%** | **40.0%** [31.1, 49.6] | 34.4% | 100% | **far WORSE than a constant** |
| `stills_not_video` | 80.8% | 58.5% | 18.5% | 100% | **worse than a constant** |

`person_on_camera` fails in a specific way: it answers **true on 96 of 105** pages. It catches
every real one (recall 100%) by saying yes to almost everything — 63 false positives. As a
gate that is not a filter, it is a shredder.

### Is the confidence informative? No — and that alone settles it

| model | scored answers | acc @ `high` | acc @ `low` | high − low | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite` | 655 | 80.2% (n=617) | 83.3% (n=12) | **−3.1 pp** | **DECORATIVE** |

Across **708 answers: zero `unsure`**, 94.2% `high`, and the `low` band holds 12. There is
nowhere to route the hard cases, and high-confidence answers are *slightly less* accurate than
low-confidence ones. **A confidence that is never spent cannot carry information** — this is the
prior failure ("high confidence on 50 of 50 while wrong on 15") reproduced on the model that is
already in production.

**The visible consequence: the confidence threshold is a no-op knob.** In every table below,
`any`, `medium+` and `high-only` give **identical** drop and kill counts on almost every rule —
because there is nothing below the threshold to exclude. Agent B saw this at n=48; it holds at
n=708.

⚠️ **The other four models' verdicts are unknown, and that gap matters most for the one ranked
first.** `nova-lite-v1` was the top pick precisely because its confidence is genuinely spread
(50/38/13) — the one property that cannot be inferred from another model's behaviour.

   models. `nova-lite-v1` is the one to watch: it is the only candidate whose confidence is

5. **Consider replacing the shipped model on cost alone** — it is 4.6× `gemma-3-12b-it` at no
