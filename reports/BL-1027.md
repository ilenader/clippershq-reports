## 1. `ocr_gate_text` — declared, filled, readable

Declared in `CLIP_FIELDS` at the **MEASURED** tier, additive to `vision_on_screen_text` and
never a replacement. `clippershq/clip_ocr.py` carries BL-1002b's one-frame method unchanged,
so the stored values carry the precision and recall it measured — and those figures live in
the module beside the code, because a field is only safe to read if what it is worth travels
with it:

```
burned-in @handle card    P = 80.0 %   R = 100.0 %    <- CLEARS, this is what is claimed
third-party watermark     P = 50.0 %   R =  14.3 %    <- DOES NOT. Not a watermark oracle.
```

**64 clips readable through `field()`** — 60 of them BL-1002b had already measured and never
persisted, which cost nothing to store now.

### The write held

```
clips whose account was blanked          : 0   (merge_append held)
clips whose vision_on_screen_text changed: 0
```

Every write went through `merge_append`, one clip at a time — not batched, because a killed
sweep once lost everything it held in memory; and not `build_record + append`, because a
partial record carries no `posted_at`, lands in `clips-unknown.jsonl`, and at equal rev the
near-empty row wins and blanks caption, account and every vision field.

**`None` and `""` are kept distinct.** `None` means *the frame could not be read*; `""` means
*it was read and carries no text*. Collapsing them would recreate the exact bug this field
exists to fix — a blank that means two different things.

## 2. The watermark term at the union — and what it still cannot see

Re-implementation verified against the shipped term first: **0 disagreements** on the same
field, so the delta is comparable.

| | clips |
|---|---:|
| excluded by the vision field alone | 1 |
| excluded by the **union** | 3 |
| **newly excluded** | **2** |
| unchanged | 62 |

Both newly-excluded clips were hand-checked by extracting the frame and reading it:

| clip | OCR handle | frame says | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `moviezar` | `@maxpryme1991` | a **tweet card**: `maximuspryme1991 / @maxpryme1991`, on a `moviezar` account | **correctly excluded** |
| `matheuxmendex` | `@saprutd` | Portuguese caption + burned-in subtitle *"esses 4 dragões"* — **no @handle anywhere** | **WRONGLY excluded** |

**1 of 2 correct.** The phantom is the one BL-1002b named: OCR mangled Portuguese into
`@saprutd`, a handle that does not exist. One-frame OCR reads **subtitles as readily as
watermarks and cannot tell them apart** — that is the 20% in P=80.

### The finding underneath the recall number

The union surfaces **`PRIMECUTTV` on three clips the vision field called empty** — a
third-party watermark, named as such by MEMEBOT-098 — and **BL-988's term cannot act on any
of them.** The term matches `@handle` and `domain.tld` only, so a bare watermark *word* is
invisible to it no matter how clearly OCR reads it.

That is `R = 14.3%` explained mechanically rather than statistically: the recall ceiling is
not OCR's eyesight, it is the term's pattern. **Widening the pattern is a separate decision
with its own false-positive cost**, and this round does not take it.

The re-point is delivered as `scratch/bl1027_postable_patch.diff` — `git apply --check` clean,
patched copy compiles — because `clip_postable.py` is held by BL-1015.

## 3. What is now independent

BL-988's own drift warning: *every landed term reads the fields it was validated on, so the
gate drifts silently when the vision model changes.*

**After this round the watermark term has two inputs that fail differently.**
`vision_on_screen_text` comes from a vision model reading the whole clip; `ocr_gate_text`
comes from an OCR engine reading one frame's pixels. A vision-model change moves the first
and **not** the second. A gate reading the union still sees the handle card when the model
regresses — which is precisely the case that started this: the **#8-ranked** clip carrying a
burned-in handle card the field recorded as empty.

**The honest limit:** independence is only as good as coverage, and coverage is **64 of ~2,728
clips**. On the rest the union *is* the vision field, and the drift exposure is unchanged.

## 4. Time box, metered — and the cost figure does not reproduce

| | |
|---|---|
| wall clock | **804.3 s**, stopped at the box rather than running over |
| stored | 67 (60 free + 7 OCR'd); 64 readable through `field()` |
| BL-1002b's figure | **7.53 s/clip** |
| **measured here** | **≈79 s/clip** in the sweep |

Attributed directly, so the discrepancy is not left as a mystery:

```
easyocr model load   37.1 s   (one-time)
frame extraction      2.4 s
OCR of one frame    120.8 s and 136.1 s      <- the cost
read_all per write    2.5-3.2 s              (merge_append re-reads the library)
```

**OCR of a single frame measured 120–136 s, not ~4 s.** Two candidate causes, and I am not
claiming which dominates: the frames are **full-resolution 0.7 MB PNGs** — BL-823 measured
easyocr as resolution-dominated, 1.397 s at 360×640 against 3.808 s at 720×1280 — and this
machine is running **eleven other rounds**, several of them suites and renders.

**At the rate measured here the library sweep is not 5.6 CPU-hours. It is closer to 60.**
That materially changes whether a full pass is worth running, so it is reported rather than
buried.

**The obvious lead, untested:** scale the frame before OCR. I tried to measure it at 720 px
and 480 px and the frame extraction failed on the two clips I sampled; the time box expired
before I could retry on known-good sources. **Stated as an untested lead, not a result** —
if BL-823's resolution scaling holds, it is a 30–90× reduction and the whole cost question
reopens.

## What I got wrong

**I lost fourteen minutes of local CPU to my own sequencing.** The `ocr_gate_text` declaration
sat **uncommitted** while the first sweep ran against it. Another round's whole-tree git
operation reverted `clippershq/clip_library.py` to HEAD, `build_record` no longer accepted the
keyword, and every `merge_append` failed — **silently**, because I had redirected the sweep's
stdout to a log I was not yet reading, and the only visible output was torch warnings on
stderr. The sweep looked alive and was producing nothing.

Two corrections, both applied: the declaration went into **its own commit before the sweep
restarted** (`f97239e`), and the re-run used `python -u` so a failure would be visible while
it happened. A field declaration is the *precondition* for the sweep that fills it, so leaving
it uncommitted is not a small risk in a tree where twelve rounds are committing.
