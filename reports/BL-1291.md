## 2. The finding that overturns the round's premise

BL-1274's win is credited to the free `create_time` gate: **159 discovered → 91 fresh → 45
wanted**. The 45 were counted *after* the gate, so **the gate's wanted-page cost was never in
that arithmetic.** Measured here on 244 dated, labelled pages:

| cut | dropped | rejects | **WANTED KILLED** | % of pool |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 30 d | 112 | 72 | **40** | 45.9% |
| 45 d | 93 | 57 | **36** | 38.1% |
| **62 d — the shipped cut** | **84** | **50** | **34** | **34.4%** |
| 90 d | 72 | 42 | **30** | 29.5% |
| 365 d | 38 | 24 | **14** | 15.6% |

> ### At the shipped cut it kills **34 of 84 wanted pages — 40.5% [30.6, 51.2]**
> ### and the want rate **falls**, 0.344 → 0.312

**It is not a stratum artefact.** Inside the stratum judged *without* the gate it kills 4 of
12 wanted; inside the other, 30 of 70. And the medians say why — days since newest post is
**10.5 (WANT) against 10.4 (REJECT)** in one stratum and 33.3 against 39.7 in the other.
**Staleness barely distinguishes the two populations.**

**Priced per wanted page it buys nothing:**

| | calls/wanted | $/1,000 | judged/wanted | M tokens |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| with the 62-day gate | 3.23 | **$1.94** | 2.02 | 17.29 |
| without it | 3.23 | **$1.94** | 2.10 | 17.97 |

**Identical dollars. It buys 3.9% of tokens for 40.5% of his wanted pages.** It reduces
absolute throughput, not waste. **TikTok is cheap because its want rate is high and its call
count is low — not because of this gate.**

## 3. The JUNK class he named — measured, and it is the smallest one

Coding all 87 written judge rejects into **his own five categories**:

| his category | n | share | Wilson 95% |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **creators** | **79** | **90.8%** | [82.9, 95.3] |
| **JUNK** | **3** | **3.4%** | [1.2, 9.7] |
| wrong language | 2 | 2.3% | [0.6, 8.0] |
| still pictures | 2 | 2.3% | [0.6, 8.0] |
| **businesses** | **0** | **0.0%** | [0.0, 4.2] |
| other | 1 | 1.1% | — |

**Businesses are an Instagram phenomenon** — BL-1280's IG cut has 95 business + 53 news
rejects; the TikTok cut has **zero of either**. A loose reading of junk reaches 11 of 87
(12.6%), but 8 of those 11 are already dead on the creator rule. Junk had been absorbed into
`topical_aggregator` (n=3, 100% junk), **not** into `creator` — so the 73.6%-creator figure
was not hiding it.

**Fifteen free signals were scored against junk. Zero separated.** And three of the most
intuitive **run backwards**, which is the real finding:

| "obvious" junk marker | fires on WANTED | fires on junk | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| ≥50% of tags are #fyp-class | **43%** [21, 67] | 18% [5, 48] | **backwards** |
| description is only hashtags | **36%** [16, 61] | 9% | **backwards** |
| no bio link | **64%** [39, 84] | 54% | **backwards** |

**Repost pages chase reach harder than junk pages do.** `≥50% #fyp` drops 32 pages and kills
**6 of 14 wanted**; `no bio link` drops 44 and kills **9 of 14**.

> **The sample forbade the answer before any data was read.** Three junk pages firing on a
> *perfect* signal still gives a Wilson lower bound of 43.9%; fourteen wanted pages firing on
> *nothing* still gives an upper bound of 21.5%. Settling junk needs ~30 junk and ~60 wanted
> pages — at the measured 3.4% base rate, **~880 judged pages, eight times the corpus that
> exists.** **Junk is not the waste, and a perfect junk filter would save 2.9% of judging.**

## 4. "A song over it" — his own product description, refuted structurally

The music object was the round's best hypothesis and it had **0 stored rows for all 105
judged pages** — BL-1259 stored the capture leg *post-extraction*. **Re-capturing cost 105
calls = $0.063** and closed that gap: `is_original_sound`, `has_human_voice`,
`matched_song.title`, `video.duration` and all four `statistics.*` came back at **100.0%
fill** across 1,184 videos.

**Not one music signal clears.** AUC (0.50 = coin flip): `share_original_sound` 0.555,
`share_matched_song` 0.495, `has_human_voice` 0.596, `commerce_music` 0.610,
`music_owned_by_page` **0.419**, duration 0.602. The briefed rule "≥50% original sound"
**kills 11 of 14 WANTs**. Two forms run backwards.

> **The mechanism, which is why this is a refusal and not an underpowered result.**
> `music.title` is literally *"original sound…"* on **69.9% of WANT videos and 73.1% of
> REJECT videos — 3.2 points apart.** TikTok stamps that on any audio that did not come from
> its in-app library, **including a reposter uploading borrowed footage with the song already
> baked in.** The field records *how the audio entered the app*, not *who shot the picture*.
> And the reject class's matched songs are Carol Of The Bells, Savage, My Way — **creators
> put commercial songs over their own footage too.** "A song over it" is true of both
> populations, so it discriminates neither. AUC 0.495–0.610 on 99 pages is flat, not thin.

## 5. The one rule that survived — tuned, then held out

The same $0.063 unblocked `statistics.share_count / play_count`, equally 0-row before this
round. **A repost page exists to be passed on; a creator's own clip is watched, not
forwarded.**

**Mean share-per-play: WANTED 0.0256 vs REJECT 0.0117 — 2.2×, AUC 0.818.**

It was **not** the briefed hypothesis — it fell out of the re-capture — so it was chosen on
the data it was measured on. Handles sorted case-insensitively and alternated; the threshold
chosen on the tune half by a rule fixed in advance (*largest zero-loss cut, then one step
back*), then the other half scored **once**:

| | n | wanted | dropped | rejects | **WANTED KILLED** | precision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| tune half | 50 | 6 | 16 (32.0%) | 16 | **0** | 1.000 [0.806, 1.0] |
| **HELD OUT** | 49 | 8 | **18 (36.7%)** | 18 | **0** | **1.000 [0.824, 1.0]** |
| combined | 99 | 14 | 34 (34.3%) | 34 | **0** | 1.000 [0.899, 1.0] |

The zero-loss frontier on the tune half was 0.010; **0.008 ships**, one step back, because
tuning to the edge of a zero-loss band is how "loses nothing" starts losing things.

> **What it saves, precisely.** `share_per_play` rides the capture payload, which is a
> **billed** call on TikTok. So it avoids no call — **it saves JUDGING, which is the scarce
> resource.** Anyone selling this as a dollar saving has misread it.
>
> **And what it does not prove.** Labels are a judge proxy. n=99 carries 14 WANT, so "zero
> wanted killed" honestly reads as **0 to 21.5%**. This can be refuted by a larger corpus; it
> cannot yet be confirmed by this one.

### A standing lesson in this repo is wrong

The note that `following/by/username` 403s while `by/secUid` 200s is **not about the key**.
The 403 body is `{"detail":"This Account is Private"}`, and **both keys returned 200 on a
public account and both 403'd on a private one.** It was always the account. Also: `count > 30`
**500s on every user path**, and the OpenAPI schema declares no maximum — the spec invites the
failure.

### What was measured: `suggested` loses, `following` wins

The brief ranked `/v1/user/suggested/*` first — 81.9% free bios and nothing calls it. **200
calls says it is the wrong surface.** Free bios came back even better (90.3%) and free
email-in-bio at **20.1%**, nearly double the *paid* profile call's 11.1%. And it is still
useless:

> **Depth 1 was 42 of 42 VERIFIED — 100%, Wilson [0.916, 1.000].** Fourteen small hand-judged
> clipper pages were seeded and the first hop returned nothing but verified celebrities.
> Median account: **1.4M followers**. Clipper token **2.9%**, and **0 of 281 among the
> verified**. The email rate is a celebrity-management-address artefact. It also has no
> depth — `hasMore:false, total:30` on all 197 successful calls, and 2.63 *new* accounts per
> call from 29.72 returned.

Head-to-head on the **same 14 seeds**:

| | suggested | **following** |
|---|---|---|
| new accounts per productive call | 2.63 | **27.9** |
| verified | 54.2% [0.499, 0.585] | **3.1% [0.014, 0.066]** |
| **clipper token** | 2.9% [0.018, 0.047] | **10.8% [0.072, 0.159]** |
| $ per free email | $0.0012 | **$0.0002** |

**The two clipper-token intervals do not overlap — that separation is the finding.** Following
returned `thedailyclip16`, `dailylol37`, `chaina.memes`; suggested returned 79.8M-follower
celebrities. And the snowball sustains: 35 survivors re-seeded gave **18/35 = 51.4%** exposing
a usable following list, at 25.5 accounts and 21.7 free bios per productive call. **So 21.4%
seed coverage is a seeding problem, not a wall** — seed on known-public accounts.

### One free recovery, verified independently

`aweme_id >> 32` **recovers a post's timestamp from its id alone.** I re-verified on a larger
denominator than the agent used: **91 (id, create_time) pairs from 5 stored payloads — median
absolute error 10.0 s, 89 of 91 within five minutes, 90 of 91 within a day**, one 29-day
outlier. It recovers dates for $0.00 from any corpus that kept ids but not dates. *Framed
precisely: `create_time` is already free on the live path, so this is a recovery tool and a
redundant path, not a new saving.*

## 8. What I built

**`clippershq/tiktok_triage.py`** — a three-way splitter. Pure functions, no I/O. It ships
**one DROP rule** (`share_per_play <= 0.008`), **no KEEP bucket**, and returns `JUDGE` for
anything unknown, so a store that stops populating a field degrades to judging rather than to
silently dropping his pages.

**`tests/test_tiktok_triage.py` — 24 tests, all green.** `tests/*.py` was free this round, so
unlike the last one this ships tested. The tests deliberately guard the failure *direction*:
most assert that missing or malformed input falls through to `JUDGE`. Two run against the real
corpus — one reproduces the 34-of-99 split with **zero wanted killed**, and one **records why
the recency gate does not ship**, so that number lives in the suite rather than only in a
report nobody re-reads. A third fails if a KEEP bucket ever appears without the measurement to
justify it.

⚠️ **The module is NOT WIRED — it has no caller.** Wiring `tiktok_finder` is a future round's
work, and on a judge-proxy label set that is the right order.
