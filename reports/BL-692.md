# BL-692 — How common is caption templating? 37.5% of confirmed repost pages, ~14% of the raw discovery pool — and a parser built for the BL-682 shape catches 34% of what is there

**Date:** 2026-07-29 · **Type:** Measurement, read-only · **Spend:** $0.00 (no network, no paid API calls, no code changed)

Follow-up to [BL-682](BL-682.md), which found structured caption templates on 1 of 3 accounts
(94% of that account's posts, 0% on the other two) and correctly flagged n=3 as too small.

Evidence tiers: **VERIFIED** (measured here) · **REFUTED** (checked, premise is false) ·
**GAP** (not establishable from the data held).

---

## The prevalence number, first

**VERIFIED. Among accounts the repost finder has already qualified as repost/meme pages,
37.5% use a caption template.** Not 33%, not 5%.

| population | templated | rate | 95% CI (Wilson) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **accounts with ≥3 saved posts (n=24)** | **9** | **37.5%** | **21.2 – 57.3%** |
| — FULL template (≥4 recurring fields) | 7 | 29.2% | 14.9 – 49.2% |
| — LIGHT template (2–3 recurring fields) | 2 | 8.3% | 2.3 – 25.8% |
| — PARTIAL (exactly 1 recurring field) | 1 | 4.2% | 0.7 – 20.2% |
| — untemplated | 14 | 58.3% | 38.8 – 75.5% |
| all saved posts | 186 / 493 | 37.7% | 33.6 – 42.1% |
| posts from per-account crawls | 169 / 368 | 45.9% | 40.9 – 51.0% |
| posts from raw hashtag / audio-track feeds | 17 / 125 | 13.6% | 8.7 – 20.7% |
| accounts in the raw hashtag / track pool (n=108) | 15 | 13.9% | 8.6 – 21.7% |

**The two frames are not in conflict — they are different populations, and the gap is the
finding.** The 24-account figure is measured on accounts that already passed the repost
finder and sit in `master_leads.csv` with `verdict=MEME_PAGE`. The 13.9% figure is measured
on the un-vetted discovery pool: whoever happens to appear in a hashtag or audio-track feed,
repost page or not.

So the honest answer depends on where the parser runs:

- **On qualified leads** (parsing a page you already decided to keep): **~37.5%**, CI 21–57%.
- **At discovery** (deciding whether a stranger is worth keeping): **~14%**, and
  sensitivity-corrected below (see next section) **~11%**.

### Corpus

493 unique captioned Instagram posts across 161 accounts, extracted from 66 saved payload
files in `_probe_samples/` and `_probe_samples/niche_delta/`. Deduplicated by `(account, pk)`
— the `_clips.json` and `_medias.json` payloads overlap heavily and would otherwise
double-count. 74 TikTok posts across 41 accounts were extracted separately and are **not**
pooled into any figure above; they are editor/VFX accounts, a different population.

All 20 of the deeply-crawled accounts are the repost finder's own output
(`output/repost_20260728_*.xlsx`) and all 20 are present in `master_leads.csv` as `MEME_PAGE`.

### `master_leads.csv` cannot answer this question — REFUTED as a data source

The brief asked for the measurement across master as well as the payloads. It is not
measurable there:

- **No caption text column exists** in the 60-column schema.
- `median_caption_len` — the one caption-derived column — is populated on **0 of 56,173 rows.**
- `post_hashtags` is empty on all 20 matched accounts.
- Only **20 of the 161** caption-corpus accounts appear in master at all.

Master confirms *which* accounts are meme pages. It holds nothing about their captions.

### It is all-or-nothing per account — confirmed at 10× BL-682's n

BL-682 observed this on one account. It holds across all nine:

| | posts carrying ≥2 fields |
|---|---|
| the 9 templated accounts | **149 / 156 = 95.5%** |
| the 15 untemplated accounts | **7 / 177 = 4.0%** |

Per account: 100% on six of them, 97.2% (decibeldrops), 94.4% (clipcapture.tv), 66.7%
(katicsedits — the one genuinely mixed page).

**Operational consequence: a single post is a ~95% sensitive, ~96% specific test for
templating.** You never need to fetch a back catalogue to find out. Applying that correction
to the raw discovery pool — observed 13.9%, sensitivity 0.955, specificity 0.960 — gives a
true rate of **≈10.8%** there.

## The variants — and how badly one-shape parsing does

**VERIFIED. A permissive matcher finds 1,168 field-lines across the corpus. A parser built
for the BL-682 shape (`^Label: value`, ASCII colon, no leading decoration) finds 397 of them
= 34.0%.** Two thirds of the metadata is invisible to it.

### Why it misses

| reason | field-lines missed |
|---|---|
| **emoji prefix** (`⭐ Genre:`, `📺 Streaming on:`, `🎬 Director:`) | **578** |
| **zero-width character prefix** (U+200B before the label) | **128** |
| bullet / `**markdown bold**` prefix (`**In short:**`, `• Movie:`) | 51 |
| spaced-dash delimiter (`Dealing with Tuco - Breaking Bad`) | 6 |
| other | 9 |

### The syntactic census

| axis | distribution |
|---|---|
| leading decoration | emoji 49.5% · none 35.2% · zero-width 11.0% · bullet/bold 4.4% |
| delimiter | ASCII `:` 98.9% · spaced dash 1.0% · `:-` 0.1% · fullwidth `：` never seen |
| label case | Title Case 99.4% · lower 0.4% · ALL CAPS 0.2% |
| whitespace | no space before delimiter 92.9% · **space before delimiter 7.1%** |

The space-before-colon variant matters more than 7.1% suggests: `hollywood_jan07` is
inconsistent *within itself*, writing both `Director: David Leland` and
`Director : TaricSun`. A parser keyed on the exact string will silently split one field into
two.

### Distinct template shapes actually observed

1. **Bare-label content metadata** — the BL-682 shape.
   `Title:` `Genre:` `Cast:` `Created by:` `Language:` `Release Date:` `Runtime:`
   `Where to Watch:` (clipcapture.tv, hollywood_jan07)
2. **Emoji-per-field content metadata** — same semantics, emoji as the visual bullet.
   `⭐ Genre:` `🎭 Starring:` `🏆 Rating: 8.5/10 IMDb` `📅 Original Run:` `📺 Streaming on:`
   (the six-account cluster below, clipsvaultex, bitesmoive)
3. **Value-less section headers** — no colon-value pair at all, a header followed by prose or
   bullets. `📝 Summary:` `🔥 Why Watch?` `📖 Storyline:` `🎞️ Title Info`. A key→value parser
   returns nothing for these; they need a section parser.
4. **Editor credit codes** — a different semantic entirely.
   `Ac:` `Cc:` `Oc:` `Dt:` `Movie:` (katicsedits). These name *collaborators*, not content.
5. **Legal / fair-use block** — `🎬 Credits & Fair Use:` plus a `Keywords:` line
   (decibeldrops). Present on 97% of its posts; carries a machine-readable keyword list.
6. **Unlabelled positional metadata** — `2011 ‧ Sitcom ‧ 6 seasons` under the title,
   interpunct-delimited, no labels whatsoever (bitesmoive). Copied from a search knowledge
   panel. Parseable, but only by position.
7. **`:-` delimiter** — `🎬 Series Name :- Ekaki (2025)` (animestories142).
8. **Markdown emphasis** — `**In short:** *Better Call Saul* is …` (series\_\_central).
9. **Non-English label vocabularies.** Portuguese: `Nome:` `Sinopse:` `Onde assistir:`
   `Lançamento:` `Duração:` `Gênero:` `Classificação:` `Episódios:` `Aviso:`
   (reidosfilmesbr, recortacine, somosnerdou, cine\_tv.pluss). Spanish: `Nombre:` `Género:`
   `Dónde verla:` `Descripción de la serie:` (crab.6521263). Indonesian: `Judul:`
   (nontonlayar.id).

### False positives are rare and easy to kill

**VERIFIED.** Inside accounts with ≥3 saved posts, a naive `^Label: value` parser produced
369 matches, of which **11 = 3.0%** had a "label" occurring in only one post. Every one is a
real colon-bearing title:

```
Fantastic Four: Rise of the Silver Surfer (2007) - Reed Richards' Wedding …
Austin Powers: International Man of Mystery (1997) became an instant classic
Sherlock Holmes: A Game of Shadows (2011) - The High-Stakes Train Ambush
Atlantis: The Lost Empire (2001) - Milo tries to impress Kida …
```

**Requiring a label to recur across ≥50% of an account's posts removes all of them** — a film
title with a colon never repeats, a template field always does. This is the same rule the
prevalence measurement uses, so the parser and the detector can share one pass.

There is one off-population false positive worth naming: `kelliefennellfitness` carries a
perfectly valid field template (`Calories:` `Protein:` `Fibre:` `Recipe:` `Instructions:`).
It is a recipe page. Template presence does not imply clip page.

### Shared caption generators — 50% of templated accounts are not independent

**VERIFIED, and this is the most consequential variant finding.** Sixteen accounts carry a
≥4-field signature. **Eight of them (50%) sit in a shared-generator cluster — distinct
handles emitting a byte-identical field vocabulary.**

| cluster | accounts | shared field vocabulary |
|---|---|---|
| **A (6 handles)** | jakeswims69, loste1980, richardthedis6067, shinbizarre312, thomasabg, weareassassin | `Genre` `Starring` `Summary` `Why Watch` `Rating` `Original Run` `Streaming` `Streaming on` `Format` `Credits` `IMDb` |
| **B (2 handles)** | clipcapture.tv, hollywood\_jan07 | `Title` `Cast` `Created by` `Genre` `Language` `Release Date` `Runtime` `Ratings` `Storyline` `Themes` `Where to Watch` `Hashtags` |

This cuts two ways:

- **For the parser: leverage.** One shape covers six accounts. Ranking parsers by cluster
  size is the cheapest possible build order.
- **For the statistics: deflation.** The nine "independent" templated accounts are closer to
  five independent template designs. The 37.5% CI is already 21–57%; the effective n is
  smaller than the raw n, which is exactly the `cluster_detect` / effective-n problem this
  codebase already tracks.

## Do templated accounts make better leads? Not straightforwardly — and the naive read is wrong

**VERIFIED.** Compared on the only fair denominator: accounts crawled by the same method with
≥8 saved posts. Templated n=9, untemplated n=12. Mann-Whitney U on per-account values.

| metric | templated | untemplated | ratio | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| median views / post | 14,603 | 3,404 | 4.29× | 0.064 — **not significant** |
| median likes / post | 129 | 92 | 1.40× | 0.943 — ns |
| **likes per view** | **1.0%** | **2.7%** | **0.47×** | **0.021 — significant** |
| days between posts | 0.04 | 0.18 | 0.25× | 0.177 — ns |
| reel fraction | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00× | 0.671 — ns |
| median duration (s) | 50.0 | 49.2 | 1.02× | 0.943 — ns |
| **duration CV** (lower = more consistent) | 0.40 | 0.33 | 1.20× | 0.477 — ns |
| caption length | 1,163 | 596 | 1.95× | 0.016 — significant |

Pooled at post level: templated median 19,050 views (n=144) vs untemplated 5,136 (n=166).

Three things to take from this table:

1. **Format consistency does NOT differ.** Reel fraction is 100% in both groups, median
   duration is ~50 s in both, and duration CV is if anything slightly *worse* on templated
   pages. The hypothesis that templating marks a more disciplined operator is not supported.
2. **Engagement rate is significantly WORSE on templated pages** — 1.0% likes-per-view
   against 2.7%. More views, less response per view.
3. **The caption-length result is near-tautological.** Templates add text; measuring that
   they added text is not evidence of quality.

### "Templated" is two populations, and averaging them created the 4.29× headline

**VERIFIED.** Split the templated group by whether its field vocabulary is unique or shared:

| group | n | median views | median gap between posts | span of the 12 saved posts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **BESPOKE** template (unique vocabulary) | 5 | **29,996** | 4.0 h | 120 h |
| **FARM** template (cluster-A vocabulary) | 4 | **8,186** | 0.65 h | **7.1 h** |
| untemplated | 12 | 3,404 | 4.3 h | 189 h |

The four cluster-A accounts posted their twelve most recent reels **inside a 7-hour window**,
carrying identical field lists. There is a **3.7× view gap inside "templated"** — larger than
the gap the headline claimed between templated and untemplated.

**GAP, stated plainly:** the saved payloads carry neither a follower count nor a post count on
the embedded user object, so account age and size are **not** establishable here. "Bulk,
automated posting cadence" is what the data supports; "days-old throwaway page" is a
hypothesis it does not.

### Verdict on question 3

**Templating is a weak positive signal for reach and a null signal for format discipline —
and it should not be wired into the repost finder as a quality gate on its own.** The
view-count difference is 4.29× but p=0.064 at n=9 vs 12; it does not survive a conventional
threshold, and it dissolves into a 3.7× within-group split once shared generators are
separated.

What *is* worth wiring, on the same free signal: **template-signature sharing as a page-farm
detector.** Two handles emitting an identical field vocabulary is a much stronger and much
cheaper statement than any view threshold, and it is exactly the kind of copy-detection
`cluster_detect.py` already does on tag blocks and bios.

## What is reliably in untemplated captions

**VERIFIED**, 176 posts across the 12 untemplated accounts. Both columns matter: per-post
coverage says how often a feature appears at all; **per-account coverage (present on ≥80% of
that account's own posts) is the bar that makes it safe to parse at scale.**

| feature | per post | accounts ≥80% |
|---|---|---|
| hashtags ≥1 | 81.8% | 8 / 12 = 66.7% |
| hashtags ≥5 | 73.3% | 8 / 12 |
| Title Case run (≥2 words) | 73.9% | 7 / 12 |
| emoji ≥1 | 64.8% | 5 / 12 = 41.7% |
| multi-line (≥3 lines) | 62.5% | 6 / 12 |
| `(YYYY)` release year | 25.6% | 2 / 12 |
| @mention | 18.2% | 2 / 12 |
| call-to-action | 13.6% | 1 / 12 |
| credit / attribution | 7.4% | 1 / 12 |
| `x/10` rating | 0.6% | 0 / 12 |
| fair-use / copyright notice | 0.0% | 0 / 12 |
| **URL / domain** | **0.0%** | 0 / 12 |

Notes that change what is worth building:

- **Hashtags are less universal than BL-682's 101/108 implied.** On untemplated accounts
  81.8% of posts carry one, **18.2% carry none**, and only 8 of 12 accounts are above 80%.
  `lol_buzzmemes` posts 8 of 14 with no hashtag at all.
- **Hashtags are topical, not a fixed block.** Six of the twelve accounts have **zero sticky
  tags** (no tag repeating on ≥50% of their posts). `culturemeetsonline` uses 100 distinct
  tags across 36 posts and repeats none. Where sticky tags do exist they are informative —
  `#batman #dccomics` (thedylanseek), `#90s #nostalgia #childhood #cartoon` (nostalgic9ohs).
- **Credit lines and source attribution are essentially absent** on untemplated pages: 7.4%
  of posts, 1 of 12 accounts. Fair-use notices are 0.0%. Both are *template* features — they
  appear on 63.5% and 32.7% of templated posts respectively. The brief's expectation that
  credit lines are broadly parseable does not hold outside templates.
- **No untemplated caption contains a URL or a domain. Zero of 176.** Bio-link extraction
  from captions has nothing to find here.
- **Free `Title (Year)` extraction: 25.6%** of untemplated posts, dropping to **20.2%** once
  `borcellae_` (which does carry a `🎬 Movie:` line) is excluded. For contrast, 43.6% on
  templated posts. Useful, not reliable.
- Emoji are frequent but concentrated: 🎬 (42 posts / 7 accounts), 👇 (25 / 3), 📺 (14 / 6),
  😭 (13 / 4), 😂 (12 / 4). The affect-bearing ones are thin.

## Answering question 5 directly

**Templating is not rare on the population that matters, but it is not a majority either.**

- On confirmed repost/meme pages: **37.5%** (CI 21–57%). Above the ~15% "position it as
  secondary" threshold, and comfortably so.
- On the raw discovery pool: **13.9% observed, ≈10.8% corrected.** Below it.

So the positioning depends on where the parser sits, and both readings are defensible:

**Build the parser. Do not position it as the primary metadata source.** At 37.5% with a
21–57% CI and an effective n closer to five independent template designs, it will supply
structured metadata for roughly a third of qualified pages and nothing at all for the rest.
That is real value — where it fires it yields `Genre`, `Title`, `Cast`, `Runtime`, `IMDb`,
`Streaming` across an entire back catalogue for free — but a pipeline that *depends* on it
will be empty-handed on the majority of leads.

Two conditions on the build:

1. **Match all nine shapes, not one.** The BL-682 shape alone captures 34.0% of the
   field-lines that exist. Emoji prefixes (49.5%) and zero-width prefixes (11.0%) are not
   edge cases; they are the majority.
2. **Gate on within-account label recurrence, not on the presence of a colon.** It removes
   100% of the observed false positives at no cost, and it is the same pass that detects
   whether the account is templated at all.

## What was not established

- **Account age, follower count, post count** — absent from the saved payloads. The
  bulk-posting cluster cannot be characterised further without a paid profile fetch.
- **Whether 37.5% generalises beyond 24 accounts.** The CI is 21–57%. Twenty of the 24 come
  from a single repost-finder run on one day; caption fashions on Instagram change.
- **Whether the raw-pool 13.9% describes repost pages specifically.** That pool mixes repost
  pages with ordinary creators pulled from hashtag and audio-track feeds; the figure
  describes the discovery stream, not the target population within it.
- **Non-English template prevalence.** Portuguese, Spanish and Indonesian variants were all
  found on accounts with 1–2 saved posts each. They exist; their rate is unmeasured.
- **TikTok.** 74 posts / 41 accounts were extracted and deliberately excluded — editor and
  VFX accounts, not repost pages. Templating on TikTok repost pages is unmeasured.

## Method

All measurements offline on payloads already saved to disk. No network calls, no paid API
calls, no code outside `scratch/` was touched, no configuration or data file was modified.

- Extraction deduplicates by `(account, pk)`; the `_clips.json` and `_medias.json` payloads
  for the same account overlap substantially.
- A **field-line** is `[optional emoji / bullet / zero-width / bold] LABEL <delim> VALUE`,
  where LABEL is ≤4 words and ≤30 characters and `<delim>` ∈ `{ : ： :- ：- " – " }`.
- An account is **templated** when ≥2 distinct labels each recur in ≥50% of its captions.
  The recurrence requirement is what distinguishes a template field from a film title that
  happens to contain a colon.
- Confidence intervals are Wilson score intervals. The significance test is a two-sided
  Mann-Whitney U with tie correction on per-account values; it is reported so its weakness at
  n=9 vs 12 is visible, not to launder a null result into a claim.
