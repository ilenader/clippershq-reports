## The confidence sentences — read these first

Given a random clip from this library:

- **Certain (100%, all 66 accounts).** I know its identity, its permalink, which account posted it, when, its play count, and its caption. I can retrieve the pixels on demand.
- **Near-certain (99%, 64–66 accounts).** I know its duration, its declared audio type, and a derived text valence.
- **Probable (67%, 61 accounts).** I know its on-screen layout — and this is the only high-fill field that is genuinely spread across the library rather than inherited from two pages.
- **A coin toss (45%, 34 accounts).** I might know its franchise, and if I do there is a ~48% chance that knowledge came from one of two accounts.
- **Mostly guessing (34% / 28%).** Its track title or content genre. Both are dominated by a single page — 115 of 216 track titles and 114 of 178 genres are one account. **For a random clip these are usually absent, and when present they mostly describe that account's captioning habits.**
- **Nothing at all.** Its format (music-first vs dialogue-first), its audio arousal, its spoken subtitle text. Zero clips, by construction.

### The measured tier is the good tier here, and it is the one that gets discarded

`layout` accounts for **424 measured slots — every measured slot in the library**. It is a CV read of actual frames, so unlike `audio_type` it cannot be mislabelled by the platform: it is what the pixels say.

**A consumer filtering to `declared` only silently drops all 424** — and with them the best-distributed signal in the store. That filter would leave song matching leaning on `track_title` (23 accounts) and `content_genre` (11 accounts), which is exactly backwards.

## 6. What would move the needle most, for song matching specifically

**One field: `subtitle_text`. One fix: run the walk-time fetch.**

- It is **$0** — the URI already rides on a response that has been paid for.
- **126 clips (20.4%) already flag `subtitles_available`**, so the population is identified and waiting.
- It carries **spoken dialogue**, the one axis this library has nothing on. Every populated field today describes the *frame* (layout, caption) or *platform metadata* (play count, audio type). Nothing describes what is being **said**.
- For song matching that is the difference between "a 40-second clip with a caption bar" and "a 40-second clip whose dialogue is a confession". The second is what picks a track.

Second, and cheaper than it looks: **declare `speech_frac`**. It is already written, one line from readable, and it is the input `format` needs.

**Not** `content_genre` or `track_title`. Raising their fill would raise the fill of one account's habits.

## Limits

- **629 clips at this snapshot, not 755.** The library is being appended to by another round; every figure here comes from one frozen moment across all 35 shards. Top-2 measured 32.9% against the 33.5% quoted — same picture, different instant.
- **`account` is the concentration unit.** Two handles belonging to one operator would read as two accounts, so the concentration reported is a floor, not a ceiling.
- **Fill is not accuracy.** `franchise` at 45.2% counts non-empty strings, and one sampled value is literally `"(Unknown / Short Clip Scene)"` — a populated cell that answers nothing. No field here was checked for correctness, only presence.
- **`layout`'s trustworthiness is taken from its provenance tier, not re-validated.** I did not re-run the CV on any frame this round.
- **Items 1–4 and 6 are measured; item 5 is the confidence statement above and is a reading of those measurements, not a separate experiment.**
