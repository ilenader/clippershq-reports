# BL-724 — Measured per-field error on 60 hand-labelled clips: tag-derived franchise is wrong 28.6% of the time. There is no purity setting with zero errors and usable coverage.

**Date:** 2026-07-29 · **Type:** Measurement, read-only on pipeline code · **Spend:** $0.00
Ground truth and predictions saved to the repo (see Artifacts).

---

## The decision you asked for, first

**There is no setting that gives zero wrong labels and usable coverage.** The only
zero-error tier on this sample is `declared`, and it covers **1 clip in 60**.

| purity setting | clips with a usable label | wrong | error rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1 declared genre only** | **1** | **0** | **0.0%** |
| L2 + prose-corroborated franchise | 23 | 4 | 17.4% |
| L3 all genre (declared + derived) — *current default* | 45 | 17 | **37.8%** |
| L4 + all tag-derived franchise | 60 | 27 | **45.0%** |

The honest read: **the library as it stands is not safe to pick clips from unattended.** At
the current default, more than a third of the labelled clips carry a wrong genre.

---

## Per-field measured error

n = 60 clips, 59 distinct accounts, 12 discovery tags, stratified 5 per tag.

| field | coverage | correct | **error** | tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **franchise** | 35/60 = 58% | 25/35 | **28.6%** | derived |
| **content_genre** | 44/60 = 73% | 27/44 = 61% | **39%** (7 wrong + 10 false-positive) | derived |
| content_genre | 1/60 | 1/1 | 0% | declared |
| **content_medium** | 30/60 = 50% (25 verifiable) | 15/25 | **40%** | derived |
| **valence_text** | 55/60 = 92% | 29/55 = 53% exact | **5% sign errors** | derived |

BL-703's 91.5% *coverage* claim holds — but coverage was never accuracy. **This is the first
round to measure whether the inferred label is actually right, and 28.6% of the time the
franchise is not.**

Valence is the one pleasant surprise: exact three-way agreement is only 53%, but
**sign errors are 5%**, better than the 10% previously claimed. Almost all disagreement is
neutral↔positive or neutral↔negative — the axis is directionally sound, the neutral boundary
is soft.

**False positives are the under-reported failure**: 10 of 60 clips get a confident genre when
the clip genuinely has none — concentrated in #onepiece (5), #naruto (2), #theboys (2).

## Where the errors are, and they are not evenly spread

Franchise errors by tag:

| tag | wrong / emitted |
|---|---|
| **#onepiece** | **5 / 5** |
| **#theboys** | **4 / 5** |
| #naruto | 1 / 5 |
| #batman, #mcu, #marveledit, #dragonballz | 0 / 20 |

**Two tags account for 9 of the 10 franchise errors.** The Marvel and Batman tags were
perfect on this sample. This is not a uniform 28.6% tax — it is two badly-polluted tags
dragging an otherwise decent signal down.

## Failure modes, with the actual clips

**1. Spam copypasta — the single largest cause (7 of 60 clips).**
Seven clips across **three different tags** carry the *identical* Japanese caption about BTS's
V attending a Hollywood show with Gracie Abrams and Doja Cat:

```
#onepiece   50, 51, 52, 53, 54   (all five sampled #onepiece clips)
#theboys    56
#animeedit  06
```

Seven different accounts, one copy-pasted caption, fandom hashtags bolted on for reach. The
pipeline confidently labels every one of them `franchise=One Piece`, `medium=anime`. **All
five #onepiece clips in the sample are K-pop engagement bait.**

**2. Literal-word collision.** Clip 55, `#theboys` + `#schoolfun #boysbathroom
#funnyanimation` — a school-bathroom cartoon joke. "The boys" used literally, not the TV
show. Labelled `The Boys`, `superhero`, `satire`.

**3. Homonym — the Walter White houseplant of this round.** Clip 47: *"Naruto chicken rice
master"*, Tamil food content. `naruto` is also a fish-cake garnish. It survives even the
strictest corroboration rule below, because the word genuinely appears in the prose.

**4. Style is not franchise.** Clip 00: AI-generated Champions League football rendered in
anime style. Tagged `#anime`, so `medium=anime` — arguably defensible — but there is no anime
franchise and the real genre is sports.

**5. Medium confusion inside a correct franchise.** All four `#dragonballz` medium errors are
this: a *video game* (Raging Blast 2, clip 25), an *AI-generated fan series* (27), and
*fanart edits* (29) are all labelled `medium=anime`. The franchise is right; the medium is
wrong because the tag cannot see that this is a game capture rather than the show.

**6. A silent data loss on the DECLARED tier — my own module's defect.** Clip 01 declares
`Genre: Action Fantasy Adventure Dark Fantasy`. `parse_template_fields` extracts it
correctly. Then `content_genre._declared_axes` calls `normalise_genre` on the whole
space-separated string, which is not in the canon, returns `""`, and **the entire declared
genre is dropped.** The clip falls back to the derived tier. A caption that stated four
genres contributed none. Comma-separated lists split fine; space-separated ones vanish.

**7. A parser vocabulary gap.** The same clip has five template fields — `Anime Name:`,
`Season:`, `Genre:`, `Episodes:`, `Studio:` — but only `Genre` is in `caption_parser`'s alias
map, so `is_templated` returns False (needs 3). Anime accounts use a different field
vocabulary from the film/TV accounts the parser was built on.

## The best purity rule found, and why it still is not zero

**Prose corroboration**: keep a tag-derived franchise only if the franchise (or a character
alias) is named in the caption **after stripping hashtags** — the tag must not be allowed to
corroborate itself.

| | emitted | wrong | error |
|---|---|---|---|
| before | 35 | 10 | **28.6%** |
| **after** | **23** | **1** | **4.3%** |

It costs 3 correct labels (clips 26, 45, 49 — captions that are tag-only, so there is no prose
to corroborate against) and removes 9 of the 10 errors, including **every spam-copypasta
clip**. The first version of this rule scored 25% because it matched against the raw caption,
where the hashtag itself sits — the circularity was the whole problem.

The one survivor is clip 47, the Naruto chicken rice. No text rule fixes a genuine homonym.

**4.3% is a good field. It is not zero.**

## What I would actually do with this

- **Do not consume `franchise` or `content_genre` unfiltered.** At L3/L4 you are picking from
  a pool where a third to a half of labels are wrong.
- **Add prose corroboration to the derived tier.** One text check takes franchise from 28.6%
  to 4.3% error at a 34% recall cost. That is the highest-leverage change available.
- **Fix the space-separated declared-genre drop** (failure 6). It is a bug in
  `content_genre.py`, it is silent, and it throws away the one tier that is actually
  trustworthy.
- **Treat `#onepiece` and `#theboys` as polluted** until re-measured at larger n. On this
  sample they are 9 of 10 franchise errors.
- **`valence_text` is usable as a sign**, not as a three-way class. 5% sign error is
  respectable; 53% exact agreement is not.

## Limits — and one that materially weakens a headline

- **The declared tier is effectively unmeasured (n=1), and that is my own artifact.** The
  saved payload `scratch/bl701/discovered.json` truncates captions to **300 characters**
  (my BL-702 discovery script did `caption[:300]`). Clip 57's caption is cut mid-word at
  `Genre: Act`, and clip 01's declared genres are lost to failure 6. BL-694 measured real
  captions at a **median of 908 characters**, so the declared tier would be far better
  populated on untruncated data. **The "declared is near-perfect" hypothesis is neither
  confirmed nor refuted by this round.**
- **No thumbnails were available.** `discovered.json` stores no `thumb_url` and the source
  payload was not retained, so adjudication used caption + hashtag + permalink code only.
  Vision was not part of it, and clips whose caption did not settle a field were marked
  `unverifiable` and excluded from that field's denominator rather than guessed.
- **A machine reading a 300-char caption is a weaker adjudicator than a person watching the
  clip.** The spam-copypasta and literal-collision findings are unambiguous; the medium and
  valence calls are softer.
- **n=5 per tag.** Tag-level rates (#onepiece 5/5, #theboys 4/5) are directionally strong but
  arithmetically fragile. The pooled per-field rates rest on n=60.
- The prose-corroboration rule was designed *after* seeing these errors, so its 4.3% is an
  in-sample figure and will be optimistic out of sample.
