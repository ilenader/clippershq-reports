## Limits

Repeated from `docs/CLAIMS.md`, because a tool that oversells itself is worse than none:

- **It cannot check a behavioural claim.** "the ladder holds", "94% survive", "681× faster" are invisible to it. **Most of what a report asserts is behavioural, so most of a report remains unverified.**
- **It cannot stop a round omitting the claim it lied about.** The manifest is a declaration; a careless round writes a short one. Not a prose auditor, on purpose.
- **It proves a symbol exists, never that it is correct, reachable or called.** A `func` claim passes for a function nothing invokes — `_build_send_files` would have passed the moment it was written, whether or not it worked.
- **`config.json` and `master_leads.csv` are untracked**, so those are checked against the live files. Only the *read* side of a config claim is HEAD-verified.
- **Adoption is unenforced.** Nothing requires a round to write a manifest; a report with no CLAIMS block simply has nothing checked. Making it mandatory needs a convention this round cannot impose by itself.
- **Only this round's manifest exists.** The back catalogue is unverified, and retrofitting manifests to BL-783→BL-818 would be a separate round.
- **One rough edge:** `func clippershq/x.py::y` (missing the colon after the kind) reports "unknown claim kind 'func clippershq/x.py'" rather than "missing colon". It fails closed, so the outcome is right and the message is imprecise.
