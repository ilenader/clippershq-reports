# BL-701 — The Spotify funnel's stalls are NOT O(n²). 67% of a real run is spent blocked on one global MusicBrainz lock, and the 30s watchdog is shorter than the lock is held

**Date:** 2026-07-29 · **Type:** Diagnosis, READ-ONLY · **Spend:** $0.00 (nothing written, nothing changed, no network, no paid call)

Evidence tiers: **VERIFIED** (measured here, or read off the shipped code) · **REFUTED** (checked, premise is false) · **GAP** (not establishable from what was captured).

---

## The answer, before the detail

**REFUTED: there is no O(n²) pattern.** I measured `spotify_finder.run()` end-to-end offline at N = 200/400/800/1600 and it is **linear** — 2.0× time for 2× work at every step. Every named candidate is O(1) or runs once. The gaps also do **not** grow: in the real log they oscillate, and the mean gap in the **second** half of the run is *lower* than the first (122s vs 214s). The reported sequence "41, 69, 137, 352, 470" is an ascending subsequence selected out of a non-monotone series — the 69s gap actually happened **before** the 41s one.

**What is really happening — two serial bottlenecks, both constant-per-artist, neither scaling with n:**

1. **`musicbrainz.make_country_fn` holds ONE global lock across sleeps, retries and a 10s socket timeout.** Every artist above the listener floor must pass through it, one at a time, at ≥1.1s each. Concurrency does nothing for it. It is invisible in `run.log` because it is the only network call in the funnel using stdlib `urllib` — which is exactly why the gaps look like nothing is happening.
2. **`enrich_links.site_contacts` parses up to 11 pages per artist in pure-Python regex, holding the GIL.** Measured with no network at all: **9.23s of CPU** for one artist on 1.8MB pages. That is the "78–95% of one core" — the GIL caps pure Python at one core, and one CPU-bound worker blocks the other five.

**And the amplifier that makes it pathological:** the per-artist watchdog is **30s**; the worst-case MusicBrainz lookup is **37.8s**. The watchdog fires *before* the lock is released, so the artist is abandoned, its pool slot is refilled, and the new artist immediately blocks on a lock still held by the abandoned thread. The 37.8 seconds are spent and the result is thrown away.

**Cost:** **67% of the observed run window is silence.** A 5,000-lead run costs ~5.7h where ~1.9h of work is being done — **~3.8 hours lost per run**, linearly, not quadratically.

## 1. The O(n²) hypothesis, tested and refuted

| seeds | wall | qualified | expanded | time ratio vs previous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 200 | 0.03s | 201 | 380 | — |
| 400 | 0.03s | 405 | 740 | 1.00× |
| 800 | 0.06s | 801 | 1,460 | **2.03×** |
| 1,600 | 0.12s | 1,605 | 2,920 | **1.98×** |

2× work → 2× time. **Linear. If any accumulation-scan existed in the loop, this table would read 4×.**

## 2. Bottleneck #1 — the MusicBrainz global lock

**`clippershq/musicbrainz.py:148–181`**, in `make_country_fn`:

```python
_lock = threading.Lock()

def _pace():
    wait = state["last"] + float(min_interval) - time.monotonic()
    if wait > 0:
        _sleep(wait)                       # MIN_INTERVAL = 1.1s

def country_of(name=None, artist_id=None):
    ...
    with _lock:                            # <-- held across EVERYTHING below
        attempts = 1 if state["consecutive_failures"] >= circuit_after else max_attempts
        for attempt in range(max(1, attempts)):
            _pace()                        # sleeps up to 1.1s, INSIDE the lock
            try:
                payload = opener(q)        # urllib, timeout=10, INSIDE the lock
            except Exception as exc:
                ...
                _sleep(backoff_base * (2 ** attempt))   # 1.5s then 3.0s, INSIDE the lock
```

The lock wraps the entire retry loop — the pacing sleep, the 10-second socket timeout, and both backoff sleeps. The docstring is candid that this is deliberate ("Holding it across opener() also caps MusicBrainz to ONE in-flight request"), and for a courtesy rate limit that is the right call. The consequence at `concurrency=6` was not costed.

**Per-lookup cost, from the shipped constants** (`MIN_INTERVAL=1.1`, `MAX_ATTEMPTS=3`, `BACKOFF_BASE=1.5`, opener `timeout=10`):

| case | arithmetic | total |
|---|---|---|
| healthy | 1.1 pace + response | **~1.1s** |
| all 3 attempts time out | (1.1+10+1.5) + (1.1+10+3.0) + (1.1+10) | **37.8s** |
| circuit open (`consecutive_failures ≥ 5` → 1 attempt) | 1.1 + 10 | **11.1s** |

**VERIFIED — it applies to every artist above the floor.** `find_contact` runs the country gate at `spotify_finder.py:309-310`, immediately after the monthly-listeners filter and *before* the language gate, and live config has `spotify_allowed_countries = ['US','GB','CA','AU','IE','NZ']` with `min_listeners = 50000`, so `_country_checked` is true for the whole qualifying pool.

**Quantified against the real run:** 615 artists were processed in the 2,505s window. At the *healthy* 1.1s floor that is **615 × 1.1 = 677s of forced serialization — 27% of the wall clock at best**, before a single retry. Observed silence was 1,677s.

**VERIFIED — this is why the gaps are invisible.** MusicBrainz is reached through `urllib.request` (`musicbrainz.py:115-118`), which has no logger. Everything else in the funnel goes through `httpx`/`requests` and logs at DEBUG. Confirmed by counting the window: `httpcore.http11` 13,568 lines, `httpx` 1,354, `urllib3.connectionpool` 1,122 — and **`musicbrainz` 0**. A 470-second window with zero log lines is not an idle process; it is six workers queued on a lock whose holder does not log.

### The amplifier: the watchdog is shorter than the lock hold

`DEFAULT_SPOTIFY_MAX_SECONDS_PER_ARTIST = 30.0` (`spotify_finder.py:114`), against a 37.8s worst-case MusicBrainz lookup.

`_call_with_deadline` (`spotify_finder.py:533-555`) abandons the worker thread rather than killing it — correct, since a thread cannot be killed safely. But an abandoned thread **keeps running, and it keeps holding the MusicBrainz lock**. So on any lookup that enters retry:

1. at 30s the watchdog fires, the artist is recorded `filter_reason="timeout"`, the pool slot is freed;
2. `_fill()` immediately dispatches a new artist;
3. that artist blocks on the MB lock — still held by the abandoned thread until 37.8s;
4. the 37.8 seconds of lock time produce a result that is discarded.

The pool keeps feeding fresh work into a lock saturated by abandoned threads. This also accounts for the **RSS growth from ~40MB to ~139MB**: abandoned threads retain their stacks and their fetched page buffers, and `site_contacts` (below) can hold up to 11 page bodies per worker.

## 3. Bottleneck #2 — `site_contacts` burns a core, holding the GIL

**`clippershq/enrich_links.py:255-258`:**

```python
_CONTACT_PATHS = ("/contact", "/contact-us", "/about", "/imprint", "/impressum",
                  "/kontakt", "/booking", "/legal", "/terms", "/privacy")
SITE_PAGE_BUDGET = 1 + len(_CONTACT_PATHS)      # = 11 pages per artist
```

Called per artist at `spotify_finder.py:397`, with `enrich_bio_link` defaulting to **True** (`control.py:2021`).

**MEASURED, no network — the fetch was a lambda returning a string, so this is pure CPU:**

| page size | `site_contacts` (11 pages) |
|---|---|
| 88 KB | 0.31s |
| 444 KB | 1.59s |
| 892 KB | 3.92s |
| **1.8 MB** | **9.23s** |

**And the funnel really is fetching pages that big.** Host counts in the run window: `en.wikipedia.org` **188**, `wikipedia.org` 33, `fr.wikipedia.org` 22 — **243 Wikipedia fetches**, articles that routinely run 0.5–2MB. Also present: `ib.adnxs.com` 33, `connect.facebook.net` 33, `static.assetlab.io` 32 — ad and tracker hosts being fetched and regex-scanned for an artist's email address.

This is the **78–95% of one core**. CPython's GIL caps pure-Python execution at one core, so a worker inside these regex scans blocks all five others regardless of `concurrency`.

## 4. Why the gaps dropped from 470s back to ~105–130s

**VERIFIED — the MusicBrainz circuit breaker, `musicbrainz.py:163`:**

```python
attempts = 1 if state["consecutive_failures"] >= int(circuit_after) else int(max_attempts)
```

With `CIRCUIT_AFTER = 5`, once five consecutive hard failures accumulate the retry count drops from 3 to 1, and worst-case per-lookup falls **37.8s → 11.1s, a 3.4× reduction**. Applied to six queued workers that is ~227s → ~67s. The observed 470s → ~105–130s is that transition. `consecutive_failures` resets to 0 on any success, so the run oscillates between breaker-open and breaker-closed — which is exactly the non-monotone gap series in §1.

The two alternatives in the brief are **not** the cause:
- **Queue draining — no.** `_maybe_expand` (`spotify_finder.py:590-592`) *does* self-throttle as `remaining = target - qualified` shrinks, so expansion stops firing later in the run. But that reduces *network* calls, not lock contention, and the offline run shows `expand_skipped` rising smoothly with no timing effect.
- **Target met / depth limit — no.** Both terminate the run; they do not shorten gaps mid-run, and gaps continued after the drop (219s at 15:52, 207s at 15:56).

## 5. Did the parallelization make it worse?

**The reduce is not the problem — that part of the brief's hypothesis is refuted.** `_tally` is O(1) per result and, as measured in §1, `run()` is linear. Concurrency is not feeding a serial bottleneck *in `_tally`*.

**But yes, concurrency=6 made the run worse, in two ways the commit did not measure:**

1. **It created the MusicBrainz queue.** At `concurrency=1` the lock is uncontended and free (the docstring's "an uncontended lock is a no-op" is correct). At 6, five workers are parked on it at any moment, so the effective parallelism *for every artist above the listener floor* is **1** — while still paying the 30s watchdog and the abandoned-thread pile-up of §2.
2. **The GIL means `site_contacts` cannot overlap anyway.** Six workers cannot parse six pages at once; they interleave on one core.

**Why the commit's 7.5× was real and still misleading:** it was measured on a "60-artist offline fixture" (commit `1503f80`). An offline fixture has no MusicBrainz limiter and no site scraping — it measures exactly the part that parallelizes (HTTP wait) and none of the two parts that do not. The 7.5× is true of the fixture and does not describe production.

## 6. The cost at scale

Measured from the run window (15:19:38 → 16:01:23, 41.8 min): **615 artists processed**, 1,677s silent of 2,505s.

- observed: **4.1s per artist** wall
- active (silence removed): 828s / 615 = **1.35s per artist**

| run size | observed | without the stalls | **lost** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 961 leads (the measured run) | ~1.1 h | ~0.36 h | **~0.7 h** |
| 2,000 leads | ~2.3 h | ~0.75 h | ~1.5 h |
| **5,000 leads** | **~5.7 h** | **~1.9 h** | **~3.8 h** |

**This scales linearly, so it does not explode — but the constant is 3×.** The decision the brief asked for: a 5,000-lead run wastes about **3.8 hours**, and roughly two thirds of every Spotify run is spent blocked. Independently, the floor set by the MusicBrainz limiter alone is **1.5h per 5,000 artists** even with a perfectly healthy MusicBrainz, and **15.4h** with the circuit breaker open — that floor is unaffected by any amount of concurrency.

## What was not established

- **Whether MusicBrainz was actually returning 429/5xx during the stalls.** It logs nothing (§2), so the retry path is inferred from the timing signature and the shipped constants, not observed. Adding one log line at the limiter would confirm it in a single run. This is the main **GAP**, and it is the difference between "MusicBrainz was degraded" and "MusicBrainz was fine and something else held the lock".
- **The split between bottleneck #1 and #2.** MusicBrainz accounts for ≥677s of the 1,677s silence at its healthy floor; the remainder is consistent with `site_contacts` CPU plus retries, but the two were not separated because neither is instrumented.
- **`stats["timed_out"]` for the run.** It would directly confirm the watchdog/abandoned-thread interaction, but the run summary goes to stdout via `print`, not to `run.log`, so it was not captured.
- **Whether abandoned threads ever accumulate unboundedly.** Each is bounded by its own network timeouts, so they should drain; the RSS curve is consistent with a bounded backlog, not a leak, but this was not proven.
- **Page-size distribution of the sites actually scraped.** The 9.23s figure uses a synthetic 1.8MB page chosen to match Wikipedia's range; real per-artist parse cost was not sampled.
