## 3. What predicts a productive neighbourhood: nothing I could find

### The experiment

**This is not the refuted experiment.** Ranking seeds by their **past yield** was refuted — the
"three best seeds" returned 17.5% [8.7, 31.9], *below* baseline, and the 3.2x spread that
justified it never existed. This ranks by a **structural attribute** knowable before any fetch,
chosen from §2's 1,604-page proxy *before* a single page of the test was captured.

* **Arm A — predicted productive:** 40 fresh pages from **music**-niche seeds.
* **Arm B — predicted unproductive:** 40 fresh pages from **film/tv, basketball, kids/family**.

Both drawn from the **2,303 candidates already on disk** that were never judged, are absent
from `meme_pages_seen.json` and absent from `master_leads.csv`. Same sweep, same date, same
discovery mechanism. **The only thing that differs is the niche of the seed.** Grids captured
free (Playwright, logged out, 2 lanes — 40 pages in 258.7 s and 231.3 s). Judged under
`bl1265_brief_v8.md` + the `bl1307_brief_v10.md` addendum by **ten blind agents** that were
told nothing about arms, seeds or the hypothesis and were forbidden the files that would reveal
them.

### The result

| | judged | WANT | REJECT | UNSURE | want rate | Wilson 95% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **A — music seeds** | 40 | **4** | 34 | 2 | **10.0%** | [4.0, 23.1] |
| **B — film/tv, basketball, kids** | 40 | **7** | 31 | 2 | **17.5%** | [8.8, 31.9] |

**Difference A − B = −7.5 points. Newcombe 95%: [−23.2, +8.2]. The interval contains zero.**

> **The attribute does not predict a productive neighbourhood, and what signal there is points
> the wrong way.** The proxy said music beats film/tv by 15 points; on fresh pages under the
> current judge, music came out 7.5 points *lower*, and the difference is not distinguishable
> from noise.

**That is the third independent refutation of seed selection**, and the first to rank by an
attribute rather than by history. Taken with the two before it, the honest conclusion is that
**the pool is flat**: nothing known before a fetch tells you where the good pages are.

**Pooled across both arms — the rate a planner actually gets sampling this pool without any
selection — 11 of 80 = 13.8% [7.9, 23.0]**, consistent with BL-1296's judge-side 13.0% on the
same kind of raw suggested pool.

One page, `lovestoryfx`, captured as an empty logged-out shell with no grid and was returned
UNSURE with zero tiles — flagged by the judging agent, not discovered afterwards.

### The guard that fired on me, and why it was right to and wrong to

`test_bl1307_veto_refused.py` forbids any scratch prompt from pointing a judge at the refuted
v9 brief. My judging prompt said:

> `3. **Do NOT read scratch/bl1293_brief_v9.md.** Its own first line says ⛔ REFUTED …`

— a **prohibition**, flagged as a **pointer**. BL-1308, who owns that test, had already seen
this and added a polarity check whose comment names my file by name:
*"a round doing exactly the right thing, turned red for saying so."*

**Their fix cannot fire.** `cat -A` on the running source shows the negation regex as

```
re.search(r"(?i)^H(do not|don't|never|not|refus|…)^H", line)
```

Those `^H` are literal **backspace characters, ``**, wrapping the alternation. No line of
prose contains one, so the negation branch is unreachable and **every** mention of v9 — negated
or not — is still reported. Verified directly: the compiled pattern is
`"(?i)(do not|don't|never)"` and it matches neither my line nor the bare string
`do not`.

**I did not touch their file** — `tests/test_bl1307_veto_refused.py` is ' M' and held by
BL-1308. I reworded my own prompt so it no longer names v9 at all ("those two files are the
only briefs you may read"), which makes the suite green regardless of whether they fix the
regex. **The two stray bytes are theirs to remove, and until they do, the polarity fix they
wrote is dead code.**

**What the agents actually ran** was the earlier wording quoted above; the rewording happened
after all ten had finished and changes no verdict.
