## 2. What reading the 20 found

### The evidence sentence is false on roughly half the file

The clipper message ends its opening paragraph with:

> *"— the one from {date} did {clip_views} views. That's {followers} followers and a
> {median} median — a real audience, **not one lucky post**."*

That sentence is only true when the featured clip is roughly typical of the account. In the
pilot it is not:

| # | greeting | clip | median | ratio | |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 8 | bogdy | 31.3M | 1.5M | **20.9×** | "not one lucky post" is **false** |
| 12 | nicol4s_de_castro | 51M | 1.2M | **42.5×** | **false** |
| 17 | jotace | 10.4M | 995k | **10.5×** | **false** |
| 19 | Jobro | 5.1M | 969k | **5.3×** | **false** |
| 14 | wvnderlustoff | 4.7M | 1.1M | **4.3×** | **false** |
| 7 | Cykl0psFilm | 115k | 1.7M | 0.07× | cites their **weakest** work |
| 9 | SnzEdit | 74k | 1.3M | 0.06× | cites their weakest work |
| 5 | tusk | 826k | 1.9M | 0.43× | cites their weakest work |

**8 of 20 in the pilot. Across the whole clipper block, measured on rendered output:**

| | count | share |
|---|---:|---|
| messages that cite a median at all | 1,075 / 1,578 | 68.1% |
| of those, clip ≥ 3× median → **the sentence is false** | **751** | **69.9% of citing · 47.6% of the block** |
| of those, clip ≤ 0.5× median → cites their weakest work | 104 | 9.7% of citing · 6.6% of the block |
| in the sane 0.5×–3× band | 220 | **20.5% of citing** |

The worst that currently ships, verbatim:

> *"I found you through your football edits — the one from January 2025 did **78.7M views**.
> That's 1.1M followers and a **6k median** — a real audience, **not one lucky post**."*

78.7M against a 6k median is, definitionally, one lucky post. **Every number in that sentence
is true and the sentence is false** — precisely the failure class the brief names. A recipient
reads it as proof nobody looked at their account.

The fix is small and the machinery already exists: the template **already** suppresses an
implausibly *small* median (a 27-median is dropped rather than printed — verified). It is the
*ratio* that is unguarded. When `clip_views / median ≥ 3`, drop the "not one lucky post"
clause or lead with the median instead of the outlier.

## 5. The runbook

Written into `docs/SENDING.md` as **THE SEND RUNBOOK**: the 9-step sequence with a
check-immediately-after and a **stop immediately if** for each step, the warm-up ladder, and
the abort table.

**Bounce abort thresholds**, derived from the measured 0.81% (6/737, 95% CI 0.30–1.76%):

| batch | expected | P(≥1) | P(≥2) | **abort at** |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 20 | 0.16 | 15.0% | 1.1% | **3+** |
| 50 | 0.40 | 33.4% | 6.2% | **3+** |
| 100 | 0.81 | 55.7% | 19.5% | **4+** |
| 200 | 1.62 | 80.3% | 48.2% | **6+** |

"Abort at" is the count with **under 1% probability** if 0.81% still holds — where the honest
conclusion is *the rate changed*, not *we were unlucky*. **One bounce in 20 is not a signal**
(15% of the time). Two is a warning. Three is a stop.

**And the statistic that matters most for morale:**

| reply rate | expected from 20 | **P(zero replies)** |
|---|---:|---:|
| 4% | 0.8 | **44%** |
| 6% | 1.2 | **29%** |
| 8% | 1.6 | **19%** |

**A zero-reply run happens 19–44% of the time when everything works.** This pilot tests the
mechanism — delivery, the stamp, whether a reply has anywhere to go — not the copy. It cannot
test copy: there is no second copy variant in the template, so both "arms" receive identical
text and calling it an A/B would fabricate an experiment.
