## THE HEADLINE

**The gate's verdict depended on which rendition Instagram served, and the cause is one line
of the engine's own preprocessing.** RapidOCR's detector is configured `limit_type: min,
limit_side_len: 736`, and its resize is:

```python
if min(h, w) < self.limit_side_len:      # 720-wide frame -> nudged to 736
    ratio = float(self.limit_side_len) / min_side
else:
    ratio = 1.0                          # 1080-wide frame -> NOT TOUCHED
```

So the same clip is detected at **736 px** or at **1080 px** depending only on what the CDN
handed us — **1.47× of linear resolution** — and the recogniser then crops from that same
frame, so it meets the difference twice. Below 736 the engine resizes; above it, it does not.
Two deliveries of one clip take different paths through one model.

**The fix is at the source: every frame is now read at `READ_FRAME_W = 1080`, and the answer
is scaled straight back to source pixels. `MIN_FRAMES_AGREEING` is untouched.**

## 1. THE CORPUS, AND A DENOMINATOR I GOT WRONG FIRST

Every `_batch.json` on disk carries the rendition served and the state reached: **177 rows
over 34 distinct clips**.

**The first pass of this measurement was wrong and inflated the effect's cleanliness.** 26 of
those 177 rows are refused `below_the_8s_floor`, which fires **before** the read. A clip
refused upstream cannot be refused by the read gate, so scoring it as a read-gate success
credits whichever delivery happens to carry the short clips. `DXoARykk00L` is exactly that:
its 1920 rows read "0 unreadable" only because the duration floor refused them first, and I
had been treating that as evidence the large delivery reads fine. It is evidence of nothing
about reading.

**Reached the gate** = `done` + `unreadable_text` + `render_failed` (the render runs after the
read, so a failed render still proves the read passed). **151 of 177 rows.**

| rendition | rows | unreadable | rate | 95% Wilson | clips |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 900 | 2 | 2 | **100.0%** | [34.2, 100.0] | 1 |
| **1280** | **63** | **10** | **15.9%** | **[8.9, 26.8]** | 21 |
| 1350 | 4 | 0 | 0.0% | [0.0, 49.0] | 1 |
| **1920** | **70** | **0** | **0.0%** | **[0.0, 5.2]** | 21 |
| 1936 | 7 | 0 | 0.0% | [0.0, 35.4] | 2 |

**The two intervals do not overlap.** Correcting the denominator made the effect *stronger*,
not weaker.

**These rows are not independent** — 177 rows are 34 clips, re-run across batches all day — so
the interval is narrower than the evidence deserves and the case does not rest on it.

## 2. THE CONTROLLED TEST REFUTED THE OBVIOUS STORY

If a smaller frame simply refuses more, then shrinking a good clip should refuse it. **It does
not.** Thirty sources that arrived at 1080 wide were each downscaled to 720×1280 and put
through the real gate at both sizes (`scratch/bl1233/paired.py`):

| | |
|---|---|
| passed at native 1080 | **30 of 30** |
| passed downscaled to 720 | **30 of 30** |
| **refused only because it was smaller** | **0** |
| mean frames agreeing | 4.83 large → 4.73 small |

**Pixel count alone does not cause refusals.** A synthetic downscale of a good master is not
Instagram's small delivery: mine is a clean re-encode at crf 20 and keeps quality the CDN's
own 720 does not. This is why the real files had to be read, and it is why "smaller is worse"
was not a safe thing to conclude from the observational split alone.

It did surface something else. Seven of the thirty produced **different text** at the two
sizes — and on two of them the difference is not a homoglyph:

```
05_theclipbuzzz  1080: "...that time he didn't react"
                  720: "...that time he didn't react to the reels I sent"
20_levelgonn     1080: "levelgonn @levelgonn"
                  720: "levelgonn @levelgonn When its not your first latina"
```

**The LARGE version lost the hook.** Both are `measure_label_box` differences, not read
differences — the measured band was 0.138 of frame height against 0.191, and 0.058 against
0.133. The other five differ by a comma, a quote or `I`/`l` with bands identical to three
decimal places. **The band detector is resolution-sensitive too, and that is a separate
component this round did not fix** (§7).

## 3. THE GATE IS DETERMINISTIC — SO THE SAME-RENDITION FLIP WAS DIFFERENT BYTES

`Db2nMXoPMQz` at rendition 1280 was `done` in the 14:15 batch and `unreadable_text` in the
15:48 one. Before designing anything, that had to be separated from randomness.

**Ten reads of the same file: one distinct answer, 10 of 10.** The gate does not roll a die.
Two deliveries carrying the same nominal `rendition` are not the same bytes, and the field
records the number the CDN advertised, not the file it sent.

## 4. THE LADDER, ON THE FILE THAT ACTUALLY FAILED

The real 720-wide delivery of `Db2nMXoPMQz`, read at three working widths:

| read at | verdict | frames agreeing | text |
|---|---|---|---|
| **720** (native) | passes | **3 of 5** — one frame from refusal | `...instagram and sendingreels tobro` |
| **1080** | passes | **5 of 5** | `...instagram and sending reels to bro` |
| 1440 | passes | 4 of 5 | `...instagram and sending reels to bro` |

And the same clip's real **1920** delivery reads
`Just opening instagram and sending reels to bro` at **5 of 5**.

**Reading the small delivery at 1080 recovers the large delivery's answer character for
character**, and unfuses words the small read had run together. **1440 is worse than 1080** —
consistent with the closed finding that raising the detector's own limit 736→1440 made exact
accuracy worse (58.3% → 51.4%). The point is not *more*; it is *the same every time*.

1080 is not a tuned parameter: it is the width `edit.LABEL_REF_FRAME_W` already scales the
label constants at, and the width 92.6% of these sources arrive at.

## 7. WHAT I GOT WRONG, AND WHAT IS STILL OPEN

**The first before/after sweep measured nothing and looked like a clean null.**
`def upscale_for_read(png, dst, target_w=READ_FRAME_W)` binds 1080 at import, so setting
`clip_label_text.READ_FRAME_W = 0` to disable the intervention changed nothing and **both arms
ran with the fix** — reported as "identical on 10 of 10", which I nearly believed. The default
now resolves at call time, and
`test_bl1233_read_resolution.py::test_the_default_is_read_at_CALL_time_not_import_time` fails
if that is ever restored, so no future A/B of this behaviour can go vacuous the same way.

**The denominator error in §1**, corrected in the script rather than in prose.

**A wasted first pass of the paired experiment** read 30 clips as bandless: `measure_label_box`
only creates a temp dir when `work_dir is None`, so a path that does not exist yields no
stills, no bands, and `None` — indistinguishable from "this clip has no text band".
