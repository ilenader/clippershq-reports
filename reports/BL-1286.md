## 1. THE USERNAME BAR IS SOMETHING TO COVER

His rule, verbatim: *"On the top it's their icon, their name and their @. That's 90% of
videos. WE DON'T WANT THAT. It needs to be put over with the white label so it doesn't get
seen. We just need the text below it."*

So the bar was never a candidate hook ranked wrongly. It is furniture. The verbatim rule is
untouched — we still re-type the source's own words character for character — what this
round answers is **which** of the source's words.

### 1.1 One cause, two failure shapes

`clip_pipeline._topmost_band_cluster` clusters detected text bands by vertical gap and keeps
the topmost. The identity bar is above the caption card by construction, so the gap decides
which way it breaks:

| gap | what the band holds | what shipped |
|---|---|---|
| small | bar **and** card | `"Movie Moments @moviemoments Have you ever noticed…"` — n=2, 33, 35 |
| large | **only the bar** | `"Intoxication @intoxication"` and his line never read — n=6, 8, 10, 39, 7 |

The second is his named test case. `Dbv-dLFPyrT` drew `Intoxication @intoxication` and
missed a line about a Tesla car in 2040 **entirely**.

### 1.2 Detected structurally, never by a word list

Measured on all eight, at the resolution `READ_FRAME_W` reads them:

| clip | display name | @handle | dx | his line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | `Movie Moments` x251 | `@moviemoments` x248 | 3 | x106, 108 px below |
| 6 | `Intoxication` x241 | `@intoxication` x248 | 7 | x89, 192 px below |
| 8 | `Intoxication` x242 | `@intoxication` x249 | 7 | x101, 180 px below |
| 10 | `Intoxication` x215 | `@intoxication` x225 | 10 | x121, 97 px below |
| 33 | `DailyreelX` x143 | `@dailyreelx` x143 | 0 | x48, 62 px below |
| 35 | `Get Into Al` x192 | `@getintoai` x185 | 7 | x61, 47 px below |
| 39 | `Noctis` x257 | `@noctis21` x254 | 3 | x111, 53 px below |

Three terms, all required, each ruling out a different false positive:

* **the @** — a block whose *whole* text is a handle. Anchored at both ends, so `@bro`
  quoted inside a joke arrives mid-sentence and does not match.
* **alignment** — the row above shares its left edge, 0–10 px against type 28–69 px tall.
  His caption's left edge is **42–146 px** away. There is nothing between the two.
* **position** — the bar is the topmost row. Without this the rule would read "the top row
  is not the hook", which is false on the 29 clips that rendered correctly.

Nothing consults a display name. `tests/test_bl1286_username_bar.py` asserts no handle
appears anywhere in the module outside its docstrings.

### 1.3 The @ was outside the measured band on two clips

On n=10 the band is 51 px tall and holds only `Intoxication`; `@intoxication` sits **4 px
below its bottom edge**. On n=39 the band holds `Noctis` and `@noctis21` is 10 px below. So
even *detecting* the bar needs to look past the band — the strongest signal on the frame is
just outside it. `panel_region` grows the band to the static card's own top and bottom, the
extent the strip covers anyway; the band is only *read past* when its own content is
entirely a bar, which is what keeps 40 of his 48 links unmoved.

**It has to grow UPWARD too, and the first version did not.** At its 1280 delivery n=39's
band is `(0, 343, 720, 104)` and holds **only his line** — the bar sits at y 300–340,
entirely above it. The drawn words came out right by luck, because there was no bar in the
band to mistake for a hook. But nothing looked above the band either, so `covered_box` was
his line alone, the strip was fitted to that, and **the avatar and `@noctis21` shipped
visible above the white** in my first re-render. When there is no measured card —
`measure_label_card` returned `None` on that clip — the look-up is bounded by a fraction of
the band's own height, the only length scale the clip has offered. n=39's strip moves from
`y343` to `y227`; clips with no bar do not move at all.

### 1.4 The icon, which no text detector can see

His rule names the icon first. It carries no text on five of the eight, so neither the Sobel
band detector nor the OCR can find it — and it shipped visible in **his** batch: on n=39 the
strip's top edge landed **5 px below the top of a circular avatar** and a yellow arc survived
above the white.

`avatar_extent` measures it from pixels, in the columns left of the **aligned column** — not
left of the bar's bounding box, because on n=33 and n=35 the icon carries its own burned-in
logotype (`DAILYREELX` x59, `GeintoRi` x78) that OCR reads as words, so the bounding box is
already *inside* the icon. The search window also stops at the first row below the bar:
without that bound all four icons measured ran to the window's edge and were thrown away by
the height sanity check (n=39 returned 234 px for a 97 px bar; n=2 217 for 93; n=33 156 for
68; n=35 196 for 82).

`read_source_line` now returns `covered_box` — the bar including its icon, plus the rows the
drawn line came from — and the strip is fitted to that rather than to the band.

## 2. THE EMOJI RULE I NEARLY SHIPPED WAS LUCK

Both `low_block_score` refusals are emoji: n=1 `'ii'` at 0.757 and n=38 `'S'` at 0.696, on
cards whose every real word read at 0.968 and above.

**Three attempts. The first two are wrong and the measurements that killed them matter more
than the one that survived.**

### 2.1 Colour, measured on the box — wrong method, right idea

Burned-in type is white or black; an emoji is a colour glyph. Measured over the whole OCR
box it does not separate, and it fails in **both** directions:

```
clip 23 'When you hate going to'   REAL TEXT   sat p90 255   (busy picture behind)
clip 14 'send bro one last reel'   REAL TEXT   sat p90 255
clip 38 'S'                        THE EMOJI   sat p90   0
```

An OCR box is mostly background, so this measured the scene. I dropped the idea. **That was
the mistake** — see 2.3.

### 2.2 The engine's own score — refuted by the CDN

Second rule: an isolated square block below the block floor. It was fitted on his batch and
worked on those bytes. Re-running the same link the same day, Instagram served a **1920
rendition instead of 1280**:

```
1280 delivery   'iie' 0.691   'ii' 0.672   'ii' 0.757   'iioo' 0.793   'ii' 0.618
1920 delivery   'iie' 0.807   'ii' 0.965   'ii' 0.931   'iio'  0.893   'ii' 0.941
```

Every 1920 score clears the floor. The rule fires on neither the clip nor its own
justification, and **the render drew `ii` as his words** — which is not "an emoji cost him
the clip", it is BL-1128 again. His original refusal was luck: the engine happened to be
unsure at the rendition the CDN happened to serve.

### 2.3 What ships: three signals, none of which moves with the delivery

Binarise the crop first (Otsu, minority class = ink — the operator `_ink_rows` already uses)
and take the saturation of **the glyph pixels only**:

| block | ink sat (median) |
|---|---|
| clip 1 `Doctor Doom Is Planning…` | 0 |
| clip 1 `AbsolutelyNoMercy` | 0 |
| **clip 1 the emoji** | **255** |
| clip 1 all three at the **1920** delivery | 0 / 0 / **255** |
| clip 23 two lines over a bright picture | 0 / 3 |
| clip 14 three lines over footage | 0 / 0 / 1 |

255 against 0–3, and it does not move with the rendition. A mark earns the drop on **any
one** of:

* **coloured ink** ≥ 60 — strongest, rendition-independent, blind to a monochrome emoji;
* **unstable reading** — named `iie`/`ii`/`ii`/`iio`/`ii` across five frames of a card that
  never moves, while every word beside it is byte-identical. This is the module's own
  cross-frame confidence principle applied one level down;
* **never confident** — max score below the floor, which is what catches n=38, whose emoji
  is monochrome and reads `S` steadily at 0.605–0.725.

Bounded by three guards so it can never quietly delete one of his words: **≤ 4 characters**
(the narrowest real block on these cards, `life.`, is 5), **≤ 2.60 aspect**, and **embedded
beside a stable block**, so a short line of his own can never be the target.

**Chosen handling, stated as he asked: we DROP the glyph and re-type the text around it.**
We do not reproduce it. memebot's `test_render` is **green**, not red — `test_emoji_is_
rendered_in_colour` passes and Segoe UI Emoji (11.9 MB) is installed — but that PIL
compositor belongs to the *still-meme* tool. The **reel** path is ffmpeg `drawtext`, single
monochrome font, and `edit.py:2958` refuses outright on a glyph the face cannot draw. Every
drop is named on the batch row and in the per-clip note.

### 2.4 Where it still fails, measured rather than hoped

n=38's emoji is **monochrome**, so colour cannot see it, and at the 1080 px delivery the CDN
served for the shipped render the engine reads it as `S` at **0.839** — above the floor —
and reads it the same on every frame. All three signals miss, and the clip refuses on a
genuine low block score elsewhere. At the 720 px delivery (his batch, and my first re-run)
the same clip recovers.

```
n=38   his batch  rendition 720   -> refused (score rule, pre-BL-1286)
       my run 1   rendition 720   -> RECOVERED, "…my kidney could save a life."
       my run 2   rendition 1080  -> refused, 'S' reads 0.839 and never wavers
```

This is not the region change and not a regression: **it is the CDN**, and refusing is the
correct behaviour — we never draw a wrong line. What would close it is a glyph-shape test
rather than any property of the engine's output, and that is not built.

### 2.5 A regression I caused and caught

Requiring "exactly one shaky place" made **n=38 refuse**. That card has two: the emoji, and
`save a`, which the engine garbled to `e aaes` **on the last frame only**. A garbled word is
already handled better by the vote — that frame simply loses. The count is now how many
places look like a **mark**, not how many are shaky.

## 3. WORD BREAKS ARE NOT A DISAGREEMENT — BUT LETTERS ARE

n=14 `@men`, five frames of a line that never moves:

```
"When I'm genuinely about todie in Ww3 soI send bro one last reel"
"When I'm genuinely about to die in Ww3 soI send bro one last reel"
"When I'm genuinely about todie inWw3soI send bro one last reel"
"When I'm genuinely about to die inWw3 soI send bro one last reel"
"When I'm genuinely about to die in Ww3 soI send bro one last reel"
```

Same sentence five times. The vote scored it **2 of 5** and refused. Squashed — every
whitespace character deleted, case folded — it is **5 of 5**.

**The refusal is not weakened.** n=3 reads `Iit's` on three frames and `Ilt's` on two;
squashing changes nothing because the disagreement is a letter, and **neither reading is his
word** — the card says `It's`. It stays refused. His brief groups it with the spacing cases;
the frames say otherwise.

Two counts are now reported because they are different questions: **`n_agree`** (words) is
what the gate tests, **`frames_agreeing`** (exact spelling) is what the row reports. A report
claiming 5 of 5 on a clip whose frames spelled the spaces three ways would be hiding the
engine's real state.

**Which spelling gets drawn:** the most common exact read inside the winning group — the
original vote, run inside the group. My first rule was "most spaces", on the reasoning that
the engine loses breaks and never invents them. `tests/test_label_text_verbatim.py:269`
refutes it directly: three frames of `the real line` against two of `th e real lin e`. **The
engine splits as well as fuses**, and most-spaces picked the junk.

## 4. n=17 IS NOT A TWO-BAND CASE

His brief asks me to decide n=17 by position, "since a hook sits above the picture and a
caption sits over it". The frames say it is not that shape. It is **two sequential cards**:

* 0.12–0.50 of the clip: `Global Warming Protesters lying on road to stop cars`
* 0.70–0.88: a cut to different footage reading `ME AND BRO:`

Both sit in the same place — a black band above the picture — so position cannot separate
them. It is n=42's class, and refusing is right for n=42's reason.

**It refused with the wrong reason, and that is the real defect.** A news chyron inside the
band read `wfmynews` on two frames and `wfmynews2.com` on a third. `changes_mid_clip` walked
until a read stopped matching the first *exactly*, so the chyron variant became the "second
card", `ME AND BRO:` did not match it, and the function returned **no change** — for a clip
that visibly cuts. It fell through to the vote, split 2–2, and refused as `frames_disagree`:
*"the burned-in line does not change, so disagreement is this engine failing on this clip."*
The right outcome with a message that would have sent somebody hunting an OCR bug that is not
there.

Grouping by `LINE_SAME_RATIO` — the threshold the function already defines and already
documents as separating 0.99 engine noise from 0.35 two-card clips — makes both of its tests
immune to a character. The chyron variants group with their own sentence (0.96) and
`ME AND BRO:` does not (0.10).

**This widens the guard, it does not weaken it.** n=42 fires on exactly the same evidence.

## 5b. TWO DEFECTS FOUND WHILE VERIFYING, NEITHER OF THEM MINE

Both are visible in **his** batch as well as mine, so neither is a regression — and neither
is in this round's declared scope. Named here with their causes rather than fixed, because
fixing either means another 25-minute render and a folder that no longer matches the code
that produced it.

### 5b.1 His line is silently truncated at 120 characters, with an ellipsis

Clip 41 `@nazriddinov.ae`. The source card reads, in three lines:

> *In The Hobbit (2014), the ultimate counter to elf archers was technology the dwarves
> developed specifically to destroy arrows.*

**We drew `In The Hobbit (2014)...`** — his sentence replaced by an ellipsis. That is the
BL-1128 defect class: not our words instead of his, but a fragment plus punctuation he never
wrote.

The cause is exact. `render_one` passes the full line as `--override-text` and *always*
passes `--label-verbatim` (`clip_pipeline.py:3534`), and the verbatim fitter is documented
as never ellipsizing — it raises `LabelWillNotFit`. But **`edit.py:4070-4116` runs
`--override-text` through the CAPTION cleaning chain in `main()`, before the verbatim path
is ever reached**:

```python
MAX_CAPTION_LEN = 120                                    # edit.py:4069
if len(args.override_text) > MAX_CAPTION_LEN:
    _clean  = strip_leading_punctuation(strip_emoji(args.override_text)[0])
    trimmed = caption_hook(_clean, max_chars=MAX_CAPTION_LEN)
    args.override_text = trimmed
```

`clip_label_text`'s own docstring warns that `caption_headline`, `strip_emoji` and
`caption_hook(max_chars=90)` "would silently turn a verbatim line into a different line
while every test still passed", and states that the verbatim path bypasses all of it. **It
does not** — there is a hole at the argv boundary, upstream of the flag.

It fires on exactly the clips over 120 characters and no others, which is why the readback
mismatches split so cleanly:

```
n=28  157 chars  -> "...blows up your house..."
n=41  126 chars  -> "In The Hobbit (2014)..."
n=48  135 chars  -> '...hit you with "how about finding a...'
n=9   105 chars  -> under the cap; its mismatch is readback noise
n=18  114 chars  -> under the cap; likewise
```

The fix belongs in `memebot/scraper/edit.py` — a separate repo with its own remote — and is
to skip that block when `--label-verbatim` is set, letting `LabelWillNotFit` refuse instead.

### 5b.2 One clip's label overflows the strip onto the picture

n=9 `@bixhslayer_`: the last word, *leaves*, is drawn on the video below the white strip.
**1 of 38**, measured by differencing our frame against the original below the strip rather
than by looking for dark pixels — the first attempt counted dark *footage* as ink and
reported 25 of 38, which was noise.

His batch has the same geometry (`label_box` 222/228 against my 220/230), so this is not
mine. The cause is already on record from the strip-geometry map: `label_strip_from_card`
takes optional `src_line=` / `src_type_px=` to reserve room for the type it must hold, the
discovery path passes them (`clip_pipeline.py:4454`), and **the paste path never has** —
so `type_block_height_px` is dead code on every pasted batch. One line in `paste_batch.py`,
and a re-render to prove it.
