## 1. Quality of the uncalled supply endpoints

### What was actually done

BL-1310 measured volume and said so plainly: *"quality is entirely unmeasured and no yield was
claimed."* This round drew fresh accounts from each endpoint, put them through the **free** judging
path, and scored them blind.

| step | detail |
|---|---|
| discovery | 2 calls to `fetch_music_video_list` → **24** accounts not in master |
| discovery | 4 calls to `fetch_user_repost_videos` → **28** accounts not in master |
| judgeable | 1 call each to `fetch_user_post_videos` (`unique_id`) for the page's own videos |
| grids | 46 of 47 cover grids built from the covers already inside those responses, **$0.00** |
| judging | 3 sub-agents, batches of 16/16/14, on the free path, **blind to the endpoint** |

Agent 02's batch was **deliberately mixed** across both endpoints so that no agent could learn a
source from its batch. The split below comes from joining `verdicts.jsonl` back to the `endpoint`
field in each account's pool record, not from which agent judged it.

### The want rates, with their intervals

| endpoint | judged | WANT | rate | Wilson 95% | beats 9%? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| `fetch_music_video_list` | 23 | 2 | **8.7%** | [2.4, 26.8] | cannot tell |
| `fetch_user_repost_videos` | 23 | 3 | **13.0%** | [4.5, 32.1] | cannot tell |
| **pooled** | **46** | **5** | **10.9%** | **[4.7, 23.0]** | cannot tell |

Both intervals contain 9%. **Neither endpoint is shown to beat the baseline, and neither is shown
to fall below it.** With 23 pages an interval this wide was inevitable; the measurement is
reported with it rather than as a point estimate, because a point estimate here would be a
number without its denominator — 2 versus 3 WANTs is one page of noise.

For scale, on the same question: the **shipping TikTok funnel returns 49.5%** (45 of 91, BL-1270).
These endpoints are supply for the *front* of that funnel, not a replacement for it, and at ~10%
they are ordinary front-of-funnel supply.

### Cost per wanted page, and per email

Cost per judgeable page is the discovery share plus the one post-list call:

| endpoint | $/page discovered | $/**WANTED** page | $/**EMAIL** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `fetch_music_video_list` | 0.001083 | **$0.0125** | **≥ $0.13**, no upper bound |
| `fetch_user_repost_videos` | 0.001143 | **$0.0088** | **≥ $0.09**, no upper bound |

These are **vendor dollars only**; the judging that produced the want rate ran on the subscription
and cost $0.00 in cash. A cent per wanted page is cheap in absolute terms — **the constraint on
this funnel has never been vendor cost.**

**The $/email column is a floor, and this is not a formality.** The obvious move is to divide by
BL-1268's 11.1% bio-text email rate, which would give $0.11 and $0.08. **That would be wrong in
the flattering direction**, and BL-1317 — run the same day — is why. Joining his own marks to
profile data, publishing an address **anti-correlates with being wanted**:

| | with an address | n | rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **he says WANT** | **0** | **37** | **0.0%** [0.0, 9.4] |
| he says REJECT | 9 | 63 | 14.3% |

Fisher **p=0.024**. The 11.1% is a *profiled-page* rate, and the pages carrying it are the
promo/brand pages the rubric kills. Applying it to WANT pages is precisely the error BL-1317
names. So the column above divides by **9.4% — the Wilson upper bound on 0-of-37**, the most
optimistic rate his marks permit. **At the point estimate of 0.0%, cost per email is unbounded.**

This does not single out these endpoints; it is a property of the TikTok funnel that both
inherit. But it means **no cost-per-email defence of either endpoint is available from this
data**, and any figure quoted without that caveat is too good.

### The finding inside the rejects

The reposts endpoint mostly surfaces **the wrong side of the transaction**:

| reject cause | n |
|---|---:|
| creator | 26 |
| business | 7 |
| not_english | 5 |
| still_pictures | 2 |

Of the 20 rejects on the reposts endpoint, **10 were `creator`** and agent 03 returned **zero**

It is still useful supply, but the 3 WANTs it produced came via mixed-batch accounts, and the
mechanism argues its true rate is at the low end of [4.5, 32.1] rather than the high.

## 2. The endpoints retired on a single failure

### `/v1/user/following` — the false comment, corrected

`clippershq/meme_finder.py:1982` asserted:

```
#   /v1/user/following                0         (v1 of this path does not exist; v2 does)
```

**The path exists and served 25 users.** The zero was a single failed call read as a property of
the endpoint. It now reads `25  <- IT EXISTS`, followed by a written correction that names the
shape of the error and the rule that catches it.

### `fetch_user_following_list` — private is not dead

Re-probed on 8 accounts:

| result | n | what it means |
|---|---:|---|
| answered with users | **5** | 98 users returned in total |
| HTTP 200, "hiding following list" | **3** | a statement about **the account**, not the path |
| error | 0 | — |

**62.5% available.** This reproduces the 50% carried in the brief within its (very wide) interval,
and — more importantly — **distinguishes the private-account case from a dead endpoint**, which is
exactly what the original 403 retirement failed to do. The `following/by/username` 403 was the
account being private. A 403 is a statement about *this request*; a 200 carrying *"Profile user is
hiding following list"* is a statement about *this account*. **Neither is a statement about the
path.** Availability is a property of the population.

### An overturned standing claim: "FREE BIOS FROM FOLLOWING IS FALSE" is itself false

The brief carried this as settled — schema is `UserShort`, a live probe returned `biography` on 0
of 25. **The probe used the Instagram field name on a TikTok payload.**

The following record has **120 keys**. There is **no `biography` key at all**. The bio is in
**`signature`**, and it is populated:

| | |
|---|---:|
| accounts probed with an available list | 5 |
| users returned | 98 |
| **users with a non-empty `signature`** | **87 (88.8%)** |

A field-name-vs-writer defect, the same family as several already on the books: the reader asked
for a key the writer never writes, got nothing, and the nothing was recorded as a property of the
world. **Free bios from following are real** — 87 of them, for the price of 5 calls.

### The charged-empty trap, avoided

`fetch_user_post_videos` accepts `sec_user_id` **or** `unique_id`. A handle in the `sec_user_id`
slot returns HTTP 200 with *"No more videos"* — **charged and empty**, indistinguishable from a
dead endpoint unless you read the spec. The spec is public and unauthenticated on all three
vendors. `scratch/bl1316_probe.py` reads it into its own docstring, uses `unique_id` for handles
and numeric ids where they are required, and **logs what every call asked for** so an empty answer
can always be told from a wrong question. One `empty_200` was recorded on the reposts endpoint and
is reported as such rather than as an error.

### What was NOT re-proposed

Everything below is refuted **with numbers** and stays refuted. This round proposed none of it:

- per-account music features — 0.419–0.610; "original sound" on 69.9% WANT vs 73.1% REJECT
- sound as a join — 14 of 916 sounds appear on more than one of 100 accounts
- all six engagement ratios — best held-out lower bound 0.259 against a base-rate ceiling of 0.485
- share/play — 0.548 tuning, 0.608 held out on 200 independent marks; its famous 0.818 came from a
  corpus at 14.1% wanted against his 37%

## 3. The approved cleanup, run for real

### The sibling audit ran FIRST, and it had to

`scratch_gc.protected_conflict` tests **equality and containment**. Neither relates **siblings**,
and that has already cost twice: `scratch/resume/` held the only copy of a live run's findings —
810 already-paid leads, 513 with an email — and was not protected at all; a sibling directory
holding hand-marked drop offsets survived on age alone, **eight hours** from removal.
`scratch/bl1316_sibling_audit.py` runs three sweeps and returns exit 2 on any reachable blocker.

| sweep | question | result |
|---|---|---|
| A | derived-name siblings of a protected path, unprotected | **0 reachable** |
| B | REMOVE candidates whose *name* suggests an only copy | 12, each read by hand |
| C | REMOVE candidates carrying `.jsonl` / `.csv` | 33 |

The three riskiest name matches in B were opened and cleared:

- `bl1167_resume_probe` — 2 synthetic files; the real `scratch/resume` **is** protected and is on
  the KEEP list as `protected path: scratch/resume`
- `scratch/config.backups` — 1 stale file; the real `config.backups/` at repo root holds 28,382
  entries and is **outside `scratch/`**, so the sweep cannot reach it
- `bl1091_rt/master_copy.csv` — an older, non-identical test copy, not the master

Sweep C's 33 are the reason this is a **move and not a delete**. `scratch/songs_backups` — the
directory that came eight hours from being lost — appears on the KEEP list by name.

### What moved

```
scratch_gc  measured 2026-08-16 11:36  |  lifetime 36h  |  3 live claim(s) respected
  disk free before: 242.09 GB
  211 directory(ies) qualify, holding 18.41 GB
  manifest written FIRST -> quarantine\MANIFEST_20260816_113854.jsonl
  quarantined 211 directory(ies), 18.41 GB moved (NOT deleted)
  disk free after : 240.62 GB
```

| | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| `scratch/` | 23.41 GB | **4.95 GB** |
| `quarantine/` | 0 | **19.86 GB** |
| C: free | 242.09 GB | 239.97 GB |

**87 directories stayed** — 10 by protection or live claim (`resume`, `songs_backups`, plus 3 live
claims and tracked-file holders like `bl1133_work` at 2.83 GB), the rest by age.

### An honest number: a MOVE frees no disk

Free space did not go up. It went **down** 1.5 GB over the window, from other activity. **A move on
the same volume frees nothing** — quarantine buys **restorability**, not space. The 18.41 GB
returns only when the quarantine is emptied, and **that is a separate act which has not been
approved and was not performed.** The dry run's "17.60 GB would go" was a forecast of what the
sweep would *take*, never a forecast of disk freed; those are different quantities and this round
is the first to say so with both measured.

### `--restore` demonstrated

| step | result |
|---|---|
| restore batch `20260816_113647` | **206 restored, 5 refused** — 4 that had failed to move, plus one that would have overwritten a live path |
| call `--restore` again on the same batch | correctly reported **"MISSING in quarantine"** rather than faking success |
| integrity check | a restored 24,945,043-byte file verified **byte-intact** |
| re-quarantine | 211 dirs, 18.41 GB, batch **`20260816_113854`**, manifest written first |

It **refuses rather than overwrites**, and a second restore of an already-restored batch does not
silently claim success. Both are the behaviours the round was sent to prove.

### The cleanup broke two things, and this round closed both

The sweep was approved and correct, and it still had a consequence nobody costed: it moved 211
directories **out of a protected path and into an unprotected one**.

`scratch/` is in `tests/run_all.py`'s `SKIP_DIRS` **and** in `.gitignore`. `quarantine/` was in
**neither**. The moment the sweep ran:

| | before the sweep | after it |
|---|---:|---:|
| suites the runner discovers | 338 | **682** |
| of those, inside `quarantine/` | 0 | **344** |
| untracked GB in `git status` | 0 | **19.86** |

The suite had **doubled**, and it was now running the test files of retired rounds — one of them,
mb029's `test_band.py`, costs 135.8s by itself. Worse than the wall clock: **a red inside
quarantined code would have blocked a commit**, and stale code that has already been judged
removable would have been deciding whether live work could ship. The first suite run of this round
was killed for exactly this reason (its own tracked task id, never `/IM python.exe`) and re-run
after the fix.

### …and the first fix was not enough, which is the finding

Adding `"quarantine"` to `run_all.py`'s `SKIP_DIRS` fixed discovery. The very next suite run went
**red on `tests/test_atomic_io.py`**.

That file **carries its own copy of the list**. It walks from the repo root auditing write
patterns, its copy had `scratch` and not `quarantine`, and so it began auditing the write
patterns of retired rounds — **code already judged removable, now holding a commit hostage.**

A sweep for every deny-list walker in shipping code found **three copies of essentially the same
set**, sharing a purpose and no definition:

| file | walks from ROOT | had `scratch` | had `quarantine` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tests/run_all.py` | yes | yes | **no** → fixed |
| `tests/test_atomic_io.py` | yes | yes | **no** → fixed (was RED) |
| `tests/test_guard_resolution.py` | yes | yes | **no** → fixed (same exposure) |
| `clippershq/stillness.py` | **no — allow-list** | — | not needed |

`stillness.py` is the one that got it right, and not by remembering: it walks an **allow-list** of
source directories, so a new directory at repo root is invisible to it by construction. **A
deny-list has to be told about every new thing; an allow-list does not.** That is the difference
between the three files that broke and the one that could not.

Both holes closed, in all three copies:

- `tests/run_all.py`, `tests/test_atomic_io.py`, `tests/test_guard_resolution.py` — `"quarantine"`
  added to each `SKIP_DIRS`
- `.gitignore` — `quarantine/` added; `git status` now shows **0** rows for it
- `tests/test_bl1316_quarantine_excluded.py` — **10 checks, all passing**, one of which walks
  shipping code and fails if *any* deny-list walker omits the entry, so the next copy cannot
  repeat this

**The guard is written to be capable of failing.** The obvious test — grep `run_all.py` for the
word — passes on its own comment, which is the *sweep-matches-its-own-prose* defect already
shipped twice here. So it imports `SKIP_DIRS`, calls `discover_suites()`, and asserts on **the
answer**. Reverting the fix in memory takes discovery from 338 to 682 and fails check 3. It also
asserts it discovers *itself*, because a guard excluded from the run proves nothing.

**The general shape, which outlives the fix:** a directory's protections do not follow its
contents. Quarantine is strictly *more* disposable than scratch — everything in it has already
been judged removable — yet it inherited none of scratch's protections, because those protections
were written against a **name**, in **three separate places**, none of which knew about the
others. `outputs_gc.py` writes to the same `quarantine/`, so this was latent from the day the
first gc tool stopped deleting; it only became visible when something was finally moved.

The durable lesson is not "add quarantine to the list". It is that **the same exclusion policy
lives in three hand-maintained copies**, so any change to it is applied N times or not at all, and
the copy you forget announces itself as a red in whatever it happened to be auditing — which is a
terrible way to learn you missed one. The new guard makes the omission fail *as an omission*. The
better fix, not made here because it touches more than this round claimed, is for the three to
import one definition, or to become allow-lists like `stillness.py`.

## What this changes, and what it does not

**Does not change:** the plan. Neither endpoint beat 9%, so neither changes the economics. They
remain what BL-1310 said they were — cheap volume — and cheap volume at the base rate scales the
cost of judging linearly with the cost of supply, which is the wrong lever while judging is the
expensive half.

**Does change, and is free:**

1. **`/v1/user/following` is live** and its 25-user zero is corrected in the file that carried it.
2. **Bios come free from `signature`**, 87 of 98. Any plan that assumed a paid profile call to read
   a bio on a followed account was buying something it already had.
3. **`fetch_user_repost_videos` returns creators, not reposters** — worth knowing before anyone
   builds on the name.
4. **211 directories are recoverable rather than gone**, with a manifest and a working `--restore`.
5. **The suite is back to 338 suites from 682**, and `quarantine/` can no longer turn it red or
   fill `git status` — with a guard that fails if either protection is removed.

**The sample that would settle item 1**, computed rather than guessed (one-sample, 95%
confidence, 80% power, against a fixed 9%):

| if the true rate is | pages needed per endpoint | 46-page equivalent |
|---|---:|---|
| 13% (what reposts showed) | **446** | 56 sub-agents |
| 15% | 207 | 26 sub-agents |
| 20% | 67 | 9 sub-agents |

At 8 pages per agent on the free path, 446 pages is ~56 sub-agents and about $0.50 of vendor spend
for the supply. **It is affordable, and it is probably not worth it**: at 13% the interval only
narrows to [10.2, 16.5], which clears 9% by a hair and still changes nothing. The cheap version is
the third row — **67 pages settles whether either endpoint is at 20%**, and 20% is the rate that
*would* change the plan. That is the test to run if this is revisited.
