## Why BL-840 saw something different

Three separate faults, each independently sufficient to produce its result.

**1. The join was broken, and it voided the corpus.** BL-840 built `clip_id` as `pk + "_" + uid`, but its `pk` held `media["id"]` — which is *already* `"<pk>_<uid>"`. So it asked the library for `<pk>_<uid>_<uid>` and joined 0 of 100. Verified here: the library's own key is `<pk>_<uid>` and `clip_pk` does **not** contain the uid. This round reconstructs no key at all; it uses the library's key verbatim.

**2. The sample was two accounts of list videos.** BL-840's usable 18 clips averaged 15.7 s against a 42.1 s library and returned titles like "Top 10 Tallest Buildings" — data-viz, not film. This round sampled `franchise IS NOT NULL` round-robin across accounts: **73 clips from 32 accounts**, median library duration 40.0 s.

**3. The free tier was the blocker, not cost.** 81 of 100 calls returned 429 against a *daily* 20-request cap, which no backoff can clear. OpenRouter has no daily cap; **0 of 89 calls here were rate-limited.**

## The control — the good part

The question is the posted date, which cannot be recovered from pixels. Any non-null answer is unambiguous fabrication.

**73 of 73 null. Zero fabrication.** Against BL-840's 55.6% [33.7, 75.4] at n=18 on the free tier, and BL-835's 9/9 on the expensive alias.

**The control was salvaged across the FULL sample, not just the clean parses.** 32 of 73 responses hit my 700-token cap and could not be parsed as whole JSON — but `posted_date` is the second field, so it survives truncation and was extracted by regex from all 73. The decline rate therefore carries **no truncation bias**: it is measured on every clip, not on the subset whose output happened to fit.

## Cost correction

**$1.39 per 1,000, not $0.66** — 2.1x the brief's figure. Median clip is ~40 s and video dominates the prompt (4,644 video tokens on a 19 s clip). Still cheap in absolute terms: labelling the whole 617-clip library costs **$0.86**.

`max_tokens` was capped at 700 **before the first call**. BL-840's single 65,524-token runaway ($0.028, 70% of its first pass) did not recur — the cap did its job, and its cost was 32 truncated bodies rather than a budget hole.

## Limits

- **n=73, not 100.** The $0.11 sub-budget for this tier bound it. The control CI is [100.0, 100.0] either way; with 0 failures in 73, the rule-of-three upper bound on the true fabrication rate is **~4.1%**, so "never fabricates" is not proven — only "below ~4%".
- **The capability numbers are biased and the control is not.** Beats/`t_end_s`/on-screen-text come from the 41 untruncated rows, and truncation correlates with beat count, so **mean beats is understated**. The control is regex-salvaged from all 73 and is unbiased.
- **700 tokens is too low for this prompt.** 32 of 73 cheap and 14 of 16 middle responses hit it. A rerun should use ~2,000 and re-measure the capability limits; the decline finding does not need it.
- **Title accuracy is not tested here.** The join is fixed and `caption_lib` was captured on every row, but comparing model titles against captions the model never saw is a separate analysis I did not run.
- **One tier, one day, one provider.** OpenRouter routes to an upstream that can change under a stable model id — the same class of risk that made `gemini-flash-latest` unusable.
