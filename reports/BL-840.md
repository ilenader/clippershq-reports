## 2. The decline result — measured, but NOT comparable to BL-835

On `gemini-2.5-flash-lite`, n = 18:

| | |
|---|---|
| **`posted_date` declined (the control)** | **10/18 = 55.6%**, 95% CI **[33.7%, 75.4%]** |
| **fabricated a date** | **8/18 = 44.4%** |
| most common fabrications | `2023-12-10` ×3, `2023-01-01` ×2, `2023-09-15`, `2023-04` |
| `source_title` declined | 9/18 = 50.0%, CI [29.0%, 71.0%] |

The CI upper bound of 75.4% excludes 100%, so **on this model, on this corpus, the 9/9 result
does not reproduce.** The fabrications are formulaic — round dates, repeated across unrelated
clips, for a fact that is physically absent from the pixels.

### Why I will not call that a refutation

**My sample differs from BL-835's on at least three axes at once**, so a single comparison
cannot attribute the difference to the model:

1. **Different content type.** BL-835's corpus was film/TV footage — Futurama, Modern Family,
   The Dark Knight Rises. Mine is list and data-visualisation video. The nine titles the model
   answered with were *"The Most Famous Movie From Every Country"*, *"FIFA World Cup
   Semi-Finalists"*, *"Top 10 Tallest Buildings on Earth"*. It was not naming a source film; it
   was describing the video's own subject. **A "source title" question means something
   different on that footage.**
2. **Different clip length.** 15.7 s mean against BL-835's 42.1 s.
3. **Different model tier**, which was the intended variable and is now confounded with the
   other two.

The usable 18 span only **2 accounts** — the quota ran out partway through the third — so this
is closer to two account-level observations than eighteen independent ones.

**A bug of mine made it worse.** I built `clip_id` as `pk + "_" + uid`, but the pk already
carries the uid, producing `..._5567603544_5567603544`. **0 of 100 joined to the library**, so
every caption came back empty and the confound check in §3 had nothing to check against. The
accounts themselves are real library accounts (9 of 9 matched); it is the per-clip join that
broke.

## 4. What it cannot do — two of three confirmed, one contradicted

Measured across 100 beats from the 18 usable clips:

| BL-835 claim | this run | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| not second-by-second, mean 5.7 beats/clip | mean **5.6**, **median 1**, range 1–39 | **CONFIRMED, and worse** |
| `t_end_s` unreliable ("several beats came back 0.0") | **73 of 100 beats have `t_end_s == 0.0`** | **CONFIRMED, and quantified** |
| on-screen text present on 94% of beats | **57%** | **CONTRADICTED on this model** |

The mean of 5.6 matching BL-835's 5.7 is coincidence: **the median is 1.** On flash-lite most
clips return a single beat and one outlier returned 39. At 15.7 s mean, per-second granularity
would need ~16 beats. **Beat-level, not per-second, holds — but on the cheap model the typical
output is one beat for the whole clip**, which is not a timeline at all.

I did not re-test verbatim text garbling: with captions unavailable there was nothing to
compare against.

**On complementarity, BL-835's framing survives** and nothing here challenges it: subtitles give
what was *said*, OCR gives what was *written* exactly, and video gives what is *happening*. This
round says nothing about the first two.

## What a real n=100 needs

1. **A paid Gemini tier, or 5 days.** 20/day/model is the hard wall. Alternatively the limit is
   *per model*, so 5 models × 20 would reach 100 — but that confounds model with sample and is
   the opposite of what the brief asked for.
2. **A corpus matched to BL-835's** — film/TV footage with stored captions, not list videos.
   Sample by `franchise IS NOT NULL` rather than by account shuffle.
3. **Fix the `clip_id` join** (`pk` already contains the uid) so the caption confound check
   works.
4. **Cap `maxOutputTokens`** before spending anything.

Estimated real cost at the pinned model: **$0.07 for 100 clips.** Cost was never the obstacle.

## Limits

- **n = 18, 2 accounts, one model.** Every figure in §2 and §4 carries that. The CI is computed
  by Wilson score, which is correct at the boundaries, but a CI does not repair a sample that is
  not representative.
- **The 55.6% vs 100% gap has at least three candidate causes** (model, content type, clip
  length) and this round separates none of them.
- **§3 is void**, not merely weak — the join bug removed its evidence.
- **The alias→3.6-flash inference rests on identical pricing**, not on a statement from the API.
- **The 4× token-rate discrepancy vs BL-835 (300 vs 71.5 per second) is unexplained.** One of
  the two measurements is wrong, or the models tokenise video differently; I did not resolve it.
- **The `gemini-2.5-flash` comparison run returned 0 usable rows** (86× 429, 14× 404) and is
  excluded entirely rather than reported at n=0.

## Method

Read BL-835 first, filed an in-flight claim (`tools/claim.py`, no path conflicts). Enumerated
models from OpenRouter's public list and from the Gemini `ListModels` endpoint on this key —
neither transmits a key to a third party, and no key was printed. Sampled 100 clips across 9
library accounts, fetched `video_dash_manifest` via 10 paid `/gql/user/clips` calls ($0.0060),
and downloaded the lowest-bitrate rendition free from the CDN. Ran the same two questions and
timeline schema BL-835 used, at temperature 0, structured output, against the pinned model.
Decline rates use the Wilson score interval. The 429 diagnosis came from one probe call reading
the `QuotaFailure` detail. **Total $0.0189.** No pipeline file was modified; all artefacts are
under `scratch/bl838_*`.
