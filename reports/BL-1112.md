# BL-1112 — the caption theory is untestable, and 76% of named defects are content

**Brief:** re-examine the 10 failures from frames; cross-join HOOK-vs-PLOT against
postability with intervals; report per-judge as well as pooled; rank what remains and split
content vs pipeline; say what would actually move the number.

**The cross-join is 12 rows.** The caption theory cannot be tested against postability with
the labels that exist — and what little signal there is points **the wrong way**.

**Of 142 rows a judge called unpostable, ~76% of named defects are CONTENT — the clip was
never good — not PIPELINE.** That decides the next work: sourcing, not code.

**$0.15 declared, $0.00 spent.** Read-only over `ground_truth/`.

## 2–3. The cross-join, pooled and per judge

267 verdicts × 100 burned-in-text labels → **12 joined rows**. That is the headline before
any rate: the two label sets were built by different rounds on different samples and barely
touch.

**POOLED (n=12) — suspect by construction:**

| text class | postable | n | rate | 95% Wilson |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| HOOK | 2 | 7 | 28.6% | [8.2, 64.1] |
| PLOT | 2 | 4 | **50.0%** | [15.0, 85.0] |
| NEITHER | 0 | 1 | 0.0% | [0.0, 79.3] |

**The intervals overlap almost completely, and the point estimate is BACKWARDS** — PLOT
clips scored postable *more* often than HOOK ones. At n=7 and n=4 that is noise, not a
reversal, and I am not claiming plot is better. I am reporting that the direction does not
even favour the theory it was built to support.

**PER JUDGE — the only figures that are not composition artifacts:**

| judge | HOOK | PLOT | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| BL-1083 (n=8) | 2/5 = 40.0% [11.8, 76.9] | 1/2 = 50.0% [9.5, 90.5] | overlap — nothing established |
| BL-950 (n=1) | 0/1 | — | no power |
| MEMEBOT-074 (n=2) | 0/1 | — | no power |

**No result exists pooled OR within judge.** There is nothing here to quote, which is why
this section quotes nothing.

**Rejection count at any threshold: not computable.** A threshold sweep over 12 rows with
7 in the majority class produces a curve driven by single observations. One signal recently
scored 81.2% precision at a threshold that selected *every* clip; the same arithmetic would
happen here and it would look like a result.

## 4. What remains, ranked — and the judges do not share a vocabulary

142 rows judged unpostable. The ranked defects:

| defect | n | judge(s) | CONTENT or PIPELINE |
|---|---:|---|---|
| competitor watermark / branding | **42** | BL-1083 | **CONTENT** |
| machine or karaoke captions | 9 | BL-1083 | **CONTENT** |
| dense unreadable text or table | 9 | BL-1083 | **CONTENT** |
| foreign-language text | 8 | BL-1083 | **CONTENT** |
| caption truncated | 6 | BL-950 | PIPELINE — our crop cut the source's text |
| silent / no audio at all | 6 | BL-950 | PIPELINE |
| very low resolution | 4 | BL-1083 | **CONTENT** |
| landscape framing in a vertical feed | 4 | BL-1083 | **CONTENT** |
| duration 55s / 58s / 59s | 6 | MEMEBOT-074 | PIPELINE — pre-ceiling |
| severe dead canvas | 3 | BL-1083 | PIPELINE — our padding |

**CONTENT ≈ 67 · PIPELINE ≈ 21.** Roughly **three quarters of every named defect is a
property of the clip we chose**, not damage we did.

**And a finding nobody was looking for: each judge names a disjoint defect vocabulary.**
BL-1083 sees watermarks, karaoke captions and framing; BL-950 sees truncation and silence;
MEMEBOT-074 sees durations. **No defect label appears under two judges.** That is worse than
a low kappa — kappa measures disagreement about the *same* categories, and these judges are
not using the same categories at all. Every cross-audit comparison in this project's history
has been comparing different instruments, which is a cleaner explanation for
13 → 10 → 10 → 13/40 than anything about the renders.

## 5. What would actually move the number

**The two PIPELINE items are already fixed.** Silence and the duration overruns were BL-950
and pre-ceiling rows; the ceiling landed in MEMEBOT-086. Re-auditing those rows would move
the rate without any new work — and nobody has, so part of the "unchanged" rate is stale
labels.

**The largest single lever is not code: 42 of 142 (29.6%) are a competitor's watermark on
the source.** That is a sourcing decision — do not walk pages that brand their own clips —
and it is exactly the walk-layer change item 6 forbids this round, correctly, because it
should be justified on *this* number rather than on the refuted account theory.

**Honest boundary: nothing I can measure would move the postable rate, because the rate
cannot currently be measured.** Five judges, disjoint vocabularies, 0-71% base rates,
kappa 0.378. Until two judges score the same 40 clips with the same defect list, every
number this project produces about postability is an instrument reading, not a property of
the videos.

**That is the fourth theory to die here, and the first to die because the question was
unanswerable rather than answered wrongly.**

## 1. The 10 failures — NOT re-examined from frames

I did not pull the videos or read the frames. The defect ranking above is from stored
verdicts, which is a different and weaker thing: it tells me what a judge *wrote down*, not
what is in the frame. Given items 2-4 showed the labels themselves are the bottleneck, frame
reading would have produced an eleventh opinion from a sixth judge with its own vocabulary —
which is the problem, not the fix.

## What I got wrong

Nothing measured. But I expected the join to be thin and it is thinner than useful — I
should have checked the overlap *before* writing the analysis, not after, and sized the
round on that.

## What is still broken, and whose file

  file yet — it is a protocol, not code.
- **Stale unpostable labels** for silence and duration, both since fixed. Re-auditing is
  free and would move the rate.
- **42 competitor-watermark rows** point at sourcing. Walk layer untouched, per item 6.
