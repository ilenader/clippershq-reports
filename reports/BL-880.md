## 5. The overlap correction, recorded where it will be read

Written into the docstring of `scratch/bl870_overlap.py` — the tool that produces the number — not only into a report:

> **The 7-of-2,003 figure was a SNAPSHOT OF AN IN-PROGRESS WALK, not structural disjointness.**

| when | vision | speech | both | vs independent |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MEMEBOT-024 (morning) | 20.9% | 49.2% | **7 (0.3%)** | **0.03×** |
| BL-872, before targeting | 52.0% | 49.2% | 458 (22.9%) | 0.894× |
| BL-872, after targeting | 74.9% | 49.2% | 784 (39.1%) | **1.063×** |
| **BL-880, round end** | **78.8%** | 49.2% | **800 (39.9%)** | **1.030×** |

By the time anyone acted on the 30× framing the overlap had already reached 0.894× — indistinguishable from random — **with no coordination at all**. Vision labelling simply ran on and caught up. **The instruction was right; the framing was wrong**, and the prize was much smaller than the number implied.

The general lesson is in the tool now, because it will recur: **an overlap statistic measured against a moving denominator says as much about when you looked as about what is true.** Two label sets that are both still growing read as disjoint long before they read as independent.

This round's own figure drifted the other way — 1.063× → 1.030× — because the pass I ran used the **full** pool, not the speech pool, so it added vision labels to clips with no speech label and diluted the ratio. Targeting is a per-pass choice, not a permanent state.
