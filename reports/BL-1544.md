**Platform:** TikTok hashtag route only. No model, no pictures, no video, no paid profile.

His rule is standing, so it is an artefact. `clippershq/dedup_guard.py` is a single object the
walk cannot get past, and **the account is skipped BEFORE its bio is read** — his exact
instruction, and also the cheap path.

**31,883 accounts — 54.2% of everything touched — were skipped as already held.** Not one
reached the output. The guard was loaded with **92,223 known account keys and 13,758 known
addresses** from master, BL-1541 and BL-1542.

**The address is a different key from the account, and both are checked.** 3 addresses
appeared twice within this run on different accounts; deduplicating on the handle alone would
have shipped them. **Normalisation happens before comparison** — case, trailing dots,
zero-width characters, fullwidth look-alikes — and the test proves that six genuinely
different addresses never collapse into one, because **a wrong drop is exactly as bad as a
wrong keep.**

**Shipped as a test, not a claim:** `tests/test_bl1544_dedup_guard.py`, 18 checks, every one
raising rather than asserting, the last parsing its own source for `ast.Assert` with a planted
control. One check is structural: it parses `walk_tag` and **fails if `skip_account` appears
after `account_row`** — because a check that runs after the parse is a different and
easier-to-delete thing.

**FIX CATEGORY: GENERAL, 2 sites** — one new module, one modified.

## 2. THE LEDGER NOW RECORDS WHICH ENDPOINT

Re-walking these tags is **not** a breach of "never walk a tag twice". That rule exists so a
tag is never re-paid for the same accounts; these were walked on the shallow surface where
three quarters was never reached. So the ledger key is now **tag AND endpoint**: `creededit`
is v1, `creededit@v2` is the deep walk. A v1 tag is not "walked" for v2; a v2 tag is never
walked on v2 again. **v1 keys stay bare**, so all 489 pre-existing entries keep their meaning
— a test fails if that changes, because renaming them would make the whole ledger read as
unwalked and re-pay for every tag ever opened.

## 4. AND YET IT IS MORE EXPENSIVE PER ADDRESS

This is the part that reverses BL-1543's recommendation, and it is worth being blunt about.

| | BL-1542 (v1, FRESH tags) | BL-1544 (v2, RE-WALK) |
|---|---|---|
| $ per 1,000 accounts | **$0.0292** | $0.1041 |
| $ per 1,000 passing addresses | **$2.47** | **$3.70** |
| accounts already held | n/a | **54.2%** |

**Re-walking is 1.5x more expensive per delivered address.** BL-1543 measured v2 as cheaper
*per account* on a fresh walk, and that is still true — but on a re-walk you pay for every
page, and more than half of every page is accounts the guard then throws away. The endpoint is
4–7x deeper; the *re-walk* is what costs money.

**What was never measured, and is the obvious next move: v2 on FRESH tags.** That combines the
7.51x depth with a page that is not half-full of accounts we already own. Nothing in this
round tells you what it costs, and I am not going to compose that price out of a carry rate —
that is how $137.31 and $78.53 were each manufactured.

**THE DECAY, and it is not the well decaying:** 2.13% → **6.27%** → 2.16% → 1.76% → 2.01% →
1.53% → 2.51% by tag position. The spike is tags 14–26, which is where the deep film tags sat.
**What varies is which tags the position happens to contain, not exhaustion.**

## 8. WHAT YOU GOT WRONG

**I shipped a defect that truncated every tag, and caught it only because I watched the first
twelve lines of the run.** The new guard made a page look "dead" when it rejected everything on
it — which conflates *the vendor has no more accounts* with *we already hold these*. Tags that
should run 90–170 pages were stopping at 3. It was the exact opposite of the round's purpose:
a deep re-walk that walked shallower than the thing it replaced. **Saturation must measure the
TAG'S SUPPLY, not what we keep.** Fixed, given its own regression test with a control proving a
genuinely repeating tag still saturates, and the 12 truncated ledger entries were removed
surgically after diffing against the verified backup — 12 keys added, all mine, nothing else
touched. Cost: $0.17 and twelve tags.

**My projection was wrong in the opposite direction from last time.** BL-1542 under-planned by
2.9x; here I assumed the deep endpoint would be cheaper per address because BL-1543 measured it
cheaper per *account*. Those are different denominators and I carried the wrong one across.
Per account it is still good; per delivered address a re-walk is 1.5x worse than fresh tags.

**And eight tags in the run carry `?` as their bucket** — `lakers`, `spiderman`, `dune`,
`topgun`, `gladiator`, `editchelsea` and two more came from BL-1541's vocabulary, which this
round's bucket map does not cover, so they are unlabelled in the per-tag table. I first wrote
"three" here from memory and the data says eight; it does not affect any rate, because bucket
totals are computed only from the labelled ones, but the table has holes in it and I would
rather say so than let you find them.

## 9. SAFETY

Nine files backed up sha256-verified before any spend — config, spend, master, all five seen
stores and the tag ledger — paths from one round constant, body found BY SHAPE. **All five
corruption controls fired on planted damage**, including the index-qualified fingerprint that
sees the deletion of one of a duplicated pair where a natural-key set is provably blind.

**The cap was proved before the first call** by lifting `reserve()`'s real source by AST and
driving it — funded allows and the meter advances, ceiling raises, $0.00 raises, the meter does
not advance on either refusal — **and under 8 threads**, where the locked counter stopped at
exactly 50 of a 50-call cap. The proof wrote nowhere. The unit was passed explicitly at the
named config price of $0.000600, never `Budget`'s Instagram default.

**A preflight ran before the funded walk**: the stop bound at a small target at 4 lanes, the
guard blocked a known account before its row was built, and one live call confirmed the v2
envelope reads **19 items, not the 2 that the `[items, cursor]` mis-count would produce**.

**Nothing latched.** No seen store written — by declaration, by AST and by grep. Of the nine
backed-up files, only the tag ledger changed, which is this round's declared write. **No
Python process was killed. No `--no-verify`.**

**The suite:** `FAILED -- 28 red of 473 suite(s)`, against 25 of 472 before. 473 suites /
10,654 checks against 472 / 10,636: **+1 suite and +18 checks, both mine.**

**THREE SUITES WENT RED DURING THIS ROUND AND NONE OF THEM IS THIS ROUND'S DOING.** By name:
`test_facts_guard`, `test_bl1487_docs_guard`, `test_bl1490_drift_is_visible`. All three have
one root cause: `docs/FACTS.md` stamps its five lifetime spend figures at **n = 32,042 ledger
rows**, and the guard fails when the live ledger runs more than 2,000 rows ahead of that.

The arithmetic, because "not my fault" is worth only as much as its evidence:

| | rows | lag vs the stamp |
|---|---|---|
| FACTS.md stamp | 32,042 | — |
| ledger at my round START | 34,016 | **+1,974 — already at 98.7% of the limit** |

**424 rows arrived during my round and my tool wrote none of them** — `api_client.py` contains

**I intended to flag this and not fix it — and I was wrong, because the pre-commit hook

**It is re-stamped, in its own separate commit**, which is what `tools/facts_guard.py:156`
actually asks for when it says arming the guard and re-stamping "are two decisions that must
not be bundled into one commit". The five figures were **re-derived independently from

total $65.358877, vision $10.082285, ig $45.770568, tiktok $8.807824, tikhub $0.697000, all at
n = 34,440 rows. Only two moved at all — total and vision, by **$0.475260 each, 6.4% of the
$7.40 bound**. The blocking condition was the row lag, not the money.

**With that done the tree is back to `FAILED -- 25 red of 473`, the same 25 by name as before
this round** — so the round ends the tree no redder than it found it, and +1 suite and +18
checks greener.
