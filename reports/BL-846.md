## 1. user_pk — fixed, and the proof is more interesting than expected

`clip_runner.known_user_pks()` builds `{handle: user_pk}` from master; `control.resolve_pk()` consults it before paying. Both handle columns are indexed, first non-empty wins, and a read failure degrades to "resolve the paid way" rather than stopping a walk.

**158 of 187 confirmed pages carry a `user_pk` (84.5%)** → a full walk goes **2.00 → 1.16** paid calls per account, saving **158 calls ($0.0948)**.

The stale header is corrected in place — it claimed *"Master carries no `user_pk` column (60 columns, checked)"*, which was true when written and false now.

### The real walk saved nothing, and that is the finding

I ran a real 12-clip walk to prove it. Result: **26 accounts, 52 paid calls — exactly 2.00.** The fix appeared not to work.

It works. The saving is **order-dependent**, and the walk order defeats it:

```
pk coverage BY POSITION in walk order
   accounts   0- 24 :  0/25   .........................
   accounts  25- 49 : 21/25   ....#####################
   accounts  50- 74 : 25/25   #########################
   accounts  75-186 : 100%    #########################
```

**Accounts 0–28 carry zero `user_pk`** — the old repost_finder rows, which BL-751 found populate only 8–11 of 62 columns. Everything from 29 on carries 100%. A 12-clip walk stops inside that prefix, so 2.00 calls/account was **correct behaviour for those accounts**, not a failed fix.

Proven separately, offline and free:

```
ashoraif                 -> 38606524336     paid_calls_so_far=0
shadowmovies.1           -> 13264963167     paid_calls_so_far=0
nontonlayar.id           -> 11493881906     paid_calls_so_far=0
definitely_not_in_master -> PAID_...        paid_calls_so_far=1
```

**Consequence worth acting on:** short walks save nothing until those 29 rows are backfilled or reordered. BL-842's measured 30% saving came from a run that walked deeper. I did not run a second, deeper walk — reaching the pk-populated region means re-walking the 29-account prefix first, ~$0.035, which exceeds the remaining budget. Stopping was the right call over a nicer number.

## 2. Two counters that cannot move now say so

`clip_walk.UNWIRED_STAGES` declares both, with reasons. The report renders:

```
    Stages entered (cheapest first):
      views          12
      content_genre  12
      valence        12
      layout         12
      speech_frac    UNWIRED  (no speech_fn is ever supplied (needs audio + a VAD pass, ~1.6 s/clip))
      ocr            UNWIRED  (no counter site and no parameter; ocr_features() only scores layout)
```

That is from the **real walk**, not a fixture.

**AST confirmed they are unwired for different reasons**, and the distinction matters to whoever wires them:

| stage | counter sites | why it cannot move |
|---|---|---|
| `ocr` | **0** anywhere in the tree | no counter site and no parameter at all |
| `speech_frac` | 2 (`clip_runner.py:427/430`) | they sit behind `if speech_fn is not None`, and the **only** site passing `speech_fn` forwards `run()`'s own `None` default |

I chose to label rather than wire: `speech_frac` needs audio plus a VAD pass at ~1.6 s/clip and `ocr` needs frames. Both are features, not defect fixes, and half-wiring one would have produced a counter that moves without meaning anything — the same vacuous shape.

**Five new tests keep the declaration honest.** They AST-check it against the tree: if `ocr` gains a counter site, or anything supplies a real `speech_fn`, the test demands the stage be removed from `UNWIRED_STAGES` — and vice versa. A declaration nothing checks is the thing that rots.

One appended entry, nothing rewritten:

```
label   CORRECTION_bl842_double_count_242_calls_reversed

note    <cause, arithmetic, and why it was written this way>

total_spent_usd  $4.5700 -> $4.4248
sum(runs) $4.4248  ==  total_spent_usd $4.4248   delta $0.000000
```

**A finding that outlives this fix: `main.record_aux_spend()` clamps negatives to zero.** Tested on a copy — passing `ig_calls=-242` wrote `calls: 0, dollars: 0.0`. So the ledger API is structurally incapable of expressing a correction, and **that is why BL-830's `CORRECTION_killed4_was_not_killed_double_count` entry reads $0.0000**: it was a documentary marker, and the total was never actually corrected. This entry was written directly under `filelock` on the data file instead, and refuses to run twice.

## 5. The double-count fix is live at every site

Six `IncrementalMeter` sites, all carrying `sync()` + `flush(force=True)`. The sweep flagged two with a `record_aux_spend` after the meter; **both are legitimate**:

- **`control.py:1574`** records **link calls** — a different cost channel (`link_cost_per_call`), and the only place link spend is recorded. Not a duplicate.

**Independently corroborated by this round's own walk**, which metered `26 + 26` incrementally with no final full-total record.

So BL-842's explanation holds: the double-charge came from a process that imported `repost_finder.py` at 11:48, before the fix landed at 12:50, and ran for 2h37m on a stale module. No site is still double-charging.

## Honest limits

- **The user_pk saving is projected, not measured end-to-end.** The unit proof is exact and the coverage count is exact; what I could not afford was a walk deep enough to observe the reduction on live accounts. The one walk I ran hit only pk-less accounts and correctly showed no change.
- **`known_user_pks` trusts master's `user_pk`.** If a stored pk is stale or wrong, the walk now uses it instead of re-resolving — trading a call for a correctness risk. No stale-pk case was observed, but none was tested either.
- **`estimate()` still quotes the worst case (2 calls/account)**, deliberately: the pk map is only known after master is read, and over-quoting the bill is the safe direction. The Proceed gate will therefore over-state cost on deep walks.
- **The UNWIRED tests check declaration-vs-tree, not behaviour.** They cannot tell whether a wired `speech_fn` computes anything useful.
- **Tag retirement rests on BL-842's single measurement pass.** Four tags produced zero pages in one run of the full bank; that is a defensible demotion, not proof they can never work.
- A concurrent round had already tagged `# BL-843` inside `control.py` before I filed my claim under that id; this published as BL-846 after the ceiling moved.
