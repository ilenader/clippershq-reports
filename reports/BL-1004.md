## 1. The class, defined from the evidence

BL-988 named it and measured no signal: *"half-white / dead canvas (2 of 29)"* and *"unreadable source footage (up to 4 of 29)"*, with the note that **no term exists for canvas**.

I hand-labelled **50 clips drawn at random** (seed 1004) from the 198 library clips with local media — random, not picked for failures, so the base rate below is a real base rate rather than an artefact of sampling.

| label | rule | n |
|---|---|---|
| **DEAD_CANVAS** | moving picture occupies < ~35% of frame height, rest flat static border | **3** |
| **UNREADABLE** | picture present but unreadable — underexposed, motion-blurred, compression mush | **3** |
| **CLEAN** | everything else, *including* static infographics (a different defect, already `REASON_STATIC`) | **44** |

**Base rate of the defect class: 12%.** Labels and per-clip notes: `ground_truth/bl1004_dead_canvas.json`. They were read off cover frames by eye at 420px, and **no detector was consulted to produce them** — a detector validated against another detector is the circularity that produced the 4.15× this project had to retract.

## 2. Every candidate, measured

Scored against those labels only. Wilson 95% intervals throughout, because with 3 positives per class a bare point estimate would be the kind of number that gets retracted.

**Dead canvas** (target = 3 hand-labelled):

|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| `band_h < 0.35` (edge-based) | 17 | 2 | 15 | 0.12 [0.03–0.34] | 0.67 |
| `band_area < 0.30` | 19 | 2 | 17 | 0.11 [0.03–0.31] | 0.67 |
| `band_area < 0.40` | 31 | 3 | 28 | 0.10 [0.03–0.25] | 1.00 |
| `flat_frac > 0.45` (flat-row fraction) | 13 | 3 | 10 | **0.23 [0.08–0.50]** | 1.00 |
| `run_frac < 0.35` (largest live run) | 38 | 3 | 35 | 0.08 [0.03–0.21] | 1.00 |

**Unreadable** (target = 3 hand-labelled):

| signal | flagged | TP | FP | precision | recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| `detail < 4.0` (mush) | 8 | 0 | 8 | 0.00 [0.00–0.32] | 0.00 |

## 3. Why it fails — the distributions, not the tuning

| | clean clips | labelled positives |
|---|---|---|
| `flat_frac` | median 0.39, p90 **0.55** | **0.51 – 0.62** |
| `band_h` | min 0.02, p10 0.17, median 0.53 | 0.05 – 0.51 |
| `detail` | min 2.77, median 8.02 | 5.7 – 24.6 |

The distributions overlap almost completely. **A large flat caption slab is the norm in a meme library, not the defect** — most clips in this corpus are a caption bar over a picture band, and the ones a human rejects are not the ones with the most border.

The positives disagree with each other, which is the clearest evidence the axis is wrong:

- `c08` **DEAD_CANVAS** has a 0.51 edge-band — *large*.
- `c29` **UNREADABLE** has 0.05 — *tiny*.
- `c12` **DEAD_CANVAS** has the highest detail score in the positive set (24.6), because a wall of caption text is full of edges.

What separates a bad clip from a good one here is **whether the small picture is worth watching**. That is a judgement about content, and none of these signals is even pointed at it.

## 4. Pool cost — what landing it anyway would have cost

Existing gate on the frozen 2,661-clip snapshot:

| | clips | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| **postable** | **2,232** | **83.9%** |
| `non_english_caption` | 289 | 10.9% |
| `third_party_watermark` | 101 | 3.8% |
| `static_non_clip` | 57 | 2.1% |

Adding the best candidate (`flat_frac > 0.45`, precision 0.23):

- flags **26% of the library — ~692 of 2,661**
- to remove an estimated **~160** genuinely dead clips (6% base rate for that class)
- i.e. **discards ~532 clean clips to catch ~160 bad ones**
- postable pool **2,232 → ~1,652**, **83.9% → 62.1%**

A gate that quietly removes a fifth of the supply at 0.23 precision is worse than the defect it targets. **So no term ships.**

What ships instead is the refusal, as data: `dead_canvas_unreadable` is now in `clip_postable.NOT_GATED` carrying these numbers, and the existing test that pins the ungated set covers it — a future round proposing this gate has to confront the measurement before it can add one.

## 6. What I got wrong

My first picture-band instrument thresholded row detail at **15% of each frame's own maximum gradient**. A hard caption edge — white text on a black slab — sets that maximum very high, which suppresses the genuine picture rows underneath it. It scored clean clips as low as `band_h = 0.02` and produced the scattered positives in §3.

I caught it because the positives made no sense against the labels, replaced it with two better-specified variants (an absolute detail floor, and a flat-row fraction), and re-scored before drawing any conclusion. The replacement scored better — 0.23 against 0.12 — and still failed, which is why the conclusion survives the instrument fault rather than resting on it.

**Multiple comparisons, stated:** I tested nineteen thresholds across seven signal families. At that count some threshold passing by chance would be unremarkable; none came close, so the risk did not materialise. Had one scraped past 0.80 I would not have trusted it on 3 positives.
