## 1. The music really does stop, on every clip, and here is the proof

Decoded sample count against declared duration, on his exact files:

| clip | container SAYS | AAC frames | frames x 1024 / 44100 | actually decodes | **audio present for** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **21_wasted** | 13.948 s | 226 | 5.25 s | **5.22 s** | **37% of the clip** |
| **19_memeflickofficial** | 9.760 s | 261 | 6.06 s | **6.04 s** | **62%** |
| 09_clips | 15.565 s | 511 | 11.87 s | **11.84 s** | **76%** |

`nb_frames x 1024 / 44100` predicts the decoded length **to within 0.03 s on all three**. So
this is not a decoder problem and not a measurement artefact: **the file genuinely contains
only that many audio frames**, while its stream header declares a duration two to three times
longer. ffmpeg decodes them and stops, with **no warning on stderr**.

That is exactly what he described — *"halfway it just cuts and nothing"* — and it is on the
two clips he named.

### I got this wrong once in the course of this round, and the way I got it wrong matters

My first pass compared the audio stream's **declared** duration to the video's and reported
**"0 of 17 clips truncated, a/v ratio 0.998 to 1.002"**. That is what the container says, and
the container is wrong. Anyone checking this with `ffprobe -show_entries stream=duration`
will get a clean bill of health on files that are audibly broken. **Count the frames.**

### The prime suspect I was handed is NOT the cause

The brief pointed at the `atrim` that a recent round added to fix the ENOSPC, on the grounds
that `atrim` truncates by design. I read it rather than assuming:

```python
amb_out_duration = max(0.0, (src_dur_for_fade - tx_seek - tx_end_trim) / speed_for_fade)
...
_bed_end = amb_out_duration
fade_chain += f",atrim=end={_bed_end + 0.25:.3f}"
```

`amb_out_duration` is the **source duration minus the trims** — that is the full output
length, not the hook window. For `21_wasted` it computes to about 13.95 s and the bed is
trimmed at 14.20 s. **It cannot be what stops the audio at 5.22 s.** The ENOSPC fix is
exonerated, and removing it would reintroduce that failure for nothing.

**What I did not get to:** naming the real mechanism. The remaining candidates, in the order
I would take them, are the `aloop` that is supposed to fill the clip from a shorter hook
window (his marked windows run 9.24 to 26.93 s against clips of 8 to 20 s, so a short window
on a long clip is the normal case, and 5.25 s is not obviously any of those numbers), and the
interaction between `-shortest` and a bed that stops early. Whose: mine, unfinished.

## 2. The trim is NOT cutting too much. It is cutting 0.00 to 0.10 seconds

Measured as original duration minus ours, per clip, on the same batch:

| clip | source | ours | removed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 01, 08, 10, 18, 19 | — | — | **0.00 s** |
| 09, 12, 20 | — | — | 0.02 s |
| 05, 13, 21 | — | — | 0.04 s |
| 06, 11 | — | — | 0.05 s |
| 17, 22 | — | — | **0.10 s** |
| **15__.actias** | **40.50 s** | **19.97 s** | **20.55 s** |

**Sixteen of seventeen clips lose between 0.00 and 0.10 seconds at both ends combined** —
far inside his rule of 0.5 s on a long clip and 0.2 s on a short one. There is nothing to
clamp: the trim is already an order of magnitude tighter than his limit.

**The seventeenth is the 20.0 s ceiling, not a trim.** `15__.actias` is a 40.5 s source cut to
19.97 s. That is the ceiling doing exactly what it is documented to do, and it is the one
place a large number of seconds legitimately disappears. Distinguishing them was the right
thing to ask for, and the answer is that they never compound in this batch: every clip is
either ceiling-cut or trim-cut, never both.

**So what he is seeing as "the trim is too aggressive" is very probably the music defect
above.** A clip whose music stops at 37% sounds truncated even though every frame of his
video is present. I would not change the trim on this evidence.
