## 2. The flakiness: the timeout was ruled OUT, not raised

You asked me to determine whether the lock test races something or has a timeout that is too tight. **Neither.** Measured, with a probe reproducing `test_filelock`'s exact shape under eight competing load processes:

| configuration | worst single lock wait | margin against the 120 s timeout |
|---|---:|---:|
| procs=4, n=25 — **this suite's shape** | 0.944 s | **127×** |
| procs=8, n=25 — 2× | 5.238 s | 23× |
| procs=16, n=15 — **4×** | **7.461 s** | **16×** |

Zero failed workers and zero lost rows in every configuration. **At four times the contention this suite generates, the worst wait uses 6.2% of the timeout.** Raising it would be cargo-cult, and it would make a genuine hang take twice as long to surface. It stays at 120 s, with the measurement recorded beside it in the code.

**And I could not reproduce the failure — 11 attempts, 0 failures:**

- 3 runs alone: 21–24 s, all pass
- 4 runs under 8 competing load processes: 44–49 s, all pass
- 4 runs with `run_all`'s exact sandbox env (`CLIPPERSHQ_SPEND_FILE`, `_STATUS_DIR`, `_LOG_DIR`): 30–36 s, all pass

Both the load hypothesis and the sandbox-environment hypothesis are **refuted**.

### So I fixed the thing that was actually broken: it could not tell you why

`_spawn()` discarded every worker's stdout and stderr. A failure produced exactly this:

```
workers exited [1, 0, 0, 0]
```

and not one word about what happened inside the process that returned 1. **The traceback existed and was thrown away.** That is precisely how an intermittent failure gets re-labelled "flaky" and then ignored — and BL-871 measured what that costs.

Workers now run with `stdout`/`stderr` captured, an explicit 600 s `communicate()` timeout that says so when it fires, and a single `_assert_workers_ok()` helper so all four proofs report the child's own traceback. **Verified**: a deliberately-failed worker's traceback now reaches the assertion message, where previously only `[1, 1]` would have.

I am not claiming to have fixed the flakiness. I am claiming the timeout is not it, that I could not reproduce it in 11 tries, and that the next occurrence will name its own cause instead of costing someone another day.
