## 2. MEASURING FIRST KILLED TWO OF MY OWN THREE THRESHOLDS

I intended to gate on dead canvas ≥35%, duration >45 s and silence. Measuring all **243**
labelled clips first refuted two of the three:

| axis | postable median | non-postable median | AUC (pooled) | AUC (prior judges only) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **dead canvas %** | **46.5** | 37.0 | **0.641** | **0.404** |
| duration s | 51.6 | 37.0 | 0.549 | 0.345 |
| silent | 13% silent | 21% silent | — | same direction |

**Pooled, MORE dead canvas predicts MORE postable** — the opposite of every judge's stated
intent — because hook-card-over-inset *is* the format, and the 0%-border clips are full-bleed
reposts carrying watermarks. **And the direction flips between judge groups** on a
mechanically measured physical quantity no judge influenced. Corpus median is **44.6%**, so my
35% cut would have failed **157 of 243** clips.

The two clips actually failed *for* dead canvas measure **50.6%** and **54.1%** — BL-950 and
MEMEBOT-074 were consistent with each other above 50%; BL-1083 simply never applied the axis.
**No cut reproduces the record.** Dead canvas and duration are therefore **recorded, not
gated**. Only `silent` gates, and it gates because it is a defect on its face, not because its
numbers are strong.

## 4. κ BEFORE AND AFTER — the rubric made it worse

| measurement | pairs | raw agreement | **κ** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| BEFORE — v1 holistic, published | 23 | 69.6% | **0.378** |
| BEFORE — v1 on these clips | 7 | 85.7% | 0.720 *(n=7, unstable)* |
| **AFTER — v2 rubric** | **31** | **64.5%** | **0.110** |
| **intra-rater — my v1 vs my v2** | 16 | **93.8%** | **0.871** |

**The rubric did not move κ meaningfully above 0.378. It moved it to 0.110, and I am
reporting that rather than reframing it.**

### But κ is also the wrong statistic here, and that matters

Raw agreement fell only **69.6% → 64.5%** while κ fell **0.378 → 0.110**. That gap is the
**kappa paradox**: κ divides out expected agreement, and when both raters say `no` most of the
time, expected agreement is high and κ collapses even though the raters mostly agree. My v2
rate is 30% postable and the prior judges' is ~20% — both heavily skewed.

**So "κ = 0.378" was never a stable description of this dataset's reliability either.** Any
future comparison must report raw agreement **and** the marginals alongside κ, or it will
measure the skew rather than the standard.

### The one thing that clearly did work

**Intra-rater κ = 0.871 / 93.8% agreement.** Applying a written rubric to clips I had already
judged holistically moved my own verdicts very little. So the instability is **not** one judge
being noisy — it is **judges differing from each other**, which is the harder problem and the
one a rubric with a single author cannot solve.

## 5. PER-CRITERION AGREEMENT — item 2's hypothesis is NOT supported

The claim was that per-criterion marks are more reproducible than a binary summary. Against
prior judges' named defects:

| criterion | pairs | agreement | κ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `foreign_text` | 31 | 90.3% | 0.350 |
| `no_hook` | 31 | 83.9% | 0.383 |
| `third_party_watermark` | 31 | 64.5% | **0.174** |

**None is clearly better than the summary's 0.378.** `no_hook` matches it, `foreign_text` is
slightly below, watermark is half.

**A real limit on this test:** prior rows carry **free text**, not criterion marks, so a judge
who saw a watermark but wrote *"caption truncated"* scores as a disagreement. These κ are a
**lower bound**. The honest reading is that this test cannot yet confirm or refute item 2 —
it needs a second judge applying v2, which is exactly what does not exist.

## 6. FIELDS PER CRITERION — and here the brief's hypothesis DOES hold

| target | best stored field | AUC | distance from chance |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `no_hook` | **`text_bands`** | **0.184** | **0.316** |
| `no_hook` | `duration_s` | 0.804 | 0.304 |
| `third_party_watermark` | `speech_frac` | 0.283 | 0.217 |
| `silent` | `speech_frac` | 0.287 | 0.213 |
| **`postable` (summary)** | `speech_dbfs` | 0.361 | **0.139** |

**A stored field predicts a criterion more than twice as well as it predicts the summary
verdict.** `text_bands` — a field that literally counts text bands — predicts *"this clip has
no burned-in hook"* at 0.316 from chance, where the best predictor of `postable` manages
0.139. This is the useful half of the round: **the summary verdict is the thing that cannot be
predicted, and criteria can be.** n is small (7 positives for `no_hook`), so this is a
direction to pursue, not a result to rely on.

## WHAT I GOT WRONG

- **My first sheets showed the prior verdict next to every clip** — I would have measured
  agreement while looking at the answer. Caught and rebuilt blind before judging.
- **I chose three thresholds from the disagreement text before measuring**, and two of the
  three were refuted by the measurement (dead canvas, duration). Had I not measured first,
  the rubric would have shipped a 35% dead-canvas gate that fails 157 of 243 clips.

## STILL OPEN

- **The rubric has one author and one user.** κ between two *rubric-users* is the test that
  matters and it is unmeasured. Everything here compares a rubric-user to non-rubric-users.
- **κ is unstable at these marginals** — future reports must publish raw agreement and the
  marginals with it.
- **Per-criterion reproducibility is untested**, not refuted: prior rows carry free text, so
  §5's numbers are lower bounds.
- **`text_bands` → `no_hook`** is worth a proper run at full n.
