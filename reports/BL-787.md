# BL-787 — YES, the pixels are obtainable. A complete video URL set arrives **free on 100% of clips** in the payload the walk already fetches, and is discarded. I downloaded one and played it.

**Date:** 2026-07-31 · **Type:** Investigation + live probe, READ-ONLY · **Spend:** **$0.0018** (3 paid calls, budget $0.02)

Repo opened read-only. All artifacts in the session scratchpad. Nothing written to the repo.

## Answer

**The pixels are obtainable, for free, today.** Not via `video_versions` — that field is **absent on 12/12 clips** from the endpoint the walk uses. The route is **`video_dash_manifest`**, present on **12 of 12 (100%)**, carrying **7 video renditions and 4 audio renditions per clip**.

Proof — I downloaded the lowest rendition and probed it:

```
DOWNLOADED 1.31 MB in 0.7s (1.9 MB/s)
magic bytes: b'\x00\x00\x00(ftypmp41'     is ISO-MP4: True
ffprobe:     stream,vp9,720,1280,57.066667
```

A real, complete, 57.07-second 720×1280 VP9 MP4. **BL-784's blocker is not a blocker.** The clip handoff is not architecturally dead — it is one unparsed field away from working.

## 1. What is actually in the payload

One paid call to `/gql/user/clips` (the walk's own endpoint) for `clipcapture.tv`:

| field | present | what it gives |
|---|---:|---|
| **`video_dash_manifest`** | **12/12 (100%)** | **7× video/mp4 + 4× audio/mp4 BaseURLs** |
| `image_versions2` | 12/12 (100%) | thumbnails / first frame |
| `video_codec` | 12/12 | `vp09.00.31.08.00.01.01.01.00` |
| `video_subtitles_uri` | 12/12 | a **`.srt` subtitle file** — free, unread |
| `video_versions` | **0/12 (0%)** | — |
| `progressive_download_url` | **0/12 (0%)** | — |

**Correction to the brief's premise.** BL-682's `video_versions[0].url` is **not** what this endpoint returns. `video_versions` is absent entirely. It presumably exists on `/v1/user/clips/chunk` or `/v2/user/medias` (BL-722 built its corpus from one of those), but the endpoint `clip_walk` actually calls does not carry it. The working route on *this* endpoint is the DASH manifest, and it is strictly richer — a bitrate ladder rather than one URL.

The ladder, per clip, all at **720×1280**:

```
183 kbps · 255 · 348 · 516 · 703 · 936 · 1220 kbps   (7 video)
+ 4 audio renditions
```

**`clip_walk.py`, `clip_library.py` and `clip_runner.py` contain zero references to any of it** — `video_versions`, `video_dash_manifest`, `progressive_download_url`, `image_versions2`. The media arrives in a response already paid for and is dropped on the floor. That is the whole answer to item 1.

**One trap worth recording.** The manifest is XML, so its BaseURLs are **HTML-escaped** — `&amp;oe=` not `&oe=`. Fetching one verbatim gives **HTTP 403**, and the expiry parameter parses as `amp;oe` rather than `oe`. `html.unescape()` on the BaseURL fixes both. This cost me two failed attempts and would silently defeat anyone wiring it.

## 2. How long do the URLs live? **~105 hours, not 33**

Decoded from the `oe` parameter (hex unix seconds), 6 clips, video and audio separately:

| | median life remaining |
|---|---:|
| **video** | **105.2 h** (n=6, range 104.1–107.8) |
| **audio** | **105.5 h** (n=6, range 104.9–106.4) |

**This corrects BL-690.** That round measured ~33 h for video and ~104 h for audio. The audio figure reproduces almost exactly. **The video figure does not — video and audio now expire together at ~105 h**, and video's life is over 3× longer than recorded.

**The architectural consequence in the brief is therefore softened, not eliminated.** A stored URL is dead in **~4.4 days**, not ~1.4. It is still far too short to be a library asset — a catalogue built in July is entirely dead by August — but it does mean the download need not happen in the same *run*, only within the same week. The safe design remains: **store the identity (account id + clip pk), never the URL.**

## 3. What storing the video costs

Real byte sizes, measured by free CDN Range requests on 8 clips:

| rendition | median per clip | 1,000 clips |
|---|---:|---:|
| **best (1220 kbps) + audio** | **8.48 MB** | **8.5 GB** |
| **lowest (183 kbps) + audio** | **2.07 MB** | **2.1 GB** |
| audio only (BL-717 baseline) | 0.87 MB | 0.87 GB |

BL-717's 478 KB audio figure is now **0.87 MB** on this sample — roughly double, but same order.

**Disk on this machine: 95 GB free of 931 GB (90% used).**

| store | GB | share of free space | download time at 1.9 MB/s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,000 clips, best quality | 8.5 | **9%** | ~75 min serial |
| 1,000 clips, lowest quality | 2.1 | 2% | ~18 min serial |
| 10,000 clips, best quality | 85 | **89% — would fill the disk** | ~12.5 h |

**1,000 clips is comfortably feasible. 10,000 is not**, at best quality, on this machine as it stands. Note every rendition is the same 720×1280 resolution — the ladder is bitrate only — so the 183 kbps tier is not a smaller picture, just a more compressed one. For an editing bot that matters: the cheap tier is 4× smaller at the same frame size.

## 4. Store the file, or re-fetch at edit time?

I tested whether the clips still exist. Two paid calls against accounts already in the library, matching stored `clip_id`s against what the account returns today:

| account | clips stored | still returned | stored since |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| clipcapture.tv | 12 | **11** | 2026-01 → 07 |
| culturemeetsonline | 6 | **6** | 2026-01 → 07 |
| **total** | **18** | **17 (94%)** | |

**94% survival after one to six months.** (The one miss may simply have dropped off page 1 — the endpoint is view-ranked, not a full archive — so 94% is a floor, not a deletion rate.)

**Re-fetch at edit time is both cheaper and, on this evidence, safe enough:**

| | store now | re-fetch at edit time |
|---|---|---|
| storage | 8.5 GB per 1,000 | **0** |
| API cost | $0.0006 per account-page (~12 clips) — **already paid during discovery** | $0.0006 per account-page again |
| per-clip marginal cost | $0 (rides the walk) | **~$0.00005** |
| risk | none once downloaded | **6% of clips unavailable**, plus any account going private |
| latency at edit time | zero | 1 API call + ~4s download |

**Recommendation: re-fetch at edit time, and store the manifest-refetch identity, not the URL.** The 94% survival and the trivial re-fetch cost do not justify 8.5 GB and a 90%-full disk. Store `account_user_id` + `clip_pk` — the library already has both, encoded in `clip_id` as `<pk>_<user_id>`, which is how I resolved every account in this probe.

**The exception that would flip this:** if a specific clip is *chosen* for an edit, download it then and keep it. Selection is rare; discovery is bulk. Pay storage only for the clips that win.

## 5. Is it a catalogue of things you cannot use?

**No.** The library is a catalogue of things you can retrieve on demand, and cheaply. The three conditions that would have made it worthless are all false:

- the media URL is **not** absent — it is on 100% of clips
- the clips are **not** gone — 94% survive months later
- the retrieval is **not** expensive — $0.00005 per clip, ~4 s, no new API surface

What remains true from BL-784 is that **none of this is wired**. The manifest is parsed by nothing, `clip_runner.py` is untracked, and the library holds 42 clips in scratch folders. The blocker was never the CDN. It is that a field arriving free on every clip has never been read.

**Also free and also unread:** `video_subtitles_uri` — a `.srt` on 12/12 clips. That is the caption track, without OCR and without Whisper, for a system whose OCR stage costs 16 s/clip.

## Limits

- **12 clips, one account, one page.** The 100% manifest presence, 7-rendition ladder and 720×1280 uniformity are from that sample. `clipcapture.tv` is the most-templated account in the library (BL-784), so it may not be typical.
- **Expiry is n=6 clips from one fetch.** The ~105 h video figure contradicts BL-690's ~33 h; I have one sample and BL-690 had its own. Both could be right at different times — CDN policy is not ours to control — so treat ~105 h as *current*, not permanent, and design for the shorter figure.
- **Survival is n=18 across 2 accounts.** The endpoint is view-ranked page 1, so "missing" conflates deleted with demoted. 94% is a lower bound on survival, not a measured deletion rate.
- **Sizes are Content-Range totals**, not downloaded bytes, except the one clip I actually pulled (1.31 MB, matching its 1.31 MB reported total exactly).
- **One download, one ffprobe.** Playability is confirmed for a single 183 kbps rendition. Higher tiers were size-checked, not decoded.
- **Download throughput (1.9 MB/s)** is one measurement on one connection; the 75-minute serial estimate scales that naively and ignores parallelism, which would improve it.

## Method

Read `clip_walk.py` / `clip_library.py` / `clip_runner.py` for any media-URL handling (none). One paid `/gql/user/clips` call for `clipcapture.tv` via the shipping `ig_client.IgClient`, parsed with the shipping `clip_walk.iter_stream_medias`, and the full 182-key media dict inventoried. DASH manifests parsed with a regex over `<Representation>` blocks, BaseURLs `html.unescape`d. Expiry decoded from the `oe` query parameter as hex unix seconds. Sizes obtained by `Range: bytes=0-0` requests reading `Content-Range` totals — free, no API cost. One full download to `proof.mp4`, verified by `ftyp` magic bytes and `ffprobe`. Survival tested with two further paid calls matching stored `clip_id` prefixes against live page-1 results. **3 paid calls, $0.0018.** No credential was printed in this report.
