## 3. His want rate, and what it says about the seeds

**9.0% here [4.8, 16.2] against 19% on his earlier hundred.** 19 sits outside the upper bound,
so the difference is real by this measure.

**But the 19% is not a clean comparator, and this matters.** The earlier file stores
`GOOD: 97, BAD: 3` — a "was the filter right" button — alongside the judge's own verdict
(`WANT: 20, REJECT: 80`). His want rate there can only be *derived* by combining the two, and
it comes out at exactly 19. So the baseline inherits the circularity it was supposed to anchor.

**And that same arithmetic is where the celebrated 97 came from: 97 is simply the number of
GOOD buttons.** A brief that changed nothing would still have scored 97.

The honest statement: his want rate on fresh, never-judged pages is **9%**, and the older 19%
should not be treated as a measured baseline. Whether the seeds have moved cannot be settled
against a comparator built this way — but a 9% want rate is what the current supply looks
like, and it is the number to plan against.

## 5. What would fix it, ranked — and what does not survive checking

### The fix a sub-agent proposed, and why I am not recommending it

A sub-agent (its own output directory, `output/bl1305_agent_a/`) proposed adding a
**position condition** to C20 rule 2: a hook must sit on a strip above the frame, so a line
burned over the picture would not qualify. It reported this recovers `isawbillie` and
`uncrustable.reel` at **zero cost** to the 9.

**I checked it and it does not hold.**

- `uncrustable.reel`'s qualifying line is, in the judge's own words, *"drawn across the top"* —
  it is already top-band, so the rule would not touch it. It recovers **1 page, not 2**.
- `noirrud` is one of his 9 WANTs, and all five of its qualifying hooks are *over* borrowed
  footage — the reason explicitly says *"the mid-frame text on two tiles is a framing hook by
  the C10 shape test, not speech."* The proposed rule puts a **wanted page at risk**.

The agent said `noirrud` survives because 3 of its 5 tiles are top-band; that claim is not in
the evidence it cited, and it did not open that page's screenshot. Recorded because a fix that
reads well and fails on checking is exactly what this project keeps buying.

### What is left, honestly ranked

| # | fix | recovers | costs | measurable? |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Do nothing.** | 0 | 0 | — |
| 2 | Treat star-sign/horoscope pages as not wanted | 2 | 0 on this sample; **reverses C6 "topic never disqualifies"** | Barely. The held-out test: the 623 contains **exactly one** astrology-shaped handle, and he rejected it. n=1 is not evidence. |
| 3 | Require ≥2 qualifying tiles | 2 | **kills 2 of his 9** (`cineflixiz`, `thesongdetective`, both 1-tile WANTs) | Yes, and it already failed |
| 4 | Gate on confidence | ≤3 | **kills 4 of his 9** — `high` covers only 5 of the 9 | Yes, and it already failed |

**Recommendation: option 1.** Option 2 recovers two pages by fitting a topic rule to the two
pages it recovers, against one held-out data point, and it contradicts a rule the brief states
positively. That is how the 97 happened.

### Why I think the judge is near its ceiling

- It **misses nothing** — 9 of 9, with no fix needed on the recall side.
- Its four errors are all pages where it applied v8 correctly as written. `evan.cancer` has
  3 of 9 qualifying tiles, a clean first-person hook over borrowed footage, no disqualifier:
  under the brief as it stands, **WANT is the right answer**. The disagreement is with the
  brief, not with the judge's reading of it.
- Four errors on n=100 has an interval of roughly ±4 points. His own marks are noisy — two
  careful raters agreed on only 55.6% of one category — and he wrote no reason on any of the
  four, so there is nothing to check the disagreement against.

**Everything measured that could raise precision costs recall.** Given he wants 9 pages in 100
and the judge finds all 9, spending recall to buy precision is the wrong trade.
