# BL-1220: RULE 8 was never broken, it was starved — the field stopped at `posts_for`

**The rule fires now.** RULE 8's logic has always been his rule exactly, and it returned
"abstain" on 623 pages out of 623 because `speech_frac` was never written to a meme-page
post. The field did not fail to be measured; it failed to be *reachable*. `posts_for`
returned seven keys and `clips_metadata` was not one of them, so
`clip_speech.audio_url_of(post)` returned `None` for every post this system has ever judged.

Every figure came from **`.venv\Scripts\python.exe`, Python 3.11.9**. Bare `python` is 3.13
and lacks the packages. **Vendor receipt: $0.0000 — this round made no billed API call.**

---

## 1. The seam, and the proof it was cut

Loaded `HEAD`'s `meme_finder.py` side by side with the fixed one and asked both the same
question:

```
HEAD post keys : ['api_index', 'caption', 'code', 'media_type',
                  'play_count', 'taken_at', 'video_url']
clip_speech.audio_url_of(post) on HEAD  ->  None
```

`audio_url_of` reads `clips_metadata.original_sound_info.progressive_download_url`.
`posts_for` dropped `clips_metadata` on the floor, so the detector could not have found the
audio on any post at any time. That is the whole of the defect. Silero was never the
problem: it runs locally, free, at about 1.6 s per clip, and has already measured 1,238 of
1,255 clips elsewhere in this system.

### What shipped

* `posts_for` now carries `clips_metadata`, `id` and `audio_type` through, so
  `clip_speech.audio_url_of` and `.declared_class` work on the post dict unchanged. No second
  copy of "where does the audio live".
* `attach_speech(posts, speech_fn)` writes `speech_frac` and returns
  `{measured, declared, absent, skipped}`.
* `run_funnel` builds a `speech_fn` from `clip_speech.make_speech_fn` over a plain
  `requests` session. **No billed call**: the audio comes from the payload's own
  `progressive_download_url`. With no detector available the sweep stays exactly as
  network-free as before and *says so* rather than going quiet.

Verified end to end across the seam, with a fake vendor client and no network:

```
attach_speech -> {'measured': 1, 'declared': 1, 'absent': 1, 'skipped': 0}
dialogue_state: all-talking    fired: ['no_text', 'dialogue']
```

**THE THREE STATES ARE KEPT APART.** `speech_frac` is written only when it was actually
measured; an unmeasured post leaves the key **absent**, never `0.0`. About 35% of these
clips carry xHE-AAC that ffmpeg 8.0 cannot decode, so "unmeasured" is the common case, and
an unmeasured post read as "nobody is talking" is precisely how a talking page went through.

## 2. The green guard that could not fail, and the one that can

`tests/test_bl1193_spec.py` proves RULE 8's logic is his rule. It has been green since
BL-1193. It also hands the rule its input: `post(speech_frac=0.9)`. Production never did,
and the rule fired zero times with that suite green the whole way.

That is not a fault in a specification test — stating a rule in isolation is what one is
for. It is a gap in coverage, and the gap now has its own fence:
**`tests/test_bl1220_speech_reachability.py`, 12 checks, all of which fail against the commit
before this one.** It asserts on the seam rather than the rule:

* `posts_for` emits the keys the detector needs, checked by calling `audio_url_of` on its
  actual output;
* a licensed-music post reads as *declared*, not measured;
* `attach_speech` writes a float, and **leaves the key absent** when it did not measure one;
* the three states are counted separately;
* a detector that raises does not take the page's judgement down with it;
* `run_funnel` calls `attach_speech(posts, speech_fn)` **before** `judge_page`, asserted on
  the source and on the order of the two.

`tests/test_bl1193_spec.py` now carries a header saying what it proves and what it cannot,
so the two are read together.

**What I have NOT observed: RULE 8 firing on live pages in a real sweep.** That needs billed
calls and this round made none. The seam is proven with a fake client and real
`posts_for`/`judge_page`; the production leg is proven by construction and by test, not by a
run.

### The energy read is refused a second time, with the sign now inverted

Sixteen formulations of `speech_dbfs − bed_dbfs` on 58 marked, measured pages. **Not one

two scorings of the same corpus is noise. Applying his own ANY/ALL shape to the energy class
— the one genuinely new idea available — does not rescue it either.

### One correction to the diagnosis I was given

"`speech_frac` is never written" is true of the **runner** and false of the **disk**:
`scratch/bl1196_speech.json` holds 2,069 clips across 425 pages and
`scratch/bl1201_pages.jsonl` holds raw per-post fractions. Feeding those stored records to
the *shipped* `judge_page` fires RULE 8 on **107 of 425 pages**; stripping the field from the
same records — which is what today's runner does — fires it on **0 of 425**. The rule was one
field away from live, and the field already existed.

### The depth limit, which nobody has stated

He said ten videos. **Nothing on disk has ever heard more than five.** An all-of-N rule
weakens strictly as N grows: 21.0% of pages fire at 3 clips, 10.2% at 5, and about **4.7% at
the ten he actually specified** — perhaps 3 of the 31. Worth telling him, because his own
number would nearly switch his own rule off.

## 4. Editing-page and recognisable-content: both REFUSED, with the numbers

Both categories were labelled by hand with the definition written to disk **before** any
page was looked at (`scratch/bl1220_editing/DEFINITION.md`,
`scratch/bl1220_lyrics/DEFINITION.md`), then scored on a `sha1(handle)` 40% held-out split.

| label | count |
|---|---:|
| meme | 142 |
| neither | 50 |
| **editing** | **8** |

Base rate **4.0% [2.04, 7.69]**, with 33 pages marked uncertain. Thirteen candidate signals
were tested. The most any of them fires on is **8 pages**, and the best precision comes from
signals that fire once or twice:

| signal | fires | precision |
|---|---:|---:|
| `handle_edit` | 1 | 1.00 |
| `cat_editor_and_no_drawn` | 1 | 1.00 |
| `bio_edit` | 4 | 0.75 |
| `bl1206_regex` (the prior detector) | 4 | 0.75 |
| `self_or_credit` | 8 | 0.50 |

**REFUSED, and not on a threshold — on arithmetic.** With eight positives in the whole
labelled set, a Wilson interval on any subset of them is wider than the gap it would have to
clear. A signal firing on 1 page at 100% has a lower bound near 5%. **No amount of tuning
fixes a base rate this thin**; what this needs is more labelled editing pages, not another
signal, and certainly not another regex. The prior detector's 53.3% against a 44.9% base
rate is reproduced here as 0.75 on four pages, which is the same non-result at higher
resolution.

### Recognisable content versus generic footage: 78 labelled, refused

77 scored after dropping one "neither". Majority class **recognisable**, base rate
**64.94% [53.80, 74.65]**. Nine signals tested — `title_share`, `filmtv_promo_share`,
`work_caption_share`, `work_ocr_share`, `handle_token`, `bio_work`, `not_ambient`,
`niche_is_work`, `work_or_title`. **None clears the bar out of sample.**

**Both refusals are the deliverable, and this project has several of them.** The honest
shape of the finding is that the 13 "editing page" and the lyrics/ambient false accepts are
not currently separable by anything derivable from the payload text, bio, category or OCR
that is already on disk.
