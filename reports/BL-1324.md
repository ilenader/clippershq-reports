## §1 — THE REPRODUCTION, AND WHAT THE SET ACTUALLY IS

**Reproduced.** Mean-pooled per account, 65/35 split at seed 1234 to match `RESULTS.md`,
regularisation chosen by 5-fold CV **inside the training split only**, scored once:

| | AUC |
|---|---:|
| shipped zero-shot (RESULTS.md) | 0.639 |
| **linear probe, held out** | **0.9335 [0.847, 0.988]** |
| probe, in sample | 0.9877 |

Training the final probe took **247 ms** on CPU. There is no CUDA path on this machine (AMD RX
6600 on torch+cpu), so no consumer-GPU timing is quoted anywhere in this round.

### Two things about that number that the headline omits

**The target is `editor`, not `wanted`.** `_vision_dataset/labels.json` holds 137 accounts as
**49 `editor` / 73 `noneditor` / 15 `ambiguous`**, and RESULTS.md's own metric is
`P(vision='edit' | EDITOR) − P(vision='edit' | NON-EDITOR)`. The positive rate of that set is
the **editor rate, 40.2%** — it is not a want rate and must not be compared with one. A 0.937
that silently swaps the question is the same failure as scoring on the 623.

**The labels are model-made.** RESULTS.md: *"Labels: Claude-vision from covers+video-frame
montages (pending user spot-check reconciliation)."* That reconciliation does not appear to have
happened, so this AUC measures agreement with an AI labeller.

### And RESULTS.md's frames conclusion reverses under a probe

Its idea 1 recorded video frames as **NEGATIVE**. That was a property of the zero-shot, not of
the features. The same protocol on each representation:

| representation | accounts | held-out AUC |
|---|---:|---|
| covers | 122 | 0.9335 [0.847, 0.988] |
| **frames** | 122 | **0.9828 [0.946, 1.000]** |
| covers + frames | 122 | 0.9754 [0.926, 1.000] |

A naive parse hides this: covers are `<handle>_<hash>.jpg` but frames are
`<handle>_v<N>_<hash>.jpg`, so a single `rsplit("_", 1)` leaves `tivqns_v1`, matches no label,
and **1,215 of the 2,272 vectors vanish silently**. The "2,272 embeddings" are 1,057 covers plus
1,215 frames.

## §2 — SCORED ON HIS 200, AND THE RECALL BAR

The 623 are excluded on purpose: a GOOD/BAD button on the judge's own verdict, the identity
that recommended the dialogue veto at +3 before it measured −7.

**His 200 had no embeddings — they had to be made.** `ViT-B-16-SigLIP / webli`, the exact model
`_vision_dataset/bench.py:74` used, was already in the HuggingFace cache
(`models--timm--ViT-B-16-SigLIP`, 778 MB). 200 pages, **11.71 s/page on CPU**, 2,342 s total.
Two representations, because the stored vectors are of single covers while his artefacts are
contact sheets: `whole` (the sheet as one image) and `tiles` (3×N cells, embedded separately,
averaged — reconstructing the per-cover domain).

**Both directions were run, and reported, because choosing the flattering one after the fact is
the same sin as choosing a threshold after the fact.**

| representation | tuned on | scored once on | held-out AUC |
|---|---|---|---|
| whole | BL-1298 (28%) | BL-1296 (9%) | 0.9121 [0.851, 0.963] |
| whole | BL-1296 (9%) | BL-1298 (28%) | 0.8438 [0.751, 0.918] |
| **tiles** | **BL-1298 (28%)** | **BL-1296 (9%)** | **0.9695 [0.929, 0.996]** |
| tiles | BL-1296 (9%) | BL-1298 (28%) | 0.9137 [0.847, 0.967] |

`tiles` wins, and the direction matters enormously.

**The other direction has no zero-loss threshold at all.** Tuned on BL-1296 and scored on
BL-1298, the lowest threshold tested (0.05) already kills 2 of 28 wanted; by 0.15 it is 13.
The probe is safe applied *from* enriched stock *to* new discovery, and unsafe in reverse. New
discovery is where the judge calls are spent, so the usable direction is the one that works —
but the asymmetry is real and is the population effect the share/play rule was killed for.

> ### THE ZERO IS NOT ZERO
> There are **9** wanted pages in the held-out set. **Wilson on 0 of 9 is [0, 29.9%].** A true
> loss rate as high as three in ten is consistent with this data. That is the same caveat
> BL-1291 put on its own zero-loss rule, and it is the reason the shipped default does not drop
> anything.

### A confound, measured rather than hoped away

The two sets differ in capture geometry (934×934 clips vs 883×1568 sheets), capture code and
date, as well as in want rate. A probe trained on the same vectors to predict **set membership**
reaches **AUC 0.8455** held out. That does not invalidate the want result — the probe was tuned
and scored on want labels — but the cross-set transfer is **not a clean natural experiment**,
and the operating threshold must be re-derived against marks on any new capture geometry. The
shipped module carries that warning as a constant, `THRESHOLD_IS_CAPTURE_SPECIFIC`, and the
manifest writes it into every `batches.json`.

## §3 — THE FUNNEL, AND WHERE IT ACTUALLY WIRES

### The dollar framing needs one correction

A judged page costs **$0.040932** — and `docs/FACTS.md` records that as
`judging_usd_per_page_paid`, the **paid vision API path**. The path that actually ships is a
free Claude sub-agent: `judging_usd_per_page_free = 0.0`. So on the shipped path this probe
saves **subscription tokens and judging hours, not vendor dollars**. The token breakdown is
what makes the family right: output is **80% of the bill and 49.3% is reasoning nobody sees**,
the screenshot is **5.1%**. An embedding and a dot product emit **no tokens at all** — which is
exactly why a cheaper model that still reasons cannot help here.

Per page: **12,393 subscription tokens** (BL-1285, 281 pages). The 8,548 figure is a lab best
case at n=3 and overstates capacity by 38%.

### At the zero-loss frontier, on the held-out 100

| | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| pages reaching the judge | 100 | **19** |
| share decided by the probe | 0% | **81%** |
| want rate of what the judge sees | 9.0% | **47.4%** (**5.26×**) |
| tokens per 1,000 wanted | 137.7 M | **26.2 M** |

**Judge share lands at 19%, against his target of 10–15%.** I am not going to round that down.
BL-1322 measured a *perfect* narrow model leaving the judge at 81.5% — this is far below that,
but the two are not like for like: that was a union of six yes/no tasks on a 28%-want set, this
is one continuous score on a 9%-want pool where far more pages are genuinely rejectable.

### The wiring, and why it is not in `run_funnel`

The shipped judge is a **free Claude sub-agent, which cannot be called from inside an unattended
Python process** — `meme_finder.load_page_verdicts` says so in its own docstring, and
`run_funnel` therefore *consumes* a verdict file that already exists. **By the time `run_funnel`
runs, judging has happened.** A pre-judge stage placed there would run after the judge.

The last artefact written before an agent looks at a page is the **batch manifest** — and no
production module has ever written one. Every round re-authored its own in `scratch/`. That is
precisely why `clippershq/triage.py`, `presort.py`, `page_capture.py` and `parallel_judge.py`
are all correct, all measured, and all have **zero production callers**.

So this round adds **`clippershq/judge_batches.py`**, the production manifest builder, which
imports and calls **`clippershq/siglip_probe.py`**. Nothing was modified or removed; the scratch
builders still work.

**The caller is proven three ways, because an import test would have passed for `triage.py` too:**

- `tests/test_siglip_probe.py::TheCallerExists` — AST on the call graph, not a substring
  (`str.index` passes on a docstring and on commented-out code, which is how two earlier guards
  here read green while the code was dead). It asserts `build()` calls `siglip_probe.load` and
  `.decide`, and that something under `clippershq/` imports the module.
- `scratch/bl1324_wireproof.py` — imports `clippershq.judge_batches` and calls `build()`
  **in-process** on the real `output/bl1296_fresh100/capture.json` with the real coefficients.
  Not `subprocess.run`: a child process is invisible to the parent, which is how an earlier
  round's off-tree proof saw one open for a 26 MB read.
- The artefacts it produced are on disk and listed below.

### The default is ORDER, not DROP — and it is better than it sounds

`decide()` **cannot return DROP unless the caller passes `allow_drop=True`.** With the default,
running the wired builder over the real capture:

**All 9 of his wanted pages rank in the top 19 of 132 — five of them in the top 5.**
Ranks: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 11, 15, 19.

Judging that queue in order finds every page he wants in **19 calls instead of 132**, and it
**cannot lose a page**, because nothing is removed. Given a 0-of-9 zero that carries a 29.9%
upper bound, that is the version worth switching on today; dropping is available, opt-in, and
should wait for more marked wanted pages.

A defect worth recording: my first version required *full* embedding coverage before sorting, so
with 100 of 132 pages embedded it silently fell back to capture order while still reporting
`probe_used: true` — a manifest that looks ranked and is not. Fixed: scored pages rank among
themselves and lead, unscored keep capture order and follow, and `score_coverage` is written
into the manifest.
