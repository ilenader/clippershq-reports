## What you actually know at send time — and it is better than generic

Measured across all 1,568 ready rows:

| field | populated | what it gives the message |
|---|---|---|
| `display_name` | **100%** | their actual name |
| `tiktok_handle` / `tiktok_url` | **100%** | the account |
| **`source_hashtag`** | **100%** | **the fandom they edit** (`bridgerton`, `<fandom>edit`, `jjkedit`) |
| `followers` | 99.0% | audience size |
| `bio` | 98.9% | their own words |
| `last_post_date` (+ `date_source=deep`) | 98.5% | whether they are active |
| `deep_median_views` | 94.0% | **real median across ~10 posts, not a lucky video** |
| `instagram` | 15.6% | second channel |
| `niche` · `post_hashtags` · `media_count` | 3–4% | unusable |
| `median_duration` · `video_share` · `repost_frac` · `dominant_lang` · `vision_verdict` | **0%** | unusable |

**94.0% of the list (1,474 rows) has all five core tokens at once** — name, fandom, followers, median views, recency. That is enough to write a message that is specific and true about every recipient without a single research pass.

**Recency makes the specificity credible:** median **17 days** since last post; **76% posted within 30 days**, 91% within 90.

**The bios are usable and revealing** — 1,550 present, median 68 characters, and **95% contain an email address**, i.e. these people are already advertising for work. Intent keywords across them: `edit` 362 · `AE`/`After Effects` 378 combined · `dm for` 250 · `business` 215 · `commission` 45 · `capcut` 30.

**So a first line can be true and specific, e.g.:** *"Your [Bridgerton] edits are averaging [23k] views — are you taking commissions?"* Every bracketed token is present on 94% of the list. That is the opposite of generic.

**What you cannot do:** there is no stored URL for the specific video that matched, only `matched_video_views`. You can say *"your video that did 4.4M"*, you cannot link to it. And 0% of the list has vision or content-classification fields, so nothing can be said about *what happens inside* their videos.

## The one piece of evidence per segment

For editors the brief's instinct inverts. **An editor does not need to be shown a clip — they make the clips.** What a freelancer opening a cold email actually needs to see is: *is this real work, at what rate, and will I get paid?*

| segment | the evidence that earns a reply | do you have it today? | cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| A+D (<10k) | a concrete first brief and a rate | **No — it is not in the repo** | $0 to write, but it is a business decision |
| B (10k–100k) | as above + proof you know their niche | **fandom token: yes, 100%** | $0 |
| C+F (100k+) | a named client and a budget | **No** | — |

**The blocker for all three is not data — it is that the offer does not exist in this repository.** There is no rate card, no brief, no client name, no turnaround, no payment terms anywhere in the codebase. The lead pipeline is complete; the thing to say to a lead is not written. That is the single largest gap in this audit and no amount of further measurement closes it.

For the **demand-side** funnels — where the brief's "clip of their own track/stream" idea is exactly right — the evidence is producible and cheap: audio understanding at **$0.16–0.45 per 1,000 clips** ([BL-734](BL-734.md)). But those funnels export zero leads, so the sequencing is: fix the verdict gate (BL-751 §2) *before* investing in sample generation.

## Research: still no credible creator-specific data — third round, same answer

**Confirmed REFUTED, and I checked the most-cited figure directly.** The number that circulates for influencer outreach is *"personalized outreach emails pull 18% reply rates vs. 6% for generic blasts."* Tracing it: prospeo.io states it and hyperlinks to **a Superhuman marketing blog** — no study, no sample size, no methodology on either page. It is a vendor claim citing another vendor's blog. **Do not plan against 18%.**

What *is* methodologically disclosed is all B2B, and it should be labelled as such:

| finding | source | scale |
|---|---|---|
| overall cold-email reply rate **3.43%** (down from 5.1% in 2024) | Instantly 2026 benchmark | platform-wide |
| **single follow-up lifts total replies +65.8%** | Woodpecker | 20M+ emails |
| **58% of replies from email 1, 42% from follow-ups** | Woodpecker | 20M+ emails |
| 3–5 follow-up steps → **8.3% reply** vs **4.1%** with none | Woodpecker | 20M+ |
| optimal sequence **4–7 touchpoints** | Woodpecker | 20M+ |
| **under 80 words**, one clear CTA, subject **36–50 chars** | Woodpecker | 20M+ |
| **recruiting/staffing is the highest-replying category, 4–8%** (5.8–7.2% typical) | industry benchmark round-up | not disclosed |

**That last row is the one that transfers.** The sendable list is a *recruiting* list, and recruiting is the best-performing cold-email category measured — roughly double the 3.43% average. It is the closest available proxy and it is a favourable one.

Two corrections to the brief's own numbers: Woodpecker reports **42%** of replies from follow-ups (not 44%), and **+65.8%** total replies from one follow-up — the brief's "~65%" is right.

## The sequence, for segment B (530 leads, the largest coherent one)

Four touches, because past four the same sources report complaints climbing. Under 80 words each, one CTA, subject 36–50 characters. Every bracket is a merge field present on ≥94% of the list.

**Touch 1 — day 0.** Subject: `Your [Bridgerton] edits — quick question` (38 chars)
> Hi [Name] — your [Bridgerton] edits are averaging about [23k] views, which is why I'm writing rather than mass-mailing.
> We're commissioning [fandom] edits and pay per finished clip. Rate is [X] and turnaround is [Y].
> Are you open to commissions right now?

**Touch 2 — day 3.** Subject: `Re: your [Bridgerton] edits`
> Short version: paid [fandom] edit work, [X] per clip. Reply "yes" and I'll send one brief to try.
*(This is the highest-performing single step in the Woodpecker data — the first follow-up peaks at 8.4%.)*

**Touch 3 — day 8.** Add the one thing you have not yet given: proof.
> Adding detail in case rates were the question: [client//example], [X] per clip, [Y] turnaround, paid on delivery.
> If the rate is off, tell me what yours is.

**Touch 4 — day 17, close the loop and stop.**
> Last one from me. If commissions aren't for you now, no problem — want me to check back in three months?

**Then stop.** A permanent-stop on any reply, and no fifth touch.

**Applied to 530 leads at the recruiting benchmark of 4–8%, expect roughly 21–42 replies.** Against the 3.43% platform average it would be ~18. Those are the honest planning numbers, not 18%.

## What to measure — the columns exist and are empty on all 57,386 rows

`date_sent`, `sent_channel`, `replied`, `reply_sentiment`, `bounced`, `converted`, `outcome_notes` are in the schema and **0% populated**. Nothing has ever been sent, so the first 200 sends are the only chance to get a clean read.

**Minimum set to record — five of these already exist, two do not:**

| field | status | why it is required |
|---|---|---|
| `date_sent` | exists | denominator and sequence timing |
| `sent_channel` | exists | email vs DM |
| `bounced` | exists | list hygiene; separates "bad address" from "not interested" |
| `replied` | exists | the primary outcome |
| `reply_sentiment` | exists | positive / neutral / negative — a reply saying "stop" is not a win |
| `converted` | exists | the only outcome that pays |
| **`touch_number`** | **MISSING** | without it you cannot tell whether follow-ups work, which is the single biggest lever in the research above |
| **`message_variant`** | **MISSING** | without it you cannot compare two messages, and A/B is the whole point of a 200-send pilot |

**Segment and funnel need no new columns** — segment is derivable from `deep_median_views`, funnel from `source_hashtag`, both already 100%/94% populated.

**Design the pilot so it can answer something.** 200 sends across one segment with two message variants and the full four-touch sequence gives ~100 per variant — enough to see a large difference, not a subtle one. Splitting 200 across three segments *and* two variants gives cells of 33 and answers nothing. **Pick segment B, two variants, 200 sends.**

## Bottom line

1. **You have one sendable audience, not three** — 1,568 TikTok/IG fandom editors, and it is a hiring list.
2. **The personalisation is genuinely good**: 94% carry name + fandom + followers + median views + recency, and 76% posted in the last 30 days.
3. **The offer is the gap.** No rate, no brief, no turnaround, no client name exists anywhere in the repo. Everything else is ready and this is not.
4. **Plan against 4–8%** (recruiting benchmark), not 18% (vendor claim), and not 3.43% (generic average — you are better than generic).
5. **Add `touch_number` and `message_variant`** before the first send, or the pilot teaches you nothing.

## Honest limits

- **No message was sent and no reply rate was measured here.** Every rate quoted is someone else's B2B data; the creator-specific gap is real and unclosed for the third round running.
- **The recruiting 4–8% figure comes from an industry round-up that does not disclose its sample.** It is the closest proxy available, not a measurement.
- Segment sizes use `deep_median_views`, absent on 6% of the list; those 94 rows are unsegmentable until measured.
- **I did not evaluate whether the offer is attractive**, only that it is undefined. Whether editors want this work at any rate is unknown and is the assumption the pilot actually tests.
- Fandom tags come from the discovery hashtag, which is where the lead was *found* — a strong signal of what they edit, but not proof it is their main subject.
