## 3. Same account or a different one — the question that decides whether anything is void

If the sheet had shown **another account**, every verdict on those rows would be meaningless.
It did not.

* **28 of 36** share tiles with the grid the judge read (average-hash per cell, Hamming ≤ 5).
* The other **8 share few or none** — and a 34% threshold called them "different account",
  **which was wrong**. A page that posts heavily turns over all 12 tiles between two captures,
  so zero shared tiles is perfectly consistent with one account. Settled three ways instead:
  * **inspected visually** — `qweposted` and `24ysr` against the judge's own grid: same
    recurring subject, same format, the same named people (*"Austin"*, *"bsf"*);
  * **the judge's own written reason names account-identifying marks** that match the picture
    this sheet showed — `imaclipper` *"stamped with an @IMACLIPPER watermark"*, `bqwrzerd`
    *"a burned-in BQWRZERD edit"*, `qweposted` *"the same tattooed man filming himself"*;
  * **structural** — both capture directories name the sheet after the handle whose videos were
    fetched, and `tiktok_finder.videos_from_handle` filters a username search to **that author
    only** before composing.

**All 36 are the same account at a different time. The judge saw the right page every time. It
is a presentation fault, not a judging one.**

The two TikTok captures are **five hours apart on the same day** (16:26 vs 21:22, 2026-08-14),
which is why a mismatched row usually differs by only one or two tiles.

## 5. The leak state of what he is about to mark

Swept with the **correct boundary test**. `\b` is wrong here: handles contain `.` and `_`, so
`\b` both misses and over-fires. The rule used is that the character on each side must not be
in `[A-Za-z0-9._]` and must not be `@` — `@` excluded so `@handle` still counts as naming it.
A **6-character floor**, because `ight`, `men`, `clips` and `techs` are real handles and would
match half the prose in every brief.

| | |
|---|---|
| marked sets swept | **8** |
| union of handles | **723** (**714** at or above the floor) |
| briefs swept | **14** |
| **briefs leaking** | **9** |
| **distinct pages leaked** | **27** |

**Two corrections to the premise I was given.**

1. **It is not "3 leak on Instagram, 0 on TikTok".** Nine briefs leak, and among them
   **`bl1265_brief_v8.md` names 15** and **`bl1270_brief_tt2.md` names 15** — those are the two
   briefs that produced the verdicts in these sheets, and the round's premise called them clean.
2. The handle union is **723**, not 832. Eight marked sets, as stated; the 832 does not
   reproduce from them and I have not been able to source it.

**Every affected row is badged in the sheet** — a red `NAMED IN n BRIEFS` chip, and when the
brief that judged that very page is one of them it says **`— INCLUDING THE ONE THAT JUDGED
IT`**. Instagram carries **5** such rows, TikTok **0**. They are shown rather than dropped so
he can see why to skip them.
