### Tier 2, the inferred cross — and the precision that governs it

The only cross left is the dangerous one: assume the handle is the same on both platforms and go
look. This is the move that scored 0-for-6 with six namesakes.

**37 handles → 13 exist on Instagram (35.1%) → adjudicated by three independent judges.** Judges
were told explicitly that a matching handle is the *hypothesis*, not evidence, and to default to
NOT_SAME when uncertain because a wrong address is worse than none.

| | n |
|---|---:|
| candidates proposed by "the handle exists on Instagram" | 13 |
| adjudicated **SAME** | **1** |
| adjudicated NOT_SAME | 10 |
| adjudicated UNSURE | 2 |
| judges would stake sender reputation on it | **1 of 13** |

> ### IDENTITY PRECISION = 1/13 = **7.7%**, Wilson 95% **[1.4, 33.3]**

**The interval's lower bound is 1.4%. This path is REFUSED.** Nine times in ten it hands you a
stranger.

What the judges actually found: **8 of the 13 Instagram accounts have never posted** (`media_count`
0), 8 have empty bios, and the follower disparities are absurd — `funny.memes584` is 56,715 TikTok
followers against 19 on Instagram with 2 posts, a ratio of 2,985. These are dormant name
reservations that happen to hold the same string, not the same pages.

### The one real match, and the direction I had wrong

`caprikiri` is a genuine cross, and it was confirmed by evidence that owes nothing to the handle:
**the Instagram bio states its own TikTok size — "✦ 28,7K on tik tok!" — against a TikTok account
holding 29,438 followers**, with the same distinctive display name (`ycarus`) and "editor" in both
bios.

That is an owner-declared cross — the strongest signal class there is. **It runs Instagram →
TikTok.** I searched TikTok → Instagram and found 0 of 37. The declaration existed; it pointed the
other way, and I only looked one way. The lesson is not that the cross is impossible but that
**the arrow has a direction, and the productive direction is the one I did not search.**

## 2. The open web: 30 tools surveyed, 2 tested

Every `stars`/`licence`/`archived`/`last_commit` came from the **GitHub REST API**, not from search
snippets. `last_commit` is the commits-endpoint committer date.

### The central finding

**No open-source tool solves handle → personal email in one step.** The field is three families
and only the third is even relevant:

| family | examples | why it cannot work here |
|---|---|---|
| **existence checkers** | Sherlock 89.6k★, Maigret 36.8k★, Blackbird, Nexfil, WhatsMyName | they answer "does this username exist there", never an address. **Many share the WhatsMyName dataset, so trying several is not independent evidence** |
| **permutation** | Buster, Mailfoguess, MailFinder, theHarvester | name + **company domain** → guess `firstname-dot-lastname` at the corporate domain. Buster's README says it wraps hunter.io; Hunter's own docs say professional addresses only. Our targets are individuals on free mailboxes |
| **bio scrapers** | Scout, socid-extractor, Instaloader, Linkook | the only viable family — and see the ceiling argument below |

### A representative selection of the 30

| tool | licence | ★ | last commit | Windows | keys | plausible |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| Maigret | MIT | 36,803 | 2026-08-16 | **yes** (README + `.exe`) | no | existence only |
| Instaloader | MIT | 13,132 | 2026-07-26 | yes | no | **tested** |
| socid-extractor | MIT | 1,066 | 2026-08-14 | unclear | no | **tested** |
| Scout | MIT | 474 | 2026-02-22 | unclear | no | see below |
| Osintgram | GPL-3.0 | 14,010 | 2025-08-25 | yes | **requires login** | no |
| holehe | GPL-3.0 | 13,224 | 2024-09-10 | unclear | no | reverse-check only |
| Toutatis | GPL-3.0 | 4,191 | 2024-12-05 | unclear | needs `sessionid` | **trap** |
| SpiderFoot | MIT | 21,103 | **2023-11-05** | unclear | many | stale |

**`pushed_at` lies, and it lies by years.** socialscan advertises `pushed_at` 2026-08-03 against a
real last commit of **2024-01-21**; SpiderFoot shows 2026-04-13 against **2023-11-05**. Both look
maintained on their listing page. Anyone judging maintenance from a repo listing is reading a
field that updates on activity that is not commits.

**Windows: not one repo in the survey runs CI on `windows-latest`.** Only Maigret and Osintgram
carry positive in-repo Windows evidence.

**Toutatis is a trap** — widely recommended for exactly our Instagram problem, but Instagram's
recovery endpoint returns a **masked** address (`m****@g****.com`), unsendable, and needs a real
logged-in session cookie.

### What was tested, on real handles from this corpus

Installed into an **isolated throwaway venv**, never the project environment, and routed through
`clippershq/pipguard.py` rather than around it.

| tool | test | result |
|---|---|---|
| **socid-extractor 0.1.1** | 5 real corpus handles (3 TikTok, 2 Instagram) | **0 fields extracted, 0 emails** |
| **Instaloader 4.15.3** | introspect `Profile` for any address-carrying attribute | **40 public attributes, none is an email** |

The mechanism behind socid-extractor's zero is worth more than the zero: **TikTok served a
12,579-byte JS shell that was byte-identical across three different handles** — there is no
profile data in an unauthenticated static fetch at all. Instagram served 606 KB and the extractor
matched nothing against current markup.

Instaloader independently confirms the survey's reading: its Instagram contact surface is
`biography` + `external_url` + `business_category_name`. **So `public_email` — the one
address-carrying Instagram field, filled 51% — is not reachable from the free OSS path.** It is
behind the paid API. That is an economic fact, not a tooling gap.

**Scout was not installed, and I am saying so rather than passing over it.** It was the survey's
most plausible candidate, claiming bio email-scraping for both platforms. But its claimed
advantage is reading the bio, and the bio is *already bought and already measured*: 0 addresses
across the 37 wanted pages. **A bio scraper cannot return what the bio does not contain**, so the
ceiling is known without installing it.

## 3. Mining what is already paid for

### Every "field absent" claim, re-checked — and I made the error twice while hunting it

The premise was right and it is worse than stated. Checking a **name** is not enough; you have to
check the **type**, the **values**, and **whose schema the field belongs to**. Three levels, and
only the third is the truth:

| level | question | TikTok answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1. my guessed names | is there a `public_email` / `business_email`? | **no** ← *this is the BL-1319 error* |
| 2. the payload's own names | is there anything contact-shaped? | **yes** — `email`, `emails`, `has_email`, `is_email_verified` |
| 3. type, values, provenance | can I send to it? | **no** — booleans, always `False`, and `email`/`emails` belong to **our** normalised schema, not the vendor's |

**I wrote level 1 into my own synonym table**, choosing Instagram-flavoured names and typing "none
seen" — the identical move to asking a TikTok payload for `biography`. The payload-driven scan
caught it within the same file.

**And I made it a second time.** I searched the TikTok **bio text** for links and reported "0 of 37
wanted pages name any link". The link is not in the text — it is a **structured field**, and it is
not called `bio_link` either. The payload calls it **`bio_url` / `bio_secure_url`**:

| | non-empty `bio_url` |
|---|---|
| **WANT** | **2 of 37 (5.4%)** |
| REJECT | 14 of 63 (22.2%) |

The anti-correlation survives. The zero did not. **Twice in one round, while hunting exactly this
defect** — which is the strongest argument available that enumerating keys must replace guessing
them.

### The structural fact, from the payloads themselves

Scanning **all** leaf names — 1,007 Instagram, 1,276 TikTok — for anything contact-shaped, then
checking types and values:

| | vendor fields whose values are address-shaped |
|---|---|
| **Instagram** | **1** — `public_email`, 51% filled |
| **TikTok** | **0** |

TikTok's vendor payload carries **no address anywhere**. And the practitioner survey supplies the
mechanism: **a personal TikTok account cannot display an email at all** — the tap-to-contact email
button is Business-Account-only. The 0-of-37 is not a sampling accident or a scraping failure. **It
is what the product does.**

### Following links deeper — and a measurement I had to throw away

The first hop run reported "0 addresses at hop 1 and hop 2" and it was **worthless**. All 16
`bio_url` values resolve to `tiktoklinksafety.us` — TikTok's *"you are leaving TikTok"*
interstitial. Hop 1 was the warning page; hop 2 was its own CDN assets. The creator's site was
never fetched. The tell was that all 16 seeds landed on **one domain**, which cannot be a real
distribution. The true destination sits in the `target=` query parameter.

Unwrapped and re-run:

| hop | pages fetched | with an address | rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 (real destination) | 16 | **1** | 6.2% |
| 2 | 78 | **0** | 0.0% |

| seeds | distinct addresses reached |
|---|---|
| WANT (2 seeds) | **0** |
| REJECT (14 seeds) | 1 |

**Going deeper did not help.** The prior "hop 2 gave 1 of 6 where hop 1 gave 0 of 6" does not
reproduce at this larger scale: 78 hop-2 pages returned nothing. The one address came at hop 1, on
a REJECT.

*Limitation, stated plainly:* the fetcher reads raw HTML, and link-in-bio pages often render
client-side, so hop-2 coverage is a floor rather than a ceiling. Three hop-1 fetches also failed
(402, 400, non-HTML JSON).

### Can TikTok be an email channel at all?

**No. It is a DM channel and should be planned as one.** Four independent lines agree:

1. **The platform.** A personal TikTok account is not given anywhere to publish an address; the
   contact button is Business-only.
2. **The payload.** Zero address-carrying vendor fields across 1,276 leaf names.
3. **His own marks.** 0 of 37 wanted pages publish one — and it survives an obfuscation-aware
   check for `[at]`, `(dot)`, "gmail", and the word "email". The only `@` signs in wanted bios are
   **TikTok @mentions of other TikTok accounts**.
4. **The pages themselves say so.** 4 of 37 invite a DM. Of the 2 that explicitly ask for business,
   **both say DM and neither gives an address.** `📩-Dm For Business Inquires`. `Dm Me For Any Promo`.

They are not hiding a channel. They are naming one, and it is not email.

**What follows.** Instagram remains the email platform: it is the only place of the two where the
owner is *given* a field, and `public_email` fills 51% on business accounts. TikTok should be
planned as DM outreach, which is a different build — rate limits, per-message composition, no
`master_leads.csv` row — and this round makes no claim about its economics. The one thing that
should **not** happen is more spend on crossing TikTok to Instagram: measured at 7.7% precision and
zero yield, it is the worst row in the table.
