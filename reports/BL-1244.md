**337 pages per million tokens.**

**§4 — Haiku 4.5 scores 70.0% against his marks; Opus 5 scores 74.0%. That gap is not

costs **6.5×** more per page. On this evidence I cannot say the expensive model is better, and I
cannot say the cheap one is as good; both CIs are ~25 points wide at n=50.

### A failure mode worth 13% of the image bill

Batching `navigate` + `screenshot` with **no wait** returned **3 blank pages out of 3** — a
full-price 1,614-token image of nothing, with no error. **3 of my 23 screenshots were blank,
13.0% of all image tokens spent.** A judge that batches naively pays for empty pages and then
reads them as "this account has no posts". The fix is one `wait` action; the cost of not knowing
is one in eight images.

### The live judge scored 65.0% — and the misses say something

Against his marks: **13/20 = 65.0%** [43.3, 81.9]. **6 of the 7 misses are me rejecting a page he
wants.** Not noise — a rubric mismatch. He marked `activeprogrammer` (an AI-news account) WANT
and wrote the reason himself:

> *"It IS a meme / repost page — the format is right"*

Same for `_driblix_` (football). **His criterion is the FORMAT — reposted footage with a drawn
line — not whether the content is comedic.** My condensed rubric (and BL-1240's, which rejects
"news, fact, quote or aggregator" accounts) is stricter than he is. That is a real, actionable
correction to the judging brief and it is the highest-value thing §2 turned up.

**Decode verified twice.** `mark` is *"was the FILTER right"*, not *"is this page good"*. My
derivation from `ground_truth/review_marks.jsonl` (669 rows → 623 handles) matches the
pre-decoded `review_marks_merged_BL-1210.jsonl` on **623 of 623 handles, 0 disagreements**. My
first attempt was wrong — I treated `PASSED` as not-kept and got 288 disagreements — and the
cross-check is what caught it.

### The two-tier design: proposed, measured, and NOT recommended

The brief asks for a two-tier proposal *if the numbers support it*. They do not, for a reason
that is specific and checkable rather than a judgement call:

**Haiku returned `confidence: "high"` on 50 of 50 pages.** Every escalation gate I tried —
escalate when confidence is low/medium, escalate when it is not high, escalate on UNSURE —
escalates **0 of 50**. A two-tier design needs the cheap tier to know when it is out of its
depth, and this one never says so.

Opus 5's confidence, by contrast, tracks its accuracy: **26/33 correct at `high` (79%), 10/15 at
`medium` (67%), 1/2 at `low`.** The calibration exists in the expensive model and is absent from
the cheap one.

| gate | escalated | blended agreement | $/1000 pages |
|---|---|---|---|
| confidence low or medium | 0 / 50 (0.0%) | 70.0% | $2.69 |
| confidence not high | 0 / 50 (0.0%) | 70.0% | $2.69 |
| verdict UNSURE only | 0 / 50 (0.0%) | 70.0% | $2.69 |

**So: I am not shipping the cheap model.** Not because it lost the accuracy contest — it did not,
and 70 vs 74 on n=50 is a coin-flip — but because it is confidently wrong on 15 of 50 pages and
gives no signal to route them. He has said quality is the point, and the browser judge is the
first thing in this project he has called near-perfect; swapping in a model that cannot flag its
own doubt trades that away for $14.77 per thousand pages.

**What I would test next, and it is cheap:** escalate on *page ambiguity* rather than model
confidence — e.g. run Haiku twice at 1 tile and 6 tiles and escalate the pages where the two
verdicts differ. That needs no calibrated confidence, only disagreement. It was not run here
(402), so it is a proposal, not a result.

### An honest limit on all of §3 and §4

These runs use **stored grid-tile covers, not whole-page screenshots** — whole-page captures were
never persisted, and `bl1240_record.py` writes a JSONL row and no image. The tiles are the thing
the grid is made of (7,016 distinct covers under `output/review_*_thumbs/`, joined to handles
through 48 `output/rows_*.json` files), and they carry the drawn line the judge decides on. But
**no profile header reaches either model — no follower count, no bio, no post count.** So 70% and
74% are a **lower bound on both** and, more importantly, a **fair comparison between them**; they
are not a prediction of live browser accuracy. Every row is labelled `grid_only`. The upside is
that both models saw **byte-identical input**, which two browser visits to the same profile can
never provide.

## 5. The recorder, fixed

`--elapsed-s` was declared with no `type=`, so argparse yielded `str` and every value was written
as text — `bl1240_record.py:136` and `bl1242_record.py:161`, one missing argument each.

**The defect is 2.6× wider than the brief thought.** Not 213 rows — **553**, across all seven
judge logs, **numeric on zero of them**:

```
bl1240_iter1   50   bl1240_iter2   52   bl1240_iter3   50   bl1240_volume   61
bl1242_iter4   50   bl1242_iter5   50   bl1242_rejudge874  240
TOTAL  elapsed_s as STRING: 553    as NUMBER: 0
```

**The brief's "213 rows over 211 handles" is also real, and it is four logs, not one:**
50+52+50+61 = 213 over 211 unique handles. The two duplicates are **`11seconds___`** and
**`_iydys._`**, both inside `bl1240_iter2.jsonl`.

**Every one of the 553 parses as a float, so no timing was lost** — worth saying plainly. What
breaks is every *ordered* operation, because Python compares strings lexicographically:

```
max(['9','42'])        -> '9'          the slowest page reads as the fastest
sorted(['9','10','8']) -> ['10','8','9']
sum(['9','42'])        -> TypeError
```

A round reporting "slowest page 9 s" off that column would be wrong and would look right.

`scratch/bl1244_recorder.py` is the fix: `type=` on every numeric field plus a range check, so a
bad value is **refused at the CLI** rather than stored. It also records what was previously
unrecordable — `screenshots`, `actions`, `turns`, `tokens_in`, `tokens_out` — which is why §2
exists at all. Redaction stays at the **write point**. Proven both ways: the audit above, and

```
--elapsed-s forty  ->  error: elapsed_s must be a number, got 'forty'.
```

**I did not edit `bl1240_record.py` or `bl1242_record.py`** — undeclared files belonging to
finished rounds. The one-line fix for each is in the module docstring.
