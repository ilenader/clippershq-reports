# BL-1543 — one hashtag holds at least 525 accounts, not 86. We were using a quarter of it.

**Round:** BL-1543 · **Cap:** $0.50 · **Spent:** $0.1890 (315 billed requests at $0.000600)
**Read-only:** no ledger write, no seen store, no addresses collected. Both guarded files
verified byte-identical afterwards.

---

## THE ANSWER

**You were right, and the number is bigger than you guessed.** A single hashtag holds **at
least 525 accounts**. We were taking about **86**.

The cause is not the stop rule. It is the **endpoint**. LamaTok exposes four hashtag paths;
the harvester has only ever used `/v1/hashtag/medias`. On the same three tags, on the same
day, `/v2/hashtag/medias` returned **4.04x more accounts**:

| tag | v1 accounts | v2 accounts | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| a film/TV character tag | 137 | **525** | 3.83x |
| a footballer tag | 119 | **485** | 4.08x |
| an anime tag that stopped early | 35 | **165** | 4.71x |
| **pooled** | **291** | **1,175** | **4.04x** |

**And 525 is not the ceiling — it is my own limit.** All three tags were still returning
net-new accounts on **page 59**, where the walk stopped because I capped it at 60 pages.
The vendor never refused, never repeated a page, and never said it was out. **Nobody has yet
found the bottom of a single hashtag.**

---

## HOW v1 ACTUALLY ENDS

Walked to 15 consecutive dead pages, far past the two-page stop:

| page | items | net-new | cumulative | has_more |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | 29–30 | 30, 24, 26, 13, 25 | 112 | true |
| 5 | 18 | 14 | 126 | **false** |
| 6–20 | **1** | **0** | 126 | false |

That shape held on all three tags: five or six full pages, one short page, then **every
subsequent page returns exactly one item and no new account, forever.** The v1 well is
genuinely finite and the harvester was right to stop — it just stops at the bottom of a
shallow well.

**The pages are not a cache.** Every response body had a distinct sha256 — 26 of 26, 21 of
21, 17 of 17, and 60 of 60 on v2. This is not the failure mode where pages 3 and 4 come back
byte-identical to page 2; the vendor really is computing each page and really has no more.

**The reader was proved not blind.** After each tag bottomed out, the same parser re-read
page 0 of the same tag in the same run and saw 29–30 accounts. Every zero above has a
positive control behind it.

---

## THE TWO-DEAD-PAGE RULE, TESTED FOR THE FIRST TIME

It has never been checked whether a third or tenth page starts producing again. It does —
but rarely, and it is not what was costing us the accounts.

- On v2, **two of three tags never once resumed** after a dead page.
- The third resumed **5 times**, and once after **two consecutive dead pages** (pages 54–55
  dead, page 56 produced again, **7 more accounts arrived after that point**).

So the rule is not strictly safe, and on that tag it would have cost 7 accounts out of 165 —
about 4%. **Worth loosening to three dead pages, but it is a rounding error next to the 4x.**
The stop rule was not the problem. The endpoint was.

---

## v2 IS ALSO CHEAPER, WHICH IS THE PART I DID NOT EXPECT

v2 returns smaller pages — a median of 14 items against v1's 30 — so the intuition is that it
costs more calls. **It does not.**

| | accounts/call | $ per 1,000 accounts |
|---|---|---|
| v1 | 291 / 64 = **4.55** | $0.1320 |
| v2 | 1,175 / 180 = **6.53** | **$0.0919** |

**v2 is 1.44x more accounts per call and 30% cheaper per thousand.** v1 spends most of its
calls on the long tail of one-item pages that add nothing; v2 keeps producing.

⚠️ **`count` is NOT honoured on v2.** The live spec documents `count` as 1–50 — where v1 caps
at 30 and rejects 40 — but v2 returned a maximum of 18–19 items whether asked for 30 or 50.
The parameter is accepted and ignored. There is no 50-per-call saving to be had; the win is
depth, not page size.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE NEXT 1,000 ADDRESSES

**BL-1542 walked 512 tags to touch 44,270 accounts. At the depth measured here, that is
roughly 113 tags.** The vocabulary problem — the thing that has been the binding constraint
for two rounds — **is about a quarter the size it appeared to be.**

Concretely, for the next 1,000 addresses at BL-1542's measured net-new rate of 2.26%:

- **before:** ~560 new tags, ~$2.68
- **after:** ~140 tags at v2 depth, and cheaper per account

That reverses the standing advice. **Fewer tags, walked far deeper, on v2** — and the
best-performing tags from BL-1542 (`creed` at 16.19%, the NBA franchise tags) are worth
re-walking on v2 even though the ledger marks them done, because v1 only ever saw a quarter
of them.

⚠️ **One caveat, stated rather than buried.** This is three tags. The 4.04x is a pooled ratio
over 291 v1 accounts and 1,175 v2 accounts, paired same-tag same-day, and the per-tag ratios
are tight (3.83, 4.08, 4.71). But three tags is three tags, and the *ceiling* is not measured
at all — only a lower bound of 525. Before re-walking 500 tags on v2, walk ten and confirm.

## WHAT YOU GOT WRONG

**I killed my own funded run with a pipe.** I ran the first probe as
`python -u probe.py | head -70`. `head` closed the pipe, Python took SIGPIPE, and the process
died **before it reached the v2 endpoint** — the entire point of the round. The v1 walks had
already been paid for and the output file was never written. Re-running cost another $0.04.
A long instrumented run goes to a file and gets read afterwards; it never goes through `head`.

**I nearly published a 3.8x measured on one tag.** The first probe walked v2 on
`saulgoodmanedit` only. One tag is an anecdote, and a 4x claim that reverses two rounds of
advice needs to be paired per tag. The other two cost $0.07 and turned it into a finding.

**And I broke a source file with shell escaping for the third time this session.** Generating
Python through a nested shell heredoc turned `\n` into a literal newline and split a string
literal in half. Twice before this round, once during it. The lesson has been available since
the first time and I kept reaching for the same tool.

## SAFETY

Read-only round. `email_harvest_tags.json` and `spend.json` sha256-verified before and after:
**both unchanged.** No ledger row written — these tags are already walked and re-marking them
would only risk a partial state. No seen store touched, nothing latched. **No addresses were
collected or reported**; accounts were counted by id and the ids were held in memory only.

The cap was proved before the first call by lifting `reserve()`'s real source by AST and
driving it — funded allows and the meter advances, the ceiling raises, $0.00 raises, the meter
does not advance on either refusal, and under 8 threads the locked counter stopped at exactly
50 of a 50-call cap. The unit was passed explicitly at the named config price of $0.000600.
Spent **$0.1890 of $0.50**.
