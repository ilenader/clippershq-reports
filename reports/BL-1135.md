# BL-1135 — Essentia on WSL2, end to end

**It hears. Every control passes at 100%. And its mood does not beat reading the clip's own
text, so the mood half is refused with numbers while the rhythm half is real.**

WSL2 was already installed here, so the cost was **1.3 GB of VHD growth**, not a new
platform. $0.00 spent — this is local CPU. Nothing was uploaded.

---

## The controls, in the order that decides things

### 1. A-vs-A reproducibility — reported first, on purpose

BL-1132's lesson was that A-vs-B looked like a capability until the third same-condition arm
showed the model disagreed with **itself** more than removing the entire audio track did. So
nothing else here is believed until this passes.

| field | identical across 3 runs |
|---|---|
| mood_happy / mood_sad / mood_aggressive / mood_relaxed / mood_party | **100.0% [90.4, 100.0]** 36/36 |
| danceability, bpm, loudness_dbfs | **100.0%** 36/36 |
| **the derived MOOD LABEL** | **100.0% [90.6, 100.0]** 37/37 |

**The 40% floor is gone.** It was never a property of audio analysis — it was a property of
asking a language model. A deterministic graph returns the same float every time.

### 2. Swap control — 12 clips whose audio was replaced by a known track

**100.0% [75.7, 100.0] — 12/12.** Every swapped clip reproduces its source track's numbers
*exactly*, to the last decimal. It is reading the waveform and nothing else.

### 3. Silence

```
ok=True   silent=True   rms=-240.0 dBFS   mood fields emitted: NONE — abstained
```

It says so, and **emits no mood at all** — absence, not zero. CLAP called silence "warm"
because a softmax always elects a winner; this abstains by construction.

### 4. CPU

**p50 2.17 s, p90 2.41 s, p99 2.72 s** per clip (n=111). **1.6 h at p50 / 2.1 h at p99** for
2,731 clips, single process, 12 cores available. Cost is not the obstacle.

## The operator's four tracks

*(n=4 is a direction, not a measurement)*

| track | operator's label | essentia's top three | bpm |
|---|---|---|---|
| song01 | melancholy *(slow sad piano ballad)* | **sad 0.93**, relaxed 0.64, happy 0.43 | 163.6 |
| song02 | triumphant *(female rap anthem)* | sad 0.70, happy 0.54, relaxed 0.49 | 102.8 |
| song03 | warm *(summer/football, upbeat)* | **party 0.85**, relaxed 0.39, aggressive 0.33 | 118.6 |
| song04 | hype *(fast aggressive battle)* | sad 0.79, relaxed 0.74, **aggressive 0.14** | 115.1 |

**song01 → sad 0.93** (the highest sad of the four) and **song03 → party 0.85** (the highest
party, and the lowest sad) are both correct, and they are the two the earlier tools got
wrong. PANNs scored 0/4, AST 0/4, CLAP 1/4.

**song02 is unreachable, and not through any fault of the model.** MTG-Jamendo trained
happy / sad / aggressive / relaxed / party. **There is no `triumphant` head.** One of the
operator's four songs cannot be described by this vocabulary however well it hears — a
vocabulary gap, which no number of clips fixes.

**song04 is the interesting failure.** An "aggressive battle" track reads
`aggressive 0.14, relaxed 0.74`. Two readings and I cannot separate them from here: either
the model is wrong, or the operator's label describes **intended use** rather than acoustic
character — the `track_title` map routes *"funk criminal (ultra slowed)"* and
*"me and the devil (slowed to perfection)"* to `hype`, and a **slowed phonk** track really
is acoustically slow and unaggressive while being used for hype edits. At 115 BPM with
danceability 0.93 the numbers are consistent with that. **This is the operator's to settle
by listening**; I flag it rather than score it.

0.9989** — the Macarena. My rule scored that a disagreement. **Both are right**, and it is
the clearest example of the two signals describing different true things.

## The verdict

Against the Phase 4 bar — swap 100%, silence handled, reproducibility above 90%, **and a
measurable gain over text alone**:

**THE FIRST THREE PASS. THE FOURTH DOES NOT.**

| | |
|---|---|
| swap control | **100%** ✓ |
| silence | abstains ✓ |
| reproducibility | **100%** ✓ |
| **gain over text on MOOD** | **6/12 loose agreement, `sad` on half the clips regardless** ✗ |

Reading the clip's own text produced a usable mood on **20 of 20** in BL-1132; audio's mood
agrees with it half the time and fires `sad` on clips whose audio is dialogue. **So the mood
fields are NOT wired**, and nothing was swept into the clip library.

**What is real and does clear everything:**

* **BPM** — 78.6 for a slow confession, 155.1 for a football highlight. Text cannot say this.
* **Danceability** — 0.0917 for that confession, **0.9989** for the Macarena. This is the
  single cleanest signal in the round.
* **Energy / loudness / key** — measured, reproducible, cheap.

Those are exactly what the operator said audio should add: *how it moves*. They are 100%
reproducible, they follow the waveform, they abstain on silence, and they measure something
neither text nor picture can express.

### What I built and left in place

* **`clippershq/audio_wsl.py`** — the boundary, with `tests/test_audio_wsl.py` (11 tests,
  all green without WSL). **It has no production call site, deliberately.** Wiring a mood
  the evidence does not support is how a helper becomes a defect.
* **`docs/WSL_AUDIO.md`** — the full rebuild, every gotcha, the disk cost.
* **`scratch/bl1135_setup.sh`** — idempotent, one command.

### What a follow-up should wire, and its precondition

`bpm`, `danceability`, `loudness_dbfs`, `key` and the raw mood scores at the MEASURED tier —
**gated on `audio_class == "music-only"`**, because that is the condition under which the
mood heads mean anything. Not a derived mood label. And the sweep needs the shard care the
brief names: `CLIP_FIELDS` *and* `build_record`'s signature and `put()`, incrementally under
the append lock, via `merge_append()`.

### The structural caveat, stated

The audio in a source clip is **whatever the reposter laid over it**. A mood read tells you
what *they* chose — the same proposition `track_title` supplies free at 28.7% fill, refused
in BL-1127 after it put a football song on a fake-note scene. A waveform measurement is
better evidence than a title string, but it answers *"match the poster"*, not *"match the
clip"*.

**Where each signal should win when they disagree:** the **text** decides mood, because it
is the clip's own claim about itself and it is what the operator's own example turns on
(*"when you hear the girls ranking the ugliest guy and you're at the top"* is sad however
the picture or the backing track sounds). The **audio** decides tempo and energy, which the
other two cannot express. The **picture** decides subject matter. On row 12 the text says
nostalgic and the audio says party at danceability 1.00 — the right output is *both*, not a
winner.

## Off-brief, stated

* **Nothing was swept into the clip library and no fields were declared.** The mood half did
  not clear its bar and I would rather ship the measurement than the capability.
* **`audio_wsl.py` ships with no caller.** Named because "a helper can be correct and called

  precondition for wiring it is written down above.
* **n=4 on the ground-truth tracks and n=12/n=8 on the clips.** Directions, not measurements;
  every interval is quoted.
* **The text moods are mine**, one annotator, recorded in `bl1135_three_signals.py` so they
  cannot drift.
* **song04 is unadjudicated.** I cannot listen; the operator can.

## Got wrong

* **I assumed the classifier polarity and inverted three of six heads.** I wrote *"checked
  against the model metadata rather than assumed"* in the code comment and then used index 0
  for all six. The first run reported the operator's upbeat summer track as `sad=0.977` and
  his sad ballad as `sad=0.150` — a perfect inversion that reads as a broken model and was a
  broken caller. `verify_polarity()` now asserts it against the shipped metadata and
  **raises**, because half-inverted moods are confident, plausible and wrong.
* **My row assembly overwrote its own identifier.** The probe returns a field called `key` —
  the *musical* key, `"Bb major"` — and `{"key": item, **result}` let it win. Every lookup
  then missed and the scorer reported **0/0 swap agreement and all-zero moods**, which read
  as total model failure and was a dict-merge order bug. The data was never wrong.
* **`audio_wsl.py` read subprocess stdout without checking the return code** — caught by
  `tests/test_no_unchecked_stdout.py`, in the one module whose entire thesis is *never return
  a clean plausible zero*. A crashed WSL would have read as "none of these clips has music".
  Fixed as a branch, not an early return: the probe deliberately exits 1 *after* writing
  per-input failures, and those must survive.

## Still broken, and whose

* **`triumphant` has no MTG head.** One of the operator's four moods is unreachable by this
  model family. His, to decide whether to relabel or accept the gap.
* **The mood heads have no music/speech gate.** They return confident numbers on dialogue
  clips. Any future wiring must gate on `audio_class`; the gate does not exist yet.
* **The Windows pip guard fires on WSL installs.** A string-matching hook cannot tell a
  Linux interpreter from a Windows one. False positive, documented in `WSL_AUDIO.md`,
  worked around by putting the install in a script rather than a bare command.
* **The VHD does not shrink.** 1.3 GB is permanent unless `diskpart … compact vdisk` is run.
