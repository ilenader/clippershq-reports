| wall clock per 1,000 | 6.07 h | 5.88 h (BL-857) | contaminated, see Limits |
| retrieval pages per clip | **1.55** | 1.00 (BL-864) | |

**Ranking does not make a clip cheaper to label. It makes it more expensive.** Ranking on `log10(play_count)` selects high-view clips, and high-view clips are longer and richer, so they cost more tokens. The baseline is measured from the unranked runner's own row log — 436 paid clips, `$0.5950`, `$0.001365` each.

This does not undermine the case for ranking, but it corrects it: **the saving was never the unit price, it is that you stop labelling all 1,591.** BL-857's $2.16-vs-$0.27 comparison is a *volume* saving, and it holds. Any claim that ranked labelling is cheaper *per clip* is now refuted.

**The second-order effect you predicted did not appear.** You expected ranking by views to select recent clips, land them on page 1, and collapse retrieval depth. Measured: **1.55 pages per clip**, *worse* than BL-864's 1.00 and worse than the library-wide estimate of 1.18 (99% of labelled clips are reachable within 2 pages). Ranking and reachability are indeed correlated — but the speech pool skews older, and that dominated. There was no depth saving to collect.

**The race guard earned its place on the first run.** With seven labellers live, two that rank identically select identically. Re-reading the library immediately before each paid call caught **7 of 40** — clips another labeller had claimed in the seconds since selection — worth ~$0.011, **18% of what the run would otherwise have spent.**

The `posted_date` control declined **32 of 32**. It cannot be read from pixels and the model did not guess.
