## 1. `SPEECH_PRESENT_MIN` 0.05 → 0.20 — the one that was doing damage

The asymmetry argument is now in the code, because it is the entire justification:

| gate | `licensed_music` → music-only | `original_sounds` → music-only |
|---|---:|---:|
| **0.05 (was)** | **50.0%** | 14.3% |
| **0.20 (now)** | **94.3%** | 22.1% |
| | **+44.3 points** | +7.9 points |

35 of 70 clips carrying a **declared** licensed track were being classified `dialogue-over-music`, and **74.3% of those sat in [0.05, 0.10)** — barely over the line. That is not dialogue. It is a sung vocal producing a small non-zero score, and a 0.05 gate converting it into "someone is talking".

Songs move 44 points; clips that really do carry speech move 8. **That asymmetry is why 0.20 and not something else.**

## 2. `SPEECH_MUSIC_MAX` 0.30 → 0.20 — moved on the tiebreaker, not because it was wrong

The inherited 0.30 was **not** wrong: ground truth from 60 hand-labelled spectrograms puts the spoken/not-spoken boundary somewhere in **0.15–0.35**, and 0.30 sits inside that band. What moved it is that the two errors do not cost the same thing, and the comment says so:

```
#   FALSE NEGATIVE  spoken dialogue scored as music -> muted and replaced -> the dialogue
#                   is DESTROYED, irreversibly, and nothing downstream can notice.
#   FALSE POSITIVE  music scored as dialogue -> ducked -> the song plays a little quieter.
#                   Harmless and reversible.
```

At 0.30 that costs **6 of 22** spoken clips; at 0.20, **4** — recall 72.7% → 81.8% for four extra harmless false positives. When one side destroys content and the other is a volume change, the threshold belongs at the low end of the band.

**The two constants now match deliberately.** Two different numbers answering the same question — *is anyone talking?* — is precisely how a 0.05 gate survived unnoticed next to a sane 0.30. A test pins them equal:

```python
def test_the_two_thresholds_agree(self):
    self.assertEqual(CS.SPEECH_PRESENT_MIN, CL.SPEECH_MUSIC_MAX)
```

## 3. The singing finding is recorded where it will be read

In `clip_speech.py`, above the constant it explains, because it **inverts the obvious worry**:

| label | n | median `speech_frac` | max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| instrumental | 15 | 0.005 | 0.217 |
| **sung** | 12 | **0.085** | **0.344** |
| spoken | 22 | **0.629** | 0.984 |

Silero does **not** mistake a chorus for dialogue wholesale — a 7× separation, and no sung clip in the sample exceeded 0.344. **66.7% of sung clips clear 0.05; only 8.3% clear 0.30.** Singing is harmless at 0.30 and severe at 0.05, which is exactly why the damage sat at the gate nobody was looking at.

## 4. Reclassification — read-only, and it moves more than the projection suggested

**The 580-clip measured pool.** `speech_frac` and `bed_dbfs` are measurements and do not move when a threshold moves, so this recomputes labels from stored numbers rather than re-decoding anything:

```
  class                     BEFORE (0.05)   AFTER (0.20)     change
  music-only                  127   21.9%    210   36.2%       +83
  dialogue-over-music         413   71.2%    332   57.2%       -81
  dialogue-only                40    6.9%     38    6.6%        -2

  reclassified: 83 of 580 clips (14.3%)
    dialogue-over-music -> music-only   81
    dialogue-only       -> music-only    2
```

**Every movement is in one direction** — toward music-only. Nothing became *more* dialogue, which is what you want from a change that removes false speech.

**Projected onto the live library** (now 1,016 clips: 614 `original_sounds`, 391 `licensed_music`, 11 other):

| class | BEFORE (0.05) | AFTER (0.20) | 95% CI after |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **music-only** | 47.1% | **51.9%** | 48.2 – 56.4% |
| dialogue-over-music | 44.0% | **39.7%** | 34.8 – 44.2% |
| dialogue-only | 7.8% | 7.3% | 4.7 – 11.2% |

BL-852 flagged its 48.3% as a **lower bound** because it was measured at the uncorrected gate. That was right: it lands at **51.9%**, +4.8 points. Mute-and-replace now covers slightly more than half the library, and ducking slightly under half.

## 5. The `dialogue-only` boundary — BL-848's record corrected in the code

BL-848 recorded the boundary as "untested in the region where it decides". On 60 clips that was true; on 572 it is not, and the comment in `clip_speech.py` now says so:

```
5.8% of clips fall below the line, 11.2% sit within +/-10 dB of it, and the closest
dialogue-only clip sits at -45.1 dBFS, a tenth of a decibel away.
```

The distribution is strongly bimodal — a tail at −180 dBFS (digital silence), bulk around −18 — so the boundary sits between p5 and p10: **sparse, but populated, and genuinely deciding.**
