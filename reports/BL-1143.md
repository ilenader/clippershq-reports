# BL-1143 — nothing about a clip predicts how well it does. Here is what that cost to prove.

**One sentence:** the library carries 2,748 clips and every one of them carries its own
`play_count` — the reposter's real result on the audience we are copying — and after testing
82 stored fields and 65 text terms against each clip's ratio to **its own account's median**,
**nothing predicts performance**; the two biggest apparent findings turned out to be our own
pipeline looking back at us, and the one content term currently in the shipped ranker does
not replicate.

**Spend: $0.0000 of a $0.00 hard cap.** No paid call was made or attempted. Zero ledger
entries are attributable to this round; the lifetime total moved only from other rounds
running concurrently. Everything here came off disk and had already been bought.

---

## 1. The headline: does the TEXT predict performance? No, and it is well-powered

Hook phrasing is the one thing we control on every render — `white_frame` ships with our
captions off because the source's own line beats ours 9 of 10 (MEMEBOT-076), so we copy
their line verbatim. If phrasing mattered, it would tell the operator which clips to pick.

**1,769 clips carry burned-in text (76.8%). 119 accounts qualify, 401 top-quintile clips
against 401 bottom-quintile, cut inside each account. 65 terms tested. Zero survive.**

The smallest q in the whole family is **0.130**. The raw prevalences say it more plainly
than any p-value:

| in a page's own... | best clips | worst clips |
|---|---|---|
| opens "POV" | 1.0% | 3.5% |
| opens "when" | 8.2% | 5.5% |
| opens **any** known template | 14.0% | 17.0% |
| carries a colon (the "X:" setup) | 12.5% | 14.0% |
| first person (I/me/my) | 22.7% | 21.4% |
| third person (he/they/bro/mfs) | 33.7% | 32.9% |
| is a question | 1.5% | 1.7% |
| characters | 90.7 | 94.1 |
| words | 15.0 | 15.8 |

**This is a well-powered null, not a shrug.** Median 95% half-width across the 58 terms that
actually vary is **2.0 percentage points**; the widest is 6.2. A template appearing 10 points
more often in a page's best work than in its worst would have been plainly visible. Nothing
came close.

**And the omnibus closes the door on "maybe it's a combination".** A logistic regression on
all 58 text features, 802 clips, five-fold cross-validation **held out by ACCOUNT** so no page
appears in both train and test:

```
AUC = 0.5182     (0.500 is chance)      permutation p = 0.329
the null's own 95% band: [0.4638, 0.5386]  -- the observed AUC sits INSIDE it
```

**BL-850 could not ask this question.** It swept 40 hypotheses and found one survivor, but
`ocr_gate_text` did not exist then (RapidOCR landed at BL-1061/BL-1095). This is the first
round with enough read text to try, and the answer is no.

## 2. Within account, not across — and the contrast is not subtle

Every clip is scored against its own page:

```
ratio      = play_count / median(play_count of that account)
log2ratio  = log2(ratio)          <- the analysis variable
```

log2 because play counts are lognormal across three orders of magnitude, and because a mean
on the raw ratio is one viral clip away from meaningless. On log2 the median clip of every
account is exactly 0.0, so accounts become commensurable and "+1.00" reads as "twice its
page's median".

**The unit of analysis is the ACCOUNT, not the clip.** 2,303 clips look like 2,303
independent observations; they are 125 pages. Every estimate is computed per account and
combined with accounts weighted equally, intervals come from a **cluster bootstrap over
accounts**, and p comes from a **within-account permutation** — shuffle the outcome inside
each page, 2,000 times.

### What pooling does, measured on the same fields

| field | pooled vs RAW play_count | pooled vs log2ratio | **within account** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `text_top_band` | **+0.306** | +0.094 | **−0.003** |
| `audio_bpm_confidence` | **+0.296** | +0.006 | **+0.030** |
| `days_behind_newest` | +0.364 | +0.088 | +0.131 |
| `caption_chars` | −0.111 | −0.013 | +0.004 |

`text_top_band` is the clean case: pooled against raw play counts it is a moderate
correlation; inside a page it is **−0.003**, and the per-account signs split 5 positive / 5
negative (sign test p = 1.000). Pooling manufactured it out of nothing but page identity.
This is the same shape BL-850 hit when a field read P = 0.12 pooled and exactly 0.500 —
chance — inside a single audit.

### Per account as well as pooled

| field | accounts | mean rho | median | positive / negative | range | sign test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `duration_s` | 125 | −0.060 | −0.057 | 51 / 71 | −0.75 .. +0.65 | p = 0.085 |
| `text_top_band` | 10 | −0.003 | −0.012 | 5 / 5 | −0.79 .. +0.62 | p = 1.000 |
| `days_behind_newest` | 125 | +0.131 | +0.120 | 85 / 40 | −0.49 .. +0.72 | p < 0.001 |

### The range restriction, measured rather than hoped away

`clip_walk.DEFAULT_VIEW_FLOOR` does not filter after collection — it **ends the walk**. Every
account is truncated at the bottom, which is the best available explanation for BL-850
finding 39 of 40 within-account hypotheses flat. So the surviving spread was measured first,
because a null on a variable with no variance is not evidence of no effect:

```
within-account log2 IQR   : median 1.37   (min 0.26, max 4.93)
within-account log2 range : median 3.71   (min 0.88, max 11.85)
-> a median account spans 13x from its quietest stored clip to its loudest
```

**13x is real room.** The nulls below are nulls, not artifacts of a flat outcome.

## 3. The two biggest "findings" were our own pipeline

The sweep's largest survivors were `has_ocr_text` at **+0.80 log2 (q = 0.014)** and
`has_vision_text` at **+0.59 (q = 0.014)** — a clip whose text we have read does 1.7x its
page's median. That reads as a finding about clips. It is a finding about **us**.

Neither field was ever run over the whole library. Both were backfilled on clips that earlier
rounds **selected**, and `clip_pipeline.rank_score` selects by `log10(play_count)`. Stated as
a number before it was measured: if presence were unrelated to views, the populated clips
would sit at mean percentile **0.500** of their own account's view ranking.

```
ocr_gate_text populated     mean percentile 0.660   95% CI [0.612, 0.707]   p = 6.8e-08  (81 accounts)
vision_on_screen_text       mean percentile 0.545   95% CI [0.534, 0.555]   p = 1.2e-13 (112 accounts)

CONTROL duration_s          mean percentile 0.534   95% CI [0.462, 0.594]   covers 0.500
CONTROL layout probe        mean percentile 0.291   95% CI [0.059, 0.523]   covers 0.500
```

Five accounts have exactly one OCR'd clip and in every case it is that account's **#1 by
views** — percentile 1.00.

**"We have its text" re-encodes "it already had views."** Same shape as `follower_count`,
which BL-850 measured at r = −0.566, p < 0.0001 and which was the walk's own progress
leaking into the data.

**The rule this leaves behind, and it is the transferable part of the round:** *a field whose
population was chosen by a previous round's ranker cannot be tested against that ranker's own
objective.* Check coverage before believing an effect.

## 4. One duplicate moved a finding across the line it was being judged by

`layout_top_letterbox` and `layout_bot_letterbox` are **byte-identical on all 2,106 rows**
that carry a layout probe. One variable, submitted to the family twice.

```
family WITH the duplicate (85 tests)   letterbox q = 0.099   SURVIVES
family deduped        (84 tests)       letterbox q = 0.114   does NOT
```

The p never changed — 0.0050 either way. Only the family did. Had I not checked, this report
would have led with a finding that its own bookkeeping created.

**Letterboxing is the one lead this round produced and its verdict is UNDERPOWERED, not
refuted** — a distinction BL-850 already insisted on for `layout` and which is preserved
here. It holds up otherwise: leave-one-account-out over 32 accounts keeps the estimate in
0.303–0.475, so no single page carries it, and it is not a proxy for duration (rho −0.044),
aspect (−0.024) or text length (−0.025). What it needs is the layout probe run over the
clips that lack it — only 131 of 2,106 probed clips are letterboxed — not a rerun of the same
32 accounts.

## 5. The one content term in the shipped ranker does not replicate

`clip_pipeline.rank_score` adds `SHORT_CLIP_BONUS = +0.15` to any clip under 10 seconds. It
is the **only** content term in the live ranker; everything else is `log10(play_count)`. The
code labels it *"PROVISIONAL: BL-850 q=0.037, n=24"*.

Re-tested here on **125 accounts instead of 24**, same design, same null, and in the shipped
binary form rather than as a continuous correlation:

```
duration < 10s vs >= 10s, within account
   +0.1652 log2   95% CI [-0.0770, +0.3991]   p = 0.1454
   276 short clips vs 484 long, across 39 accounts

continuous form:  rho = -0.060,  p = 0.0155,  q = 0.169   -- does not survive FDR

other thresholds (EXPLORATORY, not in the FDR family):
   < 5s   n/a          < 8s  +0.108 [-0.185, +0.396]      < 10s +0.165 [-0.070, +0.403]
   < 15s  +0.065 [-0.136, +0.269]   < 20s +0.088 [-0.096, +0.271]   < 30s +0.071 [-0.094, +0.232]
```

**No threshold from 8s to 30s produces an interval that excludes zero.**

**I did not remove it, and that is a decision rather than an omission.**
`clippershq/clip_pipeline.py` was claimed by **BL-1144** while this ran, so editing it would
have been a write into another round's declared path. And the term is a +0.15 nudge on a
log10 scale spanning 3.3 points: it reorders near-ties and cannot promote a quiet clip over a
loud one, so leaving it costs approximately nothing while removing it silently under another
round's claim costs a merge conflict in the one function that decides what gets rendered.
The number is recorded in `clip_rank.SHIPPED_TERM_DID_NOT_REPLICATE`, and
`tests/test_clip_rank.py` fails loudly if anyone changes the constant without re-measuring.


— view accumulation in the first month, and you cannot choose to post something older.

**`verdict_for()` on an untested field returns None, and None is not permission.** That is
the same confusion `has_ocr_text` embodied: mistaking the absence of a measurement for the
presence of one.

## 8. What I got wrong on the way

* **I wrote the permutation loop as 34.5 million scipy calls** — 138 tests × 2,000
  permutations × 125 accounts. It ran eleven minutes without finishing ten tests and was
  killed. Both statistics are *linear functionals* of the permuted outcome, so a permutation
  costs one dot product and all 2,000 cost one matmul. Same null, same seed, 5.8 seconds.
  The observed statistic is now computed with the same formula rather than with scipy, and
  a self-test checks that formula against `scipy.stats.spearmanr` on real columns before
  anything else runs.
* **I nearly reported the letterbox result.** It survived only because its own duplicate was
  in the family.
* **I nearly reported `has_ocr_text` as the finding of the round.** It was the largest effect
  in the sweep and it is an artifact of who we chose to look at.

## 10. What this means for the operator, in one paragraph

Stop trying to predict which of a page's clips will do well. **You cannot, and now there are
numbers.** 82 fields and 65 text terms, 2,303 clips, 125 accounts, and the best held-out
model of hook phrasing is AUC 0.518 against a chance band of [0.464, 0.539]. The lever is
**which pages are in the pool** — the same conclusion BL-850 reached from the other
direction, that 78.7% of the variance in clip performance is account identity. That is
exactly what the paste-accounts page built last round is for: your judgement about which
pages are good is the input that matters, and no amount of sorting inside a page will
substitute for it. Publish more, from better pages.

**The one thing worth measuring next** is letterboxing, and it needs the layout probe run
over the ~200 clips that lack it and the ~90 accounts that cannot currently contribute —
free, offline, ffmpeg only. If it holds at 90 accounts it is worth shipping; at 32 it is not.
