## 1. The view number: he is right, and the fix is not the one he asked for

He was shown *"latest 10 posts average 917 views"* for a page whose displayed posts include
1,059,956 and 36,120. **917 is exactly the median of those ten.** The function is called
`average_views`, the field is `avg_views`, the sentence said "average" — and all three return
a median.

So I measured whether the median or the mean is the better gate, over all 424 labelled pages:

| | |
|---|---|
| pages where median and mean disagree at the 1,000 floor | **18 of 424** |
| of those 18, the **median** agrees with him | **13** |
| of those 18, the **mean** agrees with him | **5** |

**The statistic is not the bug. The label was.** Switching to the mean, which is what he
literally asked for, would lose ground on his own marks. What was broken is that the number
lied about what it was — and a gate he cannot trust is worse than a gate he disagrees with.

**What shipped:** `median_views` and `mean_views` are both published, `avg_views` stays as an
alias so nothing breaks, and the sheet names each one. The page he caught now reads:

> *rejected by Views too low: the typical (median) post of the latest 10 has **917** views,
> below the **1,000** floor* — with **mean views 144,384** beside it on the card.

**A second wrong number in the same sentence:** it said "below the 2,000 floor" while
`MIN_AVG_VIEWS` has been 1,000. The sentence now reads the constant instead of repeating a
number somebody typed, and the test asserts the real floor rather than pinning the wrong one.

**Verdicts changed by the view work: zero.** The gate keeps the statistic it had, so nothing
he has already marked is invalidated. That was deliberate — he is marking as this ships.

## 3. Dialogue — the brief's hypothesis is refuted by his own labels

The brief expected: *"when he says 'too much dialogue' on a page the filter rejected, he is
usually confirming it IS a meme page and objecting only to the audio… it may be the single
largest class."*

**His marks say the opposite.** Of the 40 marks that mention dialogue:

| shape | n | he wants it |
|---|---:|---|
| filter **PASSED**, he marked it wrong | **29** | no |
| filter REJECTED, he agreed | 9 | no |
| filter REJECTED, he marked it wrong | 2 | *the two contradictions above* |

**He wants 2 of 40, and both of those say "I would reject it" in the free text.** Dialogue is
not an objection layered on a page he wants — it is disqualifying, and the dominant shape is
the filter **letting dialogue-heavy pages through**. The gate is not too aggressive here; it
is not aggressive enough.

### The audio read is correct, and it still cannot ship

The audio genuinely separates. On the 39 dialogue-flagged pages that carry audio:

| | median talking-share |
|---|---:|
| the pages he flagged | **1.00** |
| everyone else | **0.33** |

It finds exactly the pages he called out. But as a page-level gate it kills more than it
catches, at every threshold:

| threshold | catches (of 39 he flagged) | pages he WANTS that it also kills |
|---|---:|---:|
| talking-share ≥ 0.50 | 31 | **84** |
| talking-share ≥ 0.67 | 21 | **27** |

And as a requirement on top of the fixed filter, agreement goes **down** at every setting
(68.6% → 64.4% at 0.50, → 68.6% at 0.67, best is "off"). Wired as a rescue instead: 62.5%.

**So it is refused, with numbers.** And the reason is exactly the third state he described:
the pages it wrongly kills are dialogue-heavy pages he keeps — *"dialogue is okay if there is
a song in there."* Until dialogue-over-music is separated from dialogue-only, this signal
cannot be a gate. The energy read that was supposed to do that separating failed on its own
measurement (1.9 dB vs 2.7 dB); **why** it failed is now visible — it was asked to separate
two classes that overlap on loudness because both are loudness-normalised, and the
discriminator has to be *musical structure under speech*, not level difference.

## 4. Person versus page — derived, and it is not the face

The naive reading is refuted (a recurring subject appears on 34% of pages he wants). So I went
to the 94 labelled pages where RULE 9 fires and asked what separates the 30 he **wants** from
the 64 he rejects:

| flagged as a creator | n | median followers | meme_share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **he wants it** (false reject) | 30 | **324,471** | 0.40 |
| he rejects it (rule is right) | 64 | **23,295** | 0.20 |

**A 14x difference on audience, and nothing else measurable separated them.** An account that
size whose bio reads like a person is a large meme page whose bio happens to name someone.

**Robustness, because this project has been burned by one split:** the exemption gains
out-of-sample on **all five** independent splits tried — +4.4, +0.7, +2.6, +3.0, +1.4, mean
**+2.4** — and does not cost in-sample agreement on four of them. `CREATOR_EXEMPT_FOLLOWERS =
300000`.

## 5. RULE 10 is retired, and the collapse is visible in his own marking order

The caption floor shipped as the last round's win (69.9% → 75.5%). Against all 573 marks it is
**the single largest source of false rejects**: it fires on **237 of 424** pages and is
implicated in **83 of the 136** pages he wants that the filter kills.

**It was a real win, and then it reversed** — visible by replaying his marks in the order he
made them:

| corpus | floor 400 | floor OFF | |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| first 101 marks | **69.3%** | 68.3% | the rule is ahead |
| first 201 marks | 61.7% | **65.7%** | already reversed |
| all 424 marks | 65.6% | **68.6%** | |

And the floor is monotonically harmful above ~100: 200 → 67.9%, 300 → 67.2%, 400 → 65.6%,
**500 → 64.2%**, 700 → 60.6%. **The brief's suggestion to raise it to 500 measures 1.4 points
worse, not 1.2 better.** That is the same shape as "6-for-6 at 31 labels, 53% at 149", and it
is why the one-line change was not made.

`MIN_CAPTION_CHARS = 0` disables it. The threshold survives as a parameter so the next round
re-measures it rather than re-deriving it, and both its tests now exercise the mechanism
explicitly while asserting it is off by default.
