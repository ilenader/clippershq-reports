## Problem 1 — the label

### It spans the full width

`measure_label_box` returned the ink's own bounding box and the operator rejected it on
sight: *"it is a box that stops short on both sides, and on one clip it stops in the
middle."* `x` is 0 and `w` is the frame width now — **6 of 6 verified from the frames**. The
vertical half is unchanged and still does the work that matters: the strip sits **on** the
source's own text band, never across its picture.

`_inked_column_span` is kept and still called — the ink extent is what tells `meme_format`
how far the source's own line runs — but it no longer decides the strip. Without frame
dimensions the function returns **None**: a strip that cannot reach the right edge because
nothing knows where it is would be the same stops-short defect by another route.

### The text is 2.4× larger, measured off the frames

One instrument, run over both batches: the tallest contiguous run of black ink inside the
strip.

| | median cap height | range | as % of frame width |
|---|---|---|---|
| the batch he reviewed (BL-1127, n=15) | 39 px | 26–57 | 3.8% |
| **this batch (n=6)** | **95 px** | **49–135** | **10.3%** |

**Three changes, and the third is the biggest.**

* **the size cap, 58 → 200 px.** 58 px on a 1080 px frame is 5% of the width, and the cap
  was **binding on every clip** in the last batch — the fitter was returning its ceiling, not
  a fit. The real constraint is now the measured band height, which is what he asked to keep.
* **the height model → a measurement.** `len(lines) * size * 1.20` charges a full line
  *advance* for the last line, which only occupies its own ink. On a one-line label the model
  claimed `1.20·size` where the drawn block is nearer `0.73·size`, so a 132 px band was told
  it could hold 110 px of type when it holds 150. `_block_height` measures the block with
  Pillow's multiline bbox on the same face at the same size.
* **the text budget, 180 → 90 characters.** Type size in a fixed box is a function of
  **character count**. His own example line — *"POV: when my brother wakes up, he's feeling
  like this."* — is 54 characters. A 180-character budget cannot be drawn large in any box.

**`max_lines` stays 3, and I lowered it to 2 first and measured the opposite of what I
expected.** Fewer lines does not mean bigger type: on a tall band the binding constraint is
*width*, so an 87-character line at 2 rows is 44 characters wide and fits at 38 px, while at
3 rows it is 29 characters wide and fits at 57 px. The fitter searches size-descending and
takes the first fit, so allowing **more** rows can only let it stop **higher**. Caught by
rendering it and looking at the frame.

Everything he approved is unchanged and re-verified from the files: no added canvas
(0.0000% on magenta, against 36–68% for the old composition on the same counter), no
filters, no zoom, no rotation, no chipped corners, black on white.

### What is classified is the clip **as it would ship**

Frames **and** the line that would reach the white label. Two of his four rejection examples
are the text *we drew* — the reposter's caption — and two are the source's own frames. So a
perfect meme frame with a film-blurb caption is PROMOTIONAL, because a film blurb is what he

raw string would call almost the whole library hashtag-only, since the median stored caption
is 922 characters and most of the tail is the poster's `#fyp` block.

Every refusal reason is 100% correct on this sample — no MEME-HOOK was refused by dialogue
(19/19), infographic (2/2), hashtags (1/1) or promotional (30/30). The length term is the
only one that costs a true positive.

### The terms, and what each is worth

* **DIALOGUE** — first, because he said it twice. Already gated at selection from the
  previous round; `meme_format` does not name it twice, or the decision log would
  double-count the clip.
* **INFOGRAPHIC** — the cleanest separator and the thinnest evidence. Both hand-labelled
  infographics put OCR text in **10 of 10** row-tenths of the frame; the maximum across the
  other 58 is **5**. No overlap — and **n=2** on the positive side, so the threshold sits at
  7 to leave the gap on the side that costs supply rather than the side that ships a poster.
* **HASHTAGS** — fires only after the headline strip, so it catches the tags that were all
  there ever was.
* **PROMOTIONAL** — every marker was read off a real caption in the sample; none is invented.
* **LENGTH (>90 chars)** — **derived from the label, not fitted to the errors**, and that is
  the only thing that makes it defensible on n=60. The template gives the label
  `max_chars: 90`; a caption longer than that is ellipsised or drawn too small to read. It
  removes all three false positives (a Linkin Park trivia paragraph, an encyclopedia
  definition of a clown, a news item about an Avengers trailer — 300+ characters each) and
  **also removes one true positive**, a genuinely funny three-step caption about a blocked
  toilet.

### Two amendments, dated where they occurred

The definition was committed first and amended in place rather than quietly stretched:

* **at clip 10** — a clip whose burned-in line is a real hook but whose caption is an
  encyclopedia entry. Resolved OTHER, and a `source_line_is_hook` flag added and back-filled

* **at clip 14** — PROMOTIONAL broadened to cover a page advertising **itself** ("DM for paid

### What I am

**I am the labeller and there was no second reader.** No inter-annotator agreement is
quotable and the precision inherits whatever bias I have. The mitigation is only that the
definition was fixed first and every label is stored with the evidence I saw, so he can
overturn any of them by looking at the same frame.

## Why the batch is 6 and not 15

Under his own two rules — ship only meme format, and never force a song:

```
2,731  clips in the library
  175  are meme format at all                                    6.4%
   89  of those also pass the existing hard gate
    7  of those also have a song matched ON THE CLIP'S OWN PICTURE
    6  of those have a text band for the label to sit on
```

**The binding constraint is the song library, not the filter.** Four songs covering four
narrow subjects — a breakup, a female rap anthem, summer/football, battle — and everyday-life
memes are about none of them. Library-wide only **13.4%** of gated clips match any song on
evidence; among meme-format clips it is **7.9%**. Rendering 15 would have meant shipping film
blurbs or forcing a song, and he has rejected a batch for each.

**Two levers, both his to choose:**

1. **More songs.** Each new song with a rule unlocks whatever share of the 175 meme-format
   clips it describes. The only lever that raises the ceiling.
2. **Draw the source's own line instead of the caption.** **9 of 60 (15%)** of the labelled
   clips carry a real hook burned into the video while the caption we would draw is a film
   blurb or a hashtag block — *"I wasn't ready to cry this hard today"* sitting under a
   caption that is a Futurama cast list. Not done, because he did not ask for it and it
   changes where the label's text comes from.

**One thing I could not resolve.** He has now **twice** said sng_0002 is football and summer
only. The store says sng_0002 is `triumphant` — a female rap empowerment anthem — and that
**sng_0003** is the summer/football one; the clip he caught last round got sng_0003. Either
the store's mood for sng_0002 is wrong or the id in his note is. The store is
operator-authored and I did not edit it.

  on a tall band; more rows let the fitter stop higher. Found by rendering it and looking.

  A definition that ships with its own amendments already written is a definition written
  afterwards. The two that are there now are the two that actually occurred.
* **I broke two suites and three docs**, both from my own edits: the entrypoint fixture's
  caption `"a clip"` is six characters, which the new format test refuses as having no line
  of text at all, so the *budget* tests reported `no_candidates`; and three operator docs
  cite `clip_pipeline.py` by line number, which my insertions moved. Both fixed.
