# BL-699 — Can we identify WHICH SONG is in a reel? Yes, for about 7× what finding the lead costs — and the one free option cannot do it at all

**Date:** 2026-07-29 · **Type:** Research, read-only · **Spend:** $0.00 (no code, no API calls, no paid lookups — every figure below is read off vendor pages or published papers)

Evidence tiers: **VERIFIED** (primary source read) · **UNVERIFIED LEAD** (single or interested source) · **REFUTED** (checked, premise is false) · **GAP** (not establishable from what is public).

---

## The answer, before the detail

1. **AcoustID — the "obvious first try" — cannot do this job at any price.** Not "works badly": its own FAQ and its own author say it does not do short excerpts and never will. The €0.05/1,000 price is irrelevant because it answers a different question.
2. **ACRCloud is the only sane single vendor.** One paid call on a 10–15 s clip returns track + **ISRC** + **genre** under one commercial contract. That last part matters more than it looks — see §4.
3. **The speed-up trick works.** It is a deliberate countermeasure against exactly this technology and it roughly halves the hit rate. Two independent evidence lines agree.
4. **Dialogue over music — your actual case — has never been published by anyone.** Any hit-rate number you are quoted for it, including one from me, is a guess.
5. **An exact ID does not give you a usable genre.** Both obvious joins are licence-blocked, which is the same wall an earlier round hit. Take the genre from the recogniser or not at all.
6. **Cost reality: ~$9–10 per 1,000 accounts, against ~$1.08–1.45 per 1,000 the whole repost funnel currently costs.** Identifying the track costs roughly **7× more than finding the lead**.

### AcoustID — VERIFIED cheap, VERIFIED useless here

Commercial pricing is real and genuinely low (acoustid.biz): Small **€50/month for 1,000,000 searches**, Medium €100/15M, Large €500/150M, first 10k free. The free tier is "for non-commercial use only" at 3 req/s.

Licences are cleaner than expected and worth recording, because a previous round was killed by a NonCommercial clause: the **fingerprint database is CC BY-SA 3.0** (attribution + share-alike, *not* NonCommercial), server MIT, Chromaprint LGPL 2.1, and the MusicBrainz↔AcoustID mapping is public domain.

**None of that matters. REFUTED, by the project itself:**

- `acoustid.org/faq`: the service "has been designed for identifying full audio files" and cannot "identify short audio snippets." It explicitly rules out our scenario a second time — even if snippets were supported it would target "matching the original audio", **not** "audio with background noise".
- Lukáš Lalinský, Chromaprint's author, on the AcoustID list (2017-02-09): *"This kind of partial matching is not supported by AcoustID and most likely never will be."*
- Chromaprint's own README: *"It's designed to identify near-identical audio… **It's not a general purpose audio fingerprinting solution.** It trades precision and robustness for search performance. The target use cases are full audio file identification, duplicate audio file detection and long audio stream monitoring."*

A 15–90 s reel with dialogue over the music is none of those three things.

### AudD — VERIFIED from the vendor's own page

> "0+ requests per month - $5 per 1000 requests; 100 000 requests per month - $450; 200 000 requests per month - $800; 500 000 requests per month - $1800."

Volume discounts "as low as $2 per 1,000"; **300 requests free**, no card. Two operational advantages: it **accepts an HTTP URL** rather than requiring a local download, and it **accepts video files directly** (MP4, MOV, MKV, WebM) — so a reel's media URL goes straight over with no demux step. Standard endpoint takes **12-second chunks**; the enterprise endpoint takes files of unlimited length. Returns ISRC plus Spotify / Apple Music / Deezer / MusicBrainz IDs. No commercial prohibition found in the docs.

### ACRCloud

Recognises from a **10–15 s** clip by design (SDKs use 10 s; fingerprint default 12 s; sample must be <5 MB). Catalogue "more than 150 million tracks". Longer files need **multiple requests at different offsets** — a direct cost multiplier. Its **File Scanning** API additionally accepts audio URLs and platform URLs including TikTok/YouTube.

**GAP — pricing is not public.** acrcloud.com/pricing lists no tiers; real numbers sit behind a console login. A third-party blog quotes ¥320/10k, ¥3,200/100k, ¥30,000/1M *per year* (≈ $4.5/1,000). **UNVERIFIED LEAD — do not budget on it.**

### Shazam — ruled out on terms, not capability

**VERIFIED**, from the Apple Developer Agreement:
> "You may not use or compare the data provided by the ShazamKit APIs for the purpose of improving or creating another audio recognition service."
> Applications "may not be designed or marketed for compliance purposes (e.g., music licensing and royalty auditing)."
> "You agree not to copy, modify, translate, create a derivative work of, publish or publicly display ShazamKit Content in any way."

Writing recognised track metadata into a lead database and shipping it in a customer sheet is hard to square with "not … publish or publicly display … in any way". ShazamKit is also an **on-device SDK** (iOS/macOS/tvOS/watchOS/visionOS/Android), not a server REST API, so it does not fit a Python batch pipeline. `ShazamIO` is self-described as "reverse engineered"; that is legal exposure, not a discount.

## 2. THE HARD CASE

### Re-encoding and loudness normalisation — **not a problem**

**VERIFIED.** Peak-based (Shazam-family) fingerprinting has "high robustness to noise addition, filtering, **MP3 coding, and resampling**". Instagram's transcode and loudness normalisation are exactly those transforms. This is the one axis where the literature is unambiguously reassuring.

### Clip length 15–90 s — **fine**, for the commercial services

**VERIFIED.** ACRCloud wants 10–15 s; AudD's standard endpoint takes 12 s. Academic work agrees fingerprinting "can achieve over 90% overall accuracies using just 10-second duration data." 15–90 s is comfortably above the floor.

The cost consequence is real though: neither service scans a 90 s file in one standard call. You either pick one window (cheap, may land on a dialogue-only stretch) or send several (N× the price). **This is the main driver of the cost model in §6.**

### Pitch / speed change — **the killer**

**VERIFIED (peer-reviewed).** "Shazam-based systems are **not robust to pitch shift and tempo change attacks**." Scale transformations are the classic weakness of peak-based methods, because stretching the spectrogram along either axis moves the very peaks the hash is built from. From **PeakNetFP** (arXiv 2506.21086, 2026), measuring QuadFP, a peak-based system in the Shazam family:

| condition | QuadFP Top-1 |
|---|---|
| 10 s query, 0.7–1.4× stretch | ~90%+ |
| **2 s query, same range** | **48%** |
| **extreme stretch (0.5×)** | **3.6%** |

"Performance rapidly deteriorates as the stretching deviates further from 1." Neural systems *do* fix this — NeuralFP ~98%, PeakNetFP >90% even at extreme factors — **but neither is a service you can buy.** They are research code, and the gap between "solved in a 2026 paper" and "purchasable" is the whole problem.

**UNVERIFIED LEAD — the only published short-form test, and it is vendor marketing.** ClipMusic's own blog, 97 TikTok videos:

| service | overall | original audio | **sped-up** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shazam | 58.8% | 95.8% | **25.8%** |
| ACRCloud | 72.2% | 93.8% | **58.1%** |
| AudD | 64.9% | 91.7% | **45.2%** |
| *ClipMusic (the author)* | *89.7%* | *97.9%* | *90.3%* |

Weight it correctly: n=97, published by the party selling the winner, and the methodology favours its own link-based approach. **The absolute numbers are not trustworthy. The shape is** — a 35–70 point collapse from original to sped-up independently reproduces what the peer-reviewed literature predicts. Two unrelated sources agreeing on direction and rough magnitude is worth more than either alone.

**Conclusion: the repost trick works.** It is aimed at precisely this technology and it does what it is meant to do. Plan for roughly half the sped-up subset being unidentifiable.

### Dialogue over music — **GAP. Nobody has published this.**

This is your actual case and it is the weakest-evidenced axis in the whole report. What exists is adjacent, not on-point:

- **Encouraging, VERIFIED-ish:** Shazam-style fingerprinting maintains "high identification accuracy across 20 dB, 10 dB, and 0 dB SNR" against white/crowd/street noise with 10 s snippets (Shazam-CLAP-Embedding-Analysis). 0 dB means the interferer is at *equal power* to the music. The Philips fingerprint is documented recognising music at **−4 to −7 dB SNR**. Both suggest real headroom.
- **Discouraging, and it is the more relevant point:** speech is **not** stationary noise. "The spectral content of speech differs from that of music, necessitating modifications to current audio fingerprinting approaches." Broadband noise raises the floor evenly; speech puts strong transient harmonic peaks in the 300 Hz–4 kHz band **where music peaks also live**, competing for the exact maxima the fingerprint is built from. **Robustness to white noise does not transfer to robustness under dialogue**, and no published work found here measures the latter on a commercial service.
- The ClipMusic short-form test does not cover dialogue at all.

**Honest position: expect degradation, magnitude unknown.** A one-afternoon pilot on 100 real reels would settle it for the price of the free tiers (AudD gives 300 free requests, ACRCloud a no-card trial). That measurement is worth more than any further reading.

## 3. ACOUSTID COVERAGE OF TRENDING SHORT-FORM AUDIO

Moot after §1, but the question deserves its answer, and the answer is not "partial" — it is "effectively zero", for three independent reasons any one of which is sufficient.

1. **It cannot take a snippet at all** (§1).
2. **REFUTED by construction — sped-up edits cannot match.** Chromaprint identifies "**near-identical** audio". A sped-up or pitch-shifted edit is not near-identical to the catalogue master; it is exactly the transform the algorithm is weakest against.
3. **It is a catalogue database filled by people tagging their own music libraries.** Contents are "user-submitted audio fingerprints… crowd-sourced by thousands of contributors" (1,098,807 of them). The contribution act is running Picard or beets over a **collection of files**. Nobody is submitting fingerprints of this week's viral sound, and a viral sped-up edit is not a catalogue release, so it has no MusicBrainz recording to map to either.

**VERIFIED coverage figure worth keeping regardless:** of 75,388,039 AcoustIDs, **only 30.36% have at least one linked recording** (22,889,845), against 21,732,326 recordings and 93,019,503 fingerprints. So even on catalogue audio, ~70% of the database can at best say "this is the same as something else I've seen" with no name attached. *(Note a vendor/stats discrepancy: acoustid.biz still advertises "over 30 million fingerprints, almost 10 million mapped to MusicBrainz" — stale copy; the live stats page is the primary source.)*

## 4. WHAT COMES WITH AN IDENTIFICATION — and the licence trap on the far side

**VERIFIED — ACRCloud returns the identifiers *and* the genre in the recognition response.** From its own metadata reference: `isrc`, `upc`, `iswc`, **`genres`**, `label`, `release_date`, `composers`, `lyricists`, plus external IDs for **Spotify, Deezer, MusicBrainz and YouTube**.

**VERIFIED CAVEAT:** "Only ACRID, Track Title, Artists Name, Album Name and `*_time_offset_ms` fields are required, **other fields are optional**." `genres` and `isrc` are best-effort per match, not guaranteed. Their fill rate is a **GAP** — unmeasurable without running the API, and it should be the second thing any pilot measures.

**VERIFIED — AudD returns ISRC** (inside the Spotify/Apple Music payloads) plus Spotify, Apple Music, Deezer and MusicBrainz IDs. Genre is reachable, but only via Apple Music's `genreNames` — i.e. it rides on a third party's metadata and drags that third party's terms along with it.

### The premise is half right, and the wrong half is the expensive one

The brief says "a MusicBrainz ID or ISRC would give an exact join to genre". **The join is exact. The genre data at the far end is licence-blocked on both obvious routes.**

**VERIFIED — MusicBrainz, and the distinction is sharper than previously recorded in this repo.** The **genre entity** — the list of genre names — is **core data, CC0**. But supplementary data, CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, "includes: user submitted annotations, **tags (including genre associations)** and ratings."

> **The genre vocabulary is free. The link from a recording to a genre is NonCommercial.**

The link is the only part with any value here. MetaBrainz sells a commercial licence for it; that is the supported route, and it is a negotiation rather than a price list.

**VERIFIED — Spotify is worse, and is a hard blocker for a stored-lead product.** Developer Terms §IV.3.1: *"you may not store, aggregate or create compilations or databases of Spotify Content, other than as strictly necessary to operate your SDA"*, plus *"Do not store Spotify Content indefinitely."* §IV.2.5 forbids transferring Spotify Content to third parties. Writing a Spotify-derived genre into `master_leads.csv` and shipping it in a customer sheet is squarely inside what those clauses prohibit.

**So the only clean genre route is to take the genre from the recogniser itself, as part of the paid recognition contract.** ACRCloud does that natively; AudD only by proxying Apple Music. That is a real, non-obvious and probably decisive advantage for ACRCloud — bigger than any price difference between them.

## 5. THE FILM / TV FALLBACK

The idea is sound: knowing the clip is *Game of Thrones* gives the genre directly, and on repost pages the audio is more often a scene than a song. But the cheap route does not exist.

**REFUTED — ACRCloud does not ship a movie/TV catalogue you can query.** Its TV/film capability is **Custom Content Recognition**, and *custom* means bring-your-own. The documented workflow: create a bucket → *"Upload **my_song_1.mp3** into the bucket you just created"* → create a project → bind → integrate. Its own wording is "recognize **your own** songs, advertisements, TV shows/series or movies". To identify a Game of Thrones scene you would first have to obtain and ingest Game of Thrones audio — acquiring the licensed content you were trying to identify. The advertised capability is real; it just does not answer "what show is this?" for content you do not already hold. *(Their separate **Music** bucket is the ready-made one, and it is music only.)*

**VERIFIED — Audible Magic does have a ready-made film and TV reference database.** From audiblemagic.com/technology: it identifies "music recordings, **motion pictures, and TV shows**"; the registry spans "music, TV, and film", with "more than 140,000 music labels from over 150 different territories" and content from **Disney, Warner Bros and Sony Pictures**. Critically, **the studios register it themselves** — "fingerprint and metadata submissions… are now integrated into standard release workflows across most recognized international media companies, including major record labels, TV studios and motion picture studios." Named customers include Twitch, SoundCloud and Dailymotion.

Its robustness claims are marketing, not peer-reviewed, and unusually strong: "immune to many types of transformations or background noise", can "identify even **extreme manipulations of rate, pitch, or tempo**", "identification rates at 99.99%" with "virtually zero false positives", works from "only small clips of audio". If the rate/pitch claim survived audit it would dissolve the single biggest failure mode in §2 — which is exactly why it should not be believed without a trial.

**GAP — no public pricing.** Audible Magic is enterprise anti-piracy; pricing is by contract and there is no self-serve tier. Vobile/Pex is the other credible holder of film+music ACR at scale (Pex tracking quoted "from $1 per file per month" — a different product shape, not comparable per-lookup).

**Practical read:** the film/TV route is a procurement exercise, not an API key. It is the right answer technically and the wrong answer for a round that wants to ship this month.

## 6. VERDICT

### The cheapest stack that actually works

**ACRCloud, single vendor, standard identification API.** Reasons, in order of weight:

1. It is the only option that returns **track + ISRC + genre under one commercial contract** (§4). Everything else leaves you needing a genre join that is licence-blocked.
2. It is built for a 10–15 s clip; your clips are 15–90 s (§2).
3. It scored highest of the three real services on the only short-form test that exists, and by the widest margin on the sped-up subset that matters most (58.1% vs AudD 45.2% vs Shazam 25.8%) — vendor-published, so directional only.

**AudD is the better *pilot* instrument** — 300 free requests, no card, accepts a URL and accepts MP4 directly, so you can measure your real hit rate this week with no integration and no spend. Use it to answer the dialogue question, then buy ACRCloud if the answer is good.

### Cost, honestly

A 15–90 s reel does not fit one standard call. Assume **2 calls per account** (two windows) as the realistic floor, 3–6 if you want to be thorough on a 90 s clip.

| | per call | ×2 calls / account | per 1,000 accounts |
|---|---|---|---|
| AudD (list) | $0.005 | $0.010 | **~$10** |
| AudD (volume) | $0.002 | $0.004 | ~$4 |
| ACRCloud (*unverified* ~$4.5/1k) | ~$0.0045 | ~$0.009 | **~$9** |

**Against the repost funnel's measured $1.08–1.45 per 1,000 confirmed pages, adding track identification is roughly a 7× increase in the cost of a lead.** That is the number to decide on, and it is not a rounding error.

### Expected hit rate — and why I will not give you one number

Decomposed, with each factor's evidence tier:

| factor | effect | tier |
|---|---|---|
| clip length 15–90 s | no penalty | VERIFIED |
| IG re-encode + loudness normalisation | no penalty | VERIFIED |
| clean commercial track, unmodified | ~92–94% ceiling | UNVERIFIED LEAD (vendor) |
| **sped-up / pitch-shifted subset** | **→ ~45–58%** | UNVERIFIED LEAD, corroborated by peer-reviewed direction |
| **dialogue over the music** | **unknown, expected negative** | **GAP — nobody has measured it** |
| audio is a film score / dialogue only, not a track | not in a music catalogue at all | structural |

The ceiling is ~92% and every remaining factor pushes down, two of them hard. **A blended figure for your population would be a fabrication, and the one axis that would most move it is the one nobody has published.** The pilot in §2 costs $0 and settles it.

### If accuracy under dialogue is poor — say it plainly

Then this does not ship, and the reason is not the technology. **You already get declared genre for free from captions** on templated accounts (BL-695), at zero marginal cost and with `declared` provenance. Audio track ID would cost ~7× the funnel to fill in the *untemplated* remainder, on a population where the audio is frequently a movie scene with no catalogue track at all (BL-691 measured **0 of 108** clips carrying track metadata). The honest sequencing is: **measure the dialogue hit rate on 100 real reels for $0 first.** If it lands under ~50%, close this line the way mood classification and drop detection were closed, and say so in one line rather than building a fallback for it.

## What was not established

- **Dialogue-over-music hit rate on any commercial recogniser.** The central question. No published measurement exists; adjacent white-noise SNR results do not transfer (§2).
- **ACRCloud's real price.** Behind a console login. Every ACRCloud cost here is an unverified third-party figure.
- **`genres` / `isrc` fill rate** in ACRCloud responses — documented as optional fields, rate unknown.
- **Audible Magic and Vobile/Pex pricing** — enterprise contract, nothing public.
- **Whether Audible Magic's "extreme manipulations of rate, pitch, or tempo" claim survives audit.** It is the single most consequential unverified claim in this report.
- **Music density in the actual population** — what fraction of repost-page reels contain a commercial track at all, as opposed to score or pure dialogue. Measurable offline from payloads already held; not attempted this round.

## Method

All findings are from vendor primary pages, official documentation, project FAQs, mailing-list posts by the named maintainer, and arXiv papers, read directly. No API key was created, no lookup was performed, no audio was downloaded, and no code in the repository was read or changed.
