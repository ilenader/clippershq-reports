## Headline: the pass completed, but "the library" is not what it was

The pass was asked to resume and finish. **It finished — it exhausted its work list, was not killed, and did not hit its budget cap.** It labelled every clip that existed when it started.

That is not the same as "the library is labelled", because the library did this while it ran:

| | clips |
|---|---:|
| BL-849 start | 634 |
| BL-849 report time | 664 |
| **this round, at the end** | **2,003** |

**A different round appended ~1,339 clips mid-pass.** The labeller takes its work list once at startup, so those clips were never in scope — they arrived after the list was fixed. The result is a number that looks like a regression and is not one:

- **412 clips carry a vision description** — up from 178, the pass more than doubled its output
- **20.9% of the current library** — down from 26.8%, because the denominator tripled

Both statements are true. The first is what this round did; the second is what the library looks like. Reporting only the second would misrepresent a completed pass as a failed one.

**Finishing the remaining 1,584 costs ~$2.15 at the measured rate — more than this round's entire $1.50 budget.** That is the honest reason the library is not fully labelled, and it is a *budget* bound, not the wall-clock bound BL-849 hit.

The rate did not rise as the sample grew — the single event from BL-849 is still the only one, now measured against 2.3× the clips. The 95% interval on 1/412 runs roughly 0.006%–1.3%. At 0.24% across a fully-labelled 2,003-clip library that is **~5 rows of confident nonsense**, and unlike BL-844 **each one is identifiable**, because the control is stored per row.

**`vision_control_declined: false` survives `put()` — confirmed directly, not assumed.** `False` reads back through `field()` as `False` with `declared` provenance; `None` reads back `absent`. This was the failure mode worth checking: `put()` marks falsy values `absent`, which would have made every fabricated row unreadable — silently hiding precisely the rows that need flagging.

## The three-way comparison — 19 clips, and they are barely related

BL-849 managed 4. This round did **19**, run as two processes over disjoint candidate slices once the labelling pass freed the library.

**Content overlap (Jaccard, 4+ letter words), mean across 19 clips:**

| pair | shared content |
|---|---:|
| VISION vs OCR | **6.5%** |
| VISION vs SUBS | **2.2%** |
| OCR vs SUBS | **6.6%** |

**They are not substitutes and they are not redundant. They are three different questions:**

| | mean chars | median | empty | word-repetition |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **VISION** — what is *happening* | 216 | 206 | **0/19** | 22% |
| **OCR** — what is *burned into the frame* | 250 | 300 | **0/19** | **56%** |
| **SUBS** — what is *spoken* | 201 | 300 | **4/19** | 20% |

Four worked examples:

**`3371509042222320195_…`**
- **VISION** — *"The clip juxtaposes scenes from a TV show with a man reacting to them…"* → the **structure**
- **OCR** — *"be careful how you treat people HILDI be careful how you treat people…"* → the **caption bar**
- **SUBS** — *"How come? With your dad? Barney, that's not my dad. His name is Greg…"* → the **dialogue**

**`3459932246695800622_…`**
- **VISION** — *"an animated sequence featuring Darkseid's forces, with Batman eventually appearing…"*
- **OCR** — *"Let's not forget how Darkseid felt Batman aura from the other side of the universe MCOVEREACTSS"*
- **SUBS** — *"You're too late. Father's home… I bet you love her. I know all about you."*

**`3886124444436040912_…`**
- **VISION** — *"A woman tells a man and woman that her dog, Oliver, is terminally ill and she's about to put him to sleep."*
- **OCR** — *"When you think it's just a meal but it turns DARK real quick"*
- **SUBS** — *"Lady, that food will straight up kill your dog. Yeah, that's not so much a concern right now…"*

**`3551419273002211933_…`**
- **VISION** — *"A split image shows Wolverine and Sabretooth on top, and Captain America and the Winter Soldier on the bottom."*
- **OCR** — *"Wolverine and Sabretooth Vs Cap And Winter Soldier As Pictured"*
- **SUBS** — *"Have to take some time to look at this match up a little bit more deeply but off the top of my head…"*

**Two findings that only appear at n=19:**

**OCR repeats itself 56% of the time** — nearly triple vision's 22%. The caption bar is static, so per-frame OCR re-reads one string across every sampled frame. That is BL-839's dedup problem surfacing as text, and it means OCR's 250-char mean is worth roughly 110 chars of distinct content — **the shortest of the three, not the longest.**

**`subtitles_available` over-promises by 21%.** All 19 clips were selected *because* the field was true. **4 returned nothing.** The flag records that the platform offers a subtitle track, not that fetching it yields text. Anything gating on `subtitles_available` should expect one in five to come back empty.

## What the library can now answer

| field | clips | of library | accounts covered |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `caption` | 2,000 | 99.9% | **172 of 172** |
| `subtitles_available` | 2,003 | 100% | 172 of 172 |
| **`vision_scene`** | **419** | **20.9%** | **69 of 172** |
| **`vision_beats`** | 419 | 20.9% | 69 of 172 |
| `vision_on_screen_text` | 320 | 16.0% | 63 of 172 |
| `vision_title` | 290 | 14.5% | 63 of 172 |
| `content_genre` (BL-847) | 355 | 17.7% | **34 of 172** |

*(419 rather than 412: seven clips were labelled by BL-849 before this round's pass began.)*

**The account-independence claim holds, and it is the real result.** Vision and `content_genre` sit at similar library-wide coverage — 20.9% against 17.7% — but vision reaches **69 accounts against 34**. BL-847's `content_genre` concentrated 70.8% of its coverage in two accounts because it depends on what those accounts declare. **Vision depends only on the video existing**, so its coverage spreads across every account the pass reached, in rough proportion to how many clips each has. Per-account fill runs 9%–50% with no account at zero-among-those-reached.

**87.6% of labelled clips carry a *usable* description** — scene text, three or more timestamped beats, and a clean control. That is 18.3% of the library today, and would be ~87.6% of it if the pass were funded to completion.

**What it still cannot answer:** anything about the 1,584 unlabelled clips, and `vision_title` on any clip — **290 rows offer a source title and nothing has ever checked one against the caption.** That check is free, the captions are already stored at 99.9%, and it is the obvious next measurement.

## Limits

- **The library is 20.9% labelled, not 100%.** Completing it costs ~$2.15 against a $1.50 round budget. Nothing is lost by stopping: the labeller appends per clip, skips anything already carrying `vision_scene`, and resumes without redoing paid work.
- **The denominator moved by 3× mid-round**, so every percentage is as of the moment quoted. The absolute counts are stable; the ratios are not.
- **One fabrication is still one event.** 0.24% is a point estimate with a wide interval. It is firmer than BL-849's 0.56% only because the denominator grew, not because a second event was observed.
- **The three-way ran on 19 of 75 available candidates**, chosen as the first available in two disjoint slices rather than sampled at random. The overlap figures are stable across both slices, but this is not a random sample.
- **`vision_title` remains entirely unverified** at 290 rows.
- **The suite ran while another round held files open**; the 62 green results are trustworthy, and the one red is attributed above by file ownership rather than by re-running it in isolation.
