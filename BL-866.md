## The depth estimate against the outcome, which is the point of item 1

Budgeted **before** spending, against each clip's own `estimated_pages()` rather than a flat rate:

| | estimate | **actual** | error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| retrieval pages | 443 (1.59/clip) | **760 (2.78/clip)** | **+72%** |
| retrieval | $0.2658 | **$0.4560** | +72% |

| **total** | **$0.5160** | **$0.8863** | **+72%** |
| loaded per 1,000 | $1.86 | **$3.45** | +86% |

**The estimator under-called the bill by 72%, and it is honest about why.** `estimated_pages()` documents itself as a LOWER BOUND — it counts only clips *we hold*, while the live feed also contains posts never stored, so "true depth is worse, never better." This round measures that bound's slack for the first time: **1.75× on this pool.**

That is the practical rule worth carrying: **budget from `estimated_pages()` and you will be short by roughly three quarters.** BL-865's warning that the average rises with coverage was directionally right but incomplete — the mix moved the *other* way here (BL-865 had already consumed the deep band, leaving a pool at 1.59 estimated pages), and the cost still came in high, because the bound is loose rather than because the clips were deep.

**Where the $3.45/1,000 sits:** between BL-865's $5.36 (deep band, 5.47 pages) and the ~$1.50 one-page figure. There is still no single all-in number, and quoting one remains wrong.

## Limits

- **`clip not found in 12 page(s)` is treated as transient and gets a retry.** At the depth cap that is probably a deleted clip and arguably permanent, but "not found" can also be an API hiccup, and I would rather spend one more call than retire a live clip by mistake. 4 clips sit in that state; a later round with evidence either way should reclassify.
- **The 72% estimator gap is measured on one pool of 278 clips.** It is a real receipt against a real prediction, not a projection, but one pool is one pool — the slack depends on how much of each account's live feed we hold, which varies.
- **No library-wide all-in cost is recoverable.** $0.8196 of the ledger's vision retrieval is explicitly back-filled estimate. Only this round's and BL-865's figures are receipts.
- **8 clips are retired for `unparseable`, not for a media fault.** They hit the attempt ceiling before BL-889's shared parser landed, and that parser reportedly lifts 8/11 → 11/11. Those 8 may well be labellable now and will never be retried, because the ceiling does not know the parser changed. **That is a real cost of the retire-forever design and it should be revisited.**
- **My runner script was edited by another round after my run finished** (raising `MAX_TOKENS` 2000 → 5000 on BL-904's evidence). **This round's 257 labels were all produced at 2000**, which BL-892 found truncates a small share of beat-heavy responses.
