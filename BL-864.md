## Why it stopped at 74.7%, and it is not the budget

All six workers ran their slices to exhaustion. **None hit its budget cap** — zero `BUDGET REACHED` lines across all six. The pass ended because it ran out of clips it could *fetch*:

| | |
|---|---:|
| clips targeted | 1,584 |
| model calls actually made | 1,023 |
| **never reached the model** | **519** |
| unparsed responses | 40 |

**519 clips could not have their media retrieved.** `clip_media.retrieve()` reports the reason in `res["error"]`, and **the labeller throws it away** — `if not res.get("ok"): continue`, with no log line. That is why BL-863 never saw this: the failure is completely silent, and 519 silent skips look exactly like a finished pass.

**The cause is `max_pages=1` against a by-views ordering.** The only clip source is `/gql/user/clips`, which returns roughly 12 per page. The unlabelled share rises monotonically with account size:

| account size | unlabelled | of that bucket |
|---|---:|---:|
| ≤12 clips | 59 of 1,062 | **5.6%** |
| 13–24 | 252 of 707 | 35.6% |
| 25–60 | 15 of 27 | 55.6% |
| **60+** | **185 of 207** | **89.4%** |

A small account fits entirely on page one. A 60+ clip account exposes ~12 and hides the rest.

**Note what this is not.** The obvious guess — that the missing clips are simply the older ones — is **refuted**. Recency rank within account is essentially identical for both groups (labelled mean 50.2, unlabelled 47.5). The endpoint is `user_clips_by_views`, so page one is the *top twelve by view count*, not the twelve newest. Depth in the ranking is what matters, and it is uncorrelated with age.

**Reaching those 511 costs ~$1.99** — up to 2,555 more retrieval calls at $0.0006 plus ~$0.46 of model — against ~$0.18 left in this round. That is the honest reason the last quarter is unlabelled, and it is a *retrieval* bound, not the budget bound BL-863 hit or the wall-clock bound BL-849 hit. Each round has stopped for a different reason; this one is the first that more money would actually fix.

## `vision_title` is wrong more than a third of the time

The free check BL-863 left on the table. Captions are stored at 99.9%, so this cost nothing.

**Of 1,036 rows carrying a `vision_title`, 83 have a caption that literally declares one** (`Title:`, `Movie:`, `Show:` …). That is the only clean external label available, and it is the real accuracy number:

| verdict | rows | |
|---|---:|---:|
| exact | 41 | 49.4% |
| strong (one contains the other) | 11 | 13.3% |
| partial (≥50% token overlap) | 1 | 1.2% |
| **no agreement** | **30** | **36.1%** |
| **AGREEMENT** | **53 of 83** | **63.9%** |

**More than one row in three names a different title than the caption declares.** These are not near-misses:

| vision said | caption declared |
|---|---|
| The Black Phone | Dear Zoe |
| Crazy Rich Asians | Ocean's 8 |
| Varsity Blues | Two and a Half Men |
| The Notebook (2004) | Greenleaf |
| That '70s Show | United States of Al |
| The Dark Knight | Léon: The Professional (1994) |
| Captain America: Civil War | Avengers: Endgame (2019) |

This lands close to [BL-731](BL-731.md), which measured film identification from a single frame at **20% confidently wrong** and declined 0% of the time. Full video does not rescue it — **36.1% here is worse**, on a population where the ground truth is written on the post.

A weaker corroboration exists: **53.6%** of titled rows have the title appearing *somewhere* in the caption, and **53.9%** are corroborated by either route. But a caption mentioning a film is evidence, not a label, and it cannot distinguish a correct identification from a caption that happens to name the film being reacted to.

**Two honest caveats.** A disagreement is not automatically a vision error — a reaction clip's caption may name the reaction video while vision names the film inside it. And the declaring population is 83 rows, 8.0% of titled rows; the 95% interval on 63.9% runs roughly 53%–74%. **The direction is not in doubt; the second decimal is.**

**Recommendation: do not gate anything on `vision_title`.** `vision_scene` and `vision_beats` describe what is on screen and are what the matching signal should use. Recorded in the schema notes.

| | |
|---|---:|
| accounts with **any** vision label | **169 of 172 = 98.3%** |
| accounts **fully** labelled | 98 of 172 = 57.0% |
| accounts `content_genre` reaches | 34 of 172 = 19.8% |
| per-account vision fill | **median 100%**, mean 87% |

**Vision reaches 98.3% of accounts; `content_genre` reaches 19.8%.** That gap is the whole argument. `content_genre` is parsed from what an account chooses to declare in its caption, so it concentrates on the minority of accounts that write structured captions — BL-847 measured 70.8% of its coverage in two accounts. Vision depends only on the video existing, so its coverage tracks *retrievability* rather than *account behaviour*, and the median account is now at **100% fill**.

That is why vision is the right matching signal: it does not ask the account's permission.

## Recorded in the schema notes (item 4)

Both findings written into `CLIP_FIELDS` in `clippershq/clip_library.py`, where a consumer reading the field list will hit them:

- **On `subtitles_available`** — it is `declared` and records that the platform *offers* a track, not that fetching returns text. BL-863 selected 19 clips because the flag was true and **4 returned nothing**. A consumer must handle true-flag-plus-empty-text or silently drop one clip in five.
- **On the vision block** — the three text signals overlap by 2–7%, so they are not substitutes. **OCR is the shortest signal, not the longest**: it repeats itself 56% of the time against vision's 22% and subtitles' 20%, because the caption bar is static and per-frame OCR re-reads it on every sampled frame. Its ~250-char mean is worth ~110 chars of distinct content. Ranking by raw length picks OCR first and is wrong.
- Also noted there: `vision_title` is the least trustworthy field in the block, with a pointer to this round's measurement.

## Limits

- **25.5% of the library is still unlabelled**, and unlike previous rounds more money genuinely fixes it: ~$1.99 with `max_pages` raised. The 511 are concentrated in large accounts, so they are not a random sample — **any statistic computed over labelled clips under-represents high-volume accounts.**
- **The IG half of the spend is an estimate**, not a receipt, because `spend.json` is shared with three concurrent rounds and this round's retrieval calls carry no campaign label.
- **The `vision_title` accuracy figure rests on 83 rows.** The interval is roughly 53%–74%. It is also scored against a regex that identifies declared titles; prose captures are dropped (one, `Tower of God`, was a false disagreement caused by the un-tightened version and is excluded from the final figure), but the extractor has not been hand-audited row by row.
- **A disagreement is not proven to be a vision error** — reaction content legitimately has two titles in play. No row was hand-checked against the video.
- **Six workers were no faster than three** (~18 clips/min either way), so the retrieval path, not concurrency, sets the pace. Worth knowing before anyone scales workers to finish the tail.
- **The 40 unparsed responses were paid for and produced nothing**; they are counted in the spend and not in the coverage.
